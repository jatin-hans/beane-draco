# Promotion name for a Perth chicken-chain lunch deal

## Executive summary

The promotion was Red Rooster’s **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips for $5**. In 2015, it was a $5 quarter-chicken and small-chips lunch deal available daily from 11 am to 4 pm.[1] Red Rooster is the Perth-founded roast-chicken chain indicated by the clues.[2][3] **$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch** is a later name for a related version, not the documented 2015 wording.[4][1]

## The chicken chain

**The chain is Red Rooster, the Australian roast-chicken fast-food chain whose first store opened in Kelmscott, Perth, in 1972. Peter and Theo Kailis, brothers from a Greek-migrant family, launched it with nine other investment partners.** Establishing the operator matters because quarter chicken and chips was a common Australian takeaway combination: the brand and the offer’s formal name, rather than the food alone, identify the promotion.[2][3]

### Which chicken chain is described?

**The chain is Red Rooster, an Australian fast-food chain known for roast chicken.** Contemporary descriptions likewise identify it as an Australian fast-food restaurant chain founded in 1972 that specialises in roast chicken.[5][2]

Roast chicken is the decisive product clue. A historical account contrasts Red Rooster with Kentucky Fried Chicken by saying KFC promoted its signature fried chicken while Red Rooster focused on roast chicken.[2] Later descriptions still call Red Rooster a roast-chicken brand that had served roast chicken for more than 40 years by April 2017.[6] The brand’s own later refresh retained the original roast as a central product while adding crispy fried chicken and burgers.[7]

The red-signage clue also fits Red Rooster’s long-standing visual identity. Its refreshed logo was explicitly inspired by the original 1970s design, linking the modern brand to the period in which the chain began.[7] A Red Rooster brand-development account calls it an Australian chicken-shop icon with national recognition, while the present company description uses the shortened name Reds.[7][8]

Red Rooster’s historical menu supplies a useful distinction between its core roast-chicken identity and individual meal packs. Peter Kailis developed the Hawaiian Pack, comprising quarter roast chicken, chips, a pineapple fritter and a banana fritter; it later became the Classic Tropicana or Tropicana Pack, with a second pineapple ring replacing the banana fritter.[9][2] That is a named meal, not the lunchtime promotion addressed in The lunchtime promotion.

Taken together, the roast-chicken focus, red identity, and Perth founding account identify the chain as Red Rooster, not an unnamed chicken takeaway or a primarily fried-chicken rival.[5][8][2]

### Which Perth suburb did the chain start in back in

**Red Rooster’s first store opened in Kelmscott, a Perth suburb, in 1972.** Real Commercial states that Peter Kailis, his brother Theo, and nine other investment partners launched the first Red Rooster in 1972 by acquiring an existing chicken shop in Kelmscott.[2] PerthNow likewise says that the first store opened in Kelmscott in 1972.[10]

Other business histories give the same place-and-year account in slightly different language. Franchise Business says that Kailis and nine partners invested in an existing chicken rotisserie shop in Kelmscott, Perth, in 1972, and that this was when the Red Rooster business was born.[3] QSR Media says Red Rooster was founded in 1972 and opened its first restaurant in Kelmscott, Western Australia.[6] Perth Voice similarly describes the company as having started in Perth in 1972, with its first restaurant in Kelmscott.[11]

The early commercial setting was a chicken rotisserie shop rather than a newly constructed national-chain outlet. That detail explains why accounts often describe the event as an investment in, acquisition of, or takeover of an existing business rather than simply a new-store opening.[2][3] It also fits the chain’s early product emphasis: a 1973 price reference lists quarter chicken and chips at 79 cents.[3]

There is a competing local-origin account. Mark McGrath, commenting on a Facebook post, says that the first Red Rooster shop opened on Wanneroo Road in Tuart Hill in 1971, founded by his father Wilfred McGrath together with Bill and Lee Karas.[12] A separate SmartCompany article says that George Kailis’s son founded Red Rooster in 1974.[13] Those accounts conflict with the repeated Kelmscott-1972 first-store account rather than changing the identity of the chain.

The prevailing founding account places Red Rooster’s first store in Kelmscott in 1972; Tuart Hill in 1971 and a 1974 founding date are competing origin accounts.[2][3][10][12][13]

### Was it started by two brothers from a Greek-Australian family?

**Peter and Theo Kailis launched the first Red Rooster with nine other investment partners.** The detailed Kelmscott account names Peter and Theo together and says that they launched the first Red Rooster with nine other investment partners in 1972.[2] A general chain history also states that Peter and Theo Kailis opened the first Red Rooster in Kelmscott, and identifies them as brothers.[14]

The Kailis brothers were from a Greek-migrant family: Peter was one of four sons of Greek migrant George Kailis. Peter Kailis was the eldest of four sons of Greek migrant George Kailis, who operated a Perth fish shop from 1926.[2][3] Another account says Peter’s Greek-born parents ran a fish-and-chip shop in Perth for decades.[3] The broader Kailis family history traces the family to the Greek island of Kastellorizo and says it immigrated to Western Australia in the mid-1900s.[15] These accounts support the ordinary description of Peter and Theo as brothers from a Greek-Australian family.

Theo Kailis described his family as a large Greek family with roots in Kastellorizo; a photograph caption identified George Kailis as his father.[46]

The business was not, however, launched by the two brothers alone. Real Commercial expressly includes nine other investment partners alongside Peter and Theo in the 1972 Kelmscott launch.[2] Australian Food Timeline instead calls Peter Kailis the founder and says he invested with nine partners, without naming Theo.[9] Franchise Business repeats the Peter-and-nine-partners formulation.[3]

Peter Kailis’s own retrospective description adds another thread to the origin story. He said Red Rooster began through his brother, who had met two men from Adelaide with chicken shops and who had opened one on Wanneroo Road in Perth; Peter then decided to try the concept.[10] This account supports a brother’s role and helps explain why Wanneroo Road appears in rival origin narratives, but it does not remove the partners from the Kelmscott launch account.[2][10]

The terms founder and first-store launcher are therefore used differently across accounts. One article says Peter Kailis built and opened the first restaurant.[11] Another says Red Rooster was founded by Peter Kailis and his family.[16] The more detailed 1972 account credits Peter and Theo Kailis with the nine partners who acquired the Kelmscott shop.[2]

The two-brothers description is substantially right—Peter and Theo Kailis were brothers from the Kailis Greek-migrant family—but the 1972 Red Rooster launch also involved nine other investment partners.[2][3]

Australian Food Timeline calls Peter Kailis Red Rooster’s founder, identifies him as the eldest of four sons of Greek migrant George Kallis, and says that, with nine partners, he invested in the existing Kelmscott chicken shop that became the first Red Rooster.[9]

### The chain’s Perth origins and the history of its chicken-and-chips

Red Rooster began when Peter and Theo Kailis and nine other investment partners acquired an existing chicken shop in Kelmscott in 1972.[2] The 1972 Kelmscott business grew from a chicken rotisserie shop, while Red Rooster’s historical product distinction was its emphasis on roast chicken rather than KFC-style fried chicken.[3][2] This means that “quarter chicken and chips” is not enough to identify the offer by itself: Red Rooster has sold several chicken-and-chips combinations under different names.[9][2]

The historical Hawaiian Pack illustrates the point. It should therefore be read as a separate named meal from the lunchtime offer. Its fruit fritters are the practical menu difference, while the formal name is the decisive promotional difference.[9][2]

**Red Rooster called the promotion $5 Lunch in its archived July and September 2015 promotions pages.** One video title uses exactly that wording, and its description defines the deal as a quarter of a chicken and chips for five dollars, with no beverage.[17][18][19] Another title renders the same offer as **Red Rooster $5.00 1/4 Chicken Lunch**.[20] The lunch offer is dealt with in detail in The lunchtime promotion, including its price, food components, timing and historical continuity.

Later descriptions used variant names: a 2020 video calls it **Red Rooster $5.00 1/4 Chicken Lunch** and says **$5 quarter chicken lunch**; a 2021 video calls it the **NEW $5 Quarter Lunch Deal**; and a TikTok title uses **Red Rooster $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch**.[21][22][23]

Earlier Red Rooster advertising confirms that chicken-and-chips value offers have a longer history but does not collapse them into one promotion. A 1997 advertisement is titled Red Rooster Chicken & Chips Pack plus a Coke at $3.50.[24] A retrospective social-media post recalls $2 chicken and chips every Tuesday in the 1990s.[25] These are historically relevant Red Rooster offers, yet they have different titles, prices, inclusions and scheduling from the $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch.[19][24][25]

Red Rooster’s 2015 $5 Lunch is distinct from both the Hawaiian/Tropicana Pack and earlier chicken-and-chips specials: all involve chicken and chips, but they are different products or promotions with different names and inclusions.[9][2][17][24][25]

The 2015 $5 Lunch consisted of a quarter roast chicken and a small serve of chips for $5; Red Rooster listed it as 2650kJ. Red Rooster also advertised the same $5 quarter-chicken-and-small-chips combination on Facebook on 8 July 2015 and 15 July 2015.[17][18][26][27] On 13 March 2018, Red Rooster promoted the offer as available until 4pm for a limited time. This 4pm cutoff exemplifies the extended lunch window that distinguished Red Rooster lunch deals from hour-restricted offers at other Australian fast-food chains.[28][29]

An alternate account calls the offer the **Quarter Chicken & Chips lunch deal** and describes it as “your lunchtime favourite,” rather than assigning it a separate proprietary promotion name.[30]

The recollection of $2 chicken and chips every Tuesday in the 1990s does not give that Tuesday offer a formal promotion name or establish an uninterrupted run from the 1990s.[25]

In 2022, Frugal Feeds said that Red Rooster had brought back the **$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch**. That version included quarter roast chicken, small chips, and regular mash and gravy.[31]

Red Rooster had expanded to 80 stores by 1982, when it was sold to Myer.[12]

### Synthesis —

The clues identify Red Rooster, the roast-chicken chain launched in Kelmscott, Perth, in 1972.[5][2] Peter and Theo Kailis fit the brothers clue, and their father George’s Greek-migrant background explains the Greek-Australian family description; the launch also included nine investment partners.[2][3] The origin story is not wholly settled, because a Tuart Hill 1971 account and a 1974 founding claim coexist with the Kelmscott-1972 account.[12][13] For identifying the lunch offer, the important result is that Red Rooster sold several similarly composed chicken-and-chips meals: the relevant formal promotional wording in 2015 is $5 Lunch, rather than Hawaiian Pack, Tropicana Pack, or a generic chicken-and-chips special.[9][2][17][24]

## The lunchtime promotion

**Answer —** The chain was Red Rooster. Its archived official 2015 promotion page called the offer 《$5 Lunch》: a quarter roast chicken and a small serve of chips for A$5, available daily from 11 am to 4 pm.[17]

### What's the promotion called?

Red Rooster’s official 2015 promotional name was **《$5 Lunch》**. 《Red Rooster – Quarter Chicken & Small Chips for $5》 was a contemporaneous deal-listing title describing the meal, not a competing promotion name.[17][18][1]

**The answer is therefore 《$5 Lunch》.** **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips for $5** was the descriptive wording used in 2015 deal listings for the meal within that promotion, while Red Rooster’s own promotion page named the offer 《$5 Lunch》.[32][18]

In its 8 April 2020 Facebook post, Red Rooster wrote, 《Grab our $5* Quarter Chicken Lunch》 and said it was available until 4 pm. A 2020 video title also used the variant 《Red Rooster $5.00 1/4 Chicken Lunch》.[4][20] These later names confirm a recurring quarter-chicken lunch format, but they do not replace the wording recorded for 2015.

Several later descriptions use still other labels. A 2021 reviewer called it the 《$5 Quarter Lunch Deal》, as well as a five-dollar quarter lunch box and five-dollar quarter chicken lunch; a 2023 listing called the item 《Quarter Chicken and Chips》; and some later reports characterise it as a 《$5 Quarter Chicken & Chips Lunch Deal》. One report calls the promotion the 《Quarter Chicken and Chips》 deal and says that this was Red Rooster’s consistent branded name across decades.[33][34][35][28] Those names describe related offers, while the contemporaneous 2015 heading identifies the requested one.

Red Rooster’s archived website of 29 December 2015 also listed **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips** as a menu item.[36]

The distinction also prevents confusion with Red Rooster’s other named chicken meals. The Hawaiian Pack, later called the Classic Tropicana or Tropicana Pack, combined quarter roast chicken, chips and pineapple-and-banana fritters; PerthNow says it became a West Australian summer classic. It was a menu meal, not the lunch deal identified here.[9][2][10] Likewise, a 1997 advertisement promoted a temporary 《Chicken & Chips Pack plus a Coke》 for $3.50, which is a different named package.[24]

For an account referring to the deal around 2015, use **$5 Lunch** for the promotion and **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips** for its meal. Red Rooster’s 2020 material instead used the later label **《$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch》**.[18][4]

A 2021 Red Rooster-linked listing used both **《$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch》** and **《Quarter Chicken Lunch Meal》**; it described quarter chicken, chips, mash and gravy, available until 4 pm.[1]

A Red Rooster store-representative listing dated 30 June 2021 called the offer the “$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch” or “$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch Meal” and specified quarter chicken, chips, mash and gravy for A$5. It said the limited-time offer was available daily in restaurants and drive-throughs until 4 pm, with prices potentially varying by store.[47]

### What chicken-and-chips lunchtime deal did the promotion name refer to?

In 2015, 《$5 Lunch》 comprised a quarter roast chicken and a small serve of chips for A$5, available daily from 11 am to 4 pm.[17][18]

The title directly states the meal’s two components: a quarter chicken and a small serve of chips.[1]

Later **Quarter Chicken Lunch** versions could have more accompaniments. A 2020 video purchaser described a quarter chicken, small chips and potato with gravy, then summarised the meal as chicken, chips, gravy, mashed potato and stuffing.[20] A 2021 review of a $5 quarter lunch likewise described quarter chicken, chips, mashed potato, gravy and stuffing, while another reviewer described a $5 offer as quarter chicken and chips without a drink.[33][19] These accounts show that the bundle could change; they do not alter the more limited components stated in the 2015 deal listing.

A separate 2023 video described a newly released, limited-time “five dollar box” of quarter chicken and chips, available until 4 pm; it paired the box with a Super Boost drink without identifying the drink as part of the standard meal.[42][19]

The chicken portion itself could be selected in later versions. A 2022 reviewer said the customer could choose the quarter piece and chose a leg piece.[19] A separate 2021 reviewer was offered a drink for an extra $2, confirming that a drink was an add-on rather than necessarily part of the $5 lunch.[33] That fits the meal’s value structure: the core offer was chicken and chips, while sides and drinks could vary by promotion.

In a 2022 video about the $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch, stuffing was observed and no drink was included.[19]

The record also distinguishes this offer from lunch promotions based on other Red Rooster products. In 2021, Red Rooster offered a 《$4.95 Sub Lunch Box》 containing a roast-chicken sub roll, chips, onion rings, and potato and gravy; it also offered a 《$4.95 Rooster Roll Lunch》, with a Rooster Roll, small chips and a drink, available until 4 pm.[38][39] A later 《NEW $5 Lunch Box》 included chips, mashed potato and gravy, a quarter piece of chicken, and stuffing.[40] Those are neighbouring lunch-value promotions, not alternative names for the 2015 quarter-chicken-and-small-chips deal.

The result is that the 2015 promotion should be read literally: it was Red Rooster’s $5 quarter-chicken-and-small-chips lunch offer, not the Hawaiian/Tropicana meal, a chicken-and-Coke pack, or a later lunch box.[1][9][24][38][40]

From the documented 2015 price of $5 to the 2023 listed price of $5.95, the nominal increase was $0.95, or 19%. This comparison shows that later versions of the quarter-chicken-and-chips lunch format were priced higher than the 2015 offer.[1][34]

Red Rooster’s official 2015 promotion page states that **《$5 Lunch》** was a quarter roast chicken and a small serve of chips for $5, listed as 2650 kJ.[17]

The same quarter-chicken-and-small-chips format was again advertised by Red Rooster in March 2018 as **《$5 Quarter Chicken and small Chips》**, available until 4 pm for a limited time.[29][41]

### What was the deal’s mid-afternoon cut-off?

The 2015 Red Rooster 《$5 Lunch》 cut-off was **4 pm**; the official terms were daily availability from 11 am to 4 pm.[17]

**The requested mid-afternoon cut-off was 4 pm.** It was not merely implied by the word lunch: the 2015 listing explicitly set the 11 am–4 pm window.[1]

Red Rooster’s April 2020 Facebook post said its $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch was 《Available ‘til 4pm》; a 2020 video reviewer also said the $5 quarter-chicken lunch was available to 4 pm.[4][20] A 2022 reviewer similarly told viewers, 《you got to get in there before 4 pm to get it》.[19]

The later names and prices changed, but the time boundary persisted. The 2023 $5.95 Quarter Chicken and Chips listing stated, 《Available until 4pm in restaurant only》, while the 2024 $5.50 quarter-chicken-and-chips promotion was available until 4 pm daily for a limited time.[34][37] A January 2023 reviewer also called the 4 pm limit a lunchtime condition.[42]

This recurring timing helps explain why labels such as **Quarter Chicken Lunch** emerged later: the product was sold as a lunch-time value offer, with 4 pm marking the end of eligibility. It does not mean every related Red Rooster chicken-and-chips promotion had identical hours, but it does confirm the 2015 offer’s stated cut-off.[1][4][34]

Red Rooster’s archived July 2015 menu page also gave the meal’s availability as between 11 am and 4 pm, independently matching the promotion page’s daily 11 am–4 pm hours.[18][43]

### Had the lunchtime deal been going since the 90s at

Earlier offers were distinct: a 1997 advertisement promoted 《Red Rooster - Chicken & Chips Pack plus a Coke $3.50》 for a short time, while customer recollections describe $2 chicken-and-chips specials on Tuesdays, or $2 quarter chicken and chips on Mondays and Tuesdays, in the early 1990s.[24][1][25] A retrospective account says that customers could get **$2 chicken and chips on Tuesdays** in that decade, while another recollection asks, 《Who can remeber back in the 90’s when Red Rooster had $2.00 chicken & chips every Tuesday?》.[25][35]

Other recollections make the chicken portion explicit. In a 2014 deal discussion, customers remembered 《$2 Tuesday’s quarter chicken and chips at Red Roster》 and placed it roughly 22 years earlier, around the early 1990s. In a 2017 discussion, a former worker recalled customers buying $2 chicken and chips on Mondays and Tuesdays and said the promotion 《ran for years》.[1] Another recollection says Red Rooster launched $2 quarter chicken and chips for Mondays and Tuesdays when the writer was about 15.[35]

A January 2023 commenter recalled paying $2.50 for quarter chicken and chips on Tuesdays in the 1990s.[48]

A separate consumer recollection places a $5 quarter-chicken-and-chips buy-one-get-one-free offer in Melbourne CBD around 1993–94.[34] That is another indication that quarter chicken plus chips was an established value-promotion format in the early 1990s, although its price and mechanic differed from the $2 weekday offer.

The later offer history shows recurrent rather than demonstrably uninterrupted use. A 2023 discussion linked earlier quarter-chicken deals from 2012, 2014 and 2016; a 2022 reviewer said Red Rooster had 《bought back》 the $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch; and Red Rooster said in 2020, 《We brought it back just for Lauren.》 Those statements support repeated returns of a familiar offer, rather than one unbroken promotion with fixed terms.[34][19][4]

The names and conditions also changed across the period. The 1990s recollections refer to $2 chicken and chips, often on Mondays or Tuesdays; the documented 2015 offer was $5 Quarter Chicken & Small Chips, daily from 11 am to 4 pm; and the March 2021 version was called $5 Quarter Chicken Lunch, available until 4 pm.[25][1][35] Thus the 1990s deal is the historical antecedent of the later lunch-value format, not proof that the exact 2015 name had been used continuously since then.

One report describes the promotion as having continued into the present with price changes, but its own examples differ in names, days, prices and hours.[35] The sound historical conclusion is narrower and more useful: Red Rooster was running comparable quarter-chicken-and-chips value specials by the 1990s, while the precise 2015 lunchtime wording and daily 4 pm rule belong to the later documented offer.[1][35]

The 1997 pack was a distinct, short-time offer and does not carry the 2015 listing’s name, contents, price or 11 am–4 pm lunch hours.[1][24]

A Red Rooster advertisement recorded from Capital Canberra on 18 March 1994 grouped its offers under the name **《Red Hot Deals》**; the YouTube page was posted on 11 May 2025.[44] This is a distinct 1994 promotional name from the 2015 **《$5 Lunch》**.[44][18]

The July 2015 OzBargain discussion also records a customer recollection of a $2 Monday-and-Tuesday version in the early 1990s, followed by a half-price version.[45]

A long-time customer recalled a $2 Tuesday chicken-and-chips offer in 1991 that came with a banana fritter.[30]

A recollection of Red Rooster coupons from 1994 says that a family bought takeaway only when the $2 Quarter chicken and chips offer was available.[35]

### Synthesis

The promotion sought is Red Rooster’s **《$5 Lunch》**, the name on its archived official 2015 promotion page. It consisted of a quarter roast chicken and a small serve of chips for $5, sold daily between 11 am and 4 pm; therefore its cut-off was **4 pm**.[17] **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips for $5** was the contemporaneous descriptive deal-listing wording for that meal.[32] Later Red Rooster material calls related versions **《$5 Quarter Chicken Lunch》** or **Quarter Chicken and Chips**, often with the same 4 pm boundary but sometimes with mash, gravy or stuffing added.[4][1][34] The earlier 1990s evidence supports a related $2 weekday chicken-and-chips or quarter-chicken-and-chips special, establishing historical antecedence rather than an unchanged name or uninterrupted run.[25][1][35]

1. Identify the 2015 promotion as **$5 Lunch**, with **Quarter Chicken & Small Chips** as the meal it offered. [18]
2. Describe it as a quarter roast chicken plus a small serve of chips for $5, available daily from **11 am to 4 pm**. [17]
3. Treat 1990s $2 chicken-and-chips specials as predecessors, not as proof that the 2015 promotion had always used the same title. [1][25]
