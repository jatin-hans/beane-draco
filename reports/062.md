Daily Brinson-Fachler attribution and variance-share template

Allocation measures group weighting relative to the benchmark and selection measures stock picking relative to the benchmark. Brinson-Fachler decomposes active return into allocation, selection and interaction effects. Its required inputs are portfolio holdings and trades, benchmark composition and weights, and market prices, FX rates and corporate actions.[32][2]

```python

# Required CSVs:

# sector_daily.csv: date,sector,portfolio_weight,benchmark_weight,

# portfolio_sector_return,benchmark_sector_return,benchmark_return

# fund_benchmark_daily.csv: date,fund_return,benchmark_return

# Returns and weights are decimals, not percentages. One row per date-sector.

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

s = pd.read_csv("sector_daily.csv", parse_dates=["date"])
f = pd.read_csv("fund_benchmark_daily.csv", parse_dates=["date"])

# Brinson-Fachler daily effects, summed across sectors.

s["allocation"] = (s.portfolio_weight - s.benchmark_weight) * (s.benchmark_sector_return - s.benchmark_return)
s["selection"] = s.benchmark_weight * (s.portfolio_sector_return - s.benchmark_sector_return)
s["interaction"] = ((s.portfolio_weight - s.benchmark_weight) *
                    (s.portfolio_sector_return - s.benchmark_sector_return))

components = s.groupby("date")[["allocation", "selection", "interaction"]].sum()
daily_attr = f.set_index("date").join(components, how="inner")
daily_attr["observed_active"] = daily_attr.fund_return - daily_attr.benchmark_return
daily_attr["residual"] = (daily_attr.observed_active - daily_attr[["allocation", "selection", "interaction"]].sum(axis=1))
daily_attr["reconciles"] = np.isclose(daily_attr.observed_active,
                                       daily_attr[["allocation", "selection", "interaction", "residual"]].sum(axis=1),
                                       atol=1e-12)

# Each component receives its own variance plus half of every cross-component 2Cov term.

# This is equivalently Cov(component, observed active return) / Var(observed active return).

components4 = ["allocation", "selection", "interaction", "residual"]
active_variance = daily_attr.observed_active.var(ddof=1)
variance_shares = pd.Series({c: daily_attr[c].cov(daily_attr.observed_active) / active_variance
                             for c in components4}, name="share_of_active_return_variance")

print(daily_attr)
print("All daily observations reconcile:", daily_attr.reconciles.all())
print((variance_shares * 100).round(2).astype(str) + "%")
daily_attr.to_csv("daily_brinson_fachler_effects.csv", index_label="date")
variance_shares.to_csv("active_return_variance_shares.csv", header=True)

```

Each component receives half of every cross-component pair’s total 2Cov term. A component’s variance share can be negative when its covariance with active return is negative. Covariance identifies whether variables move together or in opposite directions.[33]

The 90-day comparison is a cumulative-total-return exhibit when adjusted closes are indexed and compounded: cumulative total return measures the investment’s growth or decline over the period, while total-return calculations reinvest dividends and distributions.[34] Morningstar Direct’s Growth chart applies the same dividend-reinvestment convention to a hypothetical $10,000 investment and can instead display cumulative return percentage.[35]

### Allocation

Brinson attribution separates relative return into allocation, selection, and interaction. Allocation measures whether the manager’s group weights differed beneficially from benchmark group weights; selection measures whether the securities selected within a group outperformed the benchmark’s group return; interaction captures the combined effect of an active group weight and active within-group return.[32][57][2]

Judgment — Use a simultaneous, three-factor Brinson-Fachler framework at daily frequency. For sector i on day t, define allocation as active sector weight multiplied by the sector’s benchmark return less the overall benchmark return; define selection as benchmark sector weight multiplied by fund-sector return less benchmark-sector return; and define interaction as active sector weight multiplied by the fund-sector return less benchmark-sector return. Sum the sector effects daily, retain cash and any unclassified positions as explicit groups, and show any reconciliation residual separately.

The treatment of interaction is not cosmetic. Under a top-down framework, interaction is commonly added to selection; under a bottom-up framework, it is commonly added to allocation; and under a simultaneous approach it is separately reported. External two-factor reporting often folds interaction into selection by using portfolio group weight in the selection calculation.[58][32][57] Thus, a reported “selection” percentage is not comparable across managers unless both use the same interaction convention.

The sources also distinguish Brinson-Hood-Beebower from Brinson-Fachler allocation. BHB credits overweighting a segment whenever that segment has a positive benchmark return, whereas BF evaluates the segment relative to the overall benchmark return. The models can produce the same total active return but allocate it differently across effects.[58][48] For an LP-facing analysis against an equal-weight benchmark, BF is more informative because it asks whether the overweight beat the benchmark’s average opportunity set rather than whether the sector simply rose.

Daily effects must be linked to the quarter using a stated multi-period convention. Daily attribution compounded over a month is not identical to a direct monthly attribution, and single-period Brinson effects do not automatically compound into a quarterly result. Carino, Menchero, or GRAP linking provides a framework for reconciling daily effects to the cumulative quarter.[58][48]

The requested allocation-versus-selection result is a **risk decomposition**, not merely a return-contribution table. Return attribution answers where the -660 basis points of active return arose; variance attribution asks which components created dispersion in daily active returns around their average. Variance is the dispersion of returns around their mean, while covariance identifies whether components move together or in opposite directions.[1][2][33]

Judgment — After calculating daily allocation (A), selection (S), interaction (I), and reconciliation residual (e), define daily active return as x = A + S + I + e. Report the covariance-complete shares of active-return variance as follows:

### articulate the forward thesis

The forward thesis should not be that Q3’s share-price moves were mistaken. It should be that the operating evidence continues to support a multi-year AI infrastructure and enterprise-adoption opportunity, while the fund explicitly recognizes that valuation, execution, capacity, and monetization risks can dominate returns over shorter periods.

For NVIDIA, the thesis is a product-cycle bridge from Hopper demand into Blackwell deployment. NVIDIA management said Hopper demand remained strong, Blackwell samples were shipping to customers and partners, and it expected several billion dollars of Blackwell revenue in fiscal Q4 2025. It also described Spectrum-X Ethernet for AI and NVIDIA AI Enterprise software as new product categories achieving significant scale.[103][83][13]

NVIDIA’s demand base extends beyond one use case. Management estimated that inference represented more than 40% of Data Center revenue over the trailing four quarters; cloud service providers represented roughly 45% of fiscal Q2 2025 Data Center revenue, with more than 50% from consumer-internet and enterprise customers. Management characterized the longer-term opportunity as a shift from general-purpose to accelerated computing and from human-engineered software to generative-AI software.[87]

That thesis has explicit risks. NVIDIA identified global economic conditions, third-party manufacturing, assembly, packaging and testing dependence, technology development and competition, product development and market acceptance, and design, manufacturing, or software defects as factors that could cause actual results to differ materially from expectations. The Blackwell production-yield change and NVIDIA’s fiscal Q2 2025 China Data Center revenue remaining below pre-export-control levels as a share of total Data Center revenue are operating issues to monitor. NVIDIA also said the China market was expected to be very competitive.[83][87]

For Microsoft, the thesis is that capacity-constrained AI demand can convert into future cloud growth as infrastructure comes online. Microsoft said demand for Azure AI services remained above available capacity, and it expected Azure growth to accelerate in the second half of FY25 as capital investment increased available AI capacity.[56] It further said its infrastructure investment would be managed against observed demand signals.[18][56]

Microsoft’s enterprise-adoption data provide the clearest early monetization markers. Management reported that Microsoft 365 Copilot customers increased more than 60% quarter over quarter; customers with more than 10,000 seats more than doubled quarter over quarter; and 50% of Azure AI users also used a data meter, which management presented as evidence of broader enterprise-workload adoption.[56]

The investment case requires accepting a near-term capital-intensity trade-off. Microsoft expected FY25 capital expenditures to exceed FY24 levels to meet growing demand for AI and cloud products; it also expected double-digit FY25 revenue and operating-income growth, single-digit FY25 operating-expense growth, and FY25 operating margins down about one point year over year.[18] Management described roughly half of FY2024 total capital expense and half of FY2024 Q4 expense as land, builds, and finance leases to be monetized over 15 years and beyond; separately, the CEO described 60-plus percent of capital spending as equipment.[18][56] Those descriptions use different categories and scopes and should not be combined into one spending-mix estimate.[18][56]

Microsoft’s own risk disclosure frames the test correctly. It identified execution and competition in cloud-based and AI services, investments that may not achieve expected returns, AI-related reputational, competitive, or legal harm, and adverse economic or market conditions as material risks. The forward case is therefore not that spending itself creates value; it is that customer adoption, capacity utilization, cloud growth, and operating leverage ultimately justify the investment.[110][111]

Judgment — The fund’s forward thesis should remain high conviction but evidence-led: retain exposure where the AI infrastructure buildout and enterprise adoption are translating into demand, capacity utilization, and durable cash-flow potential; reassess position size if the operating milestones weaken or if capital intensity rises without evidence of monetization.

The two positions express linked but different AI exposures: NVIDIA supplies GPUs and systems used to train and run AI models, while Microsoft is one of its largest customers, purchasing NVIDIA chips for Azure data centers. NVIDIA is the more direct and more volatile exposure to AI-hardware demand; Microsoft has more diversified software and cloud revenue.[112]

The semiconductor thesis also requires tracking the full set of industry conditions: hyperscaler capital-expenditure revisions, inference demand, disappointing AI returns, a hyperscaler spending pause, and tighter export controls.[17]

NVIDIA’s competitive test includes supply as well as demand. Nasdaq’s April 2024 SOX commentary stated that NVIDIA could not meet all AI-chip demand, creating an opportunity for AMD and other suppliers to gain share; it identified Microsoft, Meta and Oracle among cloud providers that had announced adoption of AMD’s Instinct MI300X.[113]

In its Q1 2025 letter covering the quarter ended March 31, 2025, Baron Technology Fund reported that NVIDIA shares came under pressure amid skepticism about AI adoption, including DeepSeek-related concerns that more compute-efficient training and inference workloads could reduce demand for accelerated-computing systems.[114] In its Q1 2025 letter covering the quarter ended March 31, 2025, Baron also reported that Broadcom shares retreated after strong results, primarily amid a broad market pullback associated with tariff and trade-relations concerns.[114]

### What remains unmeasured

The sector-attribution analysis and fund-versus-peer relative-performance comparison are the mechanisms for converting this narrative into measured attribution. Allocation effect is the return from holding different sector weights than the benchmark. Selection effect is the return from choosing different securities within a sector. Interaction captures the combined effect of active weights and active returns, while residual captures effects such as cash, derivatives, fees, trading costs, classifications, and unmodeled exposures.[1]

The reported quarterly fund and benchmark returns establish the 660-basis-point active-return gap, but not the percentage of active-return variance attributable to sector allocation versus security selection. Those percentages require aligned daily fund returns, daily benchmark returns, position-level or sector weights through the quarter, security and benchmark sector classifications, and a stated convention for the interaction and residual terms. Without those inputs, a numerical allocation-versus-selection split would be an unsupported attribution claim.

The same distinction applies to the fund’s two largest positions. NVDA and MSFT weights establish meaningful exposure, while their company results and market reactions establish plausible stock-specific context. They do not establish each security’s contribution to active return, the fund’s daily RSP-relative path, whether the positions were traded, or whether options, hedges, cash, other securities, and fees magnified or offset the result.

Judgment — LPs should expect the next attribution exhibit to answer four measurable questions: first, how much active return came from semiconductor, enterprise-software, cloud-infrastructure, and residual exposures; second, how much came from stock selection within those exposures; third, whether the loss was concentrated in a few trading dates or persistent; and fourth, whether the portfolio remains positioned consistently with the manager’s stated long-term process.

The attribution report should use the Brinson framework expressly: it decomposes manager added value into sector or factor allocation and security selection; in the cited single-period implementation, selection includes the classical interaction effect. This convention should be fixed before calculating allocation and selection shares of active-return variance.[60]

Attribution is an ex-post characterization of the effects of investment decisions, not a representation of the investment process. A complete LP review should therefore pair the arithmetic with regular challenge and follow-up by portfolio management, performance measurement, risk, operations and compliance.[60][59]

The disclosed 20% combined NVDA/MSFT weight also makes concentration a material risk consideration. Focused portfolios and holdings with high positive correlations have greater overall risk than more diversified portfolios, and software, internet and semiconductor securities can move with broader technology-sector sentiment.[116]

An August 2025 HFRRF investment policy assigns reported performance calculation to an unaffiliated custodian bank and requires aggregate, asset-class, and Investment Manager calculations to be consistent with the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS®) or a similar standard.[117] The policy assigns the CIO responsibility for annual aggregate, asset-class, and Investment Manager performance summaries and for a consolidated quarterly mandate-level summary that includes performance, cash-flow activity, fees, and expenses where applicable.[117] It also requires a quarterly Trust Universe Comparisons Services (TUCS) summary prepared by Wilshire.[117]

HFRRF’s August 2025 policy uses a 10-year investment-planning horizon, a rolling 10-year 7.00% net-of-fee return target, and a net-of-fee objective to exceed its Policy Benchmark over the same horizon.[117] The policy defines Active Management as the belief that active management will add value versus the strategic asset allocation over a long-term horizon, using tactical asset allocation, Investment Managers, and Private Investments as its primary tools.[117]

Timing is a distinct attribution category: CFA Institute defines it as the return from the manager’s variations in systematic risk, or beta, around a policy or target amount.[1] The completed attribution should therefore identify timing separately where applicable, rather than folding changes in systematic risk into sector allocation or security selection.

### LP call talking points

- **Acknowledge the return precisely:** In Q3 2024, the fund returned −8.7% versus −2.1% for its stated benchmark. - **Recognize the magnitude, concentration and benchmark-weight test rather than normalize it:** NVDA and MSFT represented 12% and 8% of assets, respectively, or 20% combined at the stated weights. The benchmark-weight test is whether those portfolio weights exceeded the corresponding verified benchmark weights; that comparison, together with security-level contribution, requires benchmark constituent weights and the fund’s holdings and return data. Performance attribution is designed to explain benchmark-relative results and connect them to active decisions, while the appropriate response to underperformance is an impartial assessment of causes and of whether the investment process was followed. [1][3][96]
