Select Honeywell as the preferred whole-of-mine platform where interoperability with a mixed legacy SCADA and PLC estate is the deciding criterion, subject to radio-survey and point-list pricing. Use MOVUS as the Australian-headquartered alternative for general rotating equipment, with published FitMachine limits of IP66 and −20 to +60 °C for FMFI 3.0 or −25 to +75 °C for the 3XT EX, and use Vayeron only for conveyor-idler monitoring.

Budget the common initial scope as 110 sensor endpoints rather than allocating sensors across 50 km² by area; this produces a conservative Siemens allowance of 22 CC220s at five sensors per collector, while Honeywell's published 110-HVT example requires the 250-device EHM bundle and approximately 7,700 points at 70 points per HVT. ### Siemens

Siemens’ identifiable predictive-maintenance stack is SITRANS MS200 sensors, SITRANS CC220 data collectors, SITRANS SCM IQ and SITRANS Anomaly IQ in Insights Hub, with Senseye Predictive Maintenance as a higher-scale cloud analytics option. MS200 measures three-axis vibration, temperature and magnetic-field strength on rotating equipment such as pumps, compressors and gearboxes.[1][8]

The general-purpose MS200 is the Siemens field component suited to the hottest exposed locations in the compared stack. Siemens specifies an operating ambient range of −30 to +80 °C at the fixation point, an IP68 rating at 2 m for 24 hours and IP69 protection under IEC 60529. Siemens also says the sensor must be protected from direct sunlight and aggressive vapours, because ultraviolet exposure can overheat the device or embrittle materials.[10][6]

The hazardous-area version has a materially lower upper environmental limit. Siemens specifies the intrinsically safe MS200 as II 2G Ex ib IIC T4 Gb, with an ambient range from −30 to +50 °C. A hot sensor mounting point that is acceptable for the general-purpose unit can therefore be outside the published operating envelope for the hazardous-area version.[10]

CC220 is the environmental constraint in the Siemens architecture. It is IP20, rated 0 to +50 °C, approved for indoor operation and intended for a cabinet, separate room or controlled area. ### Coverage

Judgment — **Use an Asset-Led Coverage Method.** First, divide the 50 km² operating footprint into fixed process plant, conveyor corridors, workshops, substations, pit-edge infrastructure, mobile-equipment service areas and remote assets. Second, identify critical assets, failure modes, required warning time and sensor mounting points. Third, design radio, power, cabinet and backhaul coverage for those assets. Fourth, test the installed sensor positions during normal production, including closed guards, steelwork, dust and vehicle traffic.

The 50 km² footprint should not be treated as a uniform radio circle. Siemens’ MS200 uses a BLE link to a CC220 at up to 30 m; Honeywell’s Versatilis architecture uses LoRaWAN gateways and a LoRaWAN server; MOVUS uses Wi-Fi or a gateway with cellular backhaul; and Vayeron uses a conveyor mesh. These are different network topologies that cannot be reduced to one sensors-per-square-kilometre figure.[6][2][3][4]

Siemens’ topology is asset-cluster-centred. Every MS200 must be assigned to a CC220 and mapped to a MindSphere asset. CC220 then connects to the plant network through Ethernet and requires working internet connectivity for SCM IQ.[6]

Honeywell’s topology is long-range sensor-to-gateway connectivity followed by server integration. HVT has a stated LoRa line-of-sight range of 4 km and BLE line-of-sight range of 25 m. The LoRaWAN gateway operates in packet-forwarder mode, sends packets to a LoRaWAN server, and the server exposes data over MQTT. Honeywell identifies LoRaWAN as suitable for large-area coverage, but gateway count must still be set by terrain, line-of-sight, structures, antenna height, update rate and backhaul availability.[16][5][91]

LoRaWAN can carry periodic condition readings, alarms, temperature and low-bandwidth vibration features, but is not the default transport for closed-loop protection or high-rate raw-waveform streams. Devices, gateways and the network server must use consistent AU915 configuration.[124][125][126]

Nominal LoRaWAN range alone does not establish gateway coverage: structures, terrain, vegetation and mobile machinery obstruct radio paths, while heat, vibration and dust affect reliability.[124][125][126]

MOVUS’ topology depends on the existing wireless estate. FitMachine communicates through customer Wi-Fi or a FitMachine Gateway; the gateway then uses 4G or CatM1 to transmit to MachineCloud. Network checks at the final installed location are mandatory because machine covers materially affect observed connectivity.[3][72]

A locally supplied LoRaWAN sensor option includes NEON vibration sensors rated −40 to +80 °C and IP65 with local algorithms, and TE 89x1N sensors rated −40 to +80 °C and IP67 with onboard FFT. The WISE-2410 offering has an IECEx Zone 21 dust option.[121][127][122]

For mobile-fleet monitoring, Dingo identifies engine temperatures and pressures, oil indicators, wheel-motor speed and power, clutch shift and slip, suspension pressures, and truck rack and pitch as available measures. Communications coverage and bandwidth constrain real-time transfer, so Trakka does not replace real-time monitoring.[115]

Vayeron’s topology follows conveyors rather than areas. Smart Rollers mesh data along the conveyor at 433 MHz to a gateway, and Vayeron says each gateway can communicate with up to 5,000 rollers. The critical design variables are conveyor length, roller spacing, hop count, steelwork, transfer towers, tunnels, bends, communications latency and desired polling frequency.[4][30]

For that locally integrated LoRaWAN option, an LG43 gateway provides Modbus RTU mapping and MQTT/HTTP publishing; an OPC UA connection requires an additional broker, gateway or middleware layer.[128]

Condition-monitoring scope must also be asset-led. The RF acceptance and installation schedule should specify measurement quality as well as radio performance. The measurement location must be clean metal and should avoid gaskets, dampers and hollow structures that reduce vibration and sound transmission.[97][98]

Very-low-speed and variable-speed assets require a separate diagnostic configuration in the asset register. Its rule of thumb is a waveform covering at least 15–20 shaft revolutions; at 10 r/min this is 90–120 seconds. SKF states that acceleration enveloping can be effective for very-low-speed applications, with measurement types and enveloping filters initially selected broadly and then reduced using actual results. For variable speed, SKF specifies order tracking with a speed reference such as a tachometer pulse to address spectrum smearing.[97]

iBwave states that mines are 《highly dynamic locations》: new seams and underground tunnels may open, older workings may close, machinery may move, access roads may be built, and pits may deepen or extend. Wireless planning is therefore an ongoing process.[99]

iBwave states that remote mines often have poor public mobile-operator macro-cellular coverage and that dedicated private 4G/5G systems are commonplace because of network-coverage limitations.[99]

iBwave states that cloud-based operations and preventative-maintenance applications use more data from more sensors, machinery, cameras and other inputs, requiring better coverage, greater capacity and support for mobile endpoints including vehicles and wearables.[99]

Oxmaint states that PLCs more than 10–15 years old rarely have native OPC UA servers and require protocol converters or SCADA-layer gateways for legacy equipment.[100]

The P-F interval is the time between the earliest detectable change in equipment condition and functional failure, and inspections must occur at intervals shorter than that interval. A hypothetical conveyor-pulley bearing defect detectable through vibration analysis about six weeks before failure can be addressed with a three-week inspection interval.[101]

iBwave identifies deep pits, underground tunnels and shafts, and isolated roads or railways as difficult radio-propagation and coverage environments. Because mine layouts change as seams and tunnels open or close, machinery moves, roads are built, and pits deepen or extend, wireless coverage planning must be revisited as operations and infrastructure move.[99]

Spectrum selection must account for the range-and-capacity trade-off: iBwave identifies sub-1 GHz as having the best wide-area range, 2–6 GHz mid-band as generally optimal for mid-size private networks including specific sites up to 10 km scale, and mmWave as having poor propagation and generally very short range despite high capacity.[99]

Oxmaint’s 15 April 2026 SCADA and OPC-UA integration guidance states that reliable correlation between SCADA and maintenance data depends on aligning timestamps through a unified time-synchronisation layer and mapping SCADA tags to a common asset hierarchy.[100]

The SCADA integration boundary shall be read-only: the condition-monitoring platform may publish measurements, health status, alarms and events to the OT environment but shall not receive control authority over production equipment. The RFQ shall allocate responsibility for tag mapping, timestamp and quality propagation, alarm ownership, user authentication, certificate management, patching and cyber-security review.

Use a common asset identifier across condition-monitoring, SCADA, historian and maintenance systems, synchronise timestamps, and record standardised failure-mode codes in completed maintenance work orders.[101]

Legacy-data integration design basis. The integration schedule should map SCADA tags to an asset hierarchy, align timestamps across systems, retain quality flags, and define the historian-access method. Edge compute can normalise tag names, align timestamps, convert engineering units and add equipment ID, location and maintenance-history context. OPC UA carries live structured data including types, engineering units, quality codes and metadata; it does not migrate proprietary historian data or itself create work orders, maintenance alerts or CMMS workflows.[100]

Where the existing SCADA estate exposes OPC UA, OPC UA provides vendor-neutral client access without proprietary drivers. Older PLCs can be bridged with protocol converters or SCADA-layer gateways.[100]

Judgment — **Do not rank total implementation cost before comparable supplier quotes are received.** Siemens’ cost schedule comprises MS200 endpoints, CC220s, protected cabinets, connectivity and SCM IQ connection/model extensions; Honeywell’s comprises HVTs, LoRaWAN infrastructure, provider/server services, EHM capacity and integration; MOVUS’ comprises FitMachine endpoints, Wi-Fi or cellular topology and PlantOS/MachineCloud services; Vayeron’s comprises roller count, Field Gateways and conveyor scope.[35][2][21][3][14]

Common implementation-cost boundary. Each supplier’s once-off installed total must include sensor hardware and mounts; supplier-compatible radio and gateways; field power and backhaul; protected cabinets; initial licences; SCADA and cyber engineering; installation; commissioning; and spares. Quote annual subscriptions, connectivity, battery replacement and support separately from the once-off installed total. Apply identical GST, currency and contingency treatment to every bid.[1][2][21][3][14][35]

The common cost schedule should separate capital cost—the upfront purchase and installation amount—from ongoing cost for connectivity, hosting, support and maintenance. Installation and commissioning should include site inspection and planning, mounting, antenna placement, powered-gateway electrical work, network testing, calibration checks, dashboard configuration, alert testing, training, handover documentation, travel, access equipment, inductions, safe-work method statements, permits and licensed trades.[92]

The scope must identify heat, dust, washdown areas, humidity, chemicals, vibration, flooding risk and other exposure conditions so suppliers price the required enclosures, cables and mounting hardware. Connectivity pricing must state the selected Ethernet, Wi-Fi, 4G, 5G, NB-IoT, LTE-M, LoRaWAN, satellite or private-radio service, its data allowance, recurring fee and connection-failure treatment.[92]

Honeywell states that HVT’s compact, low-power design and rapid installation enable scale deployment with low CAPEX and negligible OPEX. This is a supplier cost-positioning claim; the RFQ cost schedule remains HVT endpoints, mounts, LoRaWAN gateways, server/application layer, EHM licences, installation, backhaul, integration and support.[57][2][21][27]

Honeywell’s EHM cost schedule must also include the selected deployment topology and software prerequisites. Its model-selection guide makes SQL CAL 2019 Standard Runtime Embedded mandatory for every installation and lists Windows 10 COA, Windows Server 2019 and Windows Server 2022 licences as optional selections. EHM capacity tiers are up to 50, 51–100, 101–300, 301–1,000, 1,001–2,000 and 2,001–4,000 HVT devices, while the user count is selected separately.[60]

Vayeron Smart-Idler cost is driven by the number of rollers made smart, because the product is installed internally during roller manufacture and each roller carries an additional cost. The Smart-Idler price is quote-only.[104][34]

Siemens’ five-year operating-cost schedule should separately include CC220 backup-battery replacement at Siemens’ stated five-year preventative interval, MS200 replacement batteries using the qualified two-to-five-year current product range and the July 2023 manual’s up-to-3.5-year-at-25 °C statement, periodic sensor inspection, and controlled clean-area battery-change work. The MS200 battery is specified as 3.6 V and 2.6 Ah.[20][31][1]

Honeywell’s OneWireless cost schedule should distinguish HVT LoRaWAN infrastructure from any ISA100 Wireless/WirelessHART scope. For the latter, include WDM, FDAP or PCAP access points, RF assessment and layout validation, Ethernet/fibre backhaul, and applicable Modbus, HART, OPC UA, OPC DA or Experion PKS CDA interfaces. Honeywell states that a network can scale from one instrument to plant-wide multi-application use, but gateway and access-point quantities follow the site-specific RF design rather than the 50 km² area alone.[62][24]

Honeywell’s optional OneWireless scope adds identifiable cost and design drivers beyond the HVT LoRaWAN architecture: FDAP or PCAP access points, external antennas, Ethernet or PoE connections, power distribution, lightning protection, RF design, WDM configuration and, where required, redundant WDMX hardware. FDAP has one 10/100 Mbps Ethernet port and supports ISA100 Wireless (IEC 62734) and WirelessHART (IEC 62591). PCAP has one 10/100/1000 BASE-T autosensing PoE+ input, two 10/100/1000 BASE-T autosensing PoE outputs, a management-console RJ-45 port and external antenna ports. A high-availability WDM arrangement uses two identical WDMs, primary and secondary, and is supported only on WDMX hardware with three Ethernet ports.[22][23][24][66]

These supplier claims do not replace the common installed-cost schedule because the schedule must still price the selected sensors, field access points, radio survey, backhaul, integration, commissioning and support.[23][64][2][21][27]

The common implementation cost schedule must include asset criticality assessment, sensor-mount preparation and permanent mounting hardware, baseline and alarm-threshold development, diagnostic training, and the selected monitoring model—walk-around, wireless or continuous online—because SKF links monitoring approach to machine criticality and Fluke states that vibration-pattern recognition requires significant training and experience.[96][97][95][98]

### Procurement verdict and what would change it

**Ranked technical verdict for the stated requirements**

1. **Honeywell HVT/EHM is provisionally preferred for the stated whole-of-site general rotating-equipment requirement.** HVT provides transmitter-level RMS, FFT, RPM, statistical and static-threshold processing; its sensing enclosure is IP66/IP67 at −40 to +80 °C; and EHM OPC UA delivers current values, historical values and alarms to the assumed legacy SCADA. [16][5]

2. **Siemens MS200/SCM IQ ranks second.** General-purpose MS200 is −30 to +80 °C with IP68/IP69 ratings, but the short BLE link is up to 30 m and each CC220 is IP20, 0 to +50 °C and cabinet-mounted. [10][1][6]

3. **MOVUS FitMachine 3XT/PlantOS ranks third.** FitMachine 3XT is specified at −20 to +80 °C and self-certified IP65, while MOVUS documents PLC, SCADA and DCS integration and a RESTful API; the selected architecture requires PlantOS-to-SCADA delivery to be configured for the installed system. [29][28][3]

4. **Vayeron Smart-Idler ranks fourth as a conveyor specialist.** It provides on-board roller analysis, 433 MHz mesh communications, PLC/SCADA and REST integration, and one Smart-Idler per roller for full coverage; it requires another system for the 110 non-conveyor positions. [30][4]

The decision criterion is a demonstrated sensor-to-existing-SCADA path meeting the site environmental requirements at a comparable installed price. Experion HS publishes the broadest driver list; CC220 is MQTT-centred and uses a data collector for protocol conversion; MindConnect IoT2050 lists source-collection protocols; MOVUS states PLC, SCADA and DCS integration with REST API integrations; and Vayeron states site-PLC/SCADA and REST routes for conveyor monitoring.[27][8][32][28][3][14][4]

The provisional order changes when measured mounting ambient temperature or dust exposure exceeds a quoted component rating; when local alert operation and exchange of condition values and alarms with the installed SCADA fails; when surveyed collector positions or backhaul extensions change the A$ totals; or when asset and roller counts change. A lower compliant same-scope installed bid changes the preferred supplier. Conveyor-dominated scope favours a Vayeron-inclusive system.[10][16][29][1][5][6][4][7]

The verdict changes in favour of Vayeron for conveyor-dominated failure exposure. Smart-Idler can be phased into high-wear, inaccessible and high-risk conveyor zones, then expanded through normal roller change-outs; full coverage should be selected only where the roller count, polling interval and avoided consequence justify it.[14][30]

The verdict changes in favour of Honeywell where the existing OT architecture requires direct use of Modbus serial/TCP, OPC UA or OPC DA and the project can accommodate LoRaWAN survey, gateway infrastructure, Experion EHM licensing and EHM/SCADA engineering.[33][27][2]

Honeywell’s technical ranking strengthens where the mine can consolidate ISA100 Wireless, WirelessHART, Wi-Fi and Ethernet/IP devices onto OneWireless infrastructure while maintaining a segregated IIoT and asset-management interface network. FDAP and PCAP hardware provide IP66/IP67, −40 to +70 °C access-point specifications, and WDM provides Modbus serial/TCP, OPC UA, OPC DA, HART serial/IP, GCI, Enraf and Experion PKS CDA interfaces.[23][64][65]

The Honeywell selection changes downward where the nominated field locations require explosive dust-proof access-point protection, because the OneWireless Network Planning and Installation Guide states that FDAP/Cisco AP dust protection does not include explosive dust-proof protection.[24]

The selection changes at asset level where critical equipment runs at very low or variable speed. SKF states that damage detection at 20 r/min or lower is difficult because of low impact energy and nearby noise, and recommends high-frequency acceleration enveloping with application-specific filter selection; variable-speed equipment requires order tracking with a speed reference such as a tachometer pulse. SKF’s twin-roll-press example used an online system with enveloping and order-tracking capabilities, a 100 mv/G accelerometer, an inductive tachometer, a 5–40 kHz enveloping filter and 100 orders. Require each contender to demonstrate the selected sensing, sample duration, filtering and speed-reference configuration on these assets in the pilot.[97]

Siemens, Honeywell, MOVUS and Vayeron do not offer interchangeable architectures. Siemens combines a high-temperature, high-ingress field sensor with a protected BLE gateway and cloud analytics; Honeywell provides the strongest documented multi-protocol SCADA integration through Experion and WDM; MOVUS provides the broader Australian rotating-equipment option with FitMachine and PlantOS; Vayeron provides conveyor-specific Smart-Idler monitoring with on-roller analysis and mesh networking.[10][1][33][3][30]

The procurement choice should therefore be made by asset class and integration architecture, not nominal wireless reach or site area. A 50 km² installed-cost comparison becomes usable only after suppliers price the same asset register, sensor count, radio survey, gateway and cabinet design, backhaul, SCADA mappings, platform licences, commissioning and five-year support scope.[6][2][3][4][14]

1. Issue one RFQ with the 110-endpoint common scenario for Siemens, Honeywell and MOVUS, and a separate conveyor-roll-count scenario for Vayeron.
2. Require component-level environmental compliance for every sensor, gateway, cabinet and antenna location.
3. Require a radio survey and installed-position acceptance test before gateway quantity is finalised.
4. Require suppliers to price all SCADA, historian, API, cyber-zone and commissioning work as separate line items.
5. Run an asset-class pilot before final award: critical rotating assets for Siemens, Honeywell and MOVUS; high-consequence conveyor sections for Vayeron.

At the published upper operating-temperature limits, the general-purpose MS200 has a 20 °C advantage over MOVUS FitMachine FMFI 3.0 and a 5 °C advantage over FitMachine 3XT EX. This quantifies Siemens’ field-sensor heat-envelope advantage among the compared general rotating-equipment sensors.[10][17][18]

For the common 110-HVT scenario, the selected 250-device EHM bundle’s 16,000 SCADA-point allowance would be 48.1% utilised using Honeywell’s approximate 70 points per HVT, leaving 8,300 SCADA points. This provides substantial published point-capacity headroom within that specific bundle selection.[2]

In Vayeron’s cited 10 km, 26,500-roller full-coverage example, the implied density is 2,650 Smart-Idlers per conveyor kilometre. This makes conveyor roller population, rather than the mine’s 50 km² footprint, the dominant endpoint-count driver for a full-coverage Smart-Idler scope.[7]

A Vayeron Start Smart minimum deployment of 500 sensors represents 10.0% of one gateway’s stated 5,000-Smart-Roller capacity. This illustrates that the supplier’s minimum data-pricing tier and gateway capacity are separate commercial and network-sizing thresholds.[14][19]

The new product specifications strengthen the comparative result on field processing and heat tolerance. Honeywell HVT now has an explicit −40 to +80 °C operating range, IP66/IP67 enclosure ratings and sensor-level RMS, FFT, RPM, statistical and static-threshold processing.[57]

The February 2026 FitMachine 3XT specification independently states −20 to +80 °C ambient operation, IP65 self-certified enclosure protection, 6.6 kHz configurable sampling, configurable FFT transfer intervals and a Wi-Fi design requirement of at least −60 dBm RSSI and 50 Kbps in each direction per sensor.[29]

ACE adds a local communications implementation option where the selected sensor platform requires mine-wide radio, cellular, satellite, fibre or LoRaWAN design and protocol integration to legacy SCADA, PLC and RTU systems.[77]

For Siemens, the published €12-per-month Sensor Upgrade implies €15,408 per year for 107 extensions in the 110-MS200 scenario (110 connections less the three included in the base package). This is the published connection-extension amount only, before prediction upgrades, base subscription, gateways, cabinets, installation and integration.[2][35][39]

At Siemens’ current stated maximum of 50 MS200 sensors per CC220, 110 endpoints have a capacity-only minimum of three CC220 gateways. This is 19 fewer than the 22-gateway allowance under the earlier five-sensor limit, demonstrating why the quoted firmware, measurement configuration and supported capacity must be fixed before gateway cost comparison.[2][8][6]

The published CC220 capacity statements have a material commercial consequence: moving from a five-sensor limit to a 12-sensor limit reduces the capacity-only allowance for 110 endpoints by 12 CC220 gateways, from 22 to 10.[2][6][38]

Honeywell HVT has a 10 °C wider published operating-temperature span than Siemens’ general-purpose MS200: −40 to +80 °C (120 °C span) versus −30 to +80 °C (110 °C span). The advantage is from Honeywell’s lower-temperature limit; both publish a +80 °C upper limit.[16][10]

The selected 250-device EHM bundle would use 44.0% of its nominal 250-HVT device entitlement in the common 110-HVT scenario, leaving 140 device positions before considering SCADA-point capacity.[2]

For the cited 10 km, 26,500-roller full-coverage conveyor example, the required six-gateway capacity allocation equates to an average of 4,416.7 Smart-Idlers per gateway, or 88.3% of the stated 5,000-roller-per-gateway ceiling. This indicates that the rounded gateway schedule leaves limited nominal capacity before allowing for polling-latency, physical-placement or resilience requirements.[7][19]

In Vayeron’s shuttle return-section case, the published $14,595 hardware amount plus $1,000 per month software equals $26,595 in first-year known hardware and software cost. That total is 40.6% below the case’s stated $44,800 annual batch-change-out comparator.[75]

Comparable installed and lifecycle costs remain open for Siemens, Honeywell, MOVUS and Vayeron, including sensor unit rates, gateway and cabinet quantities, radio infrastructure, integration engineering, subscriptions, battery replacement and support. The required number and placement of LoRaWAN gateways, CC220 cabinets and MOVUS gateways across the 50 km² operating footprint also remain open. These will be settled by a common asset register and criticality ranking, physical and RF survey, confirmed legacy-SCADA tag and interface list, environmental survey of mounting locations, and supplier fixed-price design proposals with stated battery-life and service assumptions.
