# Admission and Charpak Scholarship Requirements for Paris-Saclay Economics

## Executive summary

For the English-taught Master of Economics at Université Paris-Saclay, apply separately for M1 with an economics or mathematics bachelor’s background and strong quantitative preparation, or for M2 with validated M1-level study, then submit the required online academic file.[1][2][3] An eligible Indian citizen or OCI cardholder aged 30 or under can pursue the separate France Excellence Charpak Master scholarship for the full-time Paris-Saclay degree, provided they have applied to the French institution first and meet the India-linked education and exclusion rules.[4] Charpak Master, not Charpak Exchange, is the relevant funding route for the one- or two-year degree.[4][5] The scholarship application requires its own documents and selection, and a pending admission letter may be supported by correspondence where that cycle permits it.[4] Treat each published deadline as cycle-specific and complete both the university and scholarship applications by their respective closing dates.

## Applicable program and scholarship routes

**As of 30 November 2025, the relevant study route is Université Paris-Saclay’s English-taught, selective two-year Master of Economics (MoE): normally entry at M1 Economics (Master’s Year 1), followed by M2 Economics (Master’s Year 2), or direct M2 entry for an applicant who has validated an M1 programme (240 ECTS credits). For an eligible applicant in India pursuing the full degree, the corresponding funding route is the France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship.** Degree admission and scholarship selection are separate processes.[1][6][4][5][3]

### Which program and scholarship routes apply to a student pursuing

**The applicable degree is the Master of Economics (MoE), normally entered at M1 Economics and followed by M2 Economics. Direct entry to M2 is a distinct option for an applicant who has validated an M1 programme (240 ECTS credits).** Université Paris-Saclay lists M1 Economics as Master’s Year 1 and M2 Economics as Master’s Year 2 within the Economics master’s, which it describes as a selective, two-year programme taught in English.[1][7][6][3] M1 is the first year of the route and provides the common foundation for M2; M2 is the second year and enables faculty-supervised individual curricula and research specialisation.[7][2]

The institutional arrangement matters when an applicant is identifying the degree on an application or scholarship form. The CEPS Paris-Saclay description states that ENS Paris-Saclay certifies M1, while Université Paris-Saclay certifies M2 in cooperation with AgroParisTech, CentraleSupélec and the University of Evry-Val d’Essonne.[2] ENS Paris-Saclay also presents Master 1 of Economics and Master 2 Economics as English-taught and open to international students.[8]

Do not confuse the MoE with the separate Master 1 Economics, Organisations and Society (EOS), which is taught in French: the requested route is the English-taught MoE at M1 or M2.[6][8]

For an Indian applicant seeking French-government funding for a full Economics master’s degree, the relevant scholarship route is the **France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship**, administered through the French Embassy in India and Campus France India, not Université Paris-Saclay’s Economics admissions committee.[5][9]  Its four funding types are Bachelor’s Degree Programmes, Master’s Degree Programmes, Summer Training Scholarships, and Exchange programmes.[5][9] The Master scholarship is offered to Indian students admitted to a master’s programme in France.[5]

Charpak Master supports one to two years of master’s-level study in France across all streams and fields; Economics is consequently within its stated field scope.[4]

Use Charpak Master, not **France Excellence Charpak Exchange**: Exchange funds one semester under a partnership between the Indian home institution and a French host institution, in autumn (September–December) or spring (January–May/June), rather than the one- or two-year full-degree MoE.[4][5]

Université Paris-Saclay’s **International Master’s Scholarships Program** (also called the IDEX scholarship in Campus France India material) is a separate university scholarship, not Charpak.[9][10] The Paris-Saclay programme covers M1 and M2, whereas the Charpak Master scholarship is the Indian French-government route for a master’s programme in France.[5][10]

Paris-Saclay’s International Master’s Scholarships Program has separate eligibility rules: foreign nationality; admission to a master’s operated by one of the listed Paris-Saclay institutions; first-time enrolment in France, subject to stated exceptions; and age under 30 during the application year.[10] It also excludes students receiving other funding above €600 per month and cannot be combined with France Excellence Eiffel, France Excellence Europa or Erasmus Mundus scholarships.[10] Its stated value is €10,000 per year, paid monthly from September to June, plus a yearly travel-and-visa flat fee of up to €900 depending on country of origin; payment begins only after arrival in France and registration in the admitted programme.[10] These rules belong to the Paris-Saclay scholarship and must not be attributed to Charpak.

Accordingly, the relevant pairing is MoE admission at M1 or M2 plus, for an eligible Indian applicant, the full-degree Charpak Master scholarship; Charpak Exchange and Paris-Saclay’s International Master’s/IDEX scholarship are distinct routes.[6][2][4][5][10]

Université Paris-Saclay’s M1 Economics page lists Economics, Social sciences and Mathematics as the programme’s admission-route categories.[11]

The France Excellence Charpak Summer Training Scholarship is also a distinct route and does not fund the MoE degree. It is for a short-term internship or research project at a French laboratory or institution during an academic break between May and August; the project must last from one to two months.[12]

The degree-application route is separate again from the scholarship route. Université Paris-Saclay directs an applicant to its Master’s applications-and-enrolment guidance to identify the applicable application platform. For an international applicant who is not on an exchange programme and is seeking an individual degree, the university states that the enrolment procedure depends on country of residence, country of origin and level of study.[13][14] Campus France states that Mon Master is the national common-calendar application platform for first-year national-master’s programmes: it applies to international students already resident in France or the European Economic Area and to those resident in countries not covered by Études en France, while international students resident in an Études en France country apply through Études en France.[15]

Campus France India’s Charpak Master’s page confirms that the Master’s Scholarship, rather than the Exchange Scholarship, is designed for one or two years of master’s-level study in France in all streams and fields of study.[16] By contrast, the Charpak Exchange Scholarship is for Indian students enrolled at bachelor’s or master’s level in an Indian institution who undertake a four-to-six-month exchange semester in France.[17]

For the relevant M1 route, applications are dossier-based and online. Paris-Saclay gives monmaster.gouv.fr as the default M1 platform, while stating that the platform depends on the chosen programme and the applicant’s situation.[18] Mon Master permits one application file for first-year master’s applications on a nationally common calendar and covers first-year programmes leading to the diplôme national de master (DNM).[15]

For M1 applicants living in an Études en France country, Paris-Saclay requires application first through INCEPTION when the programme opens; if selected, the applicant then completes Études en France in the “I am accepted” section.[19][3] Thus, for applicants following this M1 route, the university application comes before any Études en France step.[19][3]

For an M1 applicant who is European or lives outside an Études en France country, Paris-Saclay directs applicants to INCEPTION when the programme appears on its listed M1-programme list and to monmaster.gouv.fr when it does not.[3] The M1 Economics page identifies Economics, Social Sciences and Mathematics as admission-route categories, without assigning a particular category to a particular applicant.[11]

M2 applications are dossier-based through the university’s INCEPTION platform regardless of the applicant’s situation.[20] An M2 applicant who has validated an M1 programme has 240 ECTS credits; an applicant living in an Études en France country applies through INCEPTION first and, if admitted, then uses Études en France’s “I am accepted” section.[3] Accordingly, for the relevant M1 route the university application precedes the Études en France step, while M2 applications are dossier-based through INCEPTION.[19][20]

A Paris-Saclay M1 repeater in Economics and Management is directed to contact the Master’s academic manager, Christophe.roca@universite-paris-saclay.fr.[21][18]

Charpak Master is for full-time master’s programmes. It does not cover semester exchanges, internships or research projects, and a student already pursuing a master’s in France may apply for funding for the second year.[22][23]

Paris-Saclay’s separate International Master’s Scholarships Program covers all academic fields and all Paris-Saclay master’s programmes except vocational programmes (“formations en apprentissage”).[10] Both M1 and M2 are eligible; for an M1 laureate, renewal for the second year is automatic after completion of all required credits and admission to a Université Paris-Saclay M2 programme.[10] Only students contacted by the Master’s admission jury may submit an application for this scholarship; unsolicited applications are not accepted.[24]

The M1 application platform depends on the applicant’s residence category and, for European applicants and applicants living outside the Études en France procedure, whether the chosen programme is on Paris-Saclay’s listed M1-programme list. European applicants use INCEPTION for a programme on that list and monmaster.gouv.fr for one outside it. Applicants living in a country not covered by Études en France likewise use INCEPTION for a listed programme and monmaster.gouv.fr for a programme not on the list.[3]

Applicants living in a country covered by the Études en France procedure apply to M1 through INCEPTION first, irrespective of that M1-programme list; if admitted, they then use the Études en France portal’s I am accepted section.[3]

### The circumstances and purpose of Université Paris-Saclay’s Economics master’s and

The MoE is a “competitive research program” offered by University Paris Saclay and Ecole Normale Supérieure Paris Saclay, rather than a funding scheme.[1][26] It prepares students for careers in academia and national and international institutions and provides research-level economics training that can lead to PhD study.[1][7][6][26]

The programme teaches theoretical and empirical economics—the use of economic models and data to explain or test economic questions—alongside critical thinking about their use. Students are expected to formulate and contextualise complex economic problems, create economic models, analyse data, communicate results and undertake independent research through a research master’s thesis.[1][26] The degree is presented as a gateway to selective Economics PhD programmes internationally, while also leading to careers in public institutions, international organisations, consulting and expertise.[1][7][26]

Its design reinforces that research focus. M1 provides a common foundation through eight compulsory core courses, two optional discovery courses and two research projects. M2 then permits faculty-supervised construction of an individual curriculum around a research agenda, including specialisation in Trade, Geography and Development Economics; Public and Behavioral Economics; or Advances and Challenges in Macroeconomics.[1][7][6] The CEPS description additionally identifies M1 training in microeconomics, macroeconomics and econometrics, followed by M2 specialisation and a faculty-supervised thesis.[2]

The Charpak Master scholarship serves a different purpose: it is French-government funding in India for Indian students admitted to master’s programmes in France, rather than the academic programme itself. It supports one or two years of master’s study in all fields and may be renewed once on request based on first-year results.[4][5] Awardees receive French-government scholarship-holder status and a monthly living allowance of €860, student social security and supplementary health insurance, accommodation-search assistance with priority access to CROUS residences subject to availability, and €1 university-restaurant meals.[4] The scholarship does not pay travel from India to France, travel within France, or other costs related to the recipient’s stay.[4]

These are different decisions by different bodies: Université Paris-Saclay evaluates entry to the Economics curriculum, while the French Embassy in India and Campus France India evaluate Charpak eligibility and competition for financial support.[1][5][9] Charpak is additional funding, not an alternative method of entering the programme.[1][5]

**The programme’s research orientation does not change the scholarship route: an Economics applicant uses the MoE route to obtain the degree place and the Charpak Master route, rather than Charpak Exchange, to seek support for the full master’s degree.**[1][4][5]

Campus France India lists additional Charpak Master benefits: exemption from visa fees when applying from India and from Campus France procedure fees, assistance finding affordable accommodation through Campus France, €1 university-restaurant meals, and access to Campus France social and cultural activities.[16] French-government scholarship holders enrolled in a programme leading to a national diploma at a French public higher-education institution may be exempt from differentiated enrolment fees; the awardee checks the enrolled institution’s exemption policy.[16]

The Charpak Master scholarship is renewable once, on request and based on the scholarship holder’s first-year master’s results.[5]

Charpak Summer Training is a separate short-term route: its research internship or project must last at least one month and no more than two months, and an awardee covers the expenses for any period beyond two months.[12]

At the stated €860 monthly allowance and September-to-June 10-month academic-year duration, Charpak Master support totals €8,600 for one academic year and €17,200 across two academic years, before considering any benefits that are not cash allowances.[4][27]

Charpak selection also assesses the quality and coherence of the applicant’s proposed study project; admission to the Economics master’s alone does not determine the scholarship result.

### Synthesis

As of 30 November 2025, the appropriate academic route for the requested course is the English-taught, selective two-year Master of Economics: M1 Economics followed by M2 Economics, with the stated ENS Paris-Saclay and Université Paris-Saclay certification arrangement.[1][6][2][8] For an eligible Indian student, the corresponding French-government funding route is France Excellence Charpak Master, which supports one or two years of master’s study across fields and is offered to Indian students admitted to a French master’s programme.[4][5]

The routes are complementary but independent: MoE admission is academic selection for a research-oriented Economics degree, while Charpak is scholarship support. Charpak Exchange is too narrow because it funds only a partnership-based semester, and Paris-Saclay’s International Master’s/IDEX scholarship is a separate university award with its own conditions.[1][5][10]

## Université Paris-Saclay admission

The Master of Economics is a two-year programme taught in English.[1] Admission to Université Paris-Saclay Master's programmes is selective as places are limited.[28] Admission is therefore a programme-selection process, followed by administrative enrolment; it is separate from any scholarship application discussed later.[28]

### What are the admission requirements and eligibility criteria for the

For first-year entry (M1), Université Paris-Saclay identifies Economics, Social Sciences and Mathematics as admission routes. Its stated target qualifications are a BA in Economics, BS in Mathematics, Licence d’Économie or Licence de Mathématique; applications are expressly open to applicants with an economics bachelor’s degree or mathematics bachelor’s degree.[1][26]

Because the programme is taught entirely in English, applicants must be able to undertake English-language study. The M1 and M2 application lists require an English-level certificate for non-English speakers or GMAT/GRE results; GMAT and GRE are standardised graduate-admissions tests. The required proficiency level depends on the programme, and the university’s FAQ does not give an Economics-specific threshold or prescribe a particular examination.[1][7][2][24] The programme states that it requires strong knowledge in quantitative methods.

Although the programme is described as English-taught, the ENS Paris-Saclay M1 listing displays both French and English as languages.[52][11]

The Master of Economics lists English-language evidence for non-English speakers but no programme-specific French-language certificate.[52][11][7][19]

Its economics curriculum covers microeconomics, macroeconomics and applied econometrics and develops theoretical and empirical economics techniques; this illustrates why preparation in economic theory, mathematics, statistics and econometrics is relevant, rather than setting separately stated subject-by-subject admission thresholds. It describes an economics or mathematics bachelor’s degree as the normal preparation, while saying that other science or social-science degrees from leading universities may be considered.[2][29]

The Master of Economics is a two-year general research training in economics.[2] It comprises M1 and M2, its first and second years. M1 is certified by ENS Paris-Saclay, while M2 is certified by Université Paris-Saclay in cooperation with AgroParisTech, CentraleSupélec and the University of Evry-Val d’Essonne.[2]

For direct second-year entry (M2), the university’s international-applicant guidance requires a validated M1 programme, stated in that guidance as 240 ECTS credits. ECTS, the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System, is the standard European measure of study credits.[3]

The M2 Economics page also names BA Economics, BS Mathematics, Licence d’Économie and Licence de Mathématique as academic backgrounds, alongside the M1-level entry requirement.[7]

Admission to Université Paris-Saclay Master's programmes is selective as places are limited.[28] Selection for admission is separate from administrative enrolment. An admission certificate or authorisation to enrol remains conditional on completing administrative formalities and satisfying regulatory requirements, including the Registrar’s Office verification of the qualification required for entry.[28]

**The practical eligibility conclusion is that M1 principally recruits applicants with economics or mathematics bachelor’s preparation, while strong candidates from other science or social-science backgrounds may be considered individually; M2 is a distinct entry point requiring prior M1-level study.**[1][2][3]

The M1 Economics programme page lists 50 available places.[11][13]

The candidate’s account gives a different capacity figure: it says the Economics programme listed 20 places.[28]

The programme describes its objective as providing “a general curriculum in economics at the research level,” allowing continuation to a PhD programme and developing theoretical and empirical economics techniques, concepts and critical thinking about their use.[29]

It covers microeconomics, macroeconomics and applied econometrics, and includes research projects from the first year.[29]

The ENS Paris-Saclay Economics page describes both the English-taught M1 and M2 Economics routes as open to international students.[8]

Academic eligibility for the Master of Economics has no nationality restriction, although an applicant’s nationality and residence affect the application procedure.[52][11][7][19]

Mon Master’s general eligibility threshold includes students who hold, or are completing, a bac+3-level qualification; this is the platform-level threshold rather than an Economics-specific admissions rule.[15]

For applications made through Mon Master, the platform is open to students who hold, or are completing, a bac+3-level qualification; separately, the Economics programme identifies BA Economics, BS Mathematics, Licence d’Économie and Licence de Mathématique as its target academic preparation.[15][11]

After a successful application, the applicant must confirm their choice on the designated platform before completing registration. Every student enrolled full-time must obtain proof of payment of the Student and Campus Life Contribution (CVEC), or proof of exemption, before registration and send the CVEC certificate during registration.[28]

As of November 2025, admission to Université Paris-Saclay Economics is selective: the university assesses the applicant’s academic record and fit with the chosen M1 or M2 pathway, because places are limited.[28][1][2][3]

As of November 2025, applicants from related quantitative disciplines can be considered for M1 Economics where their prior studies provide adequate economics and mathematics preparation; the programme specifically says that science and social-science degrees from leading universities will be considered.[2]

The admissions assessment considers whether prior study provides adequate preparation in economic theory.

Preparation in econometrics is relevant to admission because the programme includes applied econometrics and research-level empirical economics.

For M1, the normal academic threshold was a bachelor’s degree or officially equivalent qualification representing 180 ECTS credits, with a sound background in economics. In addition to the academic record and fit with the curriculum, the admissions review considered the coherence of the proposed study plan.

For M1 entry, an undergraduate economics degree with advanced microeconomics, mathematics and statistics is a qualifying preparation pathway.[30]

For M1 entry, an undergraduate mathematics degree, or a degree in mathematics and informatics applied to the social sciences or management, is also a qualifying preparation pathway.[30]

The required language level depends on the selected programme pathway and is stated in that master’s programme record; applicants provide language-test evidence already available to them when supporting evidence is requested.[20]

## France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship eligibility

The France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship is a funding route, not an admissions route. An applicant for the Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics must therefore satisfy two separate sets of conditions: Paris-Saclay decides whether the applicant may study on the programme, while Charpak decides whether an eligible applicant receives scholarship funding.

### What are the France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship eligibility criteria

France Excellence Charpak is the French government scholarship programme in India. Its Charpak Master route supports one to two years of master’s-level study in France in all streams and fields of study.[34] Economics is therefore within its subject scope when the applicant is pursuing the full Paris-Saclay master’s degree rather than a short mobility placement.[22][34]

**Nationality and age:** The applicant must be an Indian citizen or hold an OCI card—an Overseas Citizen of India card—and must be 30 years old or younger at the time of application.[4][35]

The Charpak Master rules do not impose on OCI applicants the residence-in-India condition found in the separate Charpak Exchange scheme.[4][55]

Prior education: The applicant must currently be enrolled at, or have completed studies at, an Indian institution of higher education. A person holding a degree from a foreign institution may also apply, but the scholarship gives preference to applicants educated at an Indian institution.[4]

Type and location of study: The scholarship is for a full-time master’s degree programme in France and supports one to two years of master’s-level study. It excludes semester exchanges, internships and research projects. If the course includes one semester or more in another country, Charpak does not fund that period.[22][34]

French language: Knowledge of French is an asset—something that may strengthen an application—but is not compulsory for Charpak eligibility. This is distinct from any language requirement that Université Paris-Saclay may set for the Economics programme itself.[4]

Charpak specifies no IELTS band, TOEFL score, English-language threshold or mandatory French-language level for scholarship eligibility.[4][4][56]

Study-status exclusions: An applicant is ineligible if they are a PhD student, intend to come to France solely for a master’s thesis, training programme or research project, or have previously received the Charpak Master scholarship.[4]

Second-year route: Campus France India states that students already pursuing a master’s degree in France can apply for second-year funding.[34] The stated M1-to-M2 route is limited: a current Charpak holder completing M1 in France may reapply for M2 funding, whereas a student pursuing M1 in France without a scholarship is ineligible.[27][36] Reapplication is not automatic; it depends on M1 academic performance and a new statement of purpose.[27][36]

Institution application first: The applicant must have applied for admission to a French institution before applying for Charpak. For a prospective Paris-Saclay Economics student, that means making the separate university application before the scholarship application.[4][34]

There is no published minimum CGPA or percentage threshold. Instead, in Round 1 the Charpak selection committee evaluates complete applications for academic excellence, consistency, the quality of the statement of purpose—an applicant’s written explanation of study plans and motivation—and the applicant’s overall profile.[16][34][22]

The official Campus France India criteria therefore make the Paris-Saclay Economics master’s a potentially suitable Charpak route for an Indian or OCI applicant only where it is their full-time, France-based master’s course and they meet the nationality, age, prior-education and exclusion rules.[16]

A point requires care. Campus France India permits applicants educated at foreign institutions, although it prefers Indian educational backgrounds; a separate scholarship listing instead says that the applicant must hold a bachelor’s degree or equivalent from a recognised Indian university. The Campus France India criterion is the applicable Charpak rule stated here.[4][37]

Similarly, Campus France India excludes prior recipients of the Charpak Master scholarship. Another listing describes a broader exclusion for anyone who has previously received a French-government scholarship; that broader rule should not be substituted for the specifically stated Charpak exclusion.[4][37]

How selection works: Complete written applications are reviewed for academic excellence, consistency, the quality of the statement of purpose and the applicant’s overall profile.[38] Shortlisted applicants may also be invited to an online interview.[39]

Combination with other awards: Charpak scholarships cannot be combined with other private, public or institutional scholarships.[40]

Additional selection considerations: Alongside academic excellence and a strong statement of purpose, a tie-up between the applicant’s current Indian institution and future French institution is an asset in selection.[36][41] Relevant work experience is also named as a selection consideration.[40] The same listing names letters of recommendation, the statement of purpose and a résumé among the application materials considered.[40]

The proposed course must be a recognised master’s-level programme in France; for this applicant, that may be the Paris-Saclay Economics M1 or an eligible M2.

The France Excellence Charpak Master Scholarship’s 2025 call was updated on 17 December 2024.[42]

### How does admission to the university program differ from eligibility

Admission to Université Paris-Saclay’s Master’s in Economics is the university’s decision on whether an applicant may enrol. The university’s application route depends on nationality, country of residence and chosen studies, and applicants use the relevant Paris-Saclay application platform.[3]

An admission decision is also not the same as completed enrolment. Paris-Saclay states that an admission certificate or authorisation to enrol remains conditional on administrative formalities and regulatory requirements, including verification by the Registrar’s Office of the qualifications needed for entry. The programme-specific requirements and documents belong under **Université Paris-Saclay admission**, not under Charpak.[28]

Charpak eligibility is narrower in a different way: it is a French-government-in-India scholarship route for applicants meeting its India-linked and master’s-study conditions. First applying to Paris-Saclay is necessary for Charpak, but it neither proves Charpak eligibility nor submits a Charpak application. A Charpak applicant must also submit the scholarship application through the online scholarship portal with scans of the required documents.[4][5][43]

The two processes also assess different questions. Paris-Saclay assesses whether the applicant is admissible to the Economics master’s and can complete registration; Charpak separately selects among scholarship applicants on academic excellence, consistency, application quality and the statement of purpose, without a CGPA or percentage cut-off.[28][41]

Charpak requires two admission-evidence stages: first apply to the French institution before applying for the scholarship; then submit the French institution’s admission or acceptance letter with the scholarship application if it has been received.[4][16] The general Campus France India programme description says the Master scholarship is offered to Indian students admitted to a master’s programme in France.

The Charpak Master application page, however, requires only a prior application to a French institution and permits an applicant without an acceptance letter by the scholarship deadline to provide email correspondence showing that the letter is being obtained or is expected later.[5][16]

**Accordingly, submit the Paris-Saclay admission application first. Submit the admission or acceptance letter with the Charpak application if received; if it is pending at the scholarship deadline, submit email correspondence showing that it is being obtained or will be received after that deadline.** The university’s eventual admission decision remains necessary to enrol, while Charpak’s selection decision remains separate.[4][16][28]

Applicants should also distinguish the Charpak Master route from Charpak Exchange. Charpak Exchange supports a semester in France for bachelor’s or master’s students under a partnership between an Indian institution and a French host institution; it runs in autumn and spring sessions. That is not the appropriate route for funding the full Paris-Saclay Economics master’s degree.[5]

Nor should Charpak be confused with Université Paris-Saclay’s own International Master’s Scholarships Program or its IDEX scholarship route. Those are separate university-linked awards with their own eligibility, nomination and funding rules; Charpak is administered as a France Excellence programme for Indian students.[5][9][10]

Paris-Saclay also distinguishes a master’s application from administrative enrolment: administrative enrolment takes place after admission has been confirmed.[18]

Université Paris-Saclay states that its master’s programmes have limited capacity and are selective. Admission therefore depends on the university’s evaluation of the application and available places, rather than on meeting Charpak’s scholarship eligibility conditions.[21][15]

For an international applicant seeking an individual degree rather than participating in an exchange, Université Paris-Saclay states that the enrolment procedure depends on the applicant’s country of residence, country of origin and study level. An applicant resident in a country covered by Études en France applies first through INCEPTION during the programme’s opening period and, if selected, completes the Études en France file in the “je suis accepté” section.[14][19]

Where applicable, Études en France also supports admission administration and the student-visa process; it is not the Charpak scholarship application portal.[19][4][57][58]

A student who is neither an Indian citizen nor an OCI card holder may be able to apply for admission to the Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics under the university route applicable to their nationality, country of residence and chosen studies, but cannot meet Charpak’s nationality condition. University admission can therefore be broader than Charpak eligibility.[4][3]

Where Charpak permits an application while the university decision is pending, any scholarship award remains conditional on the applicant obtaining admission to the proposed master’s programme.

### Synthesis

Read together, the rules create a two-stage position for a prospective Paris-Saclay Economics student. First, the applicant must pursue the university admission route and apply to the French institution. Second, the applicant must independently satisfy Charpak’s Indian/OCI nationality, age, educational-background, full-time-France-based study and exclusion conditions.[4]

The strongest practical distinction is that Paris-Saclay admission determines eligibility to enrol, whereas Charpak selection determines access to funding and uses its own merit assessment. For Charpak, the admission evidence proceeds in order: apply first to the French institution, then provide the admission or acceptance letter with the scholarship application, or—if it is pending by the scholarship deadline—the permitted email correspondence showing that the letter is being obtained or will arrive after the deadline.[4][16]

1. Apply first to the Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics through the applicable university route. [3]
2. Plan separate funding for any period of one semester or more spent studying in another country, because Charpak does not cover that period. [4]
3. Apply separately for Charpak only if the nationality, age, education and prior-award conditions are met. [4]
4. Treat admission and scholarship outcomes as separate: an admission application is required for Charpak, but does not itself secure scholarship funding. [4]

## Scholarship application and deadlines

### What are the scholarship application requirements and required documents?

The France Excellence Charpak Master’s Scholarship uses a separate online scholarship application from the Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics admission application.[4][16] It is separate from an application for admission to the Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics. The scholarship application requires the French higher-education institution’s admission or acceptance letter, or specified email correspondence if that letter is still being obtained.[16]

**Complete one Charpak online form through the Institut Français India scholarship portal.** Submit the form and document scans through `scholarship.institutfrancaisindia.in`. If applying to two or more universities, name the top-choice university in the form; only one online application is permitted, and multiple applications lead to disqualification. Complete the statement of purpose (SOP), the written explanation requested at the end of the form; it undergoes a plagiarism check, and applications with significant AI-generated content are disqualified.[16]

Prepare and upload the following document set:

- **Identity documents:** a formal passport-size photograph and a copy of the first passport page showing the photograph and expiry date. An applicant who expects to receive a passport after the deadline may upload proof that a passport application is pending instead. [16]

- **Curriculum Vitae (CV):** a CV, meaning a concise academic and professional résumé, of no more than two pages. [16]

- **French-institution admission evidence:** the admission or acceptance letter from the French higher-education institution. If the letter has not arrived by the scholarship deadline, the applicant may upload email correspondence showing that the letter is being obtained or is expected after the deadline. [16]

- **Academic records:** mark sheets and degree documents for Higher Secondary School (XII), bachelor’s and master’s studies, including mark sheets for completed semesters of an ongoing degree. [4][16]

- **Applicable French or English proficiency evidence:** submit the language-proficiency evidence required by the scholarship call or proposed course; the Charpak Master’s checklist names a French-language certificate, if any (DELF/DALF). [16]

- **Recommendation letter(s):** letters from the current Indian university or employer, or from the institution most recently attended. They must be scanned copies of original typed and signed paper letters and include the referee’s signature and contact details. The instructions prefer letters that refer to the scholarship and, where possible, use institutional letterhead and stamp. [16]

All listed documents must be uploaded in PDF or JPEG format. Word, RTF, ODT, TXT and MSG files, ZIP folders, screenshots, and scans of social-media material are not accepted; an application with missing or incorrect required documents will be rejected.[16]

The Charpak Master checklist does not specify a separate no-objection certificate (NOC), a separate motivation letter outside the form, a compulsory English score, a Class X record, document translation or legalisation, a maximum file size, or direct submission by referees.[4][55][12] A NOC appears in Charpak Exchange or Summer Training materials, not the Master checklist.[55][12]

Université Paris-Saclay separately states that only students contacted by its Master’s admission jury may complete an application for a university scholarship and that unsolicited applications are not accepted. That rule concerns the University’s own scholarship procedure; the Charpak route is the separate Institut Français India online application described above.[24][4]

The practical result is that an applicant may pursue several French university applications, but must submit only one Charpak application and identify one first-choice institution in it.[4]

Statement of purpose: prepare a statement of purpose—an explanation of the applicant’s reasons for study and fit with the scholarship—for scholarship selection. The selection committee evaluates the quality of the statement of purpose alongside academic excellence, consistency, the quality of the application, and the overall applicant profile; there is no minimum CGPA or percentage cut-off.[4][41]

The scholarship form also includes a **Statement of Purpose (SOP)**, meaning the applicant’s written explanation of motivation and plans. The 2025 application instructions state that this SOP is subject to a plagiarism check; they discourage AI-tool use and state that an application containing significant AI-generated content will be disqualified.[38][43]

The selection committee then assesses complete applications on academic excellence, consistency, SOP quality and the applicant’s overall profile. The 2025 instructions state that there is no minimum CGPA or percentage requirement.[34][43]

- **Statement of purpose (SOP):** complete the SOP at the end of the scholarship application form. It is subject to a plagiarism check; the instructions discourage AI-tool use and disqualify applications containing significant AI-generated content. [16]

The Charpak statement of purpose is a separate scholarship-form response, not automatically the motivation letter submitted for Paris-Saclay admission.[4][4]

Campus France India evaluates complete applications on academic excellence, consistency, SOP quality and the applicant’s overall profile. It states that there is no minimum CGPA (cumulative grade-point average) or percentage requirement.[16]

The initial eligibility screen checks academic requirements, the age limit, and required documents. Although there is no minimum CGPA (cumulative grade-point average) or percentage requirement, applicants must still pass those initial checks.[34][16]

A 2023 webinar recommends applying to the French institution first and registering on the scholarship portal after receiving a conditional or unconditional admission offer.[44]

In an application-form walkthrough, the presenter says that each SOP response has a 200-word limit and identifies prompts on motivation and academic and professional goals.[45]

Overlapping documents; separate submissions: academic records can be required by both Université Paris-Saclay and the Charpak administrator, but uploading them to one application does not submit them to the other.[4][28][16]

Name the same Université Paris-Saclay Master’s in Economics programme in the Charpak form as the programme for which you are seeking or have obtained admission.

- **Employment or internship record:** upload an employment or internship record if applicable. [46]

Apply for admission to the French higher-education institution before submitting the Charpak scholarship application.[47][48]
