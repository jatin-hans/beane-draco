# Fortive Segment Performance and Post-Separation Portfolio Strategy

## Executive summary

Fortive’s pre-spin results support prioritizing the retained IOS and AHS businesses, but they do not yet demonstrate that spinning off Precision Technologies (PT) strengthened their competitive positions. IOS delivered the strongest reported unit economics among the retained businesses, while the margin trajectory must be assessed separately from the margin level because PT’s 2024 reported margin included a nonrecurring property-sale gain and its 2024 revenue mix changed with the EA acquisition.

Segment revenue momentum from 2023 to 2024 was shaped by both underlying demand and portfolio changes, including the Invetech realignment and divestiture and the EA acquisition, so it is not a like-for-like organic-growth ranking.

Total pre-separation Fortive operating margin improved in Q1 2025 versus Q1 2024, but that comparison covers IOS, AHS and PT and therefore cannot establish the margin trajectory of the post-spin Fortive perimeter.

The separation sharpened strategic focus by leaving Fortive with IOS’s workflow, software, productivity and safety platform and AHS’s healthcare-workflow platform, yet competitive strengthening remains a plausible strategic benefit rather than an observed causal result.[1][2][3][4][5]

## Portfolio perimeter and separation

### What were Fortive’s three segments used for the requested historical

Fortive’s historical reporting perimeter—the set of businesses included in its reported financial results—contained three reportable segments: Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS), Precision Technologies (PT), and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS). A reportable segment is a business grouping whose results management uses to assess performance and allocate resources. Fortive’s 2024 Form 10-K states that it operated and reported results in these three segments.[1]

- **Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS):** IOS provided advanced instrumentation, software and services for customers’ mission-critical workflows. Its offerings included electrical test and measurement, facility and asset-lifecycle software, and connected-worker safety and compliance solutions. Its served markets included manufacturing, process industries, healthcare, utilities and power, and communications and electronics. [6] [7]

- **Precision Technologies (PT):** PT supplied electrical test and measurement, sensing and material technologies to industrial, power and energy, automotive, medical equipment, food and beverage, aerospace and defense, semiconductor, and other general-industry markets. Fortive described PT as addressing technical challenges from food and beverage production and manufacturing to next-generation electric vehicles and clean energy, as customers sought test solutions for electrification and connectivity. [7] [3] [8]

- **Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS):** AHS supplied healthcare workflow solutions, including instrument sterilization and tracking, biomedical test tools, radiation detection and safety monitoring, and clinical productivity software. Fortive lists Advanced Sterilization Products for infection-prevention technologies, Censis for surgical asset management, Fluke Health Solutions for healthcare test and measurement, and Provation for clinical productivity solutions. Its offerings were aimed at improving patient-care efficiency, healthcare safety and the reliability of clinical workflows. [6] [3] [8] [9]

These three segments were not merely descriptive labels. Fortive stated that its chief operating decision maker assessed performance and allocated resources using the operating segments, which were also its reportable segments.[6] The segment measure used throughout the historical analysis is operating profit, which Fortive defined as revenue less operating expenses, excluding other income or expense, interest and income taxes.[10]

The requested 2023, 2024, Q1 2025 and Q2 2025 historical calculations therefore begin with the three-segment Fortive perimeter: IOS, PT and AHS. Fortive’s 2024 annual report provides the historical revenue series for all three, while the Q1 2025 and Q2 2025 pre-separation reporting also retained that three-segment structure.[1][11][2]

A reader should distinguish this historical reporting structure from the company now called Fortive after the spin-off. The former three-segment group was the basis for the requested historical calculations; it is not the same business perimeter as post-separation Fortive.

**Conclusion:** The correct historical portfolio for the requested segment calculations is IOS, PT and AHS. The analysis must retain PT in the 2023-through-Q2 2025 historical figures even though PT subsequently left Fortive.[1][2]

Scope and method. The historical perimeter used for the requested 2023, 2024, Q1 2025 and Q2 2025 calculations is the three-segment Fortive group: Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS), Precision Technologies (PT) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS). The continuing-operations perimeter after the PT separation is IOS and AHS, with PT/Ralliant presented as discontinued operations.[1] Segment operating profit is revenue less operating expenses, excluding other income or expense, interest and income taxes; company-wide operating income in this report means Fortive’s reported operating profit.[12][10] The operating-margin formula is operating profit ÷ revenue × 100; the revenue-growth formula is (current-period revenue − prior-period revenue) ÷ prior-period revenue × 100.[3][1] A percentage-point change is the later margin minus the earlier margin, rather than the percentage change in the margin itself.[12][3] Dollar inputs are shown in $ millions to one decimal place; calculated margins, growth rates and percentage-point changes are rounded to one decimal place.[1][12][3]

For the years ended December 31, 2023 and 2024, Fortive reported segment revenue, respectively, of $2,612.2 million and $2,714.7 million for IOS, $2,223.7 million and $2,229.4 million for PT, and $1,229.4 million and $1,287.7 million for AHS. Total 2024 company revenue was $6,231.8 million.[1][8]

Fortive’s chief operating decision maker was its chief executive officer. The chief executive officer used segment gross profit and operating profit to assess performance and allocate resources, including merger and acquisition targets; unallocated and other costs were excluded from the evaluation of reportable-segment operating performance.[5]

Fortive described IOS’s 2024 versus 2023 total revenue growth of 3.9% as GAAP total revenue growth; IOS operating-profit margin was 26.0% in 2024 and 24.1% in 2023.[1]

Fortive reported segment operating profit separately from Other. Other consisted of unallocated corporate costs and other costs not considered in evaluating reportable-segment operating performance; segment operating margins therefore exclude these costs, whereas company-wide operating profit includes them.[13]

### Which business units remained after the Precision Technologies separation?

Following the Precision Technologies separation, Fortive retained two strategic segments: Intelligent Operating Solutions and Advanced Healthcare Solutions. Precision Technologies became Ralliant Corporation, an independent public company. Fortive’s June 30, 2025 completion announcement described IOS and AHS as Fortive’s strategic segments after the June 28, 2025 transaction. Fortive, [Fortive Announces Completion of the Ralliant Separation and Appointment of Olumide Soroye as President, CEO and Director of Fortive](https://investors.fortive.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/269/fortive-announces-completion-of-the-ralliant-separation-and-appointment-of-olumide-soroye-as-president-ceo-and-director-of-fortive).[14]

The business boundary was clear in Fortive’s stated transaction design. Fortive said the separation would create: first, a technology-solutions company retaining the Fortive name and the brands then operating within IOS and AHS; and second, a separate global technology company containing the brands then operating within PT. The continuing Fortive business was intended to focus on resilient, high-quality recurring growth through productivity and safety solutions, while Ralliant was to focus on precision instruments and highly engineered products connected to breakthrough innovation and secular growth trends.[1][15][4]

Ralliant itself identified the former PT operations as its historical business. Within the separated company, Ralliant reported two business segments: Test and Measurement, and Sensors and Safety Systems. These are Ralliant’s operating segments, not replacements for Fortive’s former IOS, PT and AHS reporting segments.[16][17][18]

Ralliant’s former-PT portfolio includes oscilloscopes, probes, source-measurement units, semiconductor-test systems, bidirectional power supplies and measurement software used by engineers, scientists and technicians.[50]

The distinction matters because the post-separation Fortive company is not a smaller three-segment enterprise. It is a two-segment enterprise consisting of IOS and AHS. PT/Ralliant falls outside Fortive’s continuing-operations perimeter. Fortive stated that it would classify Ralliant as a discontinued operation beginning with third-quarter 2025 financial reporting.[19][2][20]

The remaining businesses have identifiable strategic profiles:

- **IOS remained Fortive’s industrial, workflow, software and safety-oriented platform.** Its offerings include facility and asset lifecycle software, electrical test and measurement, gas detection, safety monitoring, workflow tools and connected-worker solutions. [6] [9]

- **AHS remained Fortive’s healthcare workflow and clinical-productivity platform.** Its products and services include infection prevention, sterilization, surgical asset management, healthcare test and measurement, radiation safety and clinical workflow software. [3] [9]

- **PT moved to Ralliant.** Ralliant’s stated business focus is precision technologies used by engineers across semiconductors, electronics, communications, utilities, energy storage, aerospace, defense, space and industrial manufacturing. [21] [17] [18]

The company also separated management mandates. Before the transaction, the executive leading IOS and AHS was expected to become Fortive’s chief executive officer, while the PT leader was expected to become Ralliant’s chief executive officer.[1] That leadership allocation was consistent with the intended business split: Fortive management would focus on IOS and AHS, while Ralliant management would focus exclusively on PT.[8][22][21]

**Conclusion:** The Precision Technologies separation simplified Fortive from a historical three-segment group into a two-segment continuing business—IOS and AHS—while PT became Ralliant. Any assessment of the continuing Fortive portfolio must therefore use IOS and AHS as the relevant business units, not the former three-segment structure.[14][19]

New Fortive’s IOS results include Tektronix’s Shared Service Organization (SSO) service business, which had previously been reported within PT.[23]

Ralliant expected to enter a temporary transition services agreement with Fortive under which each company would provide specified information-technology, financial and administrative services to the other on arm’s-length terms.[17]

### When did the Precision Technologies separation occur?

Fortive announced its intention to separate Precision Technologies on September 4, 2024. The announcement described a planned tax-free spin-off in which a newly formed entity would hold certain PT assets and liabilities and its shares would be distributed pro rata to Fortive shareholders.[1][24][25]

The actual separation occurred on **June 28, 2025**, effective at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time. Fortive completed the transaction by transferring PT to Ralliant and distributing all outstanding Ralliant shares to Fortive shareholders. Shareholders of record on June 16, 2025 received one Ralliant share for every three Fortive shares held.[26][19][27]

Ralliant described Fortive’s contribution of the businesses’ net assets and Ralliant’s $1.15 billion payment to Fortive on June 27, 2025 as partial consideration in the separation transaction.[50]

The transaction was a 100% spin-off rather than a sale of PT to a third party. Ralliant began regular-way trading on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) under ticker RAL on June 30, 2025, while Fortive continued trading under FTV.[14][16]

The planning timeline changed before closing. Fortive’s September 2024 announcement initially targeted the fourth quarter of 2025. Its February 2025 annual-report and earnings-release materials then referred to an early-third-quarter 2025 target. By May 2025, Fortive was targeting completion by the end of the second quarter of 2025. Each target was explicitly conditional on board approval, financing, tax treatment, effectiveness of Ralliant’s Form 10 registration statement and other regulatory approvals.[24][1][28][15]

The June 28 completion date—not the original September 2024 announcement and not any interim target—is the date that changed the portfolio perimeter. Fortive’s board approved the transaction on May 27, 2025, and the SEC declared Ralliant’s Form 10 registration statement effective on May 30, 2025; these were preparatory milestones rather than the separation date.[16]

The quarter ended June 27, 2025 was the final pre-separation reporting period; Fortive distributed Ralliant on June 28, 2025, one day later. The period-by-period reporting basis is therefore:[2]

| Reporting period | Period-end date | PT status during the period | Treatment for historical analysis |
|---|---:|---|---|
| FY 2023 | December 31, 2023 | PT was a Fortive segment | Three-segment, pre-separation Fortive perimeter. [1] |
| FY 2024 | December 31, 2024 | PT was a Fortive segment; separation remained pending | Three-segment, pre-separation Fortive perimeter. [8] [8] |
| Q1 2025 | March 28, 2025 | PT remained within Fortive | Three-segment, pre-separation Fortive perimeter. [3] [4] |
| Q2 2025 | June 27, 2025 | PT remained within Fortive through quarter-end | Three-segment operating results precede the June 28 legal separation. [2] |
| Post-separation reporting | From Q3 2025 | PT was Ralliant, outside Fortive | Fortive presents Ralliant as discontinued operations and reports IOS and AHS as continuing segments. [2] [19] |

Fortive’s Q2 2025 ended on June 27, one day before the distribution. Thus, although Q2 2025 is the final quarter associated with the separation process, its underlying three-segment operating results were generated before PT legally ceased to be part of Fortive.[2] This is especially important for the requested Q2 segment-margin analysis: the historical three-segment figures describe the final pre-spin operating quarter, not post-spin Fortive.

Fortive also incurred transaction-related activity around the separation. On June 27, 2025, Ralliant borrowed $1.15 billion and paid Fortive a cash dividend before the distribution. On July 15, 2025, Fortive used approximately $302 million of the Ralliant Dividend to redeem €252 million principal of its 3.7% euro-denominated senior unsecured notes due 2026; on July 24 and July 25, 2025, it used $324 million and $98 million, respectively, to repay the Euro Term Loan and Yen Term Loan. Fortive also intended to apply the Ralliant Dividend to the special-purpose share-repurchase program.[2] The board had authorized a special-purpose repurchase program of up to $550 million, funded exclusively from Ralliant-related cash proceeds.[2]

**Conclusion:** June 28, 2025 is the portfolio-break date. FY 2024, Q1 2025 and the quarter ended June 27, 2025 all belong to the pre-separation Fortive perimeter; post-Q2 results require a continuing-operations view that excludes PT/Ralliant.[2][26][20]

### How should Fortive’s segment and company-wide results across the requested

The central analytical rule is the **perimeter rule**: compare results only when they cover the same group of businesses, or explicitly identify the difference. Fortive’s historical three-segment calculations and post-spin Fortive results describe different corporate perimeters.

For the requested 2023-to-2024 segment revenue-growth analysis and 2024 full-year segment-margin comparison, the appropriate basis is the historical, three-segment Fortive presentation: IOS, PT and AHS. Fortive’s 2024 Form 10-K presents both years on this basis.[1] This permits valid arithmetic across the named historical segments, subject to identified portfolio changes within that period.[8]

The first such portfolio change was the **Invetech segment realignment**. Effective January 1, 2024, Fortive moved Invetech from AHS to PT because it had decided to divest Invetech’s equipment-design and manufacturing businesses while retaining the Motion Solution Business, which it considered more closely aligned with PT. Fortive recast prior-period segment amounts to conform to the revised presentation.[1][8]

This recast is important. A recast means the company restated historical segment classifications for comparative presentation. It does not rewrite the company’s originally filed consolidated financial statements, but it places the 2023 segment figures on the same IOS/PT/AHS assignment used for 2024. Therefore, the requested 2023-to-2024 revenue calculations should use the recast segment values in the 2024 Form 10-K rather than the earlier 2023 Form 10-K figures, which reflect the older allocation of Invetech.[1][5]

Fortive completed the Invetech divestiture in June 2024, transferring the business other than the Motion Solution Business to Invetech management. Fortive said the divested businesses represented less than 1.0% of 2023 total revenue and less than 1.0% of total assets and did not constitute a strategic shift with a major effect on the company’s operations or financial results. It therefore did not report the divestiture as discontinued operations.[1] The Ralliant materials describe the divested operation as less than 2% of total revenue and approximately 1% of total assets on that carve-out reporting basis; this difference reflects the different denominators and reporting scopes used by Fortive and Ralliant.[8][21][17]

The second material 2024 portfolio change was Fortive’s acquisition of EA Elektro-Automatik Holding GmbH on January 3, 2024. Fortive paid approximately $1.72 billion, net of acquired cash, and assigned approximately $1.18 billion of goodwill to PT. EA supplied high-power electronic test solutions for energy storage, mobility, hydrogen and renewable-energy applications.[1] EA was included in Fortive’s 2024 consolidated financial statements and represented less than 2% of Fortive’s 2024 revenue, although it represented approximately 12% of total assets.[3][10][1]

EA therefore changes the economic composition of 2024 PT relative to 2023. Fortive reported that EA, partially offset by the Invetech divestiture, contributed 4.4 percentage points to PT revenue growth in 2024.[1] In Ralliant’s carve-out presentation, EA contributed $118.9 million of 2024 revenue and a $46.6 million operating loss, including $81.0 million of acquisition-related intangible amortization.[17] Thus, PT’s reported 2024 revenue and margin trends should not be read as an unchanged-base, organic operating trend.

On March 14, 2024, Fortive sold land and certain office buildings in PT for $90 million—$20 million in cash and a $70 million promissory note secured by a letter of credit—and recorded a $63.1 million gain during the three months ended March 29, 2024 and the six months ended June 28, 2024.[29] The property sale generated $20 million of cash proceeds and a $70 million promissory note, with principal received in August and November 2024.[30][29] The 2024 PT operating-profit figure includes this gain.[30][5] Therefore, the reported PT operating margin is the appropriate GAAP historical measure for the requested comparison, but it includes a nonrecurring property-sale gain and is not a pure recurring-margin measure.

The third and decisive perimeter change was the PT separation itself. Fortive’s Q2 2025 reporting presented two distinct analytical views:

1. **Total Fortive or consolidated results:** These include PT in both Q2 2025 and Q2 2024.
2. **Fortive continuing operations or New Fortive:** These treat PT/Ralliant as discontinued operations in both Q2 2025 and Q2 2024, thereby showing IOS and AHS on a post-spin-like basis. [29] [20] [31]

Fortive stated that the Q2 2025 consolidated figures included PT, while results labelled continuing operations excluded PT as discontinued operations for both the current and comparative periods.[12] That dual presentation means neither set of figures is wrong; each answers a different question. Consolidated figures describe the historical Fortive company. Continuing-operations figures describe the remaining IOS-and-AHS company on a comparable basis.

Fortive defined New Fortive or Fortive continuing operations as the Fortive businesses assuming the Ralliant separation had occurred at the beginning of the relevant period. The company stated that future filings would reflect discontinued-operations reporting retrospectively, but that this reporting would not revise or restate the consolidated statements previously filed with the SEC. A retrospectively presented continuing-operations series is therefore the proper basis for comparing post-separation Fortive with prior periods, while the original three-segment historical results remain the proper basis for assessing the pre-separation portfolio.[32][19]

Judgment — The requested Q1 2025 versus Q1 2024 Fortive-wide operating-margin comparison is valid as a comparison of the then-consolidated company because both quarters preceded June 28, 2025 and included IOS, PT and AHS. It is not, by itself, a measure of the margin trend of post-spin Fortive because post-spin Fortive excludes PT.

The same logic applies to Q2 2025. Q2 ended June 27, 2025, before the June 28 distribution. The historical Q2 figures can therefore be used to calculate the operating margins of the three pre-separation segments. But comparisons intended to assess the remaining Fortive business must instead use IOS-and-AHS continuing-operations figures.[2][29][32]

Ralliant’s combined condensed financial statements present the historical financial position, results of operations, changes in Fortive’s equity and cash flows of the Company under U.S. GAAP for carved-out combined condensed financial statements. The Company historically operated as part of Fortive’s Precision Technologies segment rather than as a stand-alone company; its financial statements were derived from Fortive’s historical accounting records and presented on a carve-out basis. They include allocated general, administrative, and sales-and-marketing expenses from Fortive’s corporate office and other Fortive businesses.[16][22][17]

That distinction matters for post-spin interpretation. Ralliant estimated that, as a separate public company, it would incur incremental recurring corporate costs of approximately $12 million for the three months ended March 28, 2025 and $45 million for the year ended December 31, 2024, assuming separation had occurred on January 1, 2024.[21] The company also warned that it could lose purchasing-scale benefits and face higher costs for accounting, tax, legal, human-resources and other corporate functions.[22][21]

Fortive separately identified nonrecurring separation costs in its historical operating results. Its pro forma statements removed approximately $19 million of separation costs from the three months ended March 28, 2025 and approximately $22 million from the year ended December 31, 2024, because the company did not expect those costs to continue after completion.[27] Fortive’s Q2 2025 unallocated corporate costs and other also included $41 million of separation-related costs.[2]

The following comparison framework follows from these facts:

| Analytical question | Appropriate reporting basis | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-to-2024 revenue growth by IOS, PT and AHS | Recast historical three-segment Fortive reporting | The 2024 Form 10-K recast the earlier segment amounts after the Invetech realignment. [1] [8] |
| FY 2024 operating-margin comparison across IOS, PT and AHS | Historical three-segment Fortive reporting | PT was still part of Fortive in 2024. PT’s reported result includes the property-sale gain and 2024 includes EA. [5] [1] |
| Q1 2025 versus Q1 2024 total-Fortive operating margin | Historical consolidated Fortive reporting | Both quarters were pre-separation and used the three-segment perimeter. [3] [4] |
| Q2 2025 margins for IOS, PT and AHS | Final pre-separation three-segment quarterly reporting | The quarter ended June 27, 2025, one day before the legal separation. [2] |
| Performance of post-separation Fortive | Continuing operations / New Fortive basis | This basis excludes PT/Ralliant as discontinued operations from both current and comparative periods. [32] [12] |
| Performance of Ralliant as an independent company | Post-separation Ralliant results, with standalone-cost adjustments considered | Historical PT carve-out results include Fortive allocations and do not equal a fully independent cost structure. [16] [21] |

**Conclusion:** The historical three-segment data answer the requested pre-spin performance questions, while continuing-operations data answer questions about the post-spin IOS-and-AHS company. Mixing those bases would falsely attribute a perimeter change to operating improvement or deterioration.[2][29][32]

For Q2 2025, Fortive reported IOS sales of $675.7 million and operating profit of $166.7 million, PT sales of $523.6 million and operating profit of $93.2 million, and AHS sales of $319.5 million and operating profit of $39.1 million.[12]

Fortive’s Q2 2025 total operating profit was $222.2 million, including an unallocated operating loss of $76.8 million; unallocated corporate costs and other included $41 million of separation-related costs.[2]

Fortive reported 2024 PT operating profit of $500.0 million, which included the $63.1 million property-sale gain.[5]

Fortive defines core revenue growth as year-over-year GAAP revenue growth excluding the effects of acquired or divested businesses and foreign-currency translation. Core revenue growth is therefore distinct from the reported-revenue growth calculations in this analysis, which use reported revenue and include those effects.[33]

Fortive’s May 1, 2025 first-quarter release stated that its full-year 2025 guidance did not give effect to the pending PT separation and that it expected to update guidance for both companies after completion of the spin. Thus, that guidance was not guidance for the post-separation Fortive perimeter.[34]

The New Fortive supplement provides a continuing-operations historical series for 2022, 2023 and 2024, Q1 2025, and each quarter of 2023 and 2024, assuming the separation had been completed and PT was presented as discontinued operations.[35]

Ralliant reported total related-party expenses allocated from Fortive and its non-Ralliant subsidiaries of $28.3 million in each of the three-month periods ended March 28, 2025 and March 29, 2024.[18]

Fortive’s June 2025 pro forma adjustments include general corporate overhead costs historically allocated to Ralliant that will be allocated to Fortive’s remaining segments after the separation.[27]

Ralliant stated that Fortive allocated corporate costs to each segment before the separation. During Q2 2025, Ralliant incurred incremental costs to establish a separate corporate function, primarily employee-compensation and IT-system costs, and incremental standalone-public-company costs including corporate-governance, audit and other professional-services, consulting, legal and stock-exchange-listing fees.[36]

Ralliant’s historical carve-out results also exclude Fortive corporate cash, cash equivalents and debt. Before the separation, Ralliant depended on Fortive’s centralized cash management and financing for working-capital and financing requirements, and its historical financial information did not reflect separation-related debt or associated interest expense.[21] Historically, Ralliant’s working capital, acquisitions and capital expenditures were funded through Fortive-wide cash-management policies; after separation, Ralliant expected more volatile operating results and cash flows and the possible need for additional, potentially more costly, financing.[21]

The segment definition also means that the sum of segment operating profit is not necessarily Fortive’s consolidated operating income: Fortive excluded unallocated costs and other costs from reportable-segment performance evaluation.[3]

Fortive retained the SSO service business, meaning the Shared Service Organization that was part of Tektronix, within IOS even though it had previously been reported within PT. Post-separation IOS is therefore not identical in perimeter to historical IOS, and historical PT is not identical to the business transferred to Ralliant.[23][37]

The Q1 2025 reporting table continued to present IOS, AHS and PT as Fortive segments, and the contemporaneous 2025 guidance excluded the pending PT separation. Neither the Q1 reporting nor that guidance is a forecast or measure for post-separation Fortive without a perimeter adjustment.[11][34]

Fortive incurred $22.6 million of separation-related costs in the three months ended March 28, 2025, recorded within net earnings (loss) from discontinued operations. These costs consisted primarily of professional fees for finance, tax, legal, banking and information-technology services and redundant general and administrative costs.[38]

The separation shifts general corporate overhead historically allocated to Ralliant to Fortive’s remaining segments, while Ralliant expects additional recurring standalone costs for accounting and finance, tax, legal, human resources and other general and administrative functions. It therefore creates cost changes on both sides rather than an automatic margin benefit for either company.[27][18]

Before the separation, Fortive performed corporate functions including legal, treasury, accounting, auditing, human resources, investor relations, corporate affairs and finance for the businesses, and their historical results included allocated corporate expenses. The businesses also shared economies of scope and scale in costs, employees, vendor relationships and customer relationships; used Fortive’s size and scope to obtain procurement terms; and relied on Fortive-wide cash-management policies for working capital and general corporate purposes, including acquisitions and capital expenditures. Together, these historical cost allocations, shared procurement scale and centralized cash management show that the separation changed not only the portfolio perimeter but also the businesses’ operating and financing arrangements.[18][22]

Before the recast, Fortive reported 2023 revenue of $2,612.2 million for IOS, $2,132.8 million for PT, and $1,320.3 million for AHS.[10]

Before the separation, all assets, liabilities, revenues and expenses of Ralliant were included in Fortive’s consolidated results.[1]

### What in Fortive’s portfolio history

The PT transaction was part of an established Fortive pattern of active portfolio reshaping. Fortive became an independent publicly traded company after separating from Danaher on July 2, 2016.[7] Fortive split off most of its Automation and Specialty platform on October 1, 2018 through a Reverse Morris Trust transaction with Altra Industrial Motion Corp., and separated its former Industrial Technologies segment, Vontier, on October 9, 2020.[1] The 2020 separation created Vontier, which Fortive reported as discontinued operations for all periods presented after the accounting requirements for that classification were met.[7][10]

The Ralliant transaction therefore fits a history of using separations to redefine the operating perimeter around management’s preferred strategic categories. The historical pattern alone does not prove that every separation improved operating performance, but it shows that Fortive had previously used spin-offs as a portfolio-management tool rather than treating its segment structure as fixed.[1][10]

Fortive’s stated rationale for the PT separation was strategic focus. The company said the transaction would allow each company to pursue distinct operating priorities and strategies, enable each board and management team to focus on strengthening its core businesses, address company-specific operating needs and pursue respective long-term growth and profitability opportunities.[17] This was not a claim that the businesses lacked quality before the separation.[17] Rather, it was a claim that their different market exposures, operating needs and capital-allocation priorities justified independent management attention.

For the remaining Fortive, management’s stated strategic identity was a technology-solutions company focused on productivity, safety, reliability, recurring revenue and software. Fortive said its post-separation portfolio would have approximately 50% recurring revenue and would prioritize acquisitions that enhance recurring revenue, growth and free cash flow, while balancing acquisitions against share repurchases.[24] Recurring revenue means revenue that repeats through subscriptions, services, maintenance, software contracts or other ongoing customer arrangements rather than being solely dependent on discrete product sales.[25]

This strategy aligns with the retained business mix:

- **IOS positioned Fortive around workflow technology and software-enabled productivity.** IOS combines software, instrumentation, facilities and asset-management applications, safety and compliance solutions, and connected-worker offerings. [6] [7] Fortive’s 2021 ServiceChannel acquisition broadened IOS’s software-enabled facility and asset-lifecycle workflow offering; ServiceChannel was a SaaS-based provider of multi-site facilities-maintenance solutions. [7] [7]

- **AHS positioned Fortive around safety-critical healthcare workflows and clinical productivity.** The business combines physical healthcare technologies such as sterilization and biomedical test tools with workflow, tracking and clinical software. Fortive’s 2021 Provation acquisition extended AHS’s digital offering and healthcare software capability. [8] [7]

- **PT/Ralliant positioned the separated company around precision instruments and engineered products.** Its declared focus was test and measurement, specialty sensors and aerospace, defense and space subsystems, with exposure to semiconductor, power electronics, energy storage, datacenters, utilities, industrial manufacturing and other technically demanding markets. [39] [17] [18]
- **Ralliant’s operating framework:** The Ralliant Business System targets scale, profitability and discipline across selling, general and administrative expenses, research and development, manufacturing, supply chains and working capital. [51]

Fortive reinforced the intended focus through leadership. The IOS-and-AHS leader, Olumide Soroye, became Fortive’s president and chief executive officer upon completion of the distribution, while Tamara Newcombe, the former PT leader, became chief executive officer of Ralliant.[26] This separation of leadership was designed to align executive attention with the two distinct portfolios.[8]

The separation also had a capital-allocation dimension. In the September 2024 announcement, Fortive said it expected to use approximately 75% of available free cash flow between announcement and completion for incremental share repurchases, while the remainder would fund dividends and debt reduction.[24] Fortive later reported that it deployed more than 75% of consolidated free cash flow to share repurchases during that interval.[32] The $1.15 billion Ralliant cash dividend supported subsequent debt repayment and planned Fortive share repurchases.[2][32]

This deployment supports a narrower conclusion about portfolio strategy: Fortive used the separation to simplify the operating portfolio and return or redeploy separation-related capital. It does not show that separation proceeds were primarily directed to operating investment in IOS or AHS. The reported uses were debt repayment and share repurchases.[2]

Fortive’s 2023 and 2024 investment activity also helps explain the strategic divergence between the businesses. In 2023, Fortive completed four IOS acquisitions for aggregate cash consideration of $101.4 million. It said those acquisitions were intended to accelerate strategy and strengthen the IOS product portfolio.[1] In 2024, Fortive made a much larger investment in PT by acquiring EA for approximately $1.72 billion, net of acquired cash, to strengthen high-power electronic test solutions for energy storage, mobility, hydrogen and renewable energy.[8][3][21]

The EA transaction gave PT/Ralliant a more concentrated energy-transition and electronic-test exposure. On January 3, 2024, Ralliant acquired EA Elektro-Automatik Holding GmbH for approximately $1.72 billion, net of acquired cash, and recorded approximately $1.18 billion of goodwill within its Test and Measurement segment. It also affected reported financial trends: EA added revenue but generated a reported operating loss in 2024 because of substantial acquisition-related intangible amortization.[21] That outcome illustrates why the strategic rationale for a portfolio move and the observed near-term segment economics should be kept separate.[17]

Fortive also began a separation-related restructuring program in the fourth quarter of 2024. The plan was expected to be completed by December 31, 2025 and consisted primarily of targeted workforce reductions to realign cost structures between the two companies. Fortive incurred $19.7 million of related restructuring charges in 2024, $58.6 million in 2023, and $3.9 million during the first quarter of 2025.[8] These actions show active preparation for a changed cost structure; they do not themselves prove realized post-spin margin expansion.[3]

The separation created potential benefits and potential costs. Fortive and Ralliant described sharper management focus, strategic flexibility and greater ability to pursue distinct growth opportunities as intended benefits.[24] Conversely, Ralliant disclosed possible dis-synergies from losing Fortive purchasing scale and assuming standalone public-company costs.[21][22] The two directions are economically consistent: a spin-off can sharpen strategic focus while simultaneously creating new corporate overhead and procurement disadvantages.[21]

Management characterized the remaining Fortive as having robust free-cash-flow generation, approximately 50% recurring revenue, significant competitive advantages and exposure to attractive markets with secular tailwinds.[40] Those are strategic-positioning statements.[14] The pre-separation financial evidence supports the narrower observation that IOS and AHS were the businesses Fortive selected to retain in its recurring-revenue, software, safety and workflow-oriented portfolio.[1][24][4]

Management also described differing near-term conditions among the businesses before the spin. In Q1 2025, Fortive said IOS and AHS delivered core growth supported by demand for safety and productivity solutions. PT faced delayed test-and-measurement investments because of geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainty, although demand remained strong for utility monitoring and defense-and-space solutions.[4] This divergence supports the view that the separation created more focused portfolios with different market cycles and investment narratives.

The end-market data further illustrate that distinction. In 2024 direct sales, IOS reported $947.1 million in Industrial & Manufacturing; PT reported $330.7 million in Aerospace & Defense, $255.7 million in Communication, Electronics & Semiconductor, and $241.4 million in Government; and AHS reported $1,217.2 million in Healthcare.[1] These were not identical customer environments, and the transaction separated the diversified engineering and precision-instrument exposures from Fortive’s workflow, safety, healthcare and software-related emphasis.[8]

Judgment — Portfolio prioritization is demonstrated most clearly by what management retains, separates and funds. On that basis, Fortive prioritized a two-segment IOS-and-AHS portfolio with a stated preference for recurring revenue, workflow software, safety, productivity, free cash flow and targeted acquisitions. PT was not characterized as a low-quality disposal; it was repositioned as a separately managed precision-technology company with a distinct capital structure, end-market exposure and growth agenda.

The available evidence does not yet support treating the separation itself as a demonstrated competitive strengthening of IOS or AHS. The transaction completed only on June 28, 2025, and the immediate post-separation reporting series was designed to reclassify PT as discontinued operations rather than to measure a long operating history of independently managed IOS and AHS. The supportable conclusion is that Fortive strengthened strategic focus and reporting clarity by separating unlike portfolio profiles; whether it strengthened competitive position requires sustained evidence of superior growth, margins, investment returns, customer retention or market-share outcomes after the separation.[2][32]

**Conclusion:** Fortive’s portfolio actions show a deliberate shift toward a retained IOS-and-AHS platform centered on recurring revenue, workflow software, safety and healthcare productivity, while allowing PT/Ralliant to pursue a separate precision-technology strategy. The transaction clearly changed strategic focus and capital-allocation flexibility; it does not yet establish a realized competitive advantage for the remaining businesses.[24][22][21]

At December 31, 2024, Fortive reported remaining performance obligations of $610.4 million for IOS, $57.8 million for PT and $93.9 million for AHS, totaling $762.1 million. Fortive defined remaining performance obligations as the transaction price of firm, non-cancelable orders and the average contract value for software contracts for which work had not been performed, excluding obligations with an original expected duration of one year or less.[8][41]

Fortive reported that PT operating-profit margin decreased 210 basis points in 2024 versus 2023.[1]

Fortive management stated that it believed Fortive held a market-leadership position in most served markets, while also reporting increased competition from entrants based in low-cost manufacturing locations and increasing consolidation in particular markets.[8]

Fortive’s CEO stated in its Q3 2024 release that IOS and AHS had robust recurring-revenue growth and that orders growth had positive momentum across all segments, including double-digit orders growth in PT in the third quarter.[42]

Fortive reported 2023 gross capital expenditures of $55.7 million for IOS, $28.3 million for PT and $18.5 million for AHS.[10]

Fortive’s 2024 Form 10-K describes the Fortive Business System (FBS) as a culture of continuous improvement reinforced by a disciplined operating cadence and tools and best practices consistently applied across its portfolio. Fortive stated that it is continually evolving FBS and incorporating artificial intelligence and machine learning to drive faster growth, more productivity and greater impact.[8]

Fortive’s June 30, 2025 completion release estimated that Q2 2025 revenue and core revenue for New Fortive would be flat to slightly down, while PT, then Ralliant, would decline mid-single digits.[14]

Fortive stated that the separation would give each company its own optimal capital structure and more agile, focused capital-allocation and capital-return strategies. It stated that each company could direct financial resources solely to its own operations and invest according to its distinct objectives, strategy and business needs, facilitating capital allocation based on profitability, cash flow and growth opportunities.[18]

Ralliant stated that its working-capital requirements and general corporate capital, including acquisitions and capital expenditures, had historically been satisfied through Fortive-wide cash-management policies. It stated that post-separation results of operations and cash flows were likely to be more volatile and that it might need additional financing from banks, public offerings, private debt or equity placements, strategic relationships or other arrangements.[18]

Ralliant stated that, after the separation, the cost of capital for its businesses may be higher than Fortive’s cost of capital before the separation.[18]

Ralliant stated that anticipated separation benefits might not be achieved because the separation requires significant management time and effort that may divert attention from operating and growing the businesses, and because the standalone businesses may be more susceptible to market fluctuations and adverse events as less diversified businesses than Fortive before the separation.[18]

Fortive’s stated plan separated distinct strategic portfolios even though it reported robust recurring-revenue growth in IOS and AHS and double-digit PT orders growth in the third quarter of 2024. This supports a focus-and-specialization rationale rather than a simple exit from a weak segment.[42][1]

At its 2025 Investor Day, Fortive said a potential acquisition should have an “FBS-enabled value creation plan” “ready to execute on Day 1,” be supportive of durability and growth with attractive margins or margin potential, and offer attractive returns with a clear path to ROIC greater than WACC.[23]

Fortive said it continually assesses whether existing businesses fit its strategic plan and achieve the desired return on investment, and may dispose of businesses that do not fit or do not achieve that return.[7]

Fortive said the PT separation would create two independent public companies with focused business models, tailored investment strategies and tailored capital-allocation strategies, each with a distinct investment profile.[24]

### Synthesis

Fortive’s requested historical analysis rests on a three-segment perimeter—IOS, PT and AHS—but the June 28, 2025 Ralliant spin-off changed the company into a two-segment continuing business of IOS and AHS. That is a demonstrated change in focus and reporting perimeter, not yet a demonstrated post-spin improvement in competitive position.[1][24][32]

## Portfolio strategy and competitive positioning

### What do the segment performance metrics and Fortive’s overall operating

Fortive’s portfolio strategy is best understood as a shift from managing three distinct operating segments inside one company to concentrating the continuing company on two businesses with a closer strategic fit: Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS). In corporate finance terms, this is a **portfolio prioritization** decision: management is choosing which businesses should share capital, leadership attention, operating capabilities, and an equity-market identity.

The pre-separation financial pattern supports that direction. In 2024, IOS had the highest operating margin, 26.0%, and reported revenue growth of 3.9%; AHS had the fastest reported revenue growth, 4.7%, and the largest margin expansion, 3.8 percentage points to 12.1%; and PT had 0.3% reported revenue growth and a 2.1-percentage-point margin decline to 22.4%. This pattern identifies IOS as the margin anchor, AHS as the faster-growing lower-margin improvement opportunity, and PT as the segment with the weakest reported sales momentum and margin direction.[1][28]

PT’s 22.4% reported margin was still relatively high in absolute terms, exceeding AHS’s 12.1%. But the direction differed materially: revenue rose only 0.3% and margin fell 2.1 points from 2023. The January 2024 EA Elektro-Automatik acquisition contributed 4.4 percentage points to PT revenue growth, partly offset by the Invetech divestiture, further indicating that reported revenue was not a clean measure of existing-business momentum.[1]

The PT margin decline also reflected more than one factor. Fortive identified lower net volume and higher employee compensation, acquisition and divestiture transaction costs largely associated with EA, acquired-business amortization and fair-value effects, restructuring, and the favorable property-sale gain.[8] On January 3, 2024, Fortive acquired EA Elektro-Automatik Holding GmbH (EA), a leading supplier of high-power electronic test solutions for energy storage, mobility, hydrogen, and renewable energy applications, for approximately $1.72 billion net of acquired cash.

Fortive said, “The acquisition of EA will bolster the PT segment’s innovative portfolio of products and services for engineers with complementary test and measurement solutions enabling the global energy transition.” Fortive recorded roughly $1.18 billion of PT goodwill.[1] The investment broadened PT’s technology exposure, yet acquisition-related costs and amortization weighed on its reported 2024 profitability. Thus, PT’s weaker margin trajectory cannot be reduced to customer demand alone.

The revenue and margin evidence is consistent with Fortive increasingly favoring businesses with recurring workflow software, safety applications, service revenue, and productivity-oriented customer value. IOS’s 2024 sales growth came primarily from pricing, gas detection volume, and facility-and-asset-lifecycle software and services, including software as a service (SaaS).[1] AHS’s portfolio includes sterilization, instrument tracking, biomedical testing, radiation safety, and clinical productivity software—products and services embedded in healthcare-provider workflows.[8] Those characteristics align with Fortive’s declared post-separation objective of resilient, high-quality recurring growth in productivity and safety solutions.[22][21]

That interpretation is reinforced by how Fortive says it allocates resources. Its chief operating decision maker uses segment gross profit and operating profit to assess performance and allocate resources, including merger-and-acquisition targets; segment operating profit is gross profit less operating expenses, while unallocated corporate costs are excluded from segment evaluation.[2] In other words, the margin and profit measures reviewed here are not merely investor presentation measures: Fortive says it uses them in resource allocation.

That does not mean the highest-margin segment automatically receives the most capital, but it means IOS’s margin leadership and AHS’s improvement were relevant to management’s portfolio decisions.

The company’s more specific capital-allocation framework supports a selective, return-based rather than segment-size-based strategy. At its 2025 Investor Day, Fortive listed organic growth first, followed by mergers and acquisitions, share repurchases, and dividends; it said capital should be allocated according to the highest relative risk-adjusted expected return.[23] Its Q2 2025 presentation similarly described a disciplined bolt-on acquisition approach, investment-grade credit discipline, a target gross-debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA ratio of 2.5 times or less, and capital allocation based on relative risk-adjusted equity returns.[32] The resulting priority is not simply “more capital to IOS”; it is capital to the organic initiatives and bolt-on acquisitions that best reinforce the retained portfolio’s recurring revenue, growth, free-cash-flow, and margin characteristics.

Fortive’s actions around the spin also show a shareholder-return component. In May 2025, the board authorized a special-purpose share-repurchase program of up to $550 million, funded only by Ralliant-related cash proceeds.[2] Fortive later reported that more than 75% of consolidated free cash flow from the September 2024 spin announcement through completion had been deployed to share repurchases.

It received a $1.15 billion Ralliant dividend, used approximately $725 million of those proceeds to repay debt in July 2025, and planned to apply the remainder to share repurchases.[32] The separation therefore had two related capital-allocation purposes: sharpened investment focus for the operating businesses and financial restructuring that supported debt reduction and returns of capital to Fortive shareholders.

The quarterly demand data around the spin further support the strategic separation logic. That pattern does not establish that PT was strategically inferior. PT had meaningful positions in precision measurement, sensing, aerospace and defense, communications, electronics, semiconductor, energy, automotive, and industrial markets.[8] It also showed order momentum in late 2024: Fortive reported PT’s second consecutive quarter of double-digit order growth and expected a gradual recovery through 2025.[33] But the segment’s more cyclical test-and-measurement exposure, weak 2024 existing-business volumes, and declining margin made it less aligned in the near term with Fortive’s preferred retained-company narrative of stable recurring growth, workflow software, productivity, and safety.

Fortive formally announced the intended separation on September 4, 2024. It completed the 100% spin-off of PT as Ralliant Corporation on June 28, 2025.[1][14] The strategic logic stated by Fortive and Ralliant was not that PT lacked quality; it was that the two portfolios had different profit drivers, end markets, manufacturing needs, research-and-development requirements, and capital-allocation opportunities.

The separation was intended to enable each company to pursue its own operating priorities, focus management on its core businesses, direct resources to its own needs, and pursue more strategy-aligned acquisitions.[22]

This establishes a focused-portfolio strategy rather than a simple disposal of a weak business. Ralliant inherited PT’s precision-instruments and engineered-products activities, subsequently reporting Test and Measurement and Sensors and Safety Systems as its own segments.[16] Fortive retained IOS and AHS, which management grouped as a technology-solutions company oriented toward productivity, safety, recurring growth, and software.[14][32] The separation created two more specialized investment identities: a precision-technology company exposed to electrification, digitization, semiconductors, grid modernization, defense, and space; and a Fortive centered on industrial and healthcare workflows.[17]

The strategic conclusion is that Fortive prioritized a more recurring, software-enabled productivity-and-safety portfolio while allowing PT to pursue a separate precision-technology strategy; the financial record supports that direction but does not show that PT was separated solely because of weaker financial performance. The decision is consistent with IOS’s high margins, AHS’s improvement and growth, and PT’s softer 2024 momentum, but management also emphasized distinct business models and tailored capital allocation as the core rationale.[28][22][21]

### What is the strategic positioning of the business units remaining

Following the June 28, 2025 distribution of Ralliant, Fortive’s continuing strategic segments are IOS and AHS. The Q2 operating environment was mixed for both retained businesses. In Q2 2025 continuing operations, Fortive said Intelligent Operating Solutions’ revenue and core growth were roughly flat, with tariff-related uncertainty impacting professional instrumentation and constrained spending impacting government-procurement businesses; solid growth in IOS software and gas-detection businesses partly offset those pressures.[32] AHS faced healthcare-policy and budget uncertainty and lower equipment placements, partly offset by healthcare software growth.[32] In AHS, Fortive attributed the year-over-year operating-margin movement to favorable pricing and productivity gains offset by net volume decline, higher compensation, growth investment, and existing-business amortization.[2]

On the original three-segment basis, AHS Q2 2025 revenue fell 1.3% year over year: pricing, software volume and dosimetry-service demand were outweighed by lower sterilization-equipment and biomedical-test-tool sales.[52]

IOS Q2 2025 operating profit fell 1.2% year over year on the continuing-operations presentation.[57][20][58]

The continuing portfolio nevertheless has a coherent strategic theme. Fortive described New Fortive as a simplified company focused on safety and productivity, with industry-leading operating brands in attractive markets, high recurring revenue, an attractive margin profile, and robust free-cash-flow generation. For the trailing 12 months through Q2 2025, management presented IOS as approximately 70% of New Fortive revenue, or $2.8 billion, and AHS as approximately 30%, or $1.3 billion; total New Fortive revenue was $4.1 billion.[32] IOS is consequently the economic anchor of the continuing company, while AHS provides healthcare diversification and a lower-margin improvement opportunity.

Management also describes the retained portfolio as having approximately 50% recurring revenue. It links IOS and AHS to long-term trends including software-enabled workflows, connected devices and internet-of-things offerings, productivity and safety requirements, aging industrial infrastructure, supply-chain reordering, and demand for safer and more efficient healthcare operations.[22][21] Recurring revenue means sales expected to repeat through subscriptions, software, service, support, consumables, or recurring customer relationships. It can make revenue more predictable, although the reported percentage is a portfolio-wide management characterization rather than a segment-level GAAP measure.

Several operating examples are consistent with that positioning. Fortive reported double-digit annual recurring revenue growth in Fluke’s recurring-revenue activities and high-single-digit core growth in distributed energy and data-center applications.[32] It also reported strong IOS software and gas-detection growth in Q2 2025.[32] In AHS, management said software businesses outperformed through resilient SaaS-based revenue models even as hospitals deferred some equipment purchases, including sterilization equipment and quality-assurance devices, amid reimbursement-policy uncertainty.[47] These observations show how software and service components can cushion, but not eliminate, cyclical or policy-driven weakness in equipment spending.

Fortive’s strategic language after the separation emphasizes profitable growth and capital discipline. It says the Fortive Accelerated strategy is intended to drive faster profitable growth and shareholder value creation, with the Fortive Business System as the operating engine.[32] The company’s stated organic-growth levers are innovation acceleration, commercial acceleration, and recurring customer value.[47] Its stated capital-allocation priorities are organic growth, accretive bolt-on acquisitions, share repurchases, and a growing dividend, subject to relative-return discipline.[23][44]

The priority for acquisitions is therefore not merely “software at any price.” Management said prospective acquisitions—whether software, consumables, recurring-revenue assets, or durable hardware—must meet common strategic and financial criteria.[47] This is strategically important because the continuing Fortive portfolio still includes hardware and equipment businesses, particularly in professional instrumentation and healthcare. The portfolio strategy is to improve the quality, recurrence, growth, and returns of that mix rather than abandon hardware altogether.

Fortive also began addressing the costs left behind after the spin. Management said approximately half of stranded costs had been eliminated by the Q2 2025 call and expected to address the remainder over roughly the next 12 months, while also seeking further efficiency through cost discipline.[47][44] Stranded costs are overhead expenses that remain with a parent company after a business leaves, such as corporate functions or shared-services costs that cannot immediately be removed.

Removing them is necessary for the post-spin margin profile to reflect the economics of IOS and AHS rather than a temporary burden from the former three-segment structure.

The continuing Fortive is strategically positioned as an IOS-led safety, productivity, software, and recurring-revenue platform, with AHS adding healthcare-workflow exposure and margin-improvement potential. This makes the portfolio more concentrated and more internally coherent, but the current margin gap means Fortive’s near-term earnings quality still depends disproportionately on IOS.[32][20][22]

Fortive characterized IOS and AHS as durable, growth-oriented businesses with approximately 50% recurring revenue. It linked their long-term growth to global-supply-chain reordering, aging industrial infrastructure, rising productivity requirements, safety and security requirements, and increased global demand for safer, high-quality, and more efficient healthcare operations.[17]

Management said that hospital customers had delayed some healthcare capital-equipment purchases, but that the funnel remained strong, no deals in the funnel had been lost, and the effect was a shift in timing.[44]

Management described Fluke’s industrial professional-instrumentation business as having brand strength, high gross margins, and resilience. In Q1 2026, IOS generated $743.2 million in sales, up from $690.9 million a year earlier, and $186.2 million of operating profit, for a 25.1% operating margin. Higher volumes, pricing, and Fortive Business System productivity initiatives supported IOS’s performance.[38]

In Q1 2026, AHS generated $326.2 million in sales, compared with $302.2 million a year earlier, and $32.7 million of operating profit, for a 10.0% operating margin. Growth in sterilization products and dosimetry services supported AHS’s performance.[38]

Management reported strong software renewal rates and good net dollar retention (NDR) across its software businesses in Q2 2025. Its stated focus was expansion, cross-selling, upselling, and pricing to raise NDR further.[44]

The retained segments also showed contrasting adjusted gross-margin movement in Q2 2025. IOS adjusted gross margin declined to 66.1% from just under 70% a year earlier, primarily from tariff-cost pressures, partly offset by pricing countermeasures and growth in higher-margin software. AHS adjusted gross margin rose from just under 58% to just over 59%, as favorable pricing and a mix shift toward higher-margin AHS software revenue away from lower-margin hardware contributed.[44]

These gross-margin measures reinforce the different operating priorities of the two retained units: IOS is protecting a high-margin franchise against tariff costs through pricing and higher-margin software growth, while AHS is improving mix through software revenue and pricing.[44]

Fortive identifies the competitive factors for its businesses as price, quality, performance, delivery speed, applications expertise, distribution-channel access, service and support, technology and innovation, breadth of product, service and software offerings, and brand-name recognition.[10][48] Fortive also states that effective competition requires retaining major-customer relationships, establishing relationships with new customers, continually developing new or enhanced products and services, maintaining and expanding leadership positions, and entering new markets, including high-growth markets.[10]

Management’s Q2 2025 operating commentary identifies specific retained-portfolio momentum. Industrial Scientific and IOS software businesses grew year to date; Gordian’s new cloud-based assessment and capital-planning module generated double-digit order growth; and Fluke’s recurring-revenue initiative produced double-digit annual recurring revenue (ARR) growth in Q2.[47] Fortive attributed Q2 Fluke weakness to customers delaying several large short-cycle orders while assessing tariffs, said orders were returning as customers used existing inventory, and described Fluke’s outlook as strong; its second-half guidance assumed Fluke and IOS core-growth performance equal to the first half.[47]

### Has the Precision Technologies separation strengthened the remaining business units’

The direct answer is no: the June 28, 2025 PT separation has not yet been demonstrated to have strengthened the competitive positioning of either IOS or AHS. Fortive presents both retained segments as containing iconic inventor brands with leading positions in their markets, but its own filings caution that competitive position cannot be determined accurately in aggregate or by segment because competitors do not offer the same products, services, or combinations of end markets.[29][1]

That conclusion does not negate the strategic rationale. Fortive’s board stated that the separation would let each company pursue distinct operating priorities and strategies with greater flexibility; focus boards and management teams on their respective core businesses; create company-specific capital structures; and allocate capital according to each company’s profitability, cash flow, growth opportunities, and operating needs.[22] The board also expected separate equity structures, direct capital-market access, and more flexibility for strategy-aligned selective acquisitions.[22] These are plausible mechanisms through which a separation can improve competitive outcomes over time.

But a mechanism is not the same thing as a measured result. The Q1 2025 results were wholly pre-separation, and Q2 2025 ended June 27, one day before the June 28 distribution.[34][16] Those periods can show the pre-spin condition of IOS, AHS, and PT, but they cannot isolate the effect of operating as separate companies.

The first immediate post-separation reporting period also included separation costs, stranded-cost actions, transition arrangements, tariff uncertainty, healthcare-policy uncertainty, and customer spending deferrals.[2][14][47]

There is positive evidence about the retained units’ underlying strategic quality, but it is not evidence of a spin-caused competitive gain:

On the recast Q1 2025 continuing-operations presentation, former PT had $481.1 million of sales.[59]

- **IOS:** Fortive reported Q1 2025 core growth of 2.0%, supported by pricing and higher volume in test-and-measurement instrumentation and gas detection, partly offset by weaker facility-and-asset-lifecycle product lines and foreign exchange. [3] IOS also generated the higher Q2 2025 reported GAAP operating margin of the continuing segments, at 24.5%. [12]

- **AHS:** Fortive reported Q1 2025 core growth of 2.5%. [11] Its software businesses performed well in Q2 2025, benefiting from SaaS-based revenue models, although hospitals deferred some equipment purchases because of reimbursement-policy uncertainty. [47] AHS’s Q2 2025 GAAP operating margin rose modestly to 11.2% from 11.0%. [20]

- **Portfolio attributes:** Management characterized New Fortive as having approximately 50% recurring revenue, robust free-cash-flow generation, significant competitive advantages, and attractive secular-growth exposure. [14] These are management’s positioning claims and describe the intended post-spin company rather than a before-and-after market-position measurement.

The distinction between operating performance and competitive positioning is important. A business can improve operating margin through pricing, productivity, lower overhead, or favorable product mix without gaining market share or strengthening customer loyalty. Conversely, a business can make investments that temporarily reduce margin while strengthening product capability, sales coverage, or customer retention. To demonstrate competitive strengthening, investors would normally look for sustained gains in market share, win rates, customer retention, pricing realization relative to competitors, recurring revenue growth, new-product adoption, or returns on incremental invested capital after the separation.Judgment — The available financial results identify stronger and weaker profitability patterns, but do not yet provide a post-separation before-and-after test on those competitive measures.

Fortive’s own disclosure recognizes why immediate conclusions would be premature. The separation required a restructuring plan initiated in Q4 2024, primarily involving targeted workforce reductions to realign cost structures; Fortive incurred $19.7 million of related charges in 2024.[1] In Q2 2025, unallocated corporate costs and other expenses included $41 million of separation-related costs.[2] The company said separation-related costs, higher employee compensation, growth investments, and restructuring charges increased selling, general, and administrative expense in the second quarter and first half of 2025, partly offset by productivity benefits.[2]

Transition arrangements also mean the separation was not an instant operational clean break. Fortive and Ralliant planned a transition-services agreement for temporary provision of information-technology, financial, administrative, and other services.[18] The planned intellectual-property agreement provided reciprocal rights for each company to use specified intellectual property necessary for its retained business.[21] Such arrangements preserve continuity, but they also mean early standalone results can include temporary service dependencies and allocation effects.

The separated PT business itself illustrates the difficulty of treating a spin as an automatic competitive upgrade. Ralliant’s Q2 2025 total operating margin was 11.7%, down from 19.7% in Q2 2024.[16] Ralliant attributed part of its Q2 margin decline to lower volume, higher salaries and wages, allocated corporate costs, standalone public-company costs, and tariffs, partly offset by pricing.[16] It also identified potential post-spin disadvantages from loss of Fortive purchasing scale and higher costs for accounting, tax, legal, human resources, and other standalone functions.[22][21] These facts concern Ralliant rather than the continuing Fortive segments, but they show why the strategic rationale for separation must be evaluated against both focus benefits and dis-synergy costs.

For Fortive, the central post-spin opportunity is concentration rather than an already observed competitive leap. The retained company no longer has to allocate management attention and investment capital across PT’s different cyclicality, technology markets, capital requirements, and acquisition opportunities. Management can focus on IOS and AHS, deploy the Fortive Business System, remove stranded costs, and pursue bolt-ons adjacent to existing strengths.[21][44] That is a meaningful strategic simplification.

However, the remaining units still face their own external pressures. IOS faced tariff-related uncertainty and constrained government procurement spending in Q2 2025.[32] AHS faced healthcare-policy and budget uncertainty and lower equipment placements.[32] Fortive’s CEO also cited pressure on tariff-related pricing and customer demand arising from uncertainty in trade, healthcare, and government-spending policy.[14] The separation therefore did not remove the operating risks that affect the businesses’ customer demand and margins.

Fortive’s post-spin capital policy also creates a trade-off. The company says it will invest in organic growth and selective bolt-on M&A, but it has also committed substantial capital to debt repayment and share repurchases.[32][23]Judgment — For shareholders, the key issue is whether repurchases, debt reduction, and organic reinvestment remain balanced so that Fortive does not improve short-term per-share metrics at the expense of software, innovation, sales capacity, and healthcare-product investment needed to sustain the competitive position of IOS and AHS.

The available record therefore supports a narrower but useful conclusion. IOS and AHS entered the separation with favorable attributes: IOS had the strongest margin profile, both segments reported positive Q1 2025 core growth, IOS had recurring-revenue and software momentum, and AHS software offered resilience against softer equipment demand.[20][15][32][47] The separation gives management a cleaner mandate and more direct capital-allocation authority over those businesses.[22] But those facts establish portfolio focus and potential, not a realized separation-driven competitive gain.

**Fortive’s competitive-positioning claim should therefore be treated as a strategic thesis: the spin has created a more focused IOS-and-AHS portfolio with clearer capital priorities, but strengthening of competitive position will be demonstrated only if the retained businesses sustain superior growth, margins, recurring revenue, innovation, and customer outcomes after transition costs and external demand pressures normalize.**[14][21][47][1]

Fortive identifies price, quality, performance, delivery speed, applications expertise, distribution-channel access, service and support, technology and innovation, breadth of product, service, and software offerings, and brand-name recognition as competitive factors across its businesses.[8][10][48]

Ralliant’s Q2 2025 operating profit margin was 11.7%, a decrease of 800 basis points from 19.7% in Q2 2024.[16] Ralliant attributed an 810-basis-point unfavorable change in existing-business results to a 275-basis-point volume reduction, 250 basis points from higher salaries and wages, 150 basis points from higher corporate costs allocated to its segments, 120 basis points from standalone public-company costs, and 110 basis points from the net tariff effect, partly offset by a 100-basis-point benefit from price increases.[16]

The separation also carried explicit execution and portfolio risks. Ralliant stated that the separation could require significant management time and effort that diverts attention from operating and growing the businesses, and that, as a less diversified standalone company, it could be more susceptible to market fluctuations and adverse events than it was as part of Fortive.[22]

Historical PT financial presentations require the same perimeter discipline as Fortive’s continuing-operations results. Ralliant’s unaudited combined condensed carved-out financial statements represent the historical operations of Fortive’s PT operating segment and were derived from Fortive’s historical accounting records. They include revenues, costs, assets, and liabilities directly associated with Ralliant, plus allocations of general, administrative, sales and marketing expenses and cost of sales from Fortive’s corporate office and other Fortive businesses, as well as related assets, liabilities, and Parent investment where applicable.[17]

The competitive test for IOS and AHS remains demanding. Fortive says that its businesses face intense competition and a wide variety of competitors, including larger companies or divisions with substantial sales, marketing, research, and financial capabilities; well-established specialized regional competitors; low-cost-manufacturing entrants; and consolidation in particular markets.[10]

Subsequent reported results show continued operating progress in the retained portfolio. In Q3 2025, IOS reported 2.6% GAAP total revenue growth and 2.2% core revenue growth, while AHS reported 1.9% GAAP total revenue growth and 1.1% core revenue growth; core revenue growth excludes acquired or divested businesses and foreign-currency translation.[49] IOS’s GAAP operating margin was 25.9% in Q3 2025 versus 24.8% in Q3 2024, and 25.2% for the first nine months of 2025 versus 24.6% for the first nine months of 2024.[49] AHS’s GAAP operating margin was 12.9% in Q3 2025 versus 11.4% in Q3 2024, and 10.5% for the first nine months of 2025 versus 10.1% for the first nine months of 2024.[49]

The separated PT portfolio continued to exhibit sharply different demand conditions in Q2 2025. Ralliant’s total sales declined 5.7% year over year and organic revenue declined 5.8%; Test and Measurement revenue declined 14.9% on a total basis and 16.8% organically, while Sensors and Safety Systems revenue grew 1.1% on a total basis and 2.2% organically.[16]

Fortive said its broad-based business activities helped reduce the impact that volatility in any particular area or related areas could have on overall operating profit. The separation disclosures identified the corresponding concentration cost: after separation, the businesses could be more susceptible to market fluctuations and other events because they were less diversified than Fortive had been before the separation.[8][18]

### Synthesis — portfolio implication and investor action

The historical results support Fortive’s decision to retain IOS and AHS as the core portfolio: IOS supplied the strongest margin level, AHS supplied the fastest reported growth and largest margin improvement, and PT had near-flat reported sales and margin contraction.[28] The separation thus aligns with a strategy centered on recurring revenue, software-enabled workflows, productivity, safety, and disciplined capital allocation.[22][23]

The post-spin Fortive is more focused, but it is also more dependent on IOS’s high margins while AHS remains a lower-margin improvement opportunity. Q1 and Q2 2025 data show favorable continuing-business attributes alongside tariffs, healthcare uncertainty, transition costs, and stranded-cost work.[20][32][47] The separation’s strategic logic is strong; a separation-created competitive advantage is not yet a measured outcome.

1. **Treat IOS as the retained portfolio’s earnings anchor** and monitor whether its high margin remains resilient amid tariff and procurement pressure.
2. **Treat AHS as the principal improvement opportunity** and monitor software growth, equipment-placement recovery, and sustained margin expansion.
3. **Evaluate the spin on post-separation operating evidence**, especially recurring revenue, organic growth, market-share indicators, innovation returns, and stranded-cost removal—not on management positioning alone.

### Open questions

Whether the separation has strengthened IOS and AHS competitively remains open. Comparable post-separation operating results, customer-retention and market-share evidence, and evidence on standalone costs and capital allocation would establish whether the focused two-segment Fortive has translated strategic focus into a durable competitive advantage.
