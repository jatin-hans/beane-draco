# Comparison of Three Heat Pump Dryers for a Melbourne Terrace

## Executive summary

For a heritage-listed Melbourne terrace with limited ventilation, choose the Bosch WTX88M20AU if its 8 kg capacity is sufficient, and do not treat the Samsung DV90T6240LH as a like-for-like Australian purchase until its Australian model, warranty, label, service and rebate position are confirmed.

The Bosch is the lower-risk fit because it is an Australian-market ventless heat-pump dryer, is 598 mm wide and 613 mm deep at the body, has a declared 62 dB(A) noise rating, and can collect condensate in its tank or use an internal drain connection.[1][2] The LG DVH9-09W is the better capacity option at 9 kg, but its 690 mm depth and required cupboard-door ventilation openings of at least 387 cm² at both top and bottom make it less suitable where the laundry is shallow or enclosure changes would affect heritage fabric.[3][4] Neither a declared dryer noise figure nor a heat-pump design proves compliance at a particular shared wall, and no fabric-specific, model-specific AS/NZS 2442.1 result supports a ranked wool, cotton and synthetics drying-performance claim among all three models.

The ten-year ownership comparison should therefore favour the Bosch only after applying the stated purchase, electricity, maintenance and rebate assumptions, while excluding any unconfirmed Samsung price or Victorian Energy Upgrades benefit from a purportedly precise total.

## Australian products and household fit

A heat-pump dryer is a condenser dryer that recovers and recirculates heat rather than exhausting damp air outdoors. That makes the technology well suited to a terrace where cutting a new external vent through historic fabric would be undesirable. It does not remove the need to measure the laundry, provide electrical and condensate access, control vibration, and preserve room airflow.

The three names in this comparison do not have equal Australian product status. Bosch WTX88M20AU and LG DVH9-09W have Australian-market product records and Australian-facing manuals or support material. Samsung DV90T6240LH is identifiable as a 9 kg heat-pump dryer, but the available product record is for UK and other overseas suffix variants rather than an Australian product listing. That distinction matters throughout the later sections on energy labels, noise, reliability, rebates, servicing and total cost of ownership.[1][3][5][6][7]

### What Australian product information is available for the Bosch Serie

Bosch identifies the WTX88M20AU as a free-standing Serie 8 heat-pump tumble dryer with an 8.0 kg capacity and an A+++ energy-efficiency classification. Capacity means the dry weight of laundry the drum can accept, not the wet weight removed from a washing machine.[1][8] Australian label information also gives it 10 Energy Stars and 98 kWh/year on the prescribed label basis; the separate section, *Victorian electricity use and costs*, applies that information to six to eight weekly household loads.[1][9]

Bosch’s Australian specification states that the 98 kWh/year label figure is based on Cottons, Cupboard Dry +, drying level 1, run once each week and tested under AS/NZS 2442.2. AS/NZS 2442.2 is the Australian and New Zealand test standard used for energy-label testing of household tumble dryers. It is a standardised comparison result, not a prediction that a household doing six to eight loads each week will consume only 98 kWh in a year.[1]

The Bosch user manual provides useful cycle-specific context. For an 8.0 kg Cottons programme, tested under IEC 61121, listed consumption ranges from 0.88 kWh at 1,400 rpm and 50% initial moisture to 1.66 kWh at 800 rpm and 70% initial moisture; listed programme times range from 2 hours 12 minutes to 3 hours 32 minutes. The 3.5 kg Easy Care programme is listed at 0.44–0.53 kWh and 1 hour 17 minutes to 1 hour 27 minutes under the stated conditions. Bosch says actual values vary with fabric, load composition, residual moisture, selected dryness, load size, environmental conditions and added functions.[2]

The Australian product information identifies the Bosch as a substantial free-standing appliance rather than a wall-mounted dryer. Its appliance dimensions are 84.2 cm high, 59.8 cm wide and 61.3 cm deep; the manual separately gives 66.5 cm depth with the door closed and 110.5 cm with the door open. It weighs 55.5 kg and has a maximum rated load of 8.0 kg.[2] Bosch’s Australian service record specifically identifies this supported product as WTX88M20AU/01.[10]

The Bosch specification sheet reports 842 × 598 × 613 mm in height × width × depth order and a 145 cm electrical-supply cord, while the Australian retailer listing gives 598 mm width, 665 mm depth and 842 mm height. These are compatible descriptions if the retailer’s 665 mm depth includes the closed door or handle, as the Bosch manual does.[1] A narrow 60 cm bay is therefore not by itself enough: the household needs room for the closed-door projection, the 110.5 cm open-door envelope, rear connections and service access.[1][11][2]

Bosch specifies a 220–240 V, 50 Hz supply, a nominal power of 600 W, a minimum installation protection rating of 10 A, and a 145 cm electrical cord. The retailer identifies the connection as a 10 A plug and lead with a 1.45 m cord.[11] The appliance should stand on a clean, level, firm surface, and the manual says not to trap the power cord or hoses between the appliance and wall.[2][11]

The WTX88M20AU can operate with a condensate container: Bosch states that if no drain hose is connected, condensation water from the drying cycle is stored in the condensation container. Bosch includes a water-drain hose with connection adapter, fixing material and elbow, and says condensate formed during drying is used for automatic cleaning. This gives the household a practical choice between emptying the container and arranging an internal drain connection.[1][2]

Bosch describes the unit as free-standing and says it has no removable top. It is stackable, but Bosch’s manual limits a washer-dryer stack to a washing machine from the same manufacturer and requires an original connection set from the dryer manufacturer. The manual also says not to place a Bosch washer-dryer stack on a platform.[1][11][2]

The Australian product sheet names three associated accessories: WMZ20600, a wool-in-basket accessory; WTZ27400, a stacking kit with pull-out shelf; and WTZ27410, a stacking kit. The retailer also lists those three items as optional accessories. A wool basket is relevant to garment handling, while the later section, *AS/NZS 2442.1 drying performance*, addresses the separate question of demonstrated drying results.[1][11]

Bosch lists fabric-specific programmes. The retailer names Cotton, Synthetic and Wool programmes among its special programmes, alongside Mixed Load, Super Quick 40 minutes, Hygiene/Allergy Plus, shirts/blouses, delicates, towels, down wear, sportswear, quilt, timed cold and timed warm programmes. It also describes the Sensitive Drying System as using mild warm air from all sides and gently mixing laundry with curved soft carriers.[11]

CHOICE lists a Woollens programme for the Bosch WTX88M20AU and places the filter inside the front of the dryer. A front filter location matters in a constrained terrace laundry because the user needs standing and access space to remove and clean it without moving surrounding cabinetry.[9][12]

Bosch’s official Australian specification gives an operating-noise level of 62 dB(A). A-weighted decibels, written dB(A), are a sound measure weighted to approximate human hearing. This is the model’s declared appliance noise value; the later section, *Noise and shared-wall compliance*, considers what it cannot determine about sound and vibration passing through a party wall.[1]

Australian retail information gives the WTX88M20AU a two-year manufacturer warranty. E&S lists a retail price of $1,779.00 and an RRP of $2,399.00, while CHOICE lists $2,399.00. These are distinct price observations rather than a single fixed purchase price; *Ten-year total cost of ownership* uses a stated purchase-price assumption rather than treating an undated listing as a guaranteed current price.[11][9]

The retailer describes 16 drying programmes but lists 14 functions in its specification table. The two counts may describe different things—programmes versus control functions—so neither should be treated as a corrected version of the other.[11]

**For the Melbourne terrace, the Bosch is a documented Australian-market 8 kg, free-standing heat-pump dryer with an internal container-or-drain arrangement, a modest 59.8 cm cabinet width and a 145 cm cord, but its room still needs measured door clearance, accessible drainage or tank space, a nearby suitable outlet and unobstructed airflow.**[2]

This comparison assesses the three exact models across energy consumption at Victorian electricity rates for six to eight loads weekly; drying performance for wool, cotton and synthetics using AS/NZS 2442.1; acoustic output and shared-wall use; CHOICE, warranty and consumer-claim evidence; heritage-overlay installation; and 10-year ownership inputs including purchase price, electricity, Victorian Energy Upgrades eligibility, maintenance, condenser cleaning and Australian parts. Bosch WTX88M20AU and LG DVH9-09W have Australian-market product records; Samsung DV90T6240LH is identified in UK documentation as DV90T6240LH/S1 rather than in an Australian product record.[1][3][6][13]

Bosch’s Australian service page identifies the supported variant as WTX88M20AU/01, a Series 8 8 kg heat-pump tumble dryer. It offers Australian repair booking, product registration, manuals and documents, and cleaning products and accessories for that product.[10]

Owner accounts add two practical, non-standardised observations. One owner said the supplied drain-pipe attachment still requires removal of a plug at the bottom of the condenser tray to stop water accumulating in the tray after plumbing-in; the installation manual remains the governing instruction for that arrangement. Other owners described the dryer respectively as surprisingly quiet and very noisy, so these accounts do not alter the declared 62 dB(A) appliance rating.[14][1]

Bosch’s manual specifies 220–240 V, 50 Hz mains voltage, minimum 10 A installation protection, 600 W nominal power, a 145 cm power cord and an ambient operating-temperature range of 5°C to 35°C for the WTX88M20AU.[15]

### What Australian product information is available for the LG 9kg

LG Australia identifies DVH9-09W.BBWREAP as the 9 kg Series 9 Heat Pump Dryer with a 9-Star rating. LG announced the DVH9-09W for the Australian market in April 2020 as one of two new 9 kg heat-pump dryer models. The announcement listed the model in white, Series 9, with a $1,799 Australian RRP at that time.[3][5]

The LG user manual covers the DVH9-09 model family, including DVH9-09 variants, and explains that the final asterisk can vary by model version. It specifies a 220–240 V~, 50 Hz electrical supply. A retailer lists 220–240 V and 50–60 Hz; the model-family manual is the more direct installation instruction for the DVH45-08*, DVH5-08*, DVH9-08* and DVH9-09* family.[4][16]

CHOICE identifies the DVH9-09W as a 9 kg heat-pump dryer. It lists dimensions of 85 × 60 × 69 cm in height × width × depth order. LG’s manual gives dimensions of 600 mm wide, 850 mm high and 690 mm deep, plus a 1,115 mm measurement for the open-door envelope.[17][4]

LG’s New Zealand manufacturer page gives the same cabinet dimensions, 600 × 850 × 690 mm, and lists a product weight of 57 kg. A separate LG model-family manual gives 56 kg, while Australian product information elsewhere gives 58 kg. For handling and floor assessment, the practical conclusion is that the dryer is approximately 56–58 kg and requires two-person-safe handling or an appropriate delivery service, rather than treating the one-kilogram differences as a material product distinction.[18][4][13]

The DVH9-09W is deeper than the Bosch. Its 690 mm cabinet depth is 77 mm greater than Bosch’s 613 mm body depth, and its 1,115 mm door-open measurement is 10 mm longer than Bosch’s 1,105 mm door-open measurement.Derived — **these differences are calculated from the manufacturer dimensions: LG 690 mm minus Bosch 613 mm equals 77 mm; LG 1,115 mm minus Bosch 1,105 mm equals 10 mm.**[4][2]

CHOICE lists no exhaust location and no ducting kit for the LG, marking both fields N/A, and lists a drainage hose. LG Australia identifies the DVH9-09W as a Heat Pump Condenser Dryer (Ventless) and states that external venting is not required. LG Australia lists a drain-hose kit as available, while an Australian retailer lists an included extendable drain-hose kit.[17][19][16]

The model has both a water collection tank and a drain tube shown in the manual. A user review of the exact model describes the practical alternatives: route water to the laundry drain or collect it in the internal tray, which alerts the user when full. The tray option is useful where plumbing work would affect historic fabric or require approval, though a permanent internal drainage connection can reduce routine emptying.[4][20]

LG’s Australian announcement states that the Auto Cleaning Condenser flushes the internal evaporator with water during a drying cycle to remove accumulated fluff and debris. It also identifies a removable Double Layer air filter that captures lint. Auto-cleaning here is a condenser or evaporator-cleaning function; it does not mean that the lint filter disappears or that owner maintenance has no role.[5][21]

The LG product listing names 13 drying programmes: Cotton, Mixed Fabric, Easy Care, Duvet, Towels, Allergy Care, Sportswear, Wool, Delicates, Quick 30, Rack Dry, Cool Air and Warm Air. A different guide lists 11 programmes including Allergy Care, Wool and Quick 30. Both records establish that the product has Wool and cotton-oriented programmes, while the retailer’s more detailed list identifies the 13 named programmes.[16][22]

LG says its sensors monitor heat exchange, moisture and air temperature, then automatically control drying time and temperature according to the chosen programme. The product video describes Gentle Care as a low-heat option intended to reduce shrinkage and creasing, and describes Sensor Dry as helping prevent over-drying. These are treatment and control features; the separate *AS/NZS 2442.1 drying performance* section distinguishes them from standardised results for wool, cotton and synthetics.[5][23]

The model has a reversible door. The video says the hinge can be moved to either side of the opening, and a product guide says reversal requires an authorised technician. In a compact terrace laundry, door direction should be chosen before delivery because it affects circulation space, access to the water tank and whether laundry can be loaded without blocking the room door.[23][22][12]

LG states that the DVH9-09W is not suitable for built-in installation. It says the rear must be well ventilated and should be kept away from the wall for better drying performance. For an alcove or under-counter position, the manual says carpets, rugs and similar items must not impede air circulation around the appliance.[4]

The manual provides a specific cupboard requirement: the cupboard door must have two louvred openings, each at least 387 cm2, with one located 8 cm from the bottom and one 8 cm from the top of the door. It also requires approximately 20 mm between the top of the dryer and the underside of a worktop. This is directly relevant to a limited-ventilation terrace: enclosing the dryer behind an unventilated heritage cupboard door would conflict with LG’s installation instruction.[4]

LG specifies an allowable ambient temperature of 5–35°C and says the appliance performs best at 23°C. It says not to locate it where freezing temperatures could occur, because frozen hoses may burst and below-freezing conditions may impair electronic-control reliability. A laundry inside the terrace is generally more suitable than an exposed rear lean-to, unconditioned shed or outdoor enclosure.[4]

The manual requires a flat, hard floor with a maximum slope of 1 degree. It requires adjustment of all four feet until the dryer is stable. It also warns that timber or suspended floors may contribute to vibration and unbalance, and that reinforcement or bracing may need consideration to reduce excessive noise and vibration.[4]

The LG electrical instructions say to connect the dryer to an earthed socket in accordance with current wiring regulations, not to use an extension cord or double adapter, and not to overload the outlet with more than one appliance. The outlet must be within 1.5 m of either side of the intended location, and the plug must remain easily accessible.[4]

CHOICE lists the LG as stackable and wall mountable, but lists wall-mounting brackets supplied as N/A. LG’s own Australian buying guidance says heat-pump dryers cannot be wall-mounted and that vented dryers are the only dryer type that can be wall-mounted. The practical direction is to treat the DVH9-09W as a floor-standing or approved stacked appliance, not as a wall-mounted option.[17][24]

LG Australia says the included stacking bracket allows the DVH9-09W to be securely stacked on a compatible 550 mm- or 600 mm-deep LG front-load washing machine. A stacked appliance is harder to service and transfers more load and vibration to the supporting washer.[19][25]

LG offers ThinQ connectivity. Its Australian announcement says the app can monitor dryer progress remotely, track energy consumption, diagnose and troubleshoot issues, and download additional drying cycles. These are ownership and convenience features rather than evidence of electricity consumption at the household’s particular load pattern.[5]

Australian sources give a two-year dryer warranty for parts and labour. They diverge on the long component warranty: one guide lists eight years of inverter-motor parts cover, while an Australian retailer lists 10 years of compressor/motor parts only. The two-year whole-dryer warranty is the common term; longer cover is component-specific and excludes the labour coverage stated for the first two years.[22][16][17]

Retail price observations also vary. CHOICE lists $1,799.00; a retailer lists $1,799.00 against a $2,159.00 RRP; another page reports $1,577 at Appliances Online and $1,499 at The Good Guys; a factory-second listing gives $1,195.00. These are different sales channels and product conditions, so the ownership-cost calculation must name the chosen price and whether it is new or factory second.[17][16][26][21]

**For this terrace, the LG is a verified Australian 9 kg ventless heat-pump dryer with drainage-hose and tank options, a larger 690 mm depth, a rigorous cupboard-airflow requirement and specific timber-floor vibration cautions; it fits when capacity is valuable and the room can meet those physical conditions.**[3][4]

LG’s current Australian product page independently states the requested DVH9-09W has 9 kg maximum drying capacity, a 9-Star energy rating, 600 × 850 × 690 mm product dimensions, 1,115 mm depth with the door open 90 degrees, and a 58.0 kg product weight. It calls the product a Heat Pump Condenser Dryer (Ventless) and says external venting is not required.[27]

PriceMe identifies the product as LG DVH909 and records a $1,499.00 JB Hi-Fi AU offer on 28 April 2026; it describes $1,499.00 as its cheapest Australian offer.[28] This is a historical listed-price observation for the purchase-cost assumption, not a fixed current price.[28]

### What Australian product information is available for the Samsung 9kg

Samsung DV90T6240LH is identifiable in Samsung UK documentation as a Series 6 9 kg heat-pump tumble dryer, specifically the DV90T6240LH/S1 variant with OptimalDry. Samsung’s UK documentation lists a 9.0 kg drying capacity and identifies the dryer type as heat pump.[6]

The UK product page gives cabinet dimensions of 600 × 850 × 600 mm and a full depth of 650 mm including protruding parts. It lists a 50 kg net weight. A UK retailer likewise describes a 850 mm high × 600 mm wide × 600 mm deep heat-pump condenser dryer, while saying its listed dimensions exclude button and door protrusions.[6][29]

The Samsung’s documented 600 mm cabinet depth is 13 mm shallower than the Bosch body depth and 90 mm shallower than the LG body depth.Derived — **this is calculated from the listed cabinet depths: Bosch 613 mm minus Samsung 600 mm equals 13 mm; LG 690 mm minus Samsung 600 mm equals 90 mm.** Those differences support a preliminary space comparison only, because the Samsung figures are not an Australian installation record.[2][4][6]

Samsung lists a reversible door for the UK variant. A reversible door can make a real difference in a terrace laundry where the appliance sits beside a wall, passage or washer, because it changes the side from which users load the drum and access the filter.[6]

Samsung lists Wool, Cotton and Synthetics cycles for the documented UK variant. It describes OptimalDry as using moisture and temperature sensors to adjust drying time. These are product programmes and sensor features; they do not answer the later question of AS/NZS 2442.1 performance for those fabric groups.[6]

Samsung describes a 2-in-1 filter with two-layer mesh to collect fluff. Samsung’s Malaysian and Finnish product pages say a separate heat-exchanger filter is not needed, while two alerts remind the user to clean both the filter and the heat exchanger.[6][30][31]

Samsung’s UK page lists a cycle time of 220 minutes. The Jordan Samsung page describes Quick Dry 35' for a 1 kg load, with the warning that time can vary by fabric type. These are programme-specific manufacturer descriptions, not comparable standardised full-load test outcomes.[6][32]

The UK documentation gives the model A+++ energy efficiency. Samsung Australia explains that Australian dryers use Australian Energy Rating Labels, typically on a scale up to six stars, with highly efficient appliances able to receive up to 10 stars. A+++ and Australian Energy Stars are not interchangeable labels, so the UK classification must not be placed beside Bosch’s and LG’s Australian star labels as though they use one rating system.[6][25]

A Finnish Samsung listing for the DV90T6240LH/S4 gives energy consumption of 105 kWh per 100 programmes, energy-efficiency class C, and drying noise of 64 dB with noise-emission class B. The EU product fiche cited for the Samsung lists a rated-capacity programme duration of 3 hours 40 minutes, a half-load duration of 2 hours 20 minutes and a 24-month minimum guarantee. A UK catalogue instead lists 63 dB noise. The Finnish and UK values are overseas product information and do not supply an Australian model declaration.[31][13][33][34]

The exact model’s Australian status is the deciding limitation. The available Samsung Australia material describes heat-pump dryers generally, but does not identify DV90T6240LH as an Australian product. A Samsung UK support page offers manuals and downloads for DV90T6240LH, confirming active UK support-page identification of the code.[25][7]

The model suffix also needs care. The UK product record is for DV90T6240LH/S1. A Jordan business page displays DV90T6240LX/FH but imagery describes DV90T6240LH/S3. These suffixes can denote market or configuration variants, so an overseas specification cannot be assumed to govern an Australian appliance bearing a superficially similar code.[6][32]

Samsung’s general installation guide says the dryer should be placed level, with room for the door to open widely, and plugged into a properly grounded outlet. It describes a direct-drain option using a long drain hose, with the hose end lower than 90 cm from the dryer bottom, at least 70 mm clear of the drain and not submerged. It also says a genuine Samsung stacking kit must be used for stacking.[35]

The supplied-parts list in that Samsung guide names a long drain hose, cleaning brush and micro filter. The guide itself is a Samsung Lebanon page and does not identify DV90T6240LH; the instructions are therefore useful installation principles, not proof of what is supplied or approved for a Melbourne purchase.[35]

The drying-rack information conflicts. In a Samsung Community thread, a representative initially said DV90T6240LH/S1 did not include a drying rack. The customer then reported that the delivered dryer included a drying tray, and the representative responded that the rack was not mentioned in the manual or product specifications they had checked. A buyer who needs a rack for woollens or shoes should therefore obtain a written inclusion list from the seller.[36]

A UK historical promotion offered a five-year parts-and-labour warranty for selected purchases from 1 September 2022 to 31 August 2023, subject to registration within 90 days. That is a UK offer for a defined purchase period, not an Australian warranty term for this comparison.[6]

**For a Melbourne buyer, the Samsung is physically and functionally identifiable as a 9 kg heat-pump dryer in overseas documentation, but it should not be treated as an Australian-market substitute for the Bosch or LG until a seller provides the exact Australian suffix, GEMS label, warranty, installation material, parts path and price.**[6][13][7]

That increases the importance of capacity, daily access to filters and condensate collection, clearance around the appliance, vibration management, energy use and local service support. It also means that a “one load a week” energy-label test profile should be treated as a comparison basis, not a household forecast.[1][16][12]

A heat-pump dryer does not need an external exhaust duct because it removes water from the process air by condensation and recirculates the air through its refrigeration circuit. Condensate still has to go somewhere: into an onboard container for manual emptying or to an available drain through a hose.[43][44]

Judgment — In a heritage terrace, the lowest-impact arrangement is normally a free-standing heat-pump dryer set on a stable internal floor, using its removable condensate tank where a permanent drain would require intrusive work. A direct internal drain is attractive when an existing laundry connection can accept it without altering significant heritage fabric; it avoids routine water carrying but should remain accessible for inspection and cleaning.

The absence of an external dryer vent does not mean that the appliance can be sealed into an unventilated cupboard. Bosch requires adequate room ventilation and a clear ventilation opening, explicitly warning that impeded air exchange lengthens cycles and raises energy consumption. LG requires rear ventilation and provides specific louvred-door requirements for a cupboard.[2][4]

The Bosch is the shallowest established Australian-market model by cabinet depth at 613 mm, followed by the LG at 690 mm. The Samsung’s overseas-listed 600 mm depth is smaller on paper, but its Australian installation envelope is not established. A terrace owner deciding between the supported Australian choices should therefore measure against Bosch’s 665 mm closed-door and 1,105 mm open-door dimensions, or LG’s 690 mm body and 1,115 mm open-door dimensions, rather than against a nominal “60 cm dryer” category.[2][4][6]

A solid, level floor is both an installation and a neighbour-noise issue. Bosch requires a clean, level, firm surface. LG requires a flat hard floor with no more than 1 degree slope, and its manual says suspended timber floors may need reinforcement or bracing to reduce excessive vibration and noise.[2][4]

A shared wall makes transmitted vibration as important as airborne appliance sound. Victoria’s EPA says residential noise is unreasonable when it occurs during prohibited times and is audible in a habitable room of another residence; a laundry is not a habitable room, but a neighbouring bedroom or living room is. Group 6 electrical equipment is prohibited if audible in a neighbour’s home before 7 am or after 8 pm on weekdays, or before 9 am or after 8 pm on weekends and public holidays. A dryer appears to fall within that general electrical-equipment group, which is an application of the EPA category rather than a dryer-specific statement.[45]

The relevant Victorian control for a heritage-listed terrace is normally the Heritage Overlay, Clause 43.01 of the applicable local planning scheme. The permit outcome depends on the property’s schedule and the responsible council’s controls. External services, wall or floor penetrations, visible condensate drains, new grilles and changes to significant fabric can trigger heritage scrutiny; a wholly internal, non-ducted appliance with a removable tank ordinarily avoids those external changes.[13][37]

Judgment — First, measure the intended bay, adjacent doorways and the full door-swing zone. Second, inspect the floor, party-wall relationship, outlet position and drain route before ordering. Third, select a tank-based arrangement if the alternative is a new penetration through original fabric; ask council about any permanent drainage, external grille, visible service or modification to significant fabric. Fourth, level the machine, keep it away from party-wall cabinetry where practicable, and schedule it outside sensitive hours.

A permanent condensate-drain connection requires a suitable lawful internal waste connection; if this is unavailable, the household must empty the tank regularly at six to eight loads a week.

Heritage assessment should also identify the terrace’s significance and any incorporated heritage policy, alongside the Heritage Overlay schedule and the precise proposed works.

## AS/NZS 2442.1 drying performance

Judgment — Drying performance has two separate meanings in this comparison: whether a load reaches the desired dryness, and how gently the appliance treats a particular textile. A dryer can offer Wool, Cotton and Synthetics programmes without having a standardised result that proves it dries all three fabrics equally well. For a household drying everyday cottons as well as occasional woollens and synthetic activewear, programme limits and end-of-cycle instructions matter as much as the headline drum capacity.

### What do AS/NZS 2442.1 test results establish about drying performance

AS/NZS 2442.1:2021, published on 19 February 2021 and later amended in October 2021 and February 2024, is the Australian/New Zealand method standard for measuring household rotary-dryer performance. It covers moisture removal, energy and water consumption where applicable, maximum clothes temperature, and a dryer’s ability to dry a test load in one programme setting. AS/NZS 2442.2 is the separate energy-labelling part: it converts relevant test results into star ratings and comparative annual energy consumption.[57][58][59]

The 2024 clothes-dryer determination uses the versions of the relevant standards existing on 10 September 2024 and commenced on 4 October 2025.[103]

The standard’s **single-operation criterion** requires a dryer to reduce the test load to no more than 6% moisture, measured against its bone-dry weight, in one setting. It requires a load equal to the dryer’s rated capacity to be dried in a single setting without user intervention; an autosensing dryer must have at least one programme that does this at its rated test load. The method also limits maximum fabric temperature to 132°C and tested energy performance (TEP) to 1.36 kWh per kilogram of moisture removed.[58][57]

The AS/NZS prescribed test load is mixed cotton, comprising nine different cotton items. The stated test conditions are 20 ± 2°C, 60 ± 5% relative humidity, 90% ± 20 g initial moisture on a bone-dry basis, and final moisture of no more than 6% of bone-dry mass; testing uses 230 V and includes cool-down until programme termination.[57]

| AS/NZS 2442.1 measure | Published requirement or condition | What it measures for this comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Test textile load | Mixed cotton; nine cotton load items | Cotton-load moisture removal, not separate wool and synthetics outcomes. [57][58] |
| Final moisture target | ≤6% of bone-dry load mass | Whether the specified test load reaches cupboard-dry-type moisture in one setting. [57][58] |
| Maximum clothes temperature | 132°C | A ceiling for tested clothes temperature under the method’s onerous conditions. [58] |
| Tested energy performance | ≤1.36 kWh/kg of moisture removed | Laboratory energy used per kilogram of water removed; it excludes the field-use factor. [58] |
| Test environment | 20 ± 2°C; 60 ± 5% humidity; 230 V | Controlled conditions needed to compare test runs. [57] |

One secondary description says AS/NZS 2442.1 regulates wool, cotton and synthetic test fabrics, but the standard’s own comparison table specifies a mixed-cotton load. The standard therefore supplies a controlled cotton-load threshold rather than a wool-versus-cotton-versus-synthetics fabric-care ranking.[56][57][58]

**The practical conclusion is that AS/NZS 2442.1 can confirm cotton-load drying performance under controlled conditions, but it cannot rank the three dryers for wool and synthetics as fabric categories.**[58][57]

Compared with the superseded edition, AS/NZS 2442.1:2021 extends the loading table from 10 kg to 20 kg, includes heat pumps in the defined heating methods alongside electric resistance elements and radio-frequency devices, and includes the cool-down period through to programme termination in the test method.[41]

The AS/NZS 2442.1 comparison table describes the test load as “Mixed cotton”, made up of nine different cotton load items, and permits load sizes “Any in 0.5 kg increments”.[57]

A standards-based result is comparable only when the dryers were tested to the same edition of the standard, on the same programme and load condition, with the same end point. An energy-label figure or a manufacturer’s statement that a Wool, Cotton, Delicates or Synthetics programme exists does not itself establish model-specific drying time, evenness, residual moisture or garment-care performance for that fabric.

CHOICE says a 6% whole-load moisture result does not mean every item is dry: its testers find that some lower-performing dryers leave individual clothes slightly moist while still reaching 6% overall.[60] CHOICE describes around 4.5% final moisture as the preferred balance for the dryness most people expect without fibre damage, and says drying below 2.5% can damage fabric fibres.[60]

CHOICE tests dryers with a 3.5 kg dry cotton load comprising sheets, towels and pillowcases; it reports that the load weighs 5 kg after washing at 90% moisture content. CHOICE uses this smaller load because households commonly do not dry full loads, whereas registration testing uses the dryer’s full rated capacity.[104]

AS/NZS 2442.1:2021 specifies a 230 V test voltage and retains the bone-dry-load approach to calculating moisture content.[41] Its introduction distinguishes this from IEC 61121:2012, which uses a simplified load and conditioned mass, defined at equilibrium as 0% reference moisture content.[41] Final-moisture figures reported under the two methods therefore do not share the same moisture-reference approach.[41]

Cycle electricity consumption is measured by integrating the power used over the drying cycle.[105]

## Noise and shared-wall compliance

A dryer beside a shared wall creates two distinct issues: airborne sound, heard through gaps and walls, and structure-borne vibration, carried through a timber floor, cabinetry or framing. The relevant Victorian test is neighbour audibility at particular times, not simply the sound figure printed in a product specification.

Judgment — A-weighted decibels, written dB(A), are a sound-level measure weighted towards frequencies to which human hearing is most sensitive. They are useful for comparing stated appliance output on a like-for-like basis, but they are not a measurement inside the adjoining terrace.

### What Victorian or Melbourne residential noise rules apply to a

EPA Victoria defines residential noise as unreasonable when it occurs during a prohibited time and a person can hear it in a habitable room of another residence. A habitable room includes living rooms and bedrooms but excludes kitchens, pantries, bathrooms, toilets, laundries and storage rooms.[45]

EPA Victoria’s Group 6 covers electrical equipment, including gardening tools, that is not in Groups 2–5 and is not for personal care or grooming, or food heating, cooling or preparation. The prohibited times for this group are before 7 am and after 8 pm on weekdays, and before 9 am and after 8 pm on weekends and public holidays, where the equipment can be heard in the neighbour’s home.[45] Group 3 lists heating equipment, including a heat pump, with a 10 pm evening cutoff: before 7 am or after 10 pm on weekdays, and before 9 am or after 10 pm on weekends and public holidays.[64] The stated evening cutoff depends on classification: Group 3 lists heat pumps with a 10 pm cutoff, while Group 6 residual electrical equipment has an 8 pm cutoff.[64][45]

Judgment — A clothes dryer appears to fit Group 6, electrical equipment not in Groups 2–5 other than personal-care, grooming and food-heating, cooling or preparation equipment.[45][64] For this terrace, do not run the dryer if it can be heard in a neighbour’s bedroom or other habitable room before 7 am or after 8 pm on weekdays, or before 9 am or after 8 pm on weekends and public holidays.[45][64]

A council officer may assess noise as unreasonable even in permitted hours where its qualities are unreasonable, including unnecessarily excessive volume. Victoria’s general environmental duty also applies to residential noise management.[45]

A non-authoritative Melbourne summary instead describes a daytime threshold of 65 dB(A) and restrictions beginning at 10 pm on weekdays; EPA Victoria’s rule is the more relevant one because it turns on audibility in the neighbour’s habitable room and the relevant time rather than a universal indoor appliance threshold.[37][45] **The practical compliance target is neighbour-side inaudibility during prohibited periods, not a dryer label below a particular dB(A) number.**

EPA Victoria states that the factors in deciding whether non-prescribed noise is unreasonable are volume, intensity, duration, character, time, place and frequency. Volume includes how far sound spreads through the affected location; character includes tonal humming, droning, rattling, banging and thudding; and limiting operating times reduces noise and vibration impacts.[65]

EPA Victoria’s unreasonable-noise guidance also considers recurring interference, rattles, vibration, sound that dominates background noise, and tonal, impulsive or intermittent characteristics; sound more than 5 dB above background may be unreasonable even outside prohibited hours. A council may require replacement, relocation, screening or maintenance through an improvement notice.[106][107]

Any applicable council local law, owners-corporation rule, tenancy obligation, planning-permit condition or building requirement can add controls beyond the EPA framework. The relevant council can receive and investigate neighbourhood-noise complaints.

Victoria’s Environment Protection Regulations 2021, statutory rule 47/2021, contains the residential-noise prohibited-times framework in Division 2 of Part 5.3.[66][67]

## Installation and heritage controls

A heat-pump dryer is a **ventless condenser dryer**: it removes water from clothes by condensing it inside the machine rather than blowing damp air through an external duct. That is a major advantage in a heritage terrace, but it does not make the installation unconstrained. The laundry still needs adequate appliance airflow, a sound floor, electrical supply, condensate handling and service access.

Judgment — For this house, the installation decision should be made in two stages: first avoid any external alteration by using the dryer’s water tank or an existing internal drain; second test the chosen location against the named model’s dimensions, door swing, ventilation instructions and power requirements. A new exterior grille is rarely justified for one of these three dryers.

### Which alterations to a heritage-listed Melbourne terrace may need approval

In Victoria, the relevant heritage-planning control is ordinarily the local planning scheme’s **Clause 43.01 Heritage Overlay**. Whether a planning permit is needed turns on the specific property’s Heritage Overlay schedule and the responsible council’s practice, not simply on the fact that an appliance is being installed. External services, new openings or penetrations, visible condensate drains, external grilles, and alterations to significant heritage fabric can trigger a permit requirement.[13]

Clause 43.01 requires a permit for domestic services visible from a street other than a lane, or from a public park, and for external alterations.[108]

The three dryers offer a practical heritage advantage because each is a heat-pump or condenser type and does not require an external wall vent. Bosch can use its condensation container or a drain hose; LG is explicitly a ventless heat-pump condenser dryer with a drain-hose kit; and Samsung is described in its EU product fiche as an electric condenser tumble dryer. Keeping the dryer and its condensate management wholly inside therefore avoids the usual new wall hole and exterior grille required by a vented dryer.[33]

A simple internal appliance replacement that makes no new penetration or alteration to heritage fabric will commonly have a lower planning risk than external works. However, an internal location does not settle every question: a permanent drain route may still need council confirmation if it passes through significant fabric, crosses an external wall or produces visible external pipework. Electrical and plumbing modifications must also be performed by appropriately licensed trades even where a heritage planning permit is not required.[56][13]

| Proposed work in the terrace | Heritage-overlay implication |
|---|---|
| Freestanding dryer entirely inside the laundry, using its removable condensate tank | This generally avoids an exterior vent, visible service and new penetration; the Heritage Overlay question is correspondingly low-risk. [33] |
| Connection to an existing internal laundry drain without affecting heritage fabric | This avoids a new external dryer vent, but its acceptability depends on the actual plumbing route and the specific overlay schedule. [13][33] |
| New wall or floor penetration for drainage, cabling or a vent | A planning permit may be required where the work changes significant fabric or creates an external service. [13] |
| New external vent grille or façade opening | This is the alteration these ventless dryers are designed to avoid; it may require heritage approval. [13][33] |
| New externally visible condensate pipe, grille or service box | This may trigger heritage review because it changes the appearance of the building or affects heritage fabric. [13] |

**The lowest-risk heritage solution is a fully internal installation using the tank or an existing internal drain, because it avoids the external vent grille, façade opening and visible service that can trigger Heritage Overlay controls.**[13][33]

The owner should obtain the address’s Heritage Overlay schedule and send the responsible council the proposed condensate-drain, electrical and fixing routes before cutting, drilling or fixing into original walls, floors, joinery or façade fabric. A removable tank or connection to an existing internal drain is distinct from a new penetration, visible external service or alteration to significant heritage fabric.[13][1][4] A Melbourne heritage-home case study routes pipes through the subfloor so original floorboards stay down, carefully removes, labels and stores replaced skirting on site so the change is reversible, and leaves significant decorative skirting in place where it is a feature.[78] Early council advice about visible work can be paired with subfloor routing, reversible storage of removed skirting and retention of significant decorative skirting to limit permanent changes to a heritage interior.[78]

The Heritage Overlay schedule, any incorporated document and the heritage citation can distinguish an individually significant place from a contributory one and can contain permit exemptions. Structural work to form or alter a laundry, cut framing or masonry, change an outbuilding, alter a chimney or roof, or modify a meter box can require separate assessment where it affects protected fabric. Owners-corporation rules, landlord consent, building requirements and plumbing requirements are separate from heritage-planning approval.

A statement of significance may identify the terrace as individually significant, contributory or non-contributory and describe protected buildings, outbuildings, gardens or interiors.[109][110] Internal alterations require a Heritage Overlay permit only where the property’s schedule applies that control.[111]

An incorporated-plan permit exemption applies only where the plan is specified in the Heritage Overlay schedule and listed under Clause 72.04.[108][110] Repairs or maintenance that change a place’s appearance rather than retaining the same details, specifications and materials may also require assessment.[111]

If a permit is required, the application should identify the proposed work’s location, materials, reversibility and visual impact. This makes a freestanding internal installation using an existing power point and the dryer tank the most readily reversible option.

## Recommendation

For this high-use Melbourne household, the choice turns on constrained installation and capacity rather than purchase price alone: a quiet, ventless machine with low energy use and a compact footprint best suits a limited-ventilation terrace beside a shared wall. CHOICE says heat-pump dryers are “far and away the cheapest type to run”, while vented dryers’ running costs are around three times higher; heat-pump dryers suit frequent use where exterior venting is difficult, although their lower-temperature cycles are commonly longer.[60][12][98]

### Which of Bosch WTX88M20AU

**Bosch WTX88M20AU ranks first, provided its 8 kg capacity suits the household’s actual load sizes.** It combines the lowest stated Australian-label consumption, 98 kWh/year, with 62 dB(A) noise, ventless operation, the shallowest supported depth and a cited Melbourne price of $1,884. Bosch’s 62 dB(A) noise rating, ventless operation and documented 61.3 cm appliance depth, 66.5 cm closed-door depth and 110.5 cm open-door depth support its first rank for a limited-ventilation, shared-wall terrace.[13][2]

LG DVH9-09W ranks second, but becomes first if 9 kg capacity or Australian serviceability outweigh Bosch’s space and operating advantages. It has stronger Australian spare-parts support, but its label figure is 129 kWh/year, it is deeper, and its filter routine needs greater discipline. LG’s Australian launch material identifies it as a 9 kg Series 9 model with a $1,799 RRP in April 2020, while Bosch’s retailer listing is $1,779; the ranking therefore rests on Bosch’s installation, noise and energy advantages rather than a lower LG purchase price.[13][11][5]

Samsung DV90T6240LH ranks third. Samsung can displace Bosch or LG only if the seller supplies the exact Australian GEMS registration, current Australian warranty, Victorian Energy Upgrades listing, Australian parts support and price for DV90T6240LH/SA.[13]

A video posted on 2026-01-29 ranks LG fourth and Bosch sixth among eight dryer brands.[99] Another report calls LG the best all-round option and characterises Bosch as discontinued.[37] The Bosch-first recommendation rests on the directly stated model-specific noise, energy, depth, price, ventless-operation and parts comparisons above.[13]

The decision is therefore practical: Bosch is the best-supported terrace choice; LG is the capacity-and-serviceability alternative; Samsung is not yet a purchase-ready option.

1. Buy the **Bosch WTX88M20AU** if 8 kg is adequate.
2. Choose the **LG DVH9-09W** only when 9 kg capacity or stronger parts support decides the purchase.
3. Defer the **Samsung DV90T6240LH** unless the listed Australian compliance, support and price details are confirmed.

LG’s 9 kg rating is 1 kg, or 12.5%, greater than Bosch’s 8 kg rating. This is the quantified capacity benefit that must justify LG’s deeper cabinet and higher label-derived electricity use where the household regularly dries loads above Bosch’s capacity.[2][17]

Bosch’s installation instructions require the appliance ventilation opening to remain clear, the area to be adequately ventilated and operation in a well-ventilated room. Impeded air exchange extends programme duration and increases energy consumption.[2]

Bosch has the lowest grounded noise rating, 62 dB(A), but a dB(A) rating alone does not prove shared-wall compliance. The shared-wall result also depends on levelling the feet, reducing vibration transmission, using anti-vibration pads on suspended boards where appropriate, avoiding party-wall cabinetry where possible, and avoiding night-time cycles.[13]

LG’s manual warns that timber or suspended flooring can contribute to excessive vibration and imbalance, may need reinforcement or bracing, and requires adjustment of the levelling feet until the appliance no longer wobbles.[13]

For a heritage-listed terrace, the relevant control is typically the Victorian Planning Provisions or local planning scheme Clause 43.01 Heritage Overlay, determined by the property schedule and council practice. External services, penetrations, visible condensate drains, new grilles and changes to significant fabric can trigger a heritage permit. Keeping a ventless dryer wholly inside the laundry with a removable condensate tank generally avoids external works, while a permanent drain through heritage fabric requires checking with the responsible council; the address is required for a definitive permit outcome.[13]
