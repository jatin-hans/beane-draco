Raleigh is the best financial choice in the modeled comparison, provided you buy only after matching a named Wake County 8+/10 elementary assignment to your employer’s actual industrial site.

Its $380,000 Raleigh-Cary housing benchmark and roughly 0.9191% city-and-county property-tax example create a much lower entry cost than Lake Travis at about $680,000 and Austin’s roughly 1.67%–2.12% pre-exemption tax stacks, while preserving several 8–10/10 K–5 options.[1][2][3][4] Austin is the stronger documented market for senior aerospace, defense, robotics and manufacturing mechanical-engineering roles, but its high property taxes and the separation of western high-rated school areas from southeast industrial employment make it the less reliable cost-reduction move.[5][6] Nashville’s low roughly 0.70% property-tax rate and absence of a state wage-income tax are meaningful advantages, but its $457,000 citywide home-price benchmark and dispersed Franklin, Spring Hill, Mount Juliet, Murfreesboro and airport-area employment nodes make Raleigh the better balanced ten-year financial and family choice.[7][8][9][10][11]

### Austin

Austin has named senior aerospace, defense, and manufacturing mechanical-engineering postings and multiple 8+/10 elementary options.[5][18] It also contains the most important internal trade-off in this comparison: the most attractive school districts and the main industrial zones do not naturally occupy the same geography.

#### Housing in high-rated school areas

Round Rock ISD is the lower-priced published Austin-area choice that meets the requested district-rating screen. Its reported 2026 median home price is approximately $415,000, and the same source gives the district an 8.5/10 GreatSchools rating.[3] Johnny Ronca reports a separate $475,000 Round Rock ISD median from 1,218 closed single-family sales during March 26–September 22, 2026.[16] Five school districts serve Round Rock addresses; the district map, rather than the city name on an address, identifies whether a home is in Round Rock ISD. Lake Travis ISD is the more expensive published choice at approximately $680,000, with a reported 9/10 district rating and elementary campuses across Lakeway and Bee Cave rated 8–9/10.[41][3] Johnny Ronca reports a $838,577 Lake Travis ISD median from 679 closed single-family sales in the same March 26–September 22, 2026 comparison period.[16]

Derived — The $415,000 Round Rock figure is $265,000 below Lake Travis’s $680,000 figure, calculated as $680,000 minus $415,000. The gap is large enough to change both down-payment requirements and the amount of income exposed to housing cost, even before Austin-area property taxes are considered.[3]

Eanes ISD is the premium end of the same school-market spectrum. It serves Westlake Hills, Rollingwood and western unincorporated Travis County; its reported 2026 median home price is approximately $2.1 million, while Eanes, Forest Trail and Bridge Point elementary schools are reported at 9–10/10.[3] A separate trailing-12-month MLS comparison puts Eanes ISD’s single-family median sold price at $1.9 million from 344 closed sales, while Lake Travis ISD’s is $825,000 from 1,073 closed sales.[42]

Lake Travis likewise has a $680,000 approximate 2026 median in one source and an $825,000 trailing-12-month single-family closed-sale median in another.[3][42] The useful decision point is not which one is universally correct; it is that Eanes is far beyond the housing range implied by a cost-reduction move, while Lake Travis is a premium but potentially reachable school-area market and Round Rock is the published lower-price screen.

Leander ISD is another lower-cost Austin-area school-district option. One source gives it an overall GreatSchools rating of 8.2/10 and an approximately $390,000 2026 median home price; another reports a $537,000 trailing-12-month single-family median from 2,455 closed sales, refreshed November 17, 2026.[3][43] The numbers use different sale periods and market definitions.

Dripping Springs ISD covers 198 square miles in northern Hays County and part of Travis County, less than 25 miles from Austin; it serves more than 8,900 students at nine schools, including six elementary schools.[131]

Leander’s listed school evidence is weaker for this family’s immediate elementary decision because the supplied source’s named 7–9/10 campuses are high schools, not a K–5 result.[3]

Liberty Hill ISD serves 9,812 students at 11 schools; its listed qualifying elementary options are Rancho Sienna, Santa Rita and Bar W at 9/10, and Bill Burden and Tierra Rosa at 8/10.[126]

The school-assignment premium is economically meaningful. A reported Austin-metro estimate says homes in a good school district typically carry an 8–15% premium relative to similar homes in lower-rated districts, and one Austin ISD comparison puts the Casis Elementary attendance-zone premium at 8–12% per square foot relative to a 5/10-rated campus three miles away.[3] A buyer should therefore treat the elementary-school attendance assignment as part of the house’s price, resale case and daily logistics rather than as a free amenity attached to a ZIP code.

#### Property tax

Austin does not have one property-tax rate. For a home inside the City of Austin and Austin ISD, the 2026 combined stack is $2.121064 per $100 of taxable value, or about 2.12% before exemptions; the components include the City of Austin, Travis County, Central Health, Austin Community College and Austin ISD. The published examples range from about 1.6% to 2.1% before exemptions, depending on city, school district and special districts.[4]

A Lake Travis-oriented address can be less heavily taxed than a central Austin address. The published Bee Cave plus Lake Travis ISD plus ESD 6 stack is 1.665612% of taxable value, compared with 2.121064% for City of Austin plus Austin ISD. That difference is 0.455452 percentage points of taxable value each year before exemptions; on a $680,000 taxable-value assumption, it is roughly $3,097 annually, calculated as $680,000 multiplied by 0.00455452.[4] This is a derived illustration, not a property-specific tax bill.

Homestead exemptions reduce the effective burden but do not make tax stacks interchangeable. For a $1.2 million City of Austin/Austin ISD homestead, the reported 2026 tax bill is about $21,517 with general homestead exemptions, equal to about 1.79% of value, versus about $25,453 without exemptions.[4] Texas’s school-district homestead exemption is listed at $140,000 for 2026, and the homestead appraisal-value cap is 10% annually after eligibility begins.[4]

The decisive buyer rule is to estimate from the likely purchase price or current market value, apply the address’s current tax stack, and then apply the buyer’s own homestead eligibility. A seller’s current bill can be artificially low because long-term homestead caps suppress taxable value and exemptions belong to the owner rather than the property.[4] This makes Austin’s property-tax cost a major underwriting item even when the purchase price looks favorable relative to Denver or the other two destinations.

These are postings, not an unduplicated 12-month market total. **Common 12-month search and inclusion rules —** Lightcast can apply one metro boundary, one 12-month window, senior/staff/principal mechanical-engineer titles, aerospace or manufacturing employer criteria, title normalization, and posting deduplication across Austin, Raleigh and Nashville.[45][46][47] Lightcast’s posting data are designed for that task because the provider states that it aggregates thousands of online sources, resolves duplicates and normalizes occupations and job titles.[45] Its Titles Taxonomy standardizes more than **20 million** real-world job titles collected from more than **1 billion** job postings, resumes and profiles into a proprietary taxonomy of more than **75,000** titles.[46] Austin’s published Opportunity Austin reporting also uses Lightcast Developer and states that the tool deduplicates postings and codes them to SOC 2018 occupations.[47]

Austin’s industrial geography is broad. ## Best financial outcome over a 10-year horizon

A move only reduces your cost of living if it improves the household’s cash flow after housing, state and local taxes, transportation, insurance, commuting costs, and any pay change—not merely because a city has a lower headline cost-of-living index. The Mechanical Engineering Salary Guide 2026 states that housing, taxes, transportation, insurance, and commuting can offset a higher nominal salary.[80] The appropriate baseline is your actual $95,000 salary, married household, and one school-age child; it is not the Denver market median for all mechanical engineers.
