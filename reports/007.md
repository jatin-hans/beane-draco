Choose the HP ZBook Fury 16 G11 for the principal Revit and Lumion machines because it alone reaches 128 GB RAM and can be configured with an RTX 5000 Ada GPU with 16 GB VRAM. ## Compare Dell, HP, and Lenovo

For this Dubai practice, each product name covers radically different machines. The entry configurations with integrated graphics are not substitutes for discrete-GPU workstations for Lumion, and a 64 GB configuration may be either a fixed ceiling or a field-upgradeable starting point depending on the chassis. The decision therefore turns on the quoted SKU, GPU, VRAM, memory design, power adapter, display, warranty entitlement and Dubai service level—not on the badge on the lid.

**For a five-year Dubai fleet, the preference depends on the requirement. Where a designer genuinely requires both 128 GB RAM and 16 GB VRAM, only the HP ZBook Fury 16 G11 is eligible. Where 64 GB RAM is sufficient, a 16 GB-VRAM Dell Precision 5690 and an equivalently quoted Fury G11 should be compared on the quoted sustained-load configuration and five-year service cost. Where portability is decisive and 8 GB VRAM is sufficient, the Lenovo ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 becomes a viable mobility-led option.**[2][1][5][10]

### Comparison basis

The relevant architectural workload divides into three different demands. In Notebookcheck’s SPECviewperf 13 3ds Max 3dsmax-06 result, the Dell Precision 5690 with an NVIDIA RTX 5000 Ada Generation Laptop GPU and Intel Core Ultra 9 185H recorded 258.63 fps; the HP ZBook Fury 16 G10 with an NVIDIA RTX 5000 Ada Generation Laptop GPU and Intel Core i9-13950HX recorded 278.36 fps; and the Lenovo ThinkPad P1 G6 with an NVIDIA RTX 2000 Ada Generation Laptop GPU and Intel Core i9-13900H recorded 200.54 fps. In Blender v3.3 Classroom, the Dell took 14 seconds with OPTIX/RTX and 23 seconds with CUDA, versus 28 seconds and 51 seconds respectively for the ThinkPad P1 G6. The quoted GPU TGP, cooling profile and power supply must be included in every vendor quote.[13][64]

The Dell can also be run in different Dell Optimizer modes. Notebookcheck reported a Fire Strike Graphics Score of 29,664 in Performance mode and 29,771 in Balanced mode, while the battery-powered score was 26,674. Dell’s Balanced mode therefore preserved graphics performance in that test but reduced processor performance, and operating on battery cut the graphics score by 10%.[13]

Judgment — A Lumion workstation should be operated on AC power for design review, live visualization and render export. Battery operation is appropriate for a client presentation, not for comparing workstation throughput or planning a render deadline.

The Precision 5690 also supports two M.2 2280 PCIe Gen4 storage slots. This does not improve GPU rendering directly, but it lets the firm keep project data, Lumion assets, Revit local files and scratch/export data on fast internal storage rather than forcing early replacement for capacity reasons.[3][65]

The Dell’s rendering decision is therefore straightforward: the RTX 5000 Ada model is a credible portable renderer with 16 GB VRAM; the integrated-Arc entry SKU is not an equivalent workstation for Lumion.

#### Thermal management under sustained CAD loads and warm conditions

Dell’s operating-temperature specification is 0°C to 35°C. That is an environmental operating range, not a promise of maximum CPU or GPU performance at 35°C. Dell also cautions that component operating ranges can differ and that operation outside them can affect component performance.[7]

The most useful Dell sustained-load observation comes from Notebookcheck’s Prime95 test. The Core Ultra 9 185H initially boosted to 3.7 GHz at 120 W, reached 100°C within seconds, then settled at approximately 3.0 GHz and 73 W. In combined Prime95 plus FurMark stress, the review recorded approximately 3.0 GHz CPU, 1,680 MHz GPU, 93°C CPU and 65°C GPU.[13]

A separate long-load test of a Core Ultra 7-165H Dell reported 3.06 GHz P-core, 2.50 GHz E-core, 72°C and 58 W during the 10–15-minute portion of a 100% CPU test. The difference between that result and the Core Ultra 9 figures is expected because the processors, test duration and workloads differ.[66]

GPU behavior is also configuration-specific. Notebookcheck measured 72 W GPU board power during Witcher 3 on the RTX 5000 Ada machine despite Nvidia Control Center listing 90 W. LaptopMedia tested a different Precision 5690 with RTX 2000 Ada rated at 95 W and measured 89 W after 30 minutes of gaming, at 76°C and 2,346 MHz.[13][66]

The cooling hardware is substantial for a thin workstation: two fans, two shared heat pipes, two heatsinks and two metal plates. Dell’s marketing description lists Dual Opposing Output fans, dual-intake venting and User Selectable Thermal Tables; LaptopMedia states that high-end Precision 5690 configurations use vapor-chamber cooling, while its reviewed machine used a conventional thermal system. LaptopMedia found that the pair of fans remained almost quiet during heavy stress in Ultra Performance mode, measured a 52°C keyboard hot spot near the Space key and measured 46°C at the lower display bezel near the rear exhaust. Notebookcheck found that GPU-heavy loads lasting more than several minutes could take fan noise to a 51 dB(A) ceiling.[66][62][30][13]

Surface temperature matters in an office because it affects comfort and shows the heat the cooling system must reject. Notebookcheck measured keyboard-deck hot spots of 43°C to 47°C under demanding loads, a maximum upper-surface temperature of 46.6°C and a maximum bottom temperature of 33°C. The Dell AC adapter exceeded 55°C under demanding loads.[13]

Judgment — Dubai’s outdoor summer temperature is not the relevant laptop ambient temperature if the laptops remain in air-conditioned offices. The practical risk is a warm meeting room, a site office, dust-obstructed vents, direct sun, a laptop used on fabric or soft furnishings, or a power brick enclosed under a desk. Those conditions reduce the temperature difference available to cool the CPU and GPU.

For an eight-designer practice, set a simple “thermal operating rule”: use mains power, hard flat desks, unblocked intake and exhaust paths, and air-conditioned rooms for rendering. Dell’s own service terms exclude damage associated with improper or insufficient ventilation and unsuitable physical or operating environment, so ventilation is both a performance and support-protection issue.[8]

The Precision 5690’s thermal profile makes it suitable as a portable designer workstation, but it rules out treating the slim chassis as an unattended hot-room render appliance.

#### RAM expandability toward 128 GB

The Precision 5690 cannot be expanded to 128 GB. Dell specifies dual-channel LPDDR5x-7467 memory in 16 GB, 32 GB and 64 GB configurations. The memory is integrated on the system board, is neither replaceable nor upgradeable, and Dell states that a memory error requires replacement of the system board.[2]

This is a material lifecycle limitation. The Precision 5690 is offered with 16 GB, 32 GB or 64 GB integrated LPDDR5x memory, and the soldered memory cannot be upgraded later. A buyer who orders 32 GB cannot later add a second module to reach 64 GB, and a buyer who orders 64 GB cannot later move to 128 GB. The machine must be bought at its final system-memory capacity.[2][3]

The fixed memory does not make 64 GB inadequate for every designer. Lumion’s ultra-complex tier itself identifies 64 GB as the relevant system-memory threshold, so a 64 GB Dell can meet that published system-memory target. The problem is resilience: it cannot accommodate a future requirement above that threshold without replacing the laptop rather than upgrading its memory.[20][2]

Dell partially offsets the fixed-RAM design with storage expansion. The Precision has two M.2 2280 SSD slots, and Dell documentation lists support up to 4 TB per slot in some configurations. This can postpone capacity-driven replacement, but it does not solve a memory-capacity constraint.[3][65]

The Dell’s memory design therefore makes 64 GB the only sensible fleet configuration, and it excludes the 5690 if the firm’s five-year plan requires 128 GB on any designers’ machines.

Dell can offer a UAE enterprise-support route, but its value depends on the exact Dubai-address response level and service term shown on the formal quote.

These are battery-management features, not a stated five-year capacity-retention commitment.[68]

Dell specifies a 0°C to 35°C operating-temperature range for the Precision 5690 and a -40°C to 65°C storage-temperature range.[7]

Dell ProSupport Plus exclusions list batteries among supply items and parts, together with support on them. Five-year Dell TCO must therefore include battery replacement separately rather than treating ProSupport Plus as battery-replacement cover.[69]

In the cited Precision 5690 test, battery-powered Time Spy Graphics was 10.1% below Performance mode (26,674 versus 29,664 points). This supports standardising AC-powered operation for live Lumion work and render export.[13]

### HP ZBook Fury G11

The ZBook Fury 16 G11 is the traditional expandable workstation in this comparison. It is substantially heavier than Dell and Lenovo, starting at 5.3 lb or about 2.4 kg, but that chassis accommodates four memory slots, up to four SSD slots, high-wattage adapters and RTX 5000 Ada graphics.[71][25][45]

#### GPU rendering performance

The Fury G11’s GPU range is directly comparable to Dell’s at the high end. HP lists RTX 1000 Ada with 6 GB VRAM, RTX 2000 Ada with 8 GB, RTX 3500 and RTX 4000 Ada with 12 GB, and RTX 5000 Ada with 16 GB. HP’s RTX 3500, 4000 and 5000 options support ECC graphics memory, while the discrete-GPU platform supports CUDA and Dynamic Boost.[1]

A UAE reseller lists RTX 2000 Ada, RTX 3500 Ada and RTX 5000 Ada Fury configurations, and another UAE listing identifies an i9-14900HX/RTX 5000 Ada version. These are useful local configuration routes, but the exact SKU must be confirmed because one Dubai retailer’s title identifies RTX 5000 Ada 16 GB while one product-detail bullet says RTX 3500 Ada 12 GB.[16][72][42]

The tested PCMag Fury G11 had a Core i9-14900HX, 64 GB RAM, 1 TB SSD and RTX 3500 Ada with 12 GB VRAM. PCMag described it as winning the Blender CPU test, trailing only a MacBook Pro in the Blender GPU test and being competitive in SPECviewperf. A different review’s RTX 3500 Ada Fury scored 14,129 in Cinebench 2024 GPU and 430.07 in OctaneBench 2020.1.5.[25][26]

These results establish that an RTX 3500 Fury is a substantial workstation configuration. However, the RTX 3500 is not the highest-performance Fury. Notebookcheck characterizes RTX 3500 Ada as around 20% to 30% slower than RTX 5000 Ada, and PCMag states that moving from RTX 3500 to RTX 5000 would likely improve the Fury’s standing in graphics tests.[38][25]

Judgment — For designers who will open Lumion only occasionally, RTX 3500 Ada offers a credible 12 GB workstation-GPU tier. For the designers responsible for client visualization, large scene assembly, animation and ray-traced deliverables, specify RTX 5000 Ada with 16 GB. This protects VRAM headroom rather than merely improving benchmark scores.

The Fury’s higher-capacity GPU option aligns with Lumion’s ultra-complex guidance: Lumion identifies 16 GB graphics memory as the VRAM requirement for ultra-complex projects, versus 10 GB for complex projects. RTX 2000 Ada has 8 GB and therefore meets the stated 4K rasterization and 1080p ray-tracing minimum but not Lumion’s 10 GB or 16 GB tiers.[1][20]

GPU power implementation remains important. Notebookcheck notes that RTX 3500 Ada Laptop GPUs can be configured across a wide 60 W to 140 W recommended TGP range, creating noticeable performance differences between laptops that share the same GPU name. HP’s published Fury G11 adapter options are 150 W, 200 W and 230 W, but the documentation does not map every adapter to every GPU configuration.[38][1]

The Fury G11 therefore offers the strongest high-end Lumion configuration of the three, provided the firm buys the RTX 5000 Ada SKU rather than assuming all Fury G11s perform alike.

#### Thermal management under sustained CAD loads and warm conditions

HP’s Fury G11 QuickSpecs state an operating range of 0°C to 35°C, identify 32°C as 89.6°F, state that system performance may be reduced above 32°C, and prohibit sustained direct exposure to sunlight. The maintenance guide gives a 5°C to 35°C range. Both documents agree on the 35°C upper operating limit; the QuickSpecs provide the more specific warning about performance reduction above 32°C.[37][36]

HP’s stated maximum operating power is below 230 W, and its specified adapters are 150 W, 200 W and 230 W. This matters because the high-end CPU and RTX GPU may demand more sustained input power and generate more heat than the compact Dell or Lenovo configurations.[1]

HP’s thermal design includes VaporForce thermals, curved metal blades and, on configurations with RTX 3000 Ada or higher graphics, an HP custom vapor chamber. HP describes the product as unthrottled in Best Performance mode while plugged in. These are product-design statements; the practical result still depends on the exact GPU, room temperature, power mode, fan condition and airflow around the machine.[39]

Independent observations show both the Fury’s capability and its heat output. A plugged-in high-performance Blender CPU test on an i7-14700HX/RTX 3500 Ada Fury reported CPU temperatures around 98°C, constant CPU power of 130 W, 46 dB fan noise, 37°C vent temperature and 36°C keyboard-centre temperature.[31]

A separate review reported that during render or export workloads, the Fury’s fans became very loud and the underside became almost scalding hot. In Resolve and Premiere tests, performed while plugged in, the reviewer again described the underside as blazing hot. These observations are not AutoCAD, Revit or Lumion measurements, but they indicate that the Fury actively uses its thermal envelope under sustained professional GPU workloads.[26]

Notebookcheck also says the Core i9-14900HX improvement over its predecessor is around 10% to 15%, with throttling reducing overall performance. That does not mean the Fury is unsuitable; it means the purchaser should value sustained behavior, not peak Turbo figures.[38]

Judgment — The Fury should be treated as a desk workstation that can travel, not as a lap device under render load. The large chassis and cooling system support high sustained performance, but full-speed rendering in a hot site office or unconditioned meeting room is a poor deployment assumption.

The Fury’s maintenance guidance reinforces the practical controls. HP instructs technicians to check for dust or obstructions in heat-sink fans, fins and vents, and to ensure the notebook is not sitting on a hot surface that blocks its intake vents.[45]

For Dubai deployment, the firm should set the same thermal operating rule used for Dell: air-conditioned workspace, hard desk, clear vents, accessible power brick, and planned dust inspection. HP’s explicit performance-reduction statement above 32°C makes that rule especially important.[1][45]

The Fury G11 has the largest thermal and power envelope of the three, but it should be bought for sustained plugged-in desk work rather than assumed to retain peak performance in a warm, poorly ventilated site environment.

#### RAM expandability toward 128 GB

The Fury G11 is the only laptop in this comparison with a documented 128 GB configuration. HP lists four SODIMM slots, supports both ECC and non-ECC DDR5 memory, and specifies 128 GB as four 32 GB DDR5-5600 SODIMMs.[1][39]

The memory can be physically serviced. The Fury provides tool-free entry to the internals through its bottom-panel latch, followed by removal of a metal plate for memory and storage access. HP’s maintenance procedure requires removal of the service cover and disconnection of the battery cable before memory-module removal. The memory thermal pads may be reused if undamaged, but need careful handling during upgrade work.[73][36]

A Fury G11 configured with 64 GB as two 32 GB SODIMMs can leave two of its four slots unoccupied, where the selected SKU permits that configuration.[1][36][126]

A 128 GB Fury has a performance nuance. HP documentation says four-slot architectures operate up to 4,000 MT/s, and Notebookcheck reports that the maximum memory speed is capped at 4,000 MT/s when configured with 128 GB because of SODIMM limitations. That is lower than the DDR5-5600 label associated with lower-capacity memory configurations.[1][38][39]

HP warns that third-party memory may not follow standard characteristics and that mixed-speed modules run at the speed of the slowest installed module.[1]

This is not a reason to reject 128 GB when the workload needs it. Capacity prevents disk paging and allows larger project data, linked models and applications to remain in active memory; that typically matters more in Revit-heavy work than a memory-frequency difference. The right rule is to buy 64 GB for most designers and 128 GB only for named power users whose current or planned projects justify it.

The 128 GB configuration fills all four slots. It creates maximum capacity, not a future slot-by-slot path beyond 128 GB. HP Store UAE lists 128 GB standard memory as 4 × 32 GB DDR5-5600 SODIMMs and identifies 128 GB as the maximum across four DIMM slots.[42]

The Fury’s modular memory and 128 GB ceiling make it the only choice that keeps a five-year 128 GB Revit contingency open.

### Lenovo ThinkPad P1 Gen 7

The ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 is the most portable of the three. It starts at 1.82 kg or 4.03 lb, compared with Dell’s 4.46 lb and HP’s 5.3 lb. Its design combines two M.2 SSD slots, a removable CAMM2 memory module and a 90 Wh battery in a slim 16-inch chassis.[10][25][5]

#### GPU rendering performance

The P1 Gen 7 must also be configured with a discrete GPU for architectural visualization. Lenovo’s official options include RTX 1000 Ada with 6 GB, RTX 2000 Ada and RTX 3000 Ada with 8 GB, and GeForce RTX 4060 and RTX 4070 with 8 GB. Integrated Intel Arc graphics is available but should not be specified for Lumion-centric users.[5][17]

The difference is visible in testing. An integrated-GPU P1 Gen 7 review unit achieved 23.7 fps in the SPECviewperf 3dsmax07 modelling test. The reviewer stated that RTX 4070 or RTX 2000 Ada versions would be expected to exceed 90 fps in that workload. This is a reviewer expectation, not a published head-to-head measurement, but it correctly identifies dedicated graphics as the starting point for demanding 3D work.[6]

A P1 Gen 7 with Core Ultra 7 165H, RTX 1000 Ada, 32 GB RAM and 1 TB SSD scored 19,607 in 3DMark Fire Strike and 8,505 in 3DMark Time Spy. The scores describe that RTX 1000 Ada configuration, not RTX 2000, RTX 3000, RTX 4060 or RTX 4070 versions.[28]

StorageReview tested a P1 Gen 7 with RTX 1000 Ada and found it competitive against a Dell Precision 5490 with RTX 3000 Ada in some SPECviewperf 2020 viewsets; it attributed the ThinkPad’s overall advantage to its higher-wattage GPU. Its OctaneBench results were close but showed the Dell ahead in each cited kernel. These results show that chassis implementation can offset nominal GPU tier; they do not compare the P1 Gen 7 with the requested Dell 5690 or Fury G11.[10]

Judgment — For this practice, the RTX 1000 Ada is a minimum workstation-GPU configuration rather than a preferred Lumion specification. The better P1 choices are RTX 2000 Ada, RTX 3000 Ada, RTX 4060 or RTX 4070, selected according to driver-policy needs and price. Even then, their 8 GB VRAM ceiling is below the 10 GB and 16 GB levels Lumion identifies for complex and ultra-complex tiers.[5][20]

StorageReview states that the ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 offers professional NVIDIA RTX graphics from RTX 1000 Ada through RTX 3000 Ada and consumer GeForce RTX 4060 and RTX 4070 options. Its GeForce RTX options can save money where specialized driver support is not required, while its RTX Ada options are workstation-oriented. The firm should decide whether its software-certification and support policy requires professional RTX Ada drivers before choosing between the two.[10]

Lenovo’s P1 Gen 7 has Autodesk among its stated ISV certifications. That is relevant for AutoCAD and Revit compatibility planning, although the certification list does not identify product versions or establish Lumion performance.[10]

The P1 Gen 7 is a strong portable CAD and Revit workstation when fitted with a discrete GPU, but its 8 GB VRAM ceiling makes it less appropriate than RTX 5000-equipped Dell or HP models for the firm’s heaviest Lumion work.

#### Thermal management under sustained CAD loads and warm conditions

Lenovo specifies a 5°C to 35°C operating temperature range for the P1 Gen 7. Its Intelligent Cooling controls power consumption, fan speed, machine temperature and performance; Best Performance mode intentionally accepts higher fan noise and temperature in exchange for maximum performance.[5][77]

The strongest published sustained-load evidence among the three comes from IT Pro’s six-hour FurMark plus Prime95 test. The P1 Gen 7 ran at 100% CPU and GPU utilization until the reviewer stopped the test after six hours, without reported throttling. The reviewer described the fans as audible but not intrusive and the exterior as no more than warm.[6]

A different reviewer tested a P1 Gen 7 at 19°C room temperature and measured a maximum keyboard-area temperature of 43°C, fan noise of 49 dB, average internal core temperature of 93°C and maximum bottom-cover temperature of 35°C at 100% load. That reviewer also observed four performance cores at approximately 100°C during full load without being throttled down.[35]

These observations are not contradictory. They use different test methods, sensor locations and wording: one reports utilization and observed throttling behavior over six hours, the other reports temperatures and fan noise under a 100% system-resource test. Together, they show a compact workstation willing to use high internal temperatures to sustain performance while keeping external surfaces relatively controlled.[6][35]

The cooling assembly uses twin fans, large intakes and shared CPU/GPU heat pipes. Lenovo’s maintenance manual makes clear that fan servicing requires correct thermal-grease replacement: it specifies 0.2 g of new thermal grease in marked areas and warns that too much or too little can create a thermal problem.[10][40]

For ordinary work, Laptop Mag measured 84.6°F between the G and H keys, 75.9°F on the touchpad and 88°F at the underside centre during a video-streaming test. The reviewer explicitly noted that temperatures could differ during large exports or bulk rendering, so this is a comfort figure rather than a rendering-thermal result.[28]

Judgment — The P1’s reported six-hour utilisation and throttling observation supports sustained mixed CPU/GPU work in that review. Its thermal result cannot be ranked against Dell or HP because the reported configurations and test conditions differ. Air-conditioned desks, clear airflow and periodic dust maintenance remain the operating requirement for all three models.[13][6][31][35]

The P1’s operating limit and cooling controls make controlled indoor deployment essential, but its six-hour stress behavior supports using it for sustained design work rather than only short bursts.

#### RAM expandability toward 128 GB

The ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 cannot reach 128 GB. Lenovo specifies one LPCAMM2 connector, one upgradable memory module and a maximum of 64 GB as a single 64 GB LPCAMM2 module.[5][17]

Lenovo specifies a memory speed of up to 7,467 MT/s for the P1 Gen 7’s single LPCAMM2 module, subject to platform limits.[5]

This is materially better than soldered memory in one respect: the module is removable. Lenovo’s hardware maintenance manual describes access through the base cover and CAMM2 top cover, and the user guide classifies the CAMM2 memory module as an optional-service CRU that can be replaced by a suitably skilled customer or technician.[40][77]

However, CAMM2 is not the same as SODIMM expansion. The P1 has no SODIMM slots; upgrading means replacing the entire module. IT Pro notes that LPCAMM2 is newer and more expensive than adding SODIMMs.[6]

Micron states that LPCAMM2 supplies the CPU’s full 128-bit memory bus through one module; the referenced module capacities are 32 GB and 64 GB.[127]

A memory replacement can also trigger memory retraining during POST, leaving the screen blank for several minutes. Lenovo instructs users not to interrupt that process, and warns that incorrectly tightened CAMM2-cover screws can cause boot failure.[40]

Judgment — The P1’s replaceable CAMM2 is a serviceability advantage over Dell’s soldered memory, but it is not a capacity advantage over HP. It makes a 32 GB-to-64 GB refresh possible; it does not create a 128 GB path.

The P1’s maximum 64 GB capacity excludes it wherever 128 GB is a firm five-year requirement, despite its memory being replaceable.

### Procurement direction before price negotiation

1. **Rank HP ZBook Fury 16 G11 first only for named users who require both 128 GB RAM and 16 GB VRAM.** It alone combines a documented 128 GB system-memory configuration with RTX 5000 Ada and 16 GB VRAM; Dell Precision 5690 and ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 are ineligible for that complete requirement because each stops at 64 GB RAM. [2][1][5]

3. **Reconsider Lenovo ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 where carrying weight matters most and 8 GB VRAM and 64 GB RAM suffice.** It starts at 1.82 kg, supports up to 64 GB through one LPCAMM2 module, and its discrete-GPU options stop at 8 GB VRAM. 4. **Require every UAE quote to state the exact SKU, GPU, VRAM, CPU, installed memory, adapter wattage, display, warranty term, battery warranty, onsite response level and accidental-damage entitlement.** Retail listings contain conflicting GPU and warranty descriptions, so the formal quotation must govern. [42][8]

5. **Deploy all three only as air-conditioned, plugged-in desk workstations during sustained rendering.** Each has a 35°C upper operating limit, while Dell and HP have explicit evidence of high CPU temperatures under stress and HP identifies performance reduction above 32°C. [13][1][5]

6. **Use one AED five-year TCO model for each quoted configuration.** For each model: `8 × (delivered device/configuration + per-device upgrades + per-device five-year service + expected battery parts and authorised-service labour + metered five-year energy + expected repair and downtime cost) + shared deployment + shared backup and spare cost − year-five fleet residual value`. Calculate metered energy as `average measured power in kW × annual powered hours × 5 × AED/kWh`; calculate expected downtime as `expected incidents × lost designer-hours per incident × AED per designer-hour`. Enter delivered AED quote, warranty and Care Pack price, battery part and labour price, annual hours, AED/kWh, expected incidents, lost hours, designer-hour cost, deployment cost, spare cost and residual value as explicit inputs. Treat recoverable VAT as a cash-flow item rather than a firm cost; leave unavailable inputs blank. ## What factors affect 5-year TCO

Five-year total cost of ownership (TCO) is the cost of keeping eight productive design workstations in service, not simply the invoice total for eight laptops. For a Dubai architecture practice, the largest avoidable costs are buying a configuration that becomes inadequate before year five, carrying insufficient UAE repair coverage, and treating a consumable battery as though it were protected for the full laptop-warranty period.

Judgment — Run one AED worksheet for each quoted configuration, with the five-year period beginning on that configuration’s actual purchase date. Economic five-year TCO = device and approved upgrades; plus five-year support; deployment and image management; expected battery replacements; metered electricity; repairs and accidental-damage contingency; designer downtime and recovery labour; shared spare-device and upkeep costs; minus fleet residual value. Keep recoverable VAT in a separate cash-flow comparison rather than adding it to economic TCO. The selected configuration determines the acquisition, upgrade and battery lines: the Precision 5690 is capped at 64GB integrated memory; the Fury G11 supports 128GB through four SODIMMs; and the P1 Gen 7 supports up to 64GB in one upgradable LPCAMM2 module.[2][1][5] AutoCAD, Revit and Lumion make GPU, RAM, storage and cooling decisions part of TCO because an under-specified workstation can force replacement before its nominal five-year accounting life.

Dell caps the Precision 5690 at 64GB non-upgradeable memory; HP says the Fury G11 can be upgraded to possibly extend useful life by several years; and HP recommends high-performance workstation headroom for purchases intended for four to six years of use.[2][1][52] Dell’s Precision 5690 ranges from a cited US base model with Core Ultra 5 135H, 16GB memory, 256GB SSD, Intel Arc graphics and a 1,920 × 1,200 display, starting at US$2,290, to a cited fully configured US$9,230 example with 64GB RAM, up to 8TB across two SSDs and SmartCard reader.

### battery lifecycle

A workstation battery is a consumable component: capacity declines with age, temperature, charging pattern, power draw and cycle use. Battery runtime tests are useful for understanding mobility, but they do not predict five-year battery life under AutoCAD, Revit or Lumion loads.

Dell offers the Precision 5690 with two 6-cell 99.5Wh lithium-ion battery options, one identified as a Long Calendar Life or Long Cycle Life battery. A separate review describes a 66Wh base battery and optional 99.5Wh unit, while another reviewed configuration is described with a 100Wh battery.[43][66][15]

Dell states that battery operating time varies with operating conditions and can reduce significantly under power-intensive conditions. A Precision 5690 review measured 9 hours 9 minutes in its WLAN web-surfing test and 9.2 hours in its WiFi v1.3 table, using a tested unit with a 98Wh battery. PCMag measured 17 hours 11 minutes playing a locally stored 720p video with brightness at 50%, volume at 100%, Wi-Fi and keyboard backlighting off after a full charge; another review measured 10 hours 54 minutes in PCMark 10 Modern Office.[43][13][64][15]

The Precision 5690’s 10-hour-54-minute PCMark 10 Modern Office result was measured at approximately 200 nits of display brightness.[15]

Those results are not comparable estimates of rendering autonomy because they use different tests and workloads. A Precision 5690 review also measured 98.8W average load consumption, 174.8W maximum load consumption and 144.5W in its Witcher 3 Ultra test; these are potential electricity-model inputs, not CAD or Lumion energy-use figures.[13][64][15]

Judgment — Electricity should be modelled from actual plugged-in working hours and measured wall power for the chosen configuration, not from video-playback runtime. For an architecture office, GPU rendering and sustained Revit activity will be materially more demanding than idle, web and local-video tests.

A seller lists an L23C4P70/L23D4P70-compatible replacement battery for ThinkPad P1 Gen 7 at US$61.93, rated 15.6V, 5655mAh and 88.22Wh. This gives a separately purchasable battery line for the TCO worksheet alongside authorised-service labour, VAT, shipping and disposal.[115]

Amazon.com’s HP ZBook Fury 16 G11 search page offers battery-life filters labelled 8 to 8.9 h and 9 h & above.[103]

For the Precision 5690 battery, Dell specifies 0°C to 50°C for charging, 0°C to 60°C for discharging and -20°C to 65°C for storage. These are battery temperature ranges, distinct from the laptop’s ambient operating-temperature limits.[43]

Lenovo states that the Vantage app’s automatic battery diagnostic test determines whether the built-in battery is defective and that the battery should not be replaced unless the test identifies a defect, except where it is physically damaged or a safety issue is reported.Lenovo requires a Lenovo-specified battery and a Lenovo-authorised repair facility or technician for built-in-battery replacement; a battery that is not Lenovo-specified, or is disassembled or modified, is not covered by warranty.[46]

Dell lists maximum display-panel power consumption of 6.32W for the 16-inch FHD+ display and 11.14W for the 16-inch UHD+ display. Display selection is therefore a configuration input to the battery and electricity lines of the five-year TCO model.[68]

Do not leave any of these laptops in parked cars or other non-air-conditioned spaces in Dubai. High battery temperature combined with a high state of charge accelerates lithium-ion ageing; desk-based systems should use their supported charge-limit or battery-health setting.

**The five-year TCO pattern is clear: configuration, UAE support term and battery entitlement matter more than an advertised starting price.** The Precision 5690 requires correct memory specification at purchase because its 64GB maximum is integrated; the Fury G11 offers upgrade-led longevity but needs a named Care Pack and battery-service choice; and the withdrawn P1 Gen 7 requires a verified UAE machine type, remaining stock and purchasable service term. Dell can align ProSupport Plus or Flex with five years, HP Care Pack terms must be quoted for Dubai, and Lenovo support and sealed-battery terms vary by purchased service and location. None of the three standard battery arrangements should be treated as automatic five-year coverage.[2][1][18][50][86][70]

1. Buy a named-designer fleet mix whose quoted configuration counts total eight, with serial-number or SKU-level UAE warranty terms and written Dubai onsite-service coverage. Record VAT-inclusive cash price and recoverable VAT separately from economic TCO. [2][1][5]

2. Price five-year support from each actual purchase date for every configuration in the fleet mix, then compare the premium against a documented shared-spare and downtime plan. Dell offers five-year ProSupport Plus and ProSupport Flex terms at original sale; P1 Gen 7 listed service options include three-year onsite with one-year battery cover and, for one configuration, four-year onsite and four-year Sealed Battery cover. [50][80]

3. Treat batteries as a separate lifecycle line: specify Long Cycle Life where offered, purchase a qualifying battery extension where it fits the service term, and reserve a year-four-and-five replacement contingency.

4. Standardise charging policy, battery-health monitoring, backup and replacement-device procedures across all eight designers before deployment.

5. Include an acceptance test and a service-administration process: record the exact machine type, serial number, warranty SKU, service address, BIOS and driver baseline; maintain Lenovo ID purchase history for warranty validation or insurance claims; and use Lenovo Warranty Lookup to check coverage. [116][117]

Include periodic fan cleaning in the repair and upkeep allowance, particularly where dust exposure is material. Restricted airflow can increase fan noise, reduce sustained performance and raise the risk that a workstation is reassigned or replaced before year five.

For electricity, distinguish operating state and display configuration. HP reports 4.86W at short idle, 1.41W at long idle and sleep, and 0.39W when off for a typically configured notebook at 230VAC/50Hz; Dell lists maximum panel consumption of 6.32W for the 16-inch FHD+ display and 11.14W for the 16-inch UHD+ display.[1][37][68]

The exact Dubai onsite response commitment, battery coverage, accidental-damage inclusion, and five-year price remain open for each chosen SKU. A UAE reseller or manufacturer quote tied to the serialised configuration and Dubai service address would settle the entitlement and allow a five-year fleet TCO comparison. The expected proportion of designers who will need 16 GB VRAM or 128 GB RAM is also a deployment decision: if only visualization specialists need that headroom, the firm can standardise most seats at a lower specification and reserve Fury RTX 5000 Ada/128 GB systems for those specialists.
