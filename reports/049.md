For 12+-hour vaccine journeys without external electricity, shortlist self-powered diesel Thermo King T-Series and diesel Carrier Supra configurations, but award neither until it passes a loaded route-specific thermal validation at the required vaccine set point.

Thermo King documents cellular and satellite-capable TracKing configurations, whereas Carrier Lynx Fleet documents real-time monitoring and control but its cellular, satellite or dual-mode bearer must be specified contractually.[1][2] Do not credit either supplier with maintenance coverage in Accra, Lagos, Dakar or Abidjan without a named authorized workshop, mobile-response SLA, after-hours recovery arrangement and locally held critical spares.[3][4][5][6] Select on total cost per dose with documented in-range delivery, not unit price or nominal cooling capacity, and treat WHO-related equipment certification separately from proof that the transport configuration maintained the required conditions for the particular prequalified vaccine.

A direct-drive or battery/off-grid option can only displace the diesel shortlist after it demonstrates 12+ hour runtime under the route’s ambient temperature, door-opening and payload profile.

## Evaluate temperature-controlled transport offerings

Temperature-controlled transport must be assessed as a complete operating configuration: refrigeration unit, insulated body or container, power source, monitoring, loading discipline, maintenance response and the temperature profile required by the specific vaccine. A diesel reefer, a passive box and a battery-backed vehicle solve different failure modes; none is interchangeable merely because each can refrigerate cargo.

**For the selected vaccine, procure the configuration that documents the labelled temperature condition through the actual 12+-hour route, retains the specified communications path when cellular coverage drops, and has contracted service and spares in each operating city.**[1][7][8]

Thermo King’s older North American direct-drive catalogue identifies a different product roster: B-100, e200, V-520 nose-mount and rooftop units, V-800 and V-220/V-320 systems.[11] This is not a single contemporaneous catalogue with the current V/VX line-up.

#### Reliability during 12+ hour journeys with no electricity access

The T-Series is Thermo King’s strongest road configuration for a journey with no external electricity because the refrigeration unit carries its own diesel engine. Thermo King describes the T-Series as self-powered truck refrigeration, while current T-90 and UT-1290 products are likewise described as carrying their own diesel engines.[13][14] The T-590, T-690 and T-890 use a three-cylinder TK374F diesel engine; the T-1090 and UT-1290 use the larger TK380F diesel engine. In both cases, belt drive transfers engine power to the compressor, fans and alternator.[15]

Thermo King publishes engine-powered cooling capacity rather than a no-electricity mission duration. Its 2020 T-Series brochure states capacities ranging from 6,200 W to 12,500 W at 0°C and from 3,400 W to 6,800 W at -20°C. At 30°C ambient under ATP conditions, a T-1200R is listed at 12,500 W at 0°C return air and 6,800 W at -20°C return air.[16][17] The earlier T-Series data state engine-powered system capacities across the line from 6,000 W to 11,500 W at 0°C and 3,800 W to 6,900 W at -20°C, also at 30°C ambient under ATP conditions.[13]

The candidate’s Thermo King truck reference gives T-Series set points of −30°C to +20°C and published cooling capacity of 15,500–39,000 BTU/hr at 2°C.[12][166]

Thermo King indicates the T-590 for 14–24 ft insulated bodies at a 2°C setting and the T-890 for 26–28 ft bodies. Its stated engine-cooling ratings are 15,500 BTU/hr for the T-590, 25,000 BTU/hr for the T-890 and up to 39,000 BTU/hr for the UT-1280 under specified conditions including 100°F ambient.[166][12][166]

Thermo King reports T-Series field trials and thousands of running hours in conditions up to 55°C. It also says its development programme included non-stop endurance tests, accelerated-life tests and exposure to extreme operating environments.[16][13][17] These facts support selecting the T-Series for hot-climate active refrigeration, especially where a self-contained diesel energy source is necessary.

The V/VX range can refrigerate during a long road journey without grid connection while the vehicle engine runs, because the refrigeration compressor is vehicle-engine-driven.[10][18] Thermo King also says the VX-Series can be configured for diesel, non-diesel and battery-pack applications and for alternative power sources when the engine is off.[10][18] The stated battery-pack suitability has no published duration in the supplied material.

The V/VX selection guidance uses a shorter operating assumption: precooled goods, a stated body insulation value and an 8-hour distribution period. Thermo King expressly says these recommendations are not a performance guarantee.[19] The more recent guide also bases vehicle-volume selection on road operation at 2,400 rpm, 30°C or 40°C ambient temperatures, specified insulation coefficients and limited door openings.[10]

**For a 12+-hour road mission with no mains supply, the self-powered diesel T-Series is the selected Thermo King truck configuration because it has an independent diesel engine; V/VX is the selected vehicle-powered comparator because its compressor is driven by the vehicle engine.**[9][10][14]

Reliability still depends on fuel, preventive maintenance, body heat leakage, door discipline and payload management. Thermo King advises pre-cooling goods, maintaining airflow, limiting door openings, using low-K-value insulated bodies and repairing door seals and body damage promptly.[20] It also advises that reefers be fueled and alarm-free before departure.[21]

Judgment — For procurement purposes, define the T-Series acceptance test as a fully loaded, pre-cooled vaccine-body test over at least 12 hours at the route’s upper ambient temperature, with the expected door-opening pattern, fuel reserve, set point and alarm-response procedure. Cooling capacity and high-ambient claims show that the system can remove heat; they do not substitute for a route-specific thermal validation.

Thermo King states that TracKing can update unit and logger sensor data up to every five minutes and compare air temperature with the set point.[22] It also provides real-time out-of-range temperature alarms.[22]

The broader TracKing documentation states that TracKing is available in cellular or satellite versions.[1] It lists satellite start/stop as a remote-control function, while other functions include changing set point, initiating defrost, initiating pre-trip and clearing alarms.

Thermo King makes feature availability dependent on controller, connection type and communication contract.[1] TracKing can also download EN12830 logger data over the air for historical temperature records.[22]

For current V/VX installations, Thermo King offers BlueBox Lite. BlueBox Lite is a 4G-connected telematics device that supplies temperature, position and machine data in near-real time through TracKing.[10][18] Once activated, it provides position, alarms, temperature and door-opening information and permits remote reset of the set point.[18] Thermo King’s current V/VX brochure limits BlueBox Lite to V- and VX-Series units, while its TracKing site also lists B-Series units.[10][23]

The T-Series documentation presents TracKing as an option for tracking and load monitoring.[13][17] It also says TracKing can monitor sensitive loads through the web and is compatible with Thermo King dataloggers and the TSR-3 controller.[13][17] The T-Series’ ServiceWatch logger records operational parameters for diagnosis, but it is an onboard preventive-maintenance recorder rather than the same thing as remote live monitoring.[24]

Thermo King has a separate cellular-plus-satellite offering for refrigerated and heated ISO containers. Klinge’s monitoring system uses GSM cellular communication and includes satellite connectivity when cellular networks are unavailable.[25] Its monitoring unit can receive charge from the refrigeration system when shore or ship power is connected and has a solar panel for additional monitoring-unit charging when the reefer is not running.[25] This is relevant to container operations, not evidence that a road T-Series truck has the same satellite configuration.

ORBCOMM PT 6000 is a third-party dual-mode monitoring equipment example for refrigerated transport.[167]

For routes with uncertain terrestrial coverage, TracKing in a satellite-capable configuration is Thermo King’s documented remote-monitoring route; BlueBox Lite is a 4G cellular configuration. #### Cost per vaccine dose delivered while maintaining WHO requirements

Thermo King’s documents offer useful cost drivers but not a cost per compliant delivered dose. For the V-1000, Thermo King states fuel consumption up to 54% lower than an equivalent self-powered system and maintenance costs, including parts and labour, up to 33% lower because the unit has no diesel engine.[10][18] These are relative operating-cost claims, not a price, route cost, throughput or compliant-dose denominator.

Thermo King gives a fuel-use illustration for trailer refrigeration: a unit operating 2,000 hours per year for seven years can consume more than 35,000 litres of fuel, and a 10% reduction can materially affect cost.[20] This is a useful sensitivity driver for diesel assets but not a dose-level result for a named Thermo King model.

One third-party listing displays a T-Series price of US$5,000 and separately says prices start from US$3,500.[34] Neither figure identifies the model, body, options, currency-date basis, delivery terms, freight, installation or support scope. It cannot be used as the capital cost of a comparable West African vaccine-truck configuration.

Judgment — Calculate Thermo King cost per compliant dose as: annualized unit, installation, body-validation and lifetime-spares cost; plus annual fuel, preventive maintenance, repairs, tyres and vehicle allocation, driver and per diem, telematics, insurance, security and route costs; plus the procurement value of doses lost or quarantined after an excursion; divided by doses delivered with documented in-range temperatures. Use the same route, payload, utilization, vaccine presentation, door-opening profile and annual trip schedule for every option.

The choice between T-Series and V/VX changes the cost structure. A T-Series has a separate diesel reefer engine and is suited to independent refrigeration on a no-grid journey. A V/VX system can reduce reefer fuel and maintenance but requires vehicle-engine operation or a separately specified battery/alternative-power configuration.[10][18] The low-cost choice therefore changes with stop duration, annual hours, vehicle idling policy, fuel price, battery replacement cost and the risk cost of a temperature excursion.

### innovative off-grid alternatives

Innovative off-grid alternatives fall into four distinct groups: passive vaccine boxes and carriers; active battery-backed transport vehicles; ultra-cold passive systems using PCM or dry ice; and stationary solar direct-drive refrigerators and freezers. The first three can support transport operations. Solar direct-drive equipment is principally fixed cold-chain infrastructure and pack-freezing support.

Powered portable vaccine equipment is assessed under WHO PQS E003/TS01.1, including a test of whether the entire load stays at +2°C to +8°C at +43°C ambient under the applicable cycling conditions.[174]

B Medical Systems identifies vaccine transport boxes RCW1 Freeze Free, RCW2, RCW4, RCW8, RCW12, RCW25 and RCW25 FF Freeze Free.[100] It lists gross volumes of 6.5 L, 1.9 L, 8 L, 20 L, 24 L, 44 L and 44 L respectively.[100] Its passive-range brochure describes RCW1, RCW2, RCW4, RCW8, RCW12 and RCW25 as six passive transport systems intended for difficult climatic conditions and transport periods from 24 hours to 8 days.[37] The seven-model transport-box page additionally includes RCW25 FF.[100]

The B Medical Systems RVTV is a refrigerated vaccine transport vehicle. It maintains +2°C to +8°C during transport, has two integrated rechargeable batteries giving stated 16-hour autonomy and can also use vehicle DC or external AC power.[38][101] B Medical states capacity of up to 396 L of vaccine storage and says the RVTV is designed for difficult and narrow road networks, including rough terrain, steep slopes, water-covered terrain and unpaved roads.[101][38]

For ultra-cold missions, WHO guidance identifies the Arktek YBC-5 as a modified passive vaccine-storage device using PCM instead of ice packs. It is described as capable of holding Ebola vaccines at -80°C without power for up to six days.[8] A related WHO guidance passage describes Arktek YBC-5 as keeping vaccines at -80°C to -60°C for up to five days without powered refrigeration or extra coolant, using insulation and eight 1 L PCM packs.[8] These are Ebola/ULT-specific operating descriptions; they should not be transferred to routine +2°C to +8°C vaccines without a product- and pack-out-specific validation.

Savsu Nano-Q is another named passive-cooling approach. A cited source describes it as a long-term vaccine-storage cooler without electricity, using regular ice and giving up to seven days of refrigeration at 32°C ambient before ice replacement.[102] Other passive cold boxes are described as having cold life from three to five days.[102] The cited use is storage-oriented, not a West African vehicle-route validation.

The cited Secop mobile-cooling alternative targets ultra-low-temperature products and reduced dependence on dry ice, a different temperature class from routine +2°C to +8°C vaccine transport.[175]

Gricd is a Lagos-based cold-chain monitoring and logistics company. It offers a cold box for modular transport over existing infrastructure and remote monitoring for pharmaceuticals and other temperature-sensitive goods.[53] Gricd MOTE is monitoring equipment, not refrigeration equipment.[103][104]

Vaccibox is described as suitable for a motorbike, bicycle or boat pilot; its IoT system records temperature, battery condition, location and stock.[176]

The cited vaccine-drone examples concern a delivery service rather than a refrigeration appliance with 12-hour transport endurance.[177][178]

B Medical also offers Solar Direct Drive, or SDD, vaccine refrigerators and freezers. Its SDD range works directly from solar panels without batteries or regulators.[105][37] The named models include Ultra 16 SDD, TCW 15/15R SDD, TCW 40/40R SDD, TFW 40 SDD, TCW 80 SDD, TCW 120 SDD, TCW 2000/2043 SDD, TCW 3000/3043 SDD and TCW 4000 SDD. These products are relevant to hub storage, ice-pack preparation and resilience at facilities without reliable grid supply.[106][107]

The cited Haier equipment is classified as stationary E003 off-grid storage rather than mobile E002 refrigerated-vehicle or E004 passive-transport equipment.[179][7][180]

#### Reliability during 12+ hour journeys with no electricity access

The RCW25 is the most directly evidenced routine-vaccine passive option. WHO lists RCW25 as a cold box made by B Medical Systems Sarl, with 20 L vaccine storage capacity, 24 water packs and prequalification valid until 31 May 2027.[51] B Medical’s RCW25 page gives 20 L vaccine storage capacity and 44 L gross volume.[50]

The most decision-useful RCW25 thermal figures distinguish the actual target range from a looser temperature endpoint. At +43°C ambient with a 20 L load and +5°C PCM packs, RCW25 maintains +2°C to +8°C for 10 hours with 14 PCM elements, 24.3 hours with 18 elements and 40.7 hours with 24 elements.[37] The same source gives +20°C performance of 88.3, 108.4 and 160.2 hours for 14, 18 and 24 elements, respectively.[37]

B Medical also reports RCW cold life at +43°C to a different endpoint: up to +10°C. It gives 32.1 hours for RCW1, 30.3 hours for RCW4, 57.9 hours for RCW8, 114.9 hours for RCW12, 134.6 hours for RCW25 and 110 hours 37 minutes for RCW25 FF. The RCW25’s WHO listing gives 134 hours 36 minutes of cold life at +43°C. These figures are not interchangeable with +2°C to +8°C performance because they use a warmer endpoint.[38][51]

The RCW25 can also use dry ice. B Medical specifies a -80°C set point using dry ice and cold life of 134 hours 36 minutes at +43°C up to 10°C. That endpoint and configuration are not a routine +2°C to +8°C vaccine profile.[50]

A Senegal passive-cooling field intervention provides operational evidence. PATH reports that the RCW25 had cold life of about 4.5 days in moving-warehouse transport to remote Podor and Pété districts, 325 km from the regional pharmaceutical store.[108] During three months from 1 September to 30 November 2012, RCW25 and Bigbox containers each maintained safe temperatures for 84% to 89% of monitored time. Temperatures below 2°C occurred in both containers and were associated with user error early in the intervention.[108]

**For a +2°C to +8°C journey longer than 12 hours without electricity, specify RCW25 with 18 +5°C PCM elements for the 24.3-hour case or 24 elements for the 40.7-hour longer-delay case at +43°C; the 14-element configuration provides 10 hours.**[37]

Passive transport has an upstream energy requirement. Cold boxes require correctly conditioned coolant packs. WHO guidance for frozen saltwater packs requires a freezer at or below -25°C and power availability for at least 90% of the preceding 48 hours, as well as continuous operation for at least eight hours immediately before pack removal.[109] Passive transport removes in-journey electricity dependence; it does not remove the need for reliable preparation infrastructure.

For frozen vaccine transport at -25°C to -15°C, WHO guidance says holdover may not exceed 10% of the cold box’s rated cold life; a 140-hour rated cold life therefore gives a planning assumption of no more than 14 hours.[109] It also states that this use should not include multiday travel or multiday storage.[109] A laboratory result for a long-range box rated at 153 hours for +2°C to +8°C found 15–20 hours at -25°C to -15°C at constant +43°C with saturated saltwater packs.[109]

The RVTV provides active refrigeration rather than passive holdover. This makes it a direct alternative for last-mile or district-level active transport where no en-route external electricity is available.

Ghana has used Zipline drones for routine and emergency vaccine delivery; the cited drones have a range of 100–150 km and carry 1.5–2 kg rather than offering 12-hour flight endurance.[177][178]

Judgment — The RVTV is operationally attractive where route volume exceeds cold-box capacity or frequent access is needed during distribution. The RCW25 is simpler and less mechanically dependent for sealed consignments. The decision turns on daily dose volume, stop frequency, access requirements, recharge availability and the cost of recovering from a vehicle or battery failure.

Ultra-cold passive systems are distinct. Arktek YBC-5 supports ULT vaccine transport/storage without powered refrigeration for five to six days in the cited Ebola-vaccine applications.[8] Its US$5,000 initial investment cost is separately reported.[8] For ordinary EPI vaccines, do not select an ULT passive device merely because it offers long holdover; use the equipment and pack-out designed for the vaccine’s labelled temperature.

Secop describes a battery-driven mobile ultra-low-temperature cascade system powered by 12–24 V DC and controlling temperatures from −20°C to −86°C for tropical last-mile use.[175]

B Medical’s SDD refrigerator specifications show long autonomy at +2°C to +8°C: 85 hours 24 minutes for TCW 2000 SDD, 73 hours 54 minutes for TCW 2043 SDD, 86 hours 56 minutes for TCW 3000 SDD, 116 hours 41 minutes for TCW 3043 SDD and 91 hours 16 minutes for TCW 4000 SDD. These are refrigeration-appliance autonomy figures, not vehicle-transport trials.[107]

The WHO-prequalified Haier HTCD 90 solar-direct-drive refrigerator/freezer has Grade A freeze protection, a 137-hour holdover, 114.9-hour refrigerator autonomy and stated performance at 43°C. These are stationary-equipment specifications, not a qualified moving-truck transport result.[179][180]

### WHO Prequalification

WHO PQS is category-specific equipment qualification. Refrigerated vehicles are evaluated under E002/RV01.3 and its E002/RV01-VP.3 type-examination protocol; passive cold boxes and vaccine carriers fall under E004, coolant packs and phase-change containers under E005, and temperature-monitoring, remote-monitoring and equipment-monitoring devices under E006.[59][7][122] WHO PQS accepts refrigerated-vehicle applications meeting E002 criteria for prequalification evaluation.[60][114] For the passive RCW25 cold box, WHO identifies applicable PQS specification E004/CB01.1 and verification protocol E004/CB01-VP.1.[123][51] WHO lists RCW25 as prequalified, accepted on 22 March 2010 and valid until 31 May 2027.[123]

WHO IMD-PQS also has an E007 category for cold-chain accessories.[184][185]

The Haier HTCD 90 SDD’s E003-074e listing is valid through 31 May 2027; that E003 listing is not E002 refrigerated-vehicle or E004 passive-container qualification.[179]

WHO’s E006 specifications cover cold-chain monitors, freeze indicators, threshold indicators, vaccine-vial monitors, combined VVM/threshold indicators, chemical freeze indicators, programmable remote temperature and event monitoring, user-programmable data loggers and electronic shipping indicators.[7] Its protocols include E006/TR03-VP.2 for programmable remote temperature and event monitoring, E006/TR05.VP-1 for user-programmable data loggers and E006/TR07-VP.4 for electronic shipping indicators.[122]

WHO defines Equipment Monitoring System levels. Level 1 is a data logger with machine-to-machine power and data connections; advanced local or remote communications are not required. Level 2 adds local communication through an Equipment Monitoring Device. Level 3 adds remote communication, with GSM/SIM and Wi-Fi cited as examples.[124] Satellite connectivity is therefore a commercial resilience requirement, not a stated Level 3 example.

WHO states that vaccine refrigeration appliances must achieve EMS Level 1 at minimum. It says all new vaccine refrigerators required EMS from 2024 and all vaccine refrigerators require it from 2026.[124] This appliance requirement should not be casually extended to every truck, container or passive transport box without checking the applicable PQS category.

WHO guidance on storage and transport of time- and temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical products is in Technical Report Series No. 961, 2011, Annex 9.[125] The WHO Vaccine Management Handbook says its distribution advice generally conforms to that guidance and points users to refrigerated-vehicle operations guidance and EVM SOP E7-05, Loading and operating refrigerated vehicles.[45]

Equipment prequalification is not vaccine prequalification and is not a shipment-release decision. A prequalified cold box such as RCW25 has passed the applicable equipment process. Release each dose only where the selected vaccine’s applicable labelled storage or transport condition is documented through the journey; disposition every excursion under the product-specific procedure. Vaccine profiles differ, including -80°C to -60°C, -25°C to -15°C and +2°C to +8°C with a direction not to freeze in WHO’s 14 January 2021 table.[123][8]

A cited drone wet-ice pilot recorded payload temperatures of 1°C–10°C, which does not demonstrate maintenance of a +2°C to +8°C vaccine transport range.[178]

Vaccine temperature requirements differ substantially. WHO’s 14 January 2021 candidate-vaccine table listed Pfizer-BioNTech at -80°C to -60°C in an ULT freezer, ModernaTx at -25°C to -15°C and Oxford-AstraZeneca at +2°C to +8°C with a direction not to freeze.[8] These examples show why an equipment claim such as “cold life” or “-70°C to +30°C portfolio coverage” is not enough to approve a vaccine route.

Controlled temperature chain, or CTC, is also vaccine-specific. WHO’s Controlled Temperature Chain Strategic Roadmap initially focused on human papillomavirus, oral cholera vaccine, tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccines and hepatitis B vaccine birth dose. In 2020, the CTC Working Group added typhoid conjugate vaccine as an additional priority and extended the Roadmap commitments through 2024.[126] MenAfriVac was authorized for use up to 40°C for four days immediately before administration under a controlled temperature chain.[127] Such conditions arise from product-specific authorization; they are not a general relaxation of the cold chain.

Transport frequency matters. WHO’s Vaccine Management Handbook says storage and cold-chain capacity calculations must account for supply interval and safety stock, which can reach 100% when catchment area is uncertain or weather threatens regular distribution.[45] Increasing delivery frequency may reduce safety-stock requirements but raises trips, driver time and fuel. Judgment — Run the model at three utilization cases for every configuration: planned average utilization, low utilization during campaign lull and maximum surge utilization. WHO advises mapping every routine vaccine-delivery trip, recording destinations, typical loop-trip or there-and-back completion time and, where needed, the location of 415/230-volt sockets for emergency backup cooling; the time each vehicle requires to complete its delivery route must be estimated.[139] WHO further directs route planners to verify net storage capacity for all distribution points on a route, suitability for the operating environment, vaccine-shipment weight against vehicle load limits and roadway restrictions to avoid delays or checkpoint problems.[139]

The RVTV’s stated 16-hour battery autonomy is 33.3% of the 48-hour parked-without-mains refrigeration provision in WHO PQS E002/RV01.3, leaving a 32-hour gap to that vehicle requirement.[38][60]

Satellite-connected temperature monitoring sends sensor data by satellite rather than through Wi-Fi or cellular networks.[145] Satellite IoT alerts may be real-time or near-real-time, but reported transmission intervals can be 15 minutes or hourly and delivery delays can occur.[145] Satellite equipment requires a relatively clear view of the sky because buildings, dense canopy and terrain can block the signal.[145] Satellite sensor devices can operate on batteries or solar power, and more frequent transmissions use more energy.[145] Satellite IoT hardware and service generally cost more than cellular equivalents.[145]

For any selected satellite configuration, the CabinPulse guide says a satellite-connected sensor can issue real-time or near-real-time alerts without local internet or cell service, including in Africa, provided it has a clear view of the sky.[145] The same guide states that reporting may be configured at intervals such as 15 minutes or hourly, may be delayed by store-and-forward transmission, and can be blocked by dense canopy, mountains or heavy building materials.[145] It also states that higher reporting frequency consumes more battery power and that satellite IoT hardware and service generally cost more than cellular IoT.[145]

The decision-relevant WHO vehicle benchmark is configuration-specific: the prequalified CF850 records 24-hour fuel standby/failure backup, while no equivalent WHO E002 listing or 24-hour backup field is established here for Thermo King or Carrier configurations.[139]

A direct-drive unit can continue cooling during loss of mains power while the vehicle engine supplies its road compressor, but it does not have independent refrigeration power when the vehicle engine is off.[19] A self-powered diesel unit avoids this vehicle-engine dependency but remains dependent on fuel, preventive maintenance and sufficient refrigeration capacity for the insulated body, ambient conditions and door-opening pattern.[29][16] Passive containers eliminate in-transit fuel dependence but depend on correctly frozen and conditioned coolant packs, finite holdover and correct pack-out.[146][108][109]

**Common annual equation and scenario.** For every matrix row, calculate: **CPD = [Annualized purchased-and-installed capital + annual continuing operating cost + contingency] ÷ annual released doses.** Annualized capital comprises unit or box purchase, freight, import/customs, installation, insulated body build, commissioning, qualification and initial/lifetime spares, annualized at the stated 3% discount rate and 10-year life where that convention is adopted. Continuing operating cost comprises fuel or electricity, PCM conditioning, maintenance and repair, replacement packs/batteries, monitoring and connectivity, calibration, driver and assistant, per diem, security, insurance, route, handling and emergency trans-shipment. Allocate shared vehicle/body and hub assets by route vehicle-days, trips, kilometres and cold-chain volume-days. Use one annual scenario: the same 12+-hour route, vaccine presentation, loaded doses per trip, trips per year, payload, door-opening profile, target temperature range and final released-dose count for every row. Apply a 5% to 10% contingency to operating costs.[42][8]

Report two cost-per-dose results: cost per single dose delivered without vaccine and cost per single dose delivered with vaccine, both in the stated price year and currency.[147] State whether the comparison is financial, economic or fiscal: economic cost comprises financial outlays plus the opportunity cost of health-worker time and donated items such as vaccines; financial cost comprises financial outlays and usually straight-line depreciation of capital items; fiscal cost comprises financial outlays and usually excludes depreciation of capital items.[147] Include the procurement value of doses actually written off in the with-vaccine numerator and count only accepted doses in the denominator; record quarantined doses pending product-specific disposition separately.[8]
