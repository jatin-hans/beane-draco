Select PAX A920 Pro only if the chain’s Oracle MICROS/Simphony payment partner demonstrates a production Canadian integration, French payment flow, and KDS coexistence; otherwise retain the validated Oracle route rather than replace it with any of these terminals.

Clover Station Pro, now Station Duo, is the strongest documented choice for controlled store-and-forward outages and post-authorization table-service tip adjustment, while PAX has the strongest documented Canadian bilingual-support route and portable form factor.

None of the three has a documented, terminal-only Oracle MICROS integration or a published peak-hour end-to-end transaction benchmark, so processor, network, payment application, and Simphony configuration decide both speed and compatibility.

In Quebec, the deployed terminal interface, receipts, and customer information must be available in French, and a claimed French option is insufficient without acceptance testing of the complete customer and staff payment journey.

Carbon 8 is not a preferred deployment choice because its Canadian pricing, French support, store-and-forward terms, and current Oracle route remain materially less established than the alternatives.[1][2][3][4][5][6]

**Evidence date — 17 November 2026.** Bill 96 was enacted in 2022 as a reform of Quebec’s Charter of the French Language, and PayFacto’s Interac Debit Switch fee of CAD 0.02144 per transaction took effect on 1 November 2026.[7][8]

Judgment — For this 25-location quick-service restaurant (QSR) chain, the payment terminal is the device at which the guest presents a card, phone, or wallet for payment. It is only one part of the payment system. ### Verifone Carbon 8

Carbon 8 is a dual-screen Android point-of-sale device. It has an 8-inch WXGA merchant display at 1280 × 800 resolution, a customer-facing display and contactless reader in the head unit, and Android Lollipop.[16][27] Its processor is an Intel Atom x5-Z8300, specified at up to 1.84 GHz for one or two cores and 1.60 GHz for three or four cores.[16] This processor specification describes local hardware, not the common peak-hour measure of tender initiated in MICROS through approval returned to MICROS.[16][9]

Carbon 8 supports WLAN under 802.11a/b/g/n/ac, Bluetooth 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 4.1, and BLE, and a dock with Ethernet and RJ12 cash-drawer connectivity.[16][27] The Ethernet interface is specified as 10 Base-T/100 Base-TX. Verifone’s launch announcement positioned the device for hospitality and restaurants, with a shoulder strap, optional detachable thermal printer, and pay-at-table use.[42][17]

Judgment — These specifications matter for local screen responsiveness and physical deployment, but they do not measure the end-to-end payment authorization time experienced in a lunch rush. That time includes POS request creation, local network delay, PSP routing, issuer authorization, customer cardholder-verification steps, receipt generation, and the POS response. A device processor frequency is not a peak-hour transactions-per-minute result.

Verifone says Quick Chip can shorten the time a card remains in the reader; it does not eliminate delays from host authorization, Wi-Fi contention, DNS failures or middleware retries.[142][143]

Verifone described the Carbon series as using an Intel high-speed chipset for fast processing of POS and third-party applications.[17] That is a product-positioning claim rather than a timed payment benchmark.

Verifone’s Visa Quick Chip material claims a chip read in two seconds or less; that measures card reading, not authorization of the complete sale.[142]

Oracle notes separately that restaurant POS hardware must handle short high-demand periods such as a lunch rush, and that demand on shared services increases with the number of POS clients and guest checks.[9] A Carbon proof of concept should consequently measure median and 95th-percentile time from tender selection in MICROS to approval returned to MICROS, separately for Interac debit, contactless credit, chip-and-PIN, and declined transactions.

Carbon 8’s documented network design is connected operation: the quick-installation guide says it requires Wi-Fi, while also supporting wired Ethernet.[27] A second network path improves resilience only if it reaches the payment host through a functioning internet connection. Carbon 8’s own documentation does not supply a Canadian store-and-forward configuration, transaction cap, card-entry eligibility, or processor settlement workflow.

Judgment — Carbon should be treated as online-only until the selected acquirer confirms a Carbon 8 payment application with store-and-forward enabled in writing. That confirmation needs to state which tender types qualify, whether contactless is excluded, the dollar cap per payment, the total stored-value cap, the maximum outage period, who bears a later decline, and how stored transactions are reconciled.

The distinction is commercially important. Moneris’ Offline Payments feature illustrates the usual Canadian store-and-forward design: it stores some credit-card purchase transactions while disconnected from the Moneris host and forwards them automatically when host connectivity returns.[11] The merchant sets a daily cumulative offline amount, capped at 20% of projected monthly volume approved by Moneris Credit, and a maximum amount per transaction no greater than that daily cap.[11] Each offline payment requires a valid user ID and passcode; the eligible transaction must be a chip-card purchase with the card inserted and the correct PIN entered.[11]

Moneris excludes Interac debit, Dynamic Currency Conversion, Gift and Loyalty, UnionPay, EMV fallback, cashback, and several other transaction types from Offline Payments.[11] The customer receives a printed receipt; email and text receipts are unavailable for the offline transaction.[11] A stored purchase can be voided while disconnected, but a transaction already sent to the host or not stored offline must wait for restored communication.[11] The merchant accepts the risk of insufficient funds, blocked or stolen cards, data loss, terminal storage exhaustion, and elevated chargeback exposure; the issuer has not authorized the transaction when it is accepted offline.[11]

Visa rules permit deferred authorization where a transaction cannot be submitted due to connectivity or system limitations, but the merchant or acquirer must include a deferred-authorization indicator and, for ordinary restaurant merchant categories, seek authorization within 24 hours of the transaction date.[54] Visa otherwise requires an approval response before completion of a transaction.[54] Interac Debit chip-and-PIN and contactless payments are ordinarily approved by the bank or credit union through secure online systems in real time.[55] Carbon’s connected hardware therefore cannot be credited with outage acceptance without processor-specific enablement.

Carbon 8’s relevant Oracle route is not an embedded terminal integration. Oracle Payment Interface is the bridge between Simphony and a PSP’s credit-processing solution, allowing payments on semi-integrated devices while keeping cardholder data out of the POS. Each Simphony POS client communicates with OPI when a credit-card transaction needs processing.[9] Oracle says partners perform integrations to the OPI specification and that Oracle Professional Services standardizes partner testing and validation.[1]

The integration requires site-specific configuration. OPI listens on TCP port 5098 for MGDH messages, while the PIN-pad port depends on the partner and must be confirmed with that partner.[33] Per-terminal configuration requires the EMC workstation ID, PIN-pad IP address, and partner-confirmed PIN-pad port.[33] The legacy OPI feature is supported through Simphony release 19.9 and is not supported from release 19.10 onward.[34] Oracle’s current payment resources instead direct users to release documentation and certified-provider information.[56]

Judgment — Carbon 8 can proceed only if the chain’s current MICROS product, Simphony release, PSP, payment application, and network architecture are matched to a named Oracle-supported partner route. The deciding test is a live tender flow: send amount from MICROS, display it on Carbon, obtain approval, return the approval and token to MICROS, process void/refund, close batch, and reconcile without double entry.

Carbon has no direct kitchen-display-system role in Oracle architecture. Oracle’s KDS Controller bridges the POS and KDS clients.[9] When the POS sends an order to the kitchen, the KDS shared service receives the order data on its designated host, forwards it to the KDS Controller Windows service, and that service communicates with KDS clients.[10] Simphony itself is described as sending orders to the kitchen and processing payments.[57]

The KDS environment needs independent resilience planning. If the primary KDS controller is unavailable, orders stop appearing on kitchen displays; an optional backup controller can take over communication.[9] The chain must manually switch back to the primary controller after it becomes available.[9] Backup controller and primary controller services cannot run on the same hardware.[10] These KDS controls are independent of choosing Carbon as the payment device.

For Quebec locations, Carbon must meet the French-language requirements through its configured payment software, customer display, receipt template, merchant interface, and operating documentation. Quebec consumers have the right to be informed and served in French.[58][59] The OQLF states that information displayed by electronic payment terminals must be in French, and its 2026 communiqué says terminal information must be available at least in French.[2][3]

The Charter also requires software to be available in French unless no French version exists. If another language is also available, the French version must be accessible on at least as favourable terms and have at least equivalent technical characteristics, subject to the statutory price exception.[58] The OQLF-described software user interface includes menus, commands, dialogue boxes, messages, and help boxes in French.[7] Receipts, invoices, acquittances, and similar consumer documents must be in French; another-language version cannot be sent unless the French version is available on at least equally favourable conditions.[2]

Carbon’s technical material confirms the screens and payment hardware but does not provide a French customer-payment journey.[27] Its installation manual contains French caution text, which supports French documentation content but does not configure payment prompts, staff menus, receipt language, tax text, loyalty content, or error messages.[42] The chain should require a French acceptance script covering welcome screen, amount, tip prompt, receipt choice, declined-payment instruction, refund wording, email/SMS receipt language, and all cashier-facing payment-management screens.

Carbon supports contactless card presentation; its guide tells the customer to hold the card flat against the contactless symbol.[27] It also supports chip-card insertion and magnetic-stripe reading.[27] The terminal’s contactless reader is an acceptance capability, not its own Canadian dollar limit.

Interac says Interac Debit contactless transactions are limited to up to CAD 250 per transaction, with a separate cumulative limit set by each bank or credit union. Reaching the cumulative threshold prompts a chip-and-PIN transaction.[55] Tangerine illustrates issuer variation: its Client Card supports contactless payments up to CAD 250 at participating merchants, while cards issued before October 22, 2021 have a CAD 100 limit.[60] Tangerine can require chip-and-PIN on the first purchase, above the card’s limit, or after CAD 500 in cumulative daily contactless payments; the older card threshold is CAD 200.[60]

Judgment — Carbon’s contactless user experience should always include chip-and-PIN fallback. A tap amount that fails does not prove terminal failure; it can reflect card issuer controls, cumulative limits, a merchant-configured acceptance ceiling, or processor settings.

A merchant may set a lower contactless acceptance limit but cannot raise a card-network or issuer limit.[125][74]

Carbon’s product positioning includes tableside payment processing and pay-at-table use.[51][17] In Oracle’s legacy MGDH/OPI configuration, however, QSR means Quick Service and TSR means Table Service, and the merchant must select the applicable revenue-centre mode.[33] QSR does not support tips.[33][61] QSR configuration activates Do Not Prompt for Tips and Allow Fast Transactions; TSR does not activate those options.[33]

For the documented legacy MGDH/OPI route, TSR allows the shared VOID touchscreen key to add tips, while QSR does not support tips and activates Do Not Prompt for Tips and Allow Fast Transactions.[33] The chain’s occasional table service should therefore be configured as a distinct TSR revenue centre or equivalent current Oracle configuration, not as a legacy MGDH/OPI QSR tender workflow with ad hoc tip adjustment.[33] In a validated Nets Pay@Counter integration, tips were entered only on the payment device; the separate Pay@Table interface supported a sale with or without a tip.[62] This confirms the integration principle: the PSP and OPI driver, rather than Carbon hardware alone, determine the available table-service workflow.

Verifone support is available through a help desk, Jira Service Desk for engineering incidents, account-manager coordination, and documentation resources.[44] Carbon’s manual lists a US device-service contact operating Monday to Friday from 8 a.m.

to 8 p.m. Eastern time.[42] Verifone’s lifecycle tooling supports maintenance requests for swaps, repairs, and warranty services, but the commercial coverage and French-language service level must be set in the Canadian distribution agreement.[63]

Carbon also lacks published Canadian procurement inputs for a defensible five-year financial comparison. A third-party review says Carbon pricing is set by the seller or processor rather than Verifone, and reports no published MSRP. Verifone’s commercial model uses authorized distributors and site-specific bundles that may include terminals, controllers, activation, and acquiring; support and processing are separately determined.[51][64] The device should therefore be priced as a Canadian quote for hardware, installation, payment application, key injection, replacement, warranty, French support, and MICROS integration.

### Clover Station Pro

Clover Station Pro is now called Clover Station Duo. Clover identifies it as a two-screen POS device for business management and customer payment initiation, and lists it as available in Canada.[14][13] Station Duo has a 14-inch merchant display at 1920 × 1080 and a 7-inch customer display at 1280 × 800.[14] It runs Clover-hardened Android Open Source Project software, initially AOSP 8.1 and upgradeable to AOSP 10.0, on a Qualcomm Snapdragon 660 octa-core processor with 2 GB RAM and 16 GB flash.[14]

The device includes EMV chip, NFC, and magnetic-stripe readers.[14] It supports Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and 4G/LTE, with a hub containing four USB ports and two RJ12 cash-drawer ports.[14] A Station Pro setup guide similarly identifies Ethernet, SIM-based mobile connectivity, and Wi-Fi, recommending Ethernet.[43] The merchant-facing screen accepts order input and the customer-facing terminal accepts payments and signatures.[43]

Clover’s February 2020 Station Pro plan said its processor was intended to increase transaction and application speed.[19] Clover’s broader EMV performance statement says an EMV transaction can take less than three seconds from card dip to a remove-card prompt, but the named 2.93-second measurement is for Clover Mini and varies with internet speed.[20] Neither statement measures the common peak-hour interval from tender initiated in MICROS through approval returned to MICROS; the chain needs a timed Station Duo/PSP/MICROS test under lunch-rush load.[9][20]

Judgment — Station Duo has no comparable measured peak-hour MICROS authorization result. Clover can bypass duplicate checking for transactions using the same card type and last four digits within one hour, making payments faster while increasing duplicate-payment risk.[28][20][26]

Clover’s Canadian merchant documentation states that Canadian merchants cannot process any payments in offline mode on Clover devices.[31] When the terminal cannot reach the server, the device records an offline payment and cannot verify the payment gateway, card validity, or available funds.[4] The applicable store-and-forward period, submission process, and Canadian Station Duo terms must be established in the deployment agreement.[13][4]

Canadian Clover integrations must use SDK version 2.0 or higher.[31] Outside the Canadian deployment, Clover’s platform SDK includes offline-payment controls. `allowOfflinePayment` requires a merchant challenge to accept or decline the offline transaction.[4] `approveOfflinePaymentWithoutPrompt` accepts the payment without a challenge and can complete it faster, although the merchant may not know the payment was taken offline.[4] `forceOfflinePayment` takes payments offline even when connectivity exists, overrides offline limits, and processes transactions faster because it avoids the merchant prompt.[4] Canadian merchants cannot use these controls to process payments offline.[31]

Clover can also let an application implement a monetary decision threshold. Its example automatically accepts offline payments under $20 and rejects or prompts for payments over $20.[4] Integration-level overrides can supersede merchant limits on time before reconnecting, amount per payment, and total offline payments.[4] That is a significant governance issue for a 25-site chain: a local application integrator can widen loss exposure beyond the operations team’s intended policy.

Judgment — Clover Station Duo’s Canadian outage design is online failover through its available connectivity rather than store-and-forward acceptance.[31][19][14]

Clover’s native 4G/LTE also offers a separate connectivity route.[19][14] LTE is not offline authorization; it is a way to keep sending online authorization requests when the primary wired or Wi-Fi route fails.

The chain should use Ethernet as the normal path, a segregated payment VLAN, managed Wi-Fi only where needed, and a contracted SIM data plan as failover. Clover’s setup guidance says payment networks must be closed, protected, and secure, with customer Wi-Fi separated from the payment network.[43]

Clover offers a semi-integrated architecture for businesses retaining a sophisticated or specialized existing POS. ### Quebec French-language requirements and acceptance test

Bill 96 is the 2022 reform of Quebec’s Charter of the French Language rather than a separate restaurant-terminal certification regime. The Charter’s express duty for businesses offering goods and services to respect consumers’ right to be informed and served in French took effect for customer communications on June 1, 2022.[7][109][110] Bill 96 does not apply in New Brunswick; the stated New Brunswick Official Languages Act applies to government institutions rather than private businesses.[111][12] The operative obligation for a payment terminal begins with the consumer’s right to be informed and served in French.[58][7][2] The OQLF expressly states that information displayed by electronic payment terminals must be in French.[2] Its May 1, 2026 results state that payment-terminal information must be available at least in French.[3]

The requirement is French availability, not compulsory English/French bilingualism. Other language versions may coexist where the French version is accessible under at least as favourable conditions and has at least equivalent technical characteristics.[58][112] French and English receipts may coexist if the French version is at least as prominent and accessible on at least equally favourable terms.[111] An English-only guest payment path is therefore unsuitable for Quebec deployment even if staff can provide oral French service.

Receipts are a separate compliance surface. Quebec requires invoices, receipts, quittances, and similar documents to be in French.[58][2] This requirement applies to the receipt delivered by the payment system and to restaurant bill documents.

RapidCents notes that Quebec restaurants produce each customer bill through a sales recording module connected to the POS, while the payment terminal takes payment rather than producing the bill itself.[73] The chain must test both the MICROS/SRM bill and the terminal’s merchant/customer receipt.

Staff-facing software is also relevant. Section 52.1 requires software to be available in French unless no French version exists.[58][112] OQLF-described user-interface elements include menus, commands, dialogue boxes, messages, and help boxes.[7][111] The OQLF-related guidance also describes employee-used hardware and electronic displays as requiring French inscriptions and displays, with French diacritical marks available.[7][111]

The OQLF says employers must make French versions of software available, install them by default and generalize the use of French in information technology.[153]

Quebec actively monitors terminal language. In April 2025, the OQLF told businesses in the Boulevard Taschereau area that it was checking service, invoice, and payment-terminal language.[2] In its 2026 report on visits to nearly 1,000 Greater Montreal businesses, the OQLF reported 87% compliance for French payment terminals and 89% compliance for invoices.[3] It said the visits checked whether customers had access to French service, invoices, and payment terminals.[3]

Judgment — The chain should make French functionality a contractual acceptance gate, not a vendor marketing assertion. Acceptance should require a video or onsite test in the exact Canadian payment application, proving French customer prompts, French staff operation, French default or readily available language selection, French printed and digital receipts, French help/error messages, and equivalent payment functions in French and English where English is offered.

The francization threshold is based on people, not restaurants. An enterprise employing 25 or more people in Quebec for six months must register with the OQLF within six months after that period, and then submit its linguistic-situation analysis within three months after the registration attestation.[59][111] A 25-location footprint does not itself answer that threshold, although a chain of this size will commonly need to address the broader francization requirements in its Quebec operations.

The OQLF registration requirement has applied to businesses with 25–49 Quebec employees since 1 June 2025; the threshold counts employees, not restaurant locations.[154][155]

In the OQLF’s 2026 Greater Montréal monitoring operation, French payment-terminal compliance was 87%, versus 89% for invoices: a 2-percentage-point shortfall for terminals, or an 18.2% larger non-compliance rate than invoices. This supports treating the terminal flow and receipt flow as separate acceptance tests.[3]

For Quebec restaurants, suggested tip percentages must be calculated on the price before GST and QST, be displayed the same way, and permit the customer to enter a custom amount. The restaurant remains responsible when another party programs the terminal.[113]

A surcharge, if used in Quebec, is another customer-facing payment-terminal and receipt element that must be included in the French acceptance test. Mastercard requires the surcharge percentage and merchant-imposed nature to be disclosed at the point of interaction and the dollar amount to appear on the receipt.[114]

Clover’s French Canadian page states that Clover POS is certified for Quebec MEV-WEB. MEV-WEB certification addresses Quebec restaurant fiscal requirements; the French-language acceptance test remains the control for payment-terminal information, customer receipts, and staff-facing interface content.[80][2][3]

PayFacto’s A920 Pro documentation includes a terminal-display-language configuration procedure. This configuration is part of the Quebec acceptance test for the deployed A920 Pro payment application.[98][30][3]

This is the configuration path for MICROS workstation and guest-check language content.[106]

For the PAX A920 Pro, PayFacto’s configuration supports French Canada and French France as selectable terminal languages, and the language selection changes all text displayed on the terminal. For Clover Canadian devices, Clover documents English and French payment flows and a card-language/locale value that permits a custom receipt to match the customer card/receipt language.[39]

Quebec’s Bill 72 tip-display requirements took effect May 7, 2025: tip calculations must use the pre-tax subtotal and tip choices must be presented neutrally. Quebec terminal acceptance testing must therefore verify tip-on-subtotal and neutral presentation as well as French-language payment and receipt flows.[12]

Bill 72’s Quebec tip-display rule is separate from Bill 96-related French-language duties: from 7 May 2025, suggested tips must be calculated on the pre-tax subtotal and presented neutrally.[12]

Carbon has no installed French payment-flow evidence; Clover documents English and French Canadian payment flows; and PayFacto documents selectable French Canada and French France terminal text on A920 Pro.[27][42][31][39][2][58]

Article 50.2 of the Charter of the French Language requires a business offering goods or services to consumers to respect their right to be informed and served in French; it also requires a business serving a public other than consumers to inform and serve that public in French.[2]

The OQLF states that Quebec consumers have the right to be informed and served in French and that invoices, receipts, and payment-terminal information must be available at least in French; it states that this rule has applied since 1977.[3]

Translations may accompany the French text, but no other-language inscription may be more prominent: «Toute inscription sur un produit, sur son contenant ou sur son emballage, sur un document ou objet accompagnant ce produit, y compris le mode d’emploi et les certificats de garantie, doit être rédigée en français.»[112]

For businesses employing 25 or more people, user guides, manuals, teaching material, and training manuals used in the business must be in French. Norton Rose Fulbright interprets the Charter as permitting bilingual versions where English is not more prominent than French.[111]

The provision lists a contract used in relations outside Quebec among exceptions to its first paragraph.[58]

For Quebec payment-terminal customer communications, the operative requirement is the Charter right of consumers to be informed and served in French, which businesses offering goods and services must respect. This Quebec requirement is distinct from the stated New Brunswick rule, under which the provincial Official Languages Act applies to government institutions rather than private businesses.[111][12]

### Shared five-year ownership-cost model

Judgment — A five-year total cost of ownership (TCO) model should use 60 months and a common scope. The expressly minimalist common fleet is 25 active devices and five shared spares initially, with all 30 devices replaced once at the beginning of year five after four years of service: 60 device purchases over five years. Additional counter lanes, mobile devices, KDS units, printers, cash drawers, installation, taxes, connectivity, Oracle work, and migration must be itemized rather than hidden in a terminal price.[39]

A useful TCO definition includes hardware, installation, software subscription, payment processing, integrations, support, replacement, training, and migration.[117] Payment processing often dominates terminal capital cost. The illustrative restaurant TCO model cited in the findings attributes $147,900 of a $201,040 five-year total to payment processing, compared with $11,400 initial hardware and $4,200 year-three replacement; it is a single-location full-service example rather than a 25-site QSR quote.[117]

The chain has not supplied annual card volume, transaction count, average cheque, payment-method mix, card-present versus card-not-present share, tip percentage, or current processing statements. These inputs determine the processing-cost line. RapidCents recommends collecting a busy-month statement, average bill and tip, payment-channel mix, refund and chargeback counts, number of locations, and number of terminals before comparing quotes.[73]

Visa explains that merchants do not pay interchange reimbursement fees directly; they pay the acquirer’s merchant discount fee, which can include processing services.[118] Interchange is only one component. Processor markup, network assessments, fixed authorization fees, account fees, PCI fees, chargeback fees, and terminal or software fees can all sit on the merchant statement.[119][116]

Visa’s Everyday Needs program includes restaurants and fast food under MCC 5812 and 5814, subject to program qualification criteria.[118] One Visa schedule lists 1.10% for Classic, Gold, and Platinum Everyday Needs consumer credit; 1.10% for Infinite; 1.35% for Infinite+; and 1.85% for Infinite Privilege.[118] Another Visa schedule lists 1.10%, 1.20%, and 1.95% for Classic/Gold/Platinum, Infinite, and Infinite Privilege respectively.[120] Rates are subject to change.[118]

Mastercard lists card-present EMV and contactless consumer-credit interchange at 0.92% for Core, 1.22% for World, 1.56% for World Elite, and 1.65% for Muse Mastercard in its May 2024 schedule.[121] Its Small Business category applies only where annual Mastercard credit volume is below CAD 175,000 during the qualifying period.[121] A 25-location chain should not assume Small Business rates. Interac debit interchange may be zero, while processors charge their own contract-set per-transaction fees and may pass network charges through.[119]

Judgment — The model should calculate processing cost by payment type rather than applying one advertised rate to all sales. For each year, multiply Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Interac debit, wallet, keyed, online, and foreign-card volume by the contracted all-in rate for that category; add fixed cents-per-transaction charges; then add monthly and annual account, PCI, gateway, and reporting charges. Use the processor’s current effective merchant discount rate by card type as the control total.
