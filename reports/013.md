# vTv Equity Financing and Shareholder Dilution

## Executive summary

vTv’s equity-financing and compensation pattern points to increasing potential dilution for existing shareholders, with no clear evidence of an offsetting dilution-management mechanism. Annual share-based compensation rose by $1.179 million, or 74.7%, from $1.578 million in 2023 to $2.757 million in 2024.[1][2] Quarterly stock-based compensation then rose by $154,000, or 18.1%, from $853,000 in Q3 2024 to $1.007 million in Q3 2025.[3] Multi-year, service-vesting options with exercise prices tied to grant-date market values support recruitment and retention of specialized personnel, but they also create a continuing pool of potential shares if awards vest and are exercised.

Outstanding warrants add a separate financing-related source of potential issuance, so the combined overhang can reduce existing holders’ ownership percentage and per-share claim on future value as equity-linked funding and compensation continue.

## Annual share-based compensation

**Measurement scope —** This report evaluates vTv Therapeutics’ share-based compensation expense for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2023 and 2024; stock-based compensation expense for the three months ended September 30, 2024 and 2025; and warrant holdings as of September 30, 2025. Annual expense is measured in U.S. dollars and reported in the company’s Form 10-K financial statements; the September-quarter compensation comparison is reported in the company’s unaudited Form 10-Q, in thousands of dollars.[1][3] The warrant measure is the number of Class A common shares underlying outstanding and exercisable warrants, together with their exercise prices, at September 30, 2025.[3]

Equity financing means raising capital by selling common stock or securities that can be exercised for common stock. Dilution means an increase in shares outstanding that reduces an existing shareholder’s percentage ownership unless that shareholder acquires a proportional number of additional shares. In August 2025, vTv agreed in a private placement to sell 682,018 Class A common shares and Pre-Funded Warrants for 4,561,714 Class A common shares, with accompanying Common Warrants for 5,243,732 Class A common shares; the Pre-Funded Warrants had a $0.01 exercise price and the Common Warrants were exercisable at $22.71 per share or $22.70 per Pre-Funded Warrant.[4]

Judgment — Share-based compensation is the non-cash accounting expense a company recognizes for equity awards, such as stock options, issued to employees, executives, directors, or service providers. It matters to shareholders because it indicates the scale at which a company is using equity awards as part of pay, although the expense itself is not the same thing as shares issued in a particular year.

### What were vTv’s share-based compensation expenses in 2023 and 2024?

vTv Therapeutics reported total share-based compensation expense of $1.578 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, and $2.757 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. The company reported both amounts in thousands of dollars in its audited annual financial statements.[1]

The company allocated this expense between research and development (R&D), the function responsible for developing its product candidates, and general and administrative (G&A), the function covering corporate management and support activities. R&D share-based compensation rose from $425,000 in 2023 to $742,000 in 2024, while G&A share-based compensation rose from $1.153 million to $2.015 million.[1][5]

The annual ASC 718 share-based compensation comparison states that expense increased from $1.578 million in 2023 to $2.757 million in 2024, a $1.179 million, 74.7% increase.[6]

### Calculate the increase in vTv’s share-based compensation expense from 2023

Derived — vTv’s total share-based compensation expense increased by $1.179 million from fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2024. The calculation is $2.757 million in 2024 less $1.578 million in 2023, using the company’s reported totals.[1]

Derived — The percentage increase was approximately 74.7%. The calculation is the $1.179 million increase, calculated as $2,757 thousand in 2024 less $1,578 thousand in 2023, divided by the $1,578 thousand 2023 base and multiplied by 100.[7][2][1]

This was a substantial year-over-year increase rather than a marginal movement in a single cost line. Both expense categories increased: R&D rose by $317,000, or approximately 74.6%, and G&A rose by $862,000, or approximately 74.8%, based on the company’s reported category amounts.[1][5]

vTv’s total operating expense declined from $25.502 million in fiscal 2023 to $25.197 million in fiscal 2024, even as its share-based compensation expense rose.[37][5]

The company’s disclosure also shows that recognized share-based compensation was not limited to a completed 2024 expense cycle. At December 31, 2024, vTv had approximately $4.7 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation expense expected to be recognized on a straight-line basis over a weighted-average period of 2.3 years.[5] This is an accounting recognition schedule for previously granted awards; it does not itself identify a future number of shares that will be issued.

For the six months ended June 30, 2024, vTv reported $1.079 million in total share-based compensation, versus $742,000 for the six months ended June 30, 2023—an increase of $337,000, or 45.4%.[16]

For the six months ended June 30, 2024, vTv reported $859,000 of share-based compensation allocated to research and development, versus $399,000 for the six months ended June 30, 2023.[16]

At June 30, 2024, vTv had approximately $4.7 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation expense associated with outstanding options, expected to be recognized over a weighted-average 2.5 years.[16]

Judgment — A 74.7% rise in recognized expense signals materially greater reliance on equity awards as a component of remuneration during 2024. The future expense balance also indicates that the effects of grants already made were expected to continue appearing in operating expenses after 2024.

The annual comparison should be read as an expense trend, not as a direct cash-burn measure. Equity compensation commonly reduces reported operating results when recognized, while its potential shareholder effect arises through the underlying awards and their eventual settlement in shares; the treatment of options and other potential dilution is addressed in the later sections on option grants and outstanding warrants.

**The decision-relevant result is that vTv increased annual share-based compensation by $1.179 million in one year, creating a much higher 2024 expense base and leaving $4.7 million of additional unrecognized stock-based compensation expense at year-end 2024.**[1][5]

General and administrative expense accounted for 73.1% of vTv’s total fiscal-2023-to-2024 share-based-compensation increase, while research and development accounted for 26.9%.[1][5][7]

### Synthesis

vTv’s reported annual share-based compensation rose from $1.578 million in fiscal 2023 to $2.757 million in fiscal 2024, a derived increase of $1.179 million, or approximately 74.7%.[1][2] The increase was not confined to one function: R&D expense increased from $425,000 to $742,000 and G&A expense increased from $1.153 million to $2.015 million, with G&A remaining the larger component.[1][5] In addition, vTv reported approximately $4.7 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation expense at December 31, 2024, to be recognized over a weighted-average 2.3 years.[5]

For an investor, this establishes a clear escalation in the accounting cost of equity-based compensation across 2023–2024. The following sections assess the quarterly continuation of that trend, the relevant option grants, and the associated potential dilution from awards and warrants.

## Quarterly stock-based compensation

Stock-based compensation is the accounting expense vTv records for options and similar equity awards issued to employees and service providers. It is not the same as cash payroll, nor does it by itself mean that the underlying shares have already been issued. This quarterly comparison matters because it shows whether equity compensation was becoming a larger cost in the operating functions that support drug development and corporate administration.

Judgment — The useful comparison is the same three-month period in consecutive years: Q3 2024 versus Q3 2025. It avoids treating nine-month cumulative expense as if it were a single-quarter run rate, and it separates stock-based compensation from total research-and-development (R&D) and general-and-administrative (G&A) spending.

## Option grants and talent retention

### What was the reported exercise price of the relevant stock

On November 10, 2023, vTv granted Thomas Strack, its Chief Medical Officer and Executive Vice President, an option to purchase 12,500 common shares at a $14.16-per-share exercise price; the option expires on November 10, 2033. The filing describes the inducement awards as grants made to induce employees to accept employment. Twenty-five percent of Strack’s award vested on November 10, 2024, and the remainder vests in equal quarterly installments over the following three years.[13] A stock option gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy a share at an **exercise price**—the amount the holder must pay per share.[13] Its economic value therefore depends on vTv’s market price rising above that exercise price. This matters for investors because option grants can both motivate employees and create potential future share issuance if exercised.

Beyond Strack’s precisely dated November 10, 2023 award at $14.16 per share, vTv reported a $13.14 weighted-average exercise price for 461,499 options granted in 2024 and a $19.67 weighted-average exercise price for 388,816 options granted during the nine months ended September 30, 2025. ### What do the stock options granted at the reported exercise

vTv expressly frames its 2024 Equity Incentive Plan as a tool to secure and retain eligible recipients, motivate their efforts, and allow them to benefit from increases in common-stock value. vTv stated that it relies significantly on equity grants to attract and retain key employees and considers them necessary to compete for executive and other key-employee talent; grants to new and continuing employees are based on competitive market conditions and individual performance. The plan covers awards to eligible personnel; vTv states that it has issued non-qualified options to management, other key employees, consultants, and non-employee directors.[14][19][1]

The company’s option design creates a **service-and-upside link**. vTv’s March 31, 2023 Form 10-Q stated that its non-qualified options generally vested ratably over three years; its September 30, 2025 Form 10-Q stated that options for management, other key employees, consultants, and non-employee directors vest ratably over four years. Options issued in connection with the February 27, 2024 private placement vest ratably over three years, and option awards expire ten years after grant.[15] vTv also says that, if employment or engagement ends before exercise, options generally expire and rights to purchase the shares cease, except for applicable rights relating to vested shares under the relevant award agreement.[11][1]

The November 2023 Strack inducement award illustrates the structure at an individual level. The award was granted to induce him to accept employment; 25% vested on November 10, 2024, and the remainder vests in equal quarterly installments over the subsequent three years.[13] vTv’s 2024 plan similarly states that an award does not confer a right to continued employment, consultancy, or board service, and the company may still terminate the recipient.[14]

Judgment — The service-and-upside link is a standard retention mechanism: staged vesting makes leaving costly because the recipient normally forfeits unvested options, while the exercise price links realized value to subsequent share-price appreciation. It also aligns the recipient’s financial incentive with the objective of increasing shareholder value, although it does not protect shareholders from dilution if options are ultimately exercised.

vTv identifies a business reason to use this form of pay. It says biotechnology, pharmaceutical, and other competitors create intense competition for qualified management, finance, scientific, and clinical personnel, and that many competitors have greater financial resources and research-and-development expertise.[5] Its named-executive compensation policy also says compensation is intended to recruit and retain skilled executive leadership, support business strategy, and align executive and stockholder interests.[5][20]

The grant-price pattern supports a strategy of providing employees and executives with conditional equity upside rather than cash-like value independent of market performance. A recipient benefits economically only when the market price exceeds the particular award’s exercise price, subject to vesting and the option’s other terms. The reported $13.14 and $19.67 weighted-average exercise prices therefore represent thresholds that recipients must exceed through future share-price appreciation to realize intrinsic value.[1][3][14]

This design can be particularly relevant to a development-stage biotechnology company competing for specialized personnel: it spreads compensation over multi-year service periods and reserves the largest payoff for a higher future market valuation. vTv had approximately $2.1 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation expense for outstanding options at March 31, 2023, expected to be recognized over a weighted-average 2.6 years; at September 30, 2025, it had approximately $6.2 million, also expected to be recognized over a weighted-average 2.6 years.[15] These amounts represent future accounting expense for outstanding awards and show that the service-linked compensation commitment extended beyond the respective reporting dates.[11][15]

At December 31, 2024, vTv employed 23 people, including at least 13 with graduate degrees and 10 with doctorates.[5]

### How can equity awards increase shares outstanding

Equity awards are compensation instruments linked to a company’s stock. Under vTv’s 2024 Equity Incentive Plan, the company may grant incentive and nonstatutory stock options, stock appreciation rights, restricted stock, restricted stock units (RSUs), performance stock, performance cash, and other stock awards.[14]

A stock option gives its holder the right to purchase common shares at a stated exercise price. An RSU is a right to receive shares, although vTv’s plan permits RSUs to be settled in shares, cash, a combination of shares and cash, or another form of consideration selected by the board.[14]

Restricted stock differs from an RSU because it is an award of actual common stock, usually subject to vesting restrictions and potential forfeiture or repurchase if service ends before vesting. The 2024 Plan permits the company to use authorized but unissued common stock or reacquired common stock, including repurchased shares, for plan settlements.[14]

Judgment — Equity compensation can therefore create dilution through several mechanisms, but the dilution is not mechanically equal to the number of awards granted. An option increases outstanding shares only if it is exercised and settled in newly issued shares; an RSU may be settled in shares or cash; restricted stock may be forfeited; and use of reacquired shares avoids increasing the legal share count.

Share-based compensation expense is different. vTv measures compensation expense from the grant-date fair value of an award and recognizes that expense as the recipient earns the award during the service period. It estimates stock-option grant-date fair value using the Black-Scholes option-pricing formula and estimates forfeitures using historical employee-turnover rates.[1][5]

In ordinary language, share-based compensation expense records the accounting cost of employee, consultant, director, or executive services paid for with equity-linked awards. It is a dollar measure of services consumed, not a share count and not evidence that shares were issued in the same period.

The distinction is visible in vTv’s financial statements. For the nine months ended September 30, 2025, vTv reported **$2.750 million of share-based compensation**, separately reported **700 Class A shares issued upon exercise of stock options**, and separately reported **$9 thousand of proceeds** from those option exercises.[3]

vTv also reported **4,258 net shares withheld for tax withholding** related to the settlement of equity awards during the same nine-month period, along with $51 thousand of related taxes paid.[3] This illustrates that even when an award is settled in shares, the number of shares ultimately delivered to an award holder may be lower than the gross award amount.

The plan itself provides several potential dilution-reducing or share-delivery-reducing outcomes. A nonstatutory option may be exercised through a net exercise, under which vTv reduces the number of shares issued by the number needed to cover the aggregate exercise price.[14]

Likewise, an RSU may be cash-settled rather than share-settled, and unvested restricted stock may be forfeited or repurchased if the participant’s service terminates.[14] vTv may also use reacquired shares, rather than newly authorized and unissued shares, to settle awards.[14]

A participant does not become a shareholder merely because an award has been granted. Under the 2024 Plan, a participant is not deemed to own the shares subject to an award until the applicable exercise or issuance requirements have been met and the issuance has been entered into vTv’s books and records.[14]

This distinction also applies to options. Shares become deliverable on exercise, subject to payment terms and withholding requirements; an option grant alone is not share issuance.[14] Accordingly, compensation expense may rise well before, after, or without a corresponding increase in shares outstanding.

The same principle applies to vTv’s outstanding option pool. At September 30, 2025, vTv reported 1,007,306 options outstanding, of which 372,211 were exercisable and 843,656 were vested and expected to vest.[3] These options represent potential future shares, but their exercise depends on vesting, holder decisions, exercise economics, and the applicable award terms.

Judgment — For investors, the useful analytical separation is the **expense-versus-issuance distinction**: compensation expense measures the fair-value cost of obtaining services, while dilution is driven by the net shares ultimately issued or delivered. Rising expense signals greater equity-award activity and future settlement potential, but it cannot be converted directly into a share-issuance forecast.

**What follows:** vTv’s rising equity compensation creates a potential dilution channel, but the reported expense is not itself dilution and must not be mistaken for shares issued.

vTv stated in its September 30, 2025 Form 10-Q that options issued to management, other key employees, consultants and non-employee directors generally vest ratably over a four-year service period; options issued in connection with the February 27, 2024 private placement vest ratably over three years; and option awards expire ten years after grant. ### How can exercise of vTv’s outstanding warrants increase shares outstanding?

A warrant is a contractual right to buy a fixed number of common shares on stated terms. vTv stated that its Pre-Funded and Common Warrants permit holders to receive a fixed number of common shares upon exercise and classified them as permanent equity.[4]

When a holder exercises a warrant for Class A shares, vTv issues the underlying shares, increasing the number of outstanding Class A shares. An existing holder who does not acquire an equivalent number of new shares then owns a smaller percentage of the company’s equity and voting base.

Before exercise, however, the warrant shares are not outstanding for voting purposes and carry no voting rights. vTv’s October 2025 prospectus expressly states that shares underlying warrants are not deemed outstanding for voting and do not carry voting rights until exercise and issuance.[22]

The relevant exercise mechanics differ sharply by warrant type. Pre-Funded Warrants require only $0.01 per share, are exercisable at any time after original issuance, and do not expire; Common Warrants require $22.71 for a share or $22.70 for a Pre-Funded Warrant and expire on the earlier of the fifth anniversary of issuance or 90 days after announcement of positive top-line data from the ongoing CATT1 clinical trial.[3][11]

Judgment — A $0.01 Pre-Funded Warrant is normally treated by investors as economically close to already-paid-for stock, because the holder has already funded nearly all of the share purchase price. Its exercise timing is more likely to be determined by ownership blockers, administrative timing, or resale strategy than by whether the holder wishes to invest substantial additional cash.

vTv’s accounting treatment reinforces this economic distinction. The company included all shares underlying Pre-Funded Warrants in its weighted-average share count for net-loss-per-share purposes because those shares may be issued for little or no consideration, are fully vested, and are exercisable after issuance.[4]

That treatment does not mean the shares had been issued. It means vTv regarded the Pre-Funded Warrants as sufficiently share-like that their underlying shares belonged in the weighted-average share denominator for the periods reported.

The common warrants are more conventionally contingent. Their $22.71 or $22.70 exercise price means that, as a matter of economics, exercise is generally more attractive if vTv’s market price exceeds the relevant exercise price, though holders may have strategic reasons to exercise under other circumstances. The company’s reported Common Warrant expiration condition also creates a defined window in which trial news can affect holders’ exercise decisions.[3]

At an October 2, 2025 Class A closing price of $23.13 per share, vTv’s registered common warrants had a $22.98 weighted-average exercise price, putting them only marginally in the money at that reference point.[22]

The potential dilution from the warrant package is not offset by the fact that vTv may receive cash upon cash exercise. The cash may support operations or development, but it does not preserve an existing investor’s percentage ownership once new shares are issued.

The August 2025 financing illustrates the mechanics. vTv raised approximately $80.0 million of gross proceeds while issuing 682,018 Class A shares and selling both Pre-Funded Warrants and Common Warrants.[4] The immediate shares changed the ownership base at closing; subsequent exercise of the 9,805,446 warrants issued in that financing could create further changes in the legal share count.[4]

vTv’s warrants have ownership blockers, but these do not remove the issuance pathway. The 4.99% or 9.99% beneficial-ownership limits constrain the amount an individual holder can exercise at a particular time, and the holder can adjust the threshold up to 19.99% with 61 days’ notice.[4]

**The outstanding-warrant overhang is therefore a defined contingent source of dilution: exercise converts rights to buy shares into issued Class A shares, while the Pre-Funded Warrants present the more immediate exercise risk because their remaining exercise price is nominal.**[3][4]

What follows: warrant exercise can materially expand vTv’s Class A share count because the Pre-Funded and Common Warrants permit holders to receive a fixed number of common shares upon exercise.[4]

Judgment — This is not evidence that all authorized plan shares will be granted or that all options and warrants will become issued stock. It does show that vTv’s capital structure retained substantial capacity for both compensation-driven and financing-driven equity issuance after the September 2025 financing.

There are mitigating features, but they are partial. Options can expire unexercised, awards can be forfeited or cash-settled, net exercise and withholding can reduce delivered shares, Common Warrant exercise depends on the exercise price and expiration terms, and beneficial-ownership blockers can slow individual warrant exercise.[14][3][4]

Those mechanisms address timing, exercise incentives, or the net number of shares delivered in specific cases. They do not change the fact that vTv reported a large, exercisable warrant balance and a meaningful outstanding option pool, both of which can produce future Class A share issuance.

**What follows:** the compensation surge and warrant overhang create separate but cumulative dilution risks—option-based compensation expands the potential employee and executive share pool, while financing-linked warrants, particularly $0.01 Pre-Funded Warrants, represent the larger and more immediate contingent source of additional Class A shares.

### Synthesis

vTv entered Q3 2025 with two material equity-dilution channels. First, its equity-compensation structure left 1,007,306 options outstanding and approximately $6.2 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation, demonstrating an ongoing reliance on equity-linked awards for compensation. Second, its August 2025 financing materially enlarged warrant overhang: vTv reported 13,714,356 warrants outstanding and exercisable, including 8,470,624 $0.01, non-expiring Pre-Funded Warrants.[4][3]

The warrant channel is the larger disclosed potential-share source, while the compensation channel can add further shares over time as awards vest and are exercised or settled in stock. Compensation expense and warrants outstanding are not shares already issued, but both identify contractual routes to future issuance. Beneficial-ownership blockers and award-settlement alternatives can slow or reduce particular issuances; they do not eliminate the aggregate dilution potential.[3][4][14]

vTv’s dilution-management approach was to obtain capital through direct share issuance, Pre-Funded Warrants and Common Warrants rather than eliminate future issuance. The $0.01, non-expiring Pre-Funded Warrants defer legal issuance and pace individual exercise through ownership blockers, but their underlying shares are economically close to common stock and were included in vTv’s weighted-average share count for loss-per-share purposes.[11][12]

For existing shareholders, this structure means immediate dilution from financing shares already issued, further potential legal-share-count expansion as Pre-Funded and Common Warrants are exercised, and additional contingent issuance from compensation awards as they vest and are exercised or share-settled. The company’s stated reliance on equity-based funding and its ongoing use of service-vested options therefore place the financing and compensation dilution channels alongside one another.[12][11]

vTv’s charter does not give stockholders preemptive rights to buy newly issued securities to maintain their proportionate ownership. vTv stated that additional Class A issuances can dilute existing holders’ earnings per share and voting rights.[27]

The 13,714,356-share warrant balance could expand the Class A share count without giving existing stockholders a preemptive right to buy newly issued shares to maintain their proportionate ownership.[11][27]

## Equity financing strategy and shareholder impact

Equity financing matters to existing shareholders because it funds clinical development without requiring immediate product revenue, but it can increase the number of shares competing for the same economic and voting claim. vTv’s financing during fiscal 2023 through the third quarter of 2025 combined direct Class A common-stock issuance, pre-funded warrants, common warrants and an at-the-market, or ATM, program—an arrangement that lets a company sell shares incrementally into the public market.

The relevant question is not whether equity funding is useful to vTv. It plainly supplied cash for cadisegliatin development. The investor question is whether the financing structure, equity compensation and warrant commitments collectively preserve or dilute the ownership claim of a shareholder who does not buy additional securities.

### What do these trends collectively suggest about vTv’s equity financing

### What do these trends suggest about vTv’s dilution management approach?

Dilution management is the set of decisions that controls the amount, timing, price and form of new equity issuance. It can include limiting award pools, repurchasing shares, settling awards in cash, raising capital at higher prices, using debt or partnering proceeds instead of stock, or imposing issuance caps. vTv’s disclosures point to a different emphasis: preserving flexibility to obtain funding and to manage concentration or timing in particular instruments, rather than a demonstrated company-wide policy to minimize share-count growth.

The first evidence is the form of completed financing. The February 2024 private placement raised approximately $51.0 million through common stock and pre-funded warrants; the ATM added $2.5 million through direct common-stock issuance; and the August 2025 private placement generated approximately $80.0 million through Class A shares, pre-funded warrants and common warrants.[5][3] These are not isolated plans that remained unused. They are completed financings that used share-linked instruments to provide cash.

The company’s financing approach has some mechanisms that can moderate issuance timing. The TD Cowen ATM is optional: vTv is not required to sell shares under the agreement.[5][3] While public float remains below $75 million, vTv cannot sell under the relevant registration statement in any 12-calendar-month period Class A stock with a value exceeding one-third of public float.[3][4] That restriction constrains one particular registered facility, and the option not to sell gives management discretion over timing.

The company used that discretion in a limited way through September 2025. It sold 179,400 ATM shares in 2024 and reported no ATM sales in either the three or nine months ended September 30, 2025.[3][4] The lack of 2025 ATM issuance does not mean vTv avoided equity financing: it completed the much larger $80 million private placement during the same year.[3] The correct interpretation is that management shifted the form of equity financing, not that it eliminated equity-linked dilution.

The ATM’s practical constraints may also make it less reliable as a sole dilution-management tool. vTv stated that use of the facility depends on share price and trading volume.[5][4] An issuer facing a low market price or thin trading may not be able to sell enough shares through an ATM efficiently, which can make a negotiated private placement more attractive despite its immediate dilution and potential warrant overhang.

The pre-funded warrants include beneficial-ownership blockers. A blocker restricts a holder from exercising warrants if doing so would cause that holder and its affiliates to beneficially own more than a specified percentage of common stock after exercise. vTv’s warrant terms generally use 4.99% or 9.99% thresholds, depending on the holder, and permit a holder to change the threshold on at least 61 days’ notice up to a maximum of 19.99%.[1][3][4] These limits can prevent any one holder from taking delivery of a very large number of shares at once.

The limits do not eliminate aggregate dilution. A holder can exercise over time, multiple holders can exercise separately, and some holders can increase their cap with notice. vTv itself describes future issuance under equity incentive plans and exercise of outstanding warrants as potential causes of dilution to stockholders’ percentage ownership and potential stock-price declines.[1][5] **Beneficial-ownership blockers are concentration and timing controls, not a reduction in the total number of warrant shares that can ultimately become outstanding.**

The same distinction applies to the structure of pre-funded warrants. The February 2024 warrants were immediately exercisable at $0.01, could be exercised at any time after issuance and did not expire.[1][29] At December 31, 2024, 3,970,587 pre-funded warrant shares remained available for exercise.[1][5] The nominal exercise price means the investors had already paid almost all of the economic purchase price, so exercise is less dependent on raising cash than exercise of a conventional higher-price warrant.

The August 2025 financing added more of both types of warrant. The common warrants were exercisable at $22.71 per Class A share, or $22.70 per pre-funded warrant, and expired on the earlier of the fifth anniversary of issuance or 90 days after announcement of positive top-line CATT1 data.[3][30] The common warrants therefore have a more conventional economic exercise decision: whether holders exercise may depend on the trading price relative to the exercise price and the remaining term. The pre-funded warrants, in contrast, primarily delay issuance and ownership reporting rather than representing a large future cash-funding source.

The 2024 Equity Incentive Plan also includes design features that can affect the pace of dilution. It allows awards in several forms: incentive and nonstatutory options, stock appreciation rights, restricted stock, restricted stock units, performance stock, performance cash and other stock awards.[14] Some forms can be settled in cash or a mix of cash and stock.

For example, restricted stock units may be settled in shares, cash equivalent, a combination, or another form of consideration chosen by the board.[14] The plan permits net exercise for nonstatutory options, under which the company reduces the number of shares delivered by the number needed to cover the aggregate exercise price.[14]

Those provisions create tools that could reduce gross shares delivered in individual award settlements. They do not show that vTv used cash settlement, net exercise, open-market repurchases or another offsetting approach during the period. The 2025 nine-month equity statement did report 4,258 net shares withheld for tax withholding on equity-award settlements.[3][4] Share withholding can reduce the number of shares delivered to award recipients, but it is an award-settlement mechanism, not evidence of a broader program designed to maintain the ownership percentage of all existing shareholders.

The plan’s reserve mechanics favor continuing award capacity. The 2024 Plan began with a reserve of up to 750,000 shares, composed of 742,525 new shares, 7,475 shares remaining under the prior plan and returning shares from forfeitures, expirations, withheld shares or similar events.[14] It also provides an annual January 1 increase through January 1, 2034 equal to 4% of the combined total of the preceding December 31 capital stock outstanding and shares issuable or issued upon exercise of outstanding warrants priced at $0.01 or less, unless the board reduces or eliminates that increase before the applicable January 1.[14][19][13]

The board’s power to reduce or eliminate an annual increase is a real governance control. But it is conditional and discretionary rather than a disclosed commitment to cap dilution. The plan has already added 286,408 shares to the reserve for 2025.[13] In addition, vTv’s broader authorized but unissued common shares are available for future issuance without another stockholder vote and may be used for public offerings, acquisitions or employee benefit plans.[22] The company’s post-reverse-split authorization remained 200,000,000 Class A shares, far above the issued Class A count in the period.[1][3]

The company’s conduct is consistent with preserving access to this capacity. In 2024 it replaced the prior plan with the 2024 Plan, establishing an initial 750,000-share reserve comprising 742,525 new shares and 7,475 prior-plan available shares, with qualifying returned shares added separately as they become available.[14] It reported 705,593 stock options outstanding at December 31, 2024, compared with 249,247 at December 31, 2023, and reported 461,499 options granted during 2024.[1][5] By September 30, 2025, it reported 1,007,306 outstanding options and approximately $6.2 million of unrecognized stock-based compensation expense for outstanding options expected to be recognized over 2.6 years.[3][4] These figures support the conclusion that equity compensation remained an active, expanding component of the compensation system.

The February 2024 financing also introduced governance conditions that affect future issuance decisions. The company implemented board voting procedures requiring approval by at least five directors for certain corporate actions, including issuance of new debt, equity or debt- or equity-like instruments, for three years following the securities purchase agreement.[29] Lead investors also had rights to designate three director nominees while they held specified portions of the securities purchased.[29] These provisions may impose process discipline around new financings, but they protect negotiated investor rights and approval processes rather than set an economic ceiling on total dilution.

The February 2024 financing agreement also limited annual automatic increases to the equity-plan reserve to 4% of outstanding common stock plus certain lead-investor warrants exercisable at $0.01 or less, absent lead-investor approval. Because that calculation base can grow, the requirement is not a cumulative cap on dilution.[45]

Participation rights add another constraint. The February 2024 private-placement investors had the right to purchase their proportionate share of certain future financing transactions.[1][5] Such rights may let existing financing investors maintain their stake by participating in later offerings.

That can limit dilution for those investors if they exercise their rights, but it does not protect a non-participating public shareholder from dilution. It can also complicate future fundraising if a new investor wants a larger allocation than the participation rights permit.

The company’s disclosures are direct about this risk. vTv said that it may sell Class A common stock, convertible securities or other equity securities through the ATM or pursuant to warrants issued to previous investors and lenders, and that such sales could result in substantial dilution to existing investors.[1][5] It also said that equity or convertible-debt financing could dilute common stockholders and potentially include liquidation or other preferences adverse to common-stockholder rights.[1][5] This is not language consistent with a disclosed share-count ceiling or with an assurance that dilution will be offset.

Judgment — vTv’s approach is best characterized as financing-flexibility-oriented dilution management. Management appears to manage how issuance occurs—through optional ATM sales, holder-level blockers, vesting schedules, board approvals and the ability to adjust annual plan increases—rather than managing toward a disclosed target for fully diluted shares, ownership preservation or net share-count reduction.

There are practical reasons for that posture. vTv had no drug-product revenue, expected development expenses to continue or rise, expected to need substantial additional funding and faced ongoing operating losses.[1][5][4] In those circumstances, strict limits on equity issuance could constrain clinical development or force the company toward debt covenants, unfavorable licenses or asset-rights concessions.[1][5] The company must balance ownership dilution against development and survival risk.

For existing shareholders, the important distinction is between a constraint and a protection. The Form S-3 public-float limit constrains ATM issuance. A warrant blocker constrains a particular holder’s immediate exercise. Vesting conditions constrain when employees receive awards. Board approval rules constrain internal authorization. None of these, individually or collectively, prevents future private placements, warrant exercise, stock-option exercise, equity-plan grants or other issuances that reduce a non-participating holder’s percentage ownership.[1][3][4][14][29]

The investor conclusion follows. **vTv used optional ATM issuance, warrant ownership blockers, award vesting and a board-adjustable plan reserve to govern aspects of issuance timing, concentration and capacity, while its completed placements expanded issued shares and warrant rights. The observed record therefore shows preserved access to equity capital and equity-based compensation, not a disclosed company-wide policy to preserve existing holders’ percentage ownership.**[5][4][13][14]

That approach can support continued clinical execution, but it leaves shareholders exposed to repeated dilution whenever the company uses the multiple issuance channels it has kept open.

The Plan’s capitalization-adjustment provision protects the economic rights of Plan participants, not the percentage ownership of public shareholders. For specified capitalization adjustments, the Board may proportionately adjust the Plan’s share limits and the share numbers and exercise prices of outstanding awards to prevent dilution or enlargement of participants’ rights.[19]

Ownership and board-designation rights also shape the governance setting for financing decisions. At September 30, 2025, MacAndrews held 1,490,090 Class A shares, representing approximately 37.8% of combined voting power, while the February 2024 private-placement investors retained rights to designate three directors subject to their continuing ownership conditions.[11][19]

A useful additional measure of dilution management is annual equity-award burn rate: shares granted during a year divided by shares outstanding. Tracking that measure alongside option forfeitures, exercises, repurchases and net-share settlements would show whether the company is merely preserving award capacity or is limiting the rate at which compensation expands the share base.

### What is the potential impact on existing shareholders as vTv

The principal shareholder impact is dilution: a reduction in each existing shareholder’s percentage ownership when new shares are issued to finance operations, settle equity awards or satisfy warrant exercises. If a holder owns 1% of the company before an issuance and does not buy new shares, that holder owns less than 1% afterward. Whether the financing creates value or reduces value per share is a separate economic question from the mechanical reduction in ownership percentage.[5][9][28]

vTv expressly identified this risk. It said future sales and issuances of Class A shares or rights to purchase Class A shares, including issuances under equity incentive plans and exercise of outstanding warrants, could dilute stockholders’ percentage ownership and cause the stock price to fall.[1][5] The company also warned that substantial sales of Class A stock, or the perception that they may occur, could reduce the market price and make it harder to sell equity securities later at an appropriate time and price.[1]

The risk is not only ownership percentage. Dilution is therefore not automatically value-destructive; it is value-destructive when the capital raised does not create sufficient value relative to the shares and rights issued.

Judgment — The investor’s central question is whether vTv can convert equity-financed runway into clinical and commercial value faster than it expands the fully diluted share base. The company has bought time and funded development, but its repeated use of pre-funded warrants and common warrants means the denominator in that calculation is unusually important.

The shareholder impact is intensified by vTv’s continuing capital needs. As of September 30, 2025, the company had an accumulated deficit of $319.6 million, anticipated continuing losses and negative cash flow from operations, and expected to need additional capital.[3][4] It continued to evaluate direct equity investment, licensing and monetization options.[3][4] Existing holders therefore face not just the overhang already disclosed, but the probability of future financing decisions after the current cash runway is used.

The final implication is straightforward. **Existing shareholders have already experienced Class A share growth from completed financings, face material additional dilution from outstanding options and warrants if exercised or settled, and remain exposed to further equity issuance because vTv expects to need more capital before product revenue can fund operations.**[5][3][4]

The benefit is continued funding for cadisegliatin development; the cost is a potentially much smaller proportional claim on future value for shareholders who do not participate in subsequent equity financings.

The term “exercisable” requires one further distinction. A warrant may be legally exercisable but subject to a beneficial-ownership blocker, as vTv’s pre-funded and common warrants were.[4][32] Before exercise, the underlying shares generally are not outstanding for voting purposes and do not carry voting rights.

After exercise and issuance, they become common shares and add to the voting and economic share count.[22] The blocker can delay that moment for an individual holder; it cannot erase the contractual right or eliminate the aggregate warrant-share commitment.

The 2024 plan’s purpose does not guarantee employee retention. The plan expressly states that neither the plan nor an award gives an employee, consultant or director a right to continued service.[14] It does, however, create a compensation design in which recipients typically must remain in service through vesting dates to receive the full value of their awards. That is the usual retention mechanism in option-based compensation.

vTv’s employment and director arrangements provide examples. The company’s 2024 director compensation policy included an appointment option for 6,000 shares that generally vests monthly over three years and an annual election or re-election option for 3,000 shares that generally vests on the one-year anniversary, with vesting subject to continued board service.[29] For options granted February 27, 2024 to certain executives, the proxy states that they vest in equal quarterly installments over three years beginning on that date.[29] These structures can motivate service continuity but also create an ongoing pipeline of potentially issuable shares.

The company also has mechanisms that can limit issuance in individual situations. Shares under the plan may come from authorized but unissued stock or reacquired common shares, including shares repurchased in the market.[14] The plan allows withholding shares to satisfy tax obligations and permits net exercise for certain options.[14] These provisions mean that not every award must result in the gross delivery of all underlying shares.

Yet the company’s disclosures do not identify an active repurchase program or a stated commitment to offset compensation dilution with reacquired shares during the period.

Judgment — For valuation purposes, investors should separate three layers of the capital structure: issued shares, economically share-like pre-funded warrants, and conventional higher-price options and common warrants. The first is current ownership. The second is often close to current ownership in economic substance because exercise costs are nominal. The third is contingent on exercise economics, vesting and contractual terms. Combining all three without distinction can overstate immediate legal dilution; ignoring the latter two can materially understate potential dilution.

The company’s risk disclosures support that framework. vTv said future sales or issuances of Class A shares or rights to buy Class A shares could dilute percentage ownership and reduce share price.[5] It also stated that raising capital through equity or convertible debt could dilute common holders and may involve preferences adverse to common-stockholder rights.[3][4] In a company with no product revenue and continued capital needs, those statements are not generic boilerplate detached from the funding model; they describe the mechanism through which clinical development has been funded.

The relevant financing context therefore supports several conclusions. First, vTv has repeatedly converted investor capital into clinical runway through equity-linked transactions. Second, it has used pre-funded warrants as a major financing instrument, which defers some share issuance while leaving an economically substantial share commitment outstanding. Third, it has maintained an ATM and an expandable equity-plan reserve, preserving future issuance capacity. Fourth, it has layered executive, employee and director equity compensation on top of financing-related warrants.

The conclusion for shareholders is not that every outstanding right will become a share. Options can be forfeited or expire, common warrants may not be exercised, holders may be blocked temporarily, and RSUs can sometimes settle in cash. The conclusion is that vTv has assembled several independent channels through which the Class A share count can increase, and its continuing financing need makes those channels commercially relevant rather than merely theoretical.[14][3][4]

The equity-compensation arrangements also linked executive incentives to retention, long-term performance and financing execution. The 2024 Equity Incentive Plan states that awards are intended to secure and retain recipients’ services, motivate their efforts and allow them to benefit from increases in Class A stock value; vTv also stated that it relies significantly on equity grants to attract and retain key employees and compete for executive talent.[19] CEO Paul Sekhri’s employment agreement provided options for 55,000 Class A shares at a $31.61 exercise price, with 25% vesting on the first anniversary and the remaining 75% vesting quarterly over the following three years, subject to potential performance-based acceleration.[19] CFO Steven Tuch’s agreement provided for an additional option award equal to 0.6% of outstanding Class A shares after the February 2024 financing, vesting quarterly and conditioned on stockholder approval of the 2024 Plan.[19] These terms make continued service and, for the Sekhri award, specified performance outcomes economically relevant to recipients, while the financing-linked Tuch award also tied equity compensation to capital-raising activity.[19]

Paul Sekhri’s inducement option also allowed pro-rata vesting for certain terminations.[19][29]

The February 2024 securities purchase agreement also required vTv to use commercially reasonable efforts to offer and sell securities for cash and/or obtain cash consideration through a royalty or licensing agreement relating to a preclinical or clinical drug candidate other than TTP 399, producing at least $30.0 million of aggregate gross proceeds. This commitment, together with the investors’ participation rights and right to buy a proportionate share of up to an additional $30.0 million of common stock, embedded further financing activity and potential share issuance in the 2024 funding framework.[36]

During the six months ended June 30, 2024, vTv modified 14,340 options to extend their exercise period and 7,590 options to accelerate vesting in connection with director resignations following the February 2024 private placement; the modifications reduced stock-compensation expense by $0.1 million.[16]

Under the 2022 agreement with G42 Investments, vTv sold 259,657 Class A shares for an aggregate purchase price of $25.0 million, funded partly in cash and partly through a promissory note; vTv received $12.0 million in February 2023 in full satisfaction of that note after a 3.75% discount.[1]

For 2024, vTv reported 577,349 Class B shares, together with their corresponding vTv Units, as potentially dilutive securities; each Class B share and corresponding vTv Unit is exchangeable for one Class A share or cash at vTv’s option.[5]

### Synthesis —

vTv’s financing record shows an equity-led development-capital model: the company raised approximately $51.0 million in a 2024 private placement, $2.5 million through ATM stock sales and approximately $80.0 million in a 2025 private placement, while retaining $47.5 million of ATM capacity at September 30, 2025.[5][3] Equity compensation and a large warrant balance add separate future issuance channels; vTv reported 13,714,356 equity-based warrants outstanding and exercisable at September 30, 2025, plus 1,007,306 outstanding options.[3] The company has timing and concentration controls, but they do not create a company-wide dilution cap.

The financing strengthens clinical runway and can create shareholder value if cadisegliatin succeeds; the stronger pattern over the period is continuing reliance on share-linked capital that can reduce ownership percentages for holders who do not participate.[4][5]

1. **Track fully diluted exposure, not only issued Class A shares:** include outstanding options, pre-funded warrants, common warrants and available equity-plan capacity in the shareholder’s monitoring model.
2. **Treat pre-funded warrants as economically near-share equivalents:** their $0.01 exercise price and lack of expiration make their potential dilution more immediate than conventional higher-price warrants.
3. **Monitor financing form as closely as financing size:** an ATM sale, a common-stock placement and a warrant-linked placement can raise similar cash amounts while producing very different future share-count paths.
4. **Evaluate clinical progress against dilution:** the key investment test is whether the cash raised and shares committed generate enough cadisegliatin value to outweigh the reduction in each existing share’s proportional claim.

Derived — At September 30, 2025, vTv’s 1,007,306 outstanding options plus 13,714,356 equity-based warrants represented 14,721,662 potential shares; warrants represented 93.2% of that combined options-and-warrants pool.[3]

### Open questions

The ultimate dilution from options and warrants remains contingent on vesting, forfeitures, exercise decisions, exercise prices relative to vTv’s future market price, and any cashless-exercise provisions. The resulting ownership effect would be settled by the future number of shares issued or issuable under outstanding awards and warrants relative to the then-current common shares outstanding, together with any repurchases, cancellations, or other share-count offsets.
