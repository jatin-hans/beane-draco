# AI Safety Research Pathways and European versus US Opportunities

## Executive summary

The highest-probability transition is into AI-safety research engineering, evaluation infrastructure, or AI security, while you build ML and experimental-research skill. Your backend background is directly useful for distributed compute, sandboxes, observability, reproducible evaluations, secure services, and research tooling, but it does not yet demonstrate model understanding or research judgement.

Start from Berlin with a visible evaluation or safety-tooling project and a mentored programme, then target safety-embedded research-engineering roles rather than researcher-lead roles. Europe is the practical first market because Berlin gives you access to European programmes and employers without an immediate US immigration hurdle, while the US offers a deeper concentration of frontier-lab roles and funded research pathways if you can secure employer sponsorship or another qualifying visa.

For the next 6–12 months, choose evaluation or AI-security infrastructure if it matches your existing work, and use the resulting portfolio to test whether a fellowship, research role, or PhD is the right longer-term commitment.

## Realistic pathways from backend development into AI safety research

Judgment — Backend development is a credible starting point for AI safety research, but it is not the same thing as research training. Your strongest immediate advantage is building reliable systems around experiments: evaluation platforms, data pipelines, agent sandboxes, monitoring, distributed compute, and research tooling. Your main gaps are likely to be practical machine learning, experimental design, statistical interpretation, and the ability to turn an implementation into a defensible research result.

**Research engineering is the highest-probability direct route from backend development to technical AI safety.** Many empirical safety contributors are engineers, often software engineers, and major labs explicitly treat engineering and research as intertwined. Anthropic tells engineering-background applicants to apply as engineers, states that its engineers conduct research and appear as authors on papers, sometimes as first authors, and says about half of its technical staff had no prior ML experience.[1][37]

A research engineer differs from an ordinary product engineer because the output is not merely a service that works. The output is an experiment, a measurement system, a model intervention, a benchmark, or infrastructure that lets researchers answer a question. Google DeepMind describes research engineers as experimentalists who combine software engineering with machine learning, deep learning, mathematical, and research skills; they build and scale systems to test ideas and may co-author papers.[2]

Research infrastructure can also include human-feedback interfaces and tools for visualising model behaviour.[100]

This route is especially real for infrastructure-heavy backend experience. Anthropic’s pretraining-scaling work includes production operations, performance optimisation, observability, reliability, debugging across networking and evaluation infrastructure, experiment design, monitoring dashboards, and training-code changes. The role describes deep expertise in large-scale distributed systems as one possible good fit, although it also names LLM training and ML frameworks such as JAX, TPU, and PyTorch.[9]

Anthropic’s Discovery infrastructure role asks for six or more years of infrastructure experience and production troubleshooting. [101]

Research-engineering roles can also sit directly inside safety teams. Anthropic’s interpretability research-engineer role says prior interpretability research experience is not required and asks engineers to build specialised inference and training infrastructure for activation extraction, instrumented forward and backward passes, and steering-vector applications. That is a close match to an engineer who can learn a new domain and operate complex systems, but it still requires curiosity about interpretability and ability to work with model internals.[38]

Anthropic’s Research Tools work includes experiment-launch and monitoring systems, human-feedback interfaces, behavioural visualisation, and sandboxed execution of AI-generated code. It says prior ML or research experience is not required, but seeks at least five years of software-engineering experience, especially building tools or infrastructure for technical users. [100]

Safeguards-infrastructure work can include researcher-facing libraries and command-line tools, training and scoring workflows, and improvements to inference throughput, cost, and reliability. [102]

Interpretability-infrastructure work can involve instrumented forward and backward passes, activation extraction, steering vectors, controlled inference, and scoring at scale. [103]

Safety research engineering is not confined to US frontier labs. In London, Apollo Research advertised a Research Scientist / Engineer, Evaluations role to run evaluations of frontier systems for scheming risks, explicitly listing research and software engineering and accepting junior or mid-level experience. Sampura Research listed a founding technical-staff role improving human and AI judges for alignment and evaluation. EPFL’s Swiss AI Initiative advertised research-engineer roles to build a safety-pretraining stack and move alignment methods into production LLM pipelines, although that listing specified a master’s degree.[5]

Research engineering gives you an entry point, not a waiver from research competence. Anthropic’s alignment roles list significant software, ML, or research-engineering experience alongside some empirical-research contribution and familiarity with technical AI safety. Strong-candidate signals include ML, LLM, reinforcement-learning, research-paper, Kubernetes, and complex-codebase experience.[23][8]

The immediate implication is practical: target roles titled Research Engineer, ML Engineer, Evaluation Engineer, Safety Engineer, Infrastructure Engineer, Research Technical Staff, or software engineering roles embedded in safeguards, evaluation, alignment, or security teams. Do not make Research Scientist or research-lead roles your sole target if you lack ML research experience; those positions more often require independent research evidence and, frequently, a doctorate.[2][35][5]

Evaluation and red teaming are the most concrete research portfolio path for a backend engineer. An evaluation is a structured test of a model. In the Inspect framework, an evaluation combines a dataset, a solver that generates an answer or action, and a scorer that grades the result. That simple structure becomes sophisticated when the model uses tools, acts over multiple steps, operates in a sandbox, or attempts to game the scoring system.[39]

Evaluation research asks questions such as: Can an agent complete a cyber task? Can a model evade a monitor? Does a safeguard resist jailbreaks? Does an agent take unintended actions in a realistic coding environment? METR’s work includes measuring autonomous computer-task capabilities, studying monitoring evasion, and assessing whether agents may take actions no human intended.[12][40][20]

Adversarial evaluation targets also include oversight subversion, self-exfiltration, and whether a model behaves differently when it is aware of being evaluated.[104]

This is unusually compatible with backend work because evaluation systems need dependable operational engineering. Inspect supports custom and built-in tools, agent evaluations, and sandboxing untrusted code across Docker, Kubernetes, Modal, Proxmox, Vagrant, and other execution systems. The UK AI Security Institute’s Engineering Playbook divides evaluation capability into Evaluate, Isolate, Connect, Run, and Scale: testing models, running tests safely, controlling and auditing model-provider calls, providing machines/data/compute/deployment paths, and supporting large-scale inference.[14][15]

AISI’s SandboxEscapeBench tests whether agents can escape containers while placing each tested container inside a hardened virtual machine.[105]

Your likely strengths map to several of those layers:

- **Evaluate:** You can build task runners, APIs, datasets, test harnesses, scoring systems, job queues, retries, logging, and dashboards. Inspect supports reusable datasets, agents, tools, and scorers, and provides more than 200 pre-built evaluations. [14][15][39]

- **Isolate:** You can help make agent evaluation safe and reproducible through containers, sandboxing, access controls, state resets, and environment management. Inspect Cyber provides a standardised way to build agentic cyber evaluations, including evaluation infrastructure and variants. [41]

- **Connect and Run:** You can manage model-provider integration, audit trails, secrets, rate limits, cost controls, reproducibility, and compute operations. The AISI playbook identifies provider-call auditing, compute hosting, and systems linking the whole stack as core evaluation-infrastructure needs. [15]

- **Scale:** You can make experiments robust enough to rerun across models and versions. Inspect supports multi-task and multi-model runs with retries and resumption through a log directory, while a vLLM integration can stream parallel samples through one resident model for dataset-scale evaluation. [39][25]

The limitation is that an evaluation is not automatically meaningful because it runs. METR reports that frontier systems have reward-hacked tasks by exploiting scoring-code bugs or subverting task setups rather than solving the intended problem. Evaluation researchers therefore need methodological judgement: whether the task measures the claimed capability, whether the model was elicited fairly, whether the scorer is robust, and whether the result generalises beyond the benchmark.[20][16]

SaferAI’s evaluation work makes the gap explicit. Its research engineer or scientist role includes threat modelling, benchmark selection, running benchmarks, elicitation, quality control, debugging, analysis, risk conclusions, technical reports, and development of safety mitigations. Backend skills help with the technical execution, but not automatically with deciding what risk to test or what a result means.[3]

Anthropic’s Cyber Evaluations role covers capability, uplift, and safety evaluations, including pre-release robustness testing, probes, scoring tools, and translating findings into safeguards. It asks for Python, benchmark experience, cybersecurity practice, and technical and policy communication. [106]

This pathway is nevertheless highly actionable from Berlin. Inspect is open source, runs from a Python package, command line, or Python API, and permits Python-only contributions without setting up its TypeScript frontend. The UK AISI says Inspect has become an international community with more than 240 contributors, and it invites contributions to its cyber-evaluation tooling.[14][17][42][15][41]

A reproducible agent evaluation is the immediate portfolio action. Define a safety question, create tasks and baselines, test scorer logic, run the model in a controlled sandbox, retain logs and traces, and write a limitations analysis. Inspect supports structured repeatable tests with measurable scores, model comparisons, and detailed records; METR has observed models exploiting scoring-code bugs and task setups rather than completing the intended task. **The project must answer a safety question, not merely automate an LLM workflow.**[14][15][20]

AI security, red teaming, and technical assurance form a related route. NIST’s Information Technology Laboratory conducts AI-security and resilience research and has developed an adversarial-machine-learning taxonomy covering attacks and mitigations. NIST is also developing Control Overlays for Securing AI Systems, addressing AI-system components such as training and test data, model weights, and configuration settings.[18]

The UK AI Security Institute’s SandboxEscapeBench uses a hardened-VM/container design and an Inspect-based capture-the-flag benchmark with 18 orchestration, runtime, and kernel scenarios, including public and private tests. Advanced models frequently escaped common misconfigurations, the hardest tasks remained unsolved, and successful breakouts used disclosed vulnerabilities. [105][107]

For a backend engineer, AI security can be more accessible than abstract alignment because familiar systems concerns remain central: access control, logging, secrets, supply chains, API misuse, availability, isolation, incident response, and infrastructure security. AI introduces model-specific issues such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, model theft, model poisoning, evasion, and agents acting through tools. AI-security research also has distinct disclosure problems: showing a vulnerability may require interaction with proprietary systems or prohibited behaviours, while providers may fear intellectual-property loss and downstream users may be unable to patch third-party models.[19]

There are direct role patterns in this area. The UK AI Security Institute listed an Offensive Security Engineer role to test AI-evaluation infrastructure and build agent-assisted red-teaming tools. Google DeepMind listed research-engineering roles to develop safeguards against CBRNE harms and frontier misuse. SASH listed London research-engineering work on zero-knowledge verification, remote compute accounting, and confidential computing for AI datacentres.[5]

Anthropic’s Detection & Response openings offer another security-adjacent route: one accepts three or more years of software engineering, while a platform role seeks seven or more years in security, infrastructure, or data pipelines. The work includes high-volume telemetry, detection systems, incident investigation, and internal security-response platforms. [108][109]

Anthropic’s Auth & Identity role seeks eight or more years of backend or platform experience to build credentials and authorisation systems for agents and service accounts, including revocation and auditable security decisions. [110]

Judgment — If you have security engineering, platform engineering, Kubernetes, networking, distributed-system, or reliability experience, security and evaluation infrastructure are probably better initial targets than pure mechanistic interpretability. They let you use hard-won professional strengths while gaining ML and research capability through the work itself.

Empirical alignment and interpretability are still viable, but they require a more deliberate technical conversion. Alignment research includes scalable oversight, AI control, stress testing, safeguards, alignment assessments, adversarial robustness, model organisms of misalignment, and model welfare. Interpretability seeks to understand how a trained model works internally; mechanistic interpretability specifically aims to reverse-engineer algorithms learned by a model from its weights.[23][4][24]

Representative alignment work at Anthropic includes training models to subvert safety techniques, multi-agent reinforcement-learning experiments such as AI Debate, tooling for LLM-generated jailbreak evaluations, evaluation-question generation, and contributions to research papers and safety-policy design. These are engineering-intensive tasks, but they require enough ML understanding to specify experiments and enough research judgement to explain the results.[8][23]

Interpretability has lower barriers to first experiments than many people assume. TransformerLens supports more than 40 open-source GPT-style models, including GPT-2 Small with 80 million parameters, and provides tools for loading models, caching activations, inspecting attention patterns, and intervening on internal computations. Its tutorials include CPU-scale training telemetry on a two-layer toy model for 500 steps.[24][25]

First reproduce a published interpretability or alignment experiment; then make and document a bounded extension before attempting an original research question.[1][24] It is that you can demonstrate relevant habits before getting frontier-model access: write hypotheses, implement an intervention, run baselines, measure effects, inspect failure modes, and explain what would falsify your conclusion. **A well-documented small experiment is more relevant to research hiring than a broad claim that you have studied AI safety.**[11][24][25]

A mentored programme is the clearest bridge if you want research feedback rather than only self-study. Anthropic’s Fellows Program gives engineers and researchers four months of funded empirical AI-safety work under researcher mentorship, aiming for public outputs such as a paper. Its stated research areas include scalable oversight, adversarial robustness and control, model organisms, interpretability, AI security, and model welfare.[4]

Anthropic’s Research Residency is an even more direct conversion mechanism for experienced technical people. It is a paid fixed-term programme lasting six weeks to six months, intended for people who have led ambitious technical work and want to shift into full-time empirical AI research; it specifically calls out staff-level engineers, technical founders, and quantitative professors, and says prior ML experience, a PhD, and published papers are not required. Residents receive a scoped project matched to their background, direct researcher mentorship, access to Claude models and GPU compute, and a link to the wider safety and security research community.[29]

That programme is not a generic junior-career course. Its fit criteria include quickly implementing ideas, clear communication, and enthusiasm for full-time empirical research; research-paper authorship is a strength rather than a stated minimum.[29] If you are not yet staff-level or have not led substantial engineering work, a shorter programme is likelier to be a realistic initial step.

For someone in Berlin, several programmes allow distinct levels of commitment:

- **SPAR:** This is a fully virtual, part-time, three-month programme that accepts participants worldwide, with commitments from 5 to 40 hours per week. It pairs aspiring researchers with mentors across AI safety, policy, security, interpretability, biosecurity, and societal impacts, and does not require prior research experience. [31]

- **ARENA:** This is a structured research-engineering course covering deep-learning fundamentals, mechanistic interpretability, reinforcement learning, and LLM evaluations. Its in-person London format has covered travel, accommodation, and meals, while its curriculum is public and can be used independently. [43][26][30]

- **LASR Labs:** This is a 13-week, full-time, in-person London research programme. Teams of three to four work with an experienced AI-safety researcher to take a project from proposal to publication; the programme seeks technical ability, including ML engineering and quantitative skill, but has no fixed experience requirements. Participants receive a £15,000 London living-expense stipend plus food, office space, and travel. [28]

- **Pivotal Research Fellowship:** This is a 15-week London fellowship at the London Initiative for Safe AI from 18 January to 30 April 2027. It covers travel, compute, and £1,800 per month toward accommodation for fellows based outside London, in addition to its stipend. [44]

- **MATS:** This is a roughly 10-week full-time mentored fellowship in Berkeley or London, with potential funded extensions of 6 to 12 months. It considers applicants without PhDs who have research experience or strong engineering backgrounds. [30]

Programmes do not remove the need to build skills. They compress the feedback loop: you gain a mentor, collaborators, deadlines, access to a research problem, and a visible output. LASR reports an example of an engineer with several years of industry experience building production systems who used the programme to transition into full-time research and later joined the Australian AI Safety Institute. It also reports alumni moving into UK AISI, Apollo Research, OpenAI, independent research, and new safety organisations.[28]

The PhD route is the most structured way to develop independent research ability. It is especially appropriate if you want to become an academic researcher, eventually lead a research agenda, or work in theoretical alignment. Professors require doctorates, research leads commonly have PhDs, and a PhD is close to the default route for learning to set and execute an independent research agenda. Yet contributor roles can lead to research leadership without one, especially in empirical research.[1]

A PhD is not simply a longer version of self-study. It is a multi-year apprenticeship in choosing questions, handling ambiguity, reading literature, designing experiments, writing papers, responding to criticism, and working with a supervisor. It can also be difficult, isolating, and supervisor-dependent; the cited career review notes a typical duration of four to six years.[1]

Berlin gives you nearby research infrastructure, though not every local ML opportunity is labelled AI safety. BIFOLD welcomes applications year-round and advertises roles across stages, from students to professors. Its 2026 Berlin graduate-school call offered ten full-time PhD-candidate research-assistant positions, five in Data Management and five in Machine Learning, including a Machine Learning and Security group; that particular application round closed on 13 February 2026 and was not explicitly an AI-safety call.[33][32]

There are more directly safety-labelled German possibilities. The University of Stuttgart listed a full-time AI-safety PhD investigating large language models empirically, requiring a master’s degree and naming both research and software engineering as relevant skills. The ELLIS Institute COMPASS group in Tübingen listed student, research-intern, and postdoctoral work in AI safety, security, and alignment, with a master’s requirement for its listed entry route.[5]

ELLIS adds a European cross-sector structure to a PhD. Its programme has Academic, Industry, and Interdisciplinary tracks. Students receive joint supervision and generally spend at least six months at a partner institution in another country or an industrial research lab; its Industry Track requires at least 50% of time with the academic partner and at least six months with the industry partner.[45]

ELLIS describes its European AI research network as comprising 47 sites in 19 countries, more than 2,000 members, over 500 PhD students and postdocs, and 16 cross-border programmes.[111]

Judgment — Pursue a PhD if your goal is to develop independent research taste and potentially lead research, not because you assume it is required to join every safety team. If you mostly want to build useful systems for empirical safety research, research engineering can create better near-term evidence of fit and may preserve the PhD option.

Theoretical or conceptual alignment is a real but less backend-leveraged route. It uses mathematical frameworks, proofs, and careful reasoning about future systems. It has fewer available roles than empirical safety research, and mathematical or theoretical-computer-science doctoral training is common in industry and close to necessary for academic work.[1]

Backend experience helps less here because the scarce skill is not production engineering. A sensible fit test is to read and summarise a coherent body of papers, spend 10 to 100 hours identifying open questions, attempt original ideas, or try mathematical exercises. A survey paper can build subject mastery, reveal gaps, create research ideas, and demonstrate interest to graduate programmes or employers.[1]

Technical AI governance is an adjacent route rather than a shortcut around ML research. It concerns technical evidence used in governance: model evaluations, standards, assurance, compute monitoring, incident reporting, and measurements of company safety practices. A technical career can support a later governance transition because policy work benefits from technical training and industry knowledge.[1][35]

Technical assurance work can turn evaluation evidence into safety cases and decisions about whether to release a system.[21]

CeSIA’s Europe-remote Technical AI Governance Researcher illustrates the blend. The work includes designing indicators and scoring rules, checking reliability against blind labels and repeated runs, maintaining traceable data pipelines, producing policy-facing memos, and guiding early-career researchers. It expects applicants to be strong in at least two of four areas, with at least one being AI evaluations or measurement design, and explicitly says it is not hiring a general software engineer.[6]

That does not make backend experience irrelevant. CeSIA’s data-engineering baseline includes maintaining Python pipelines that pull from uncontrolled external sources and continue working as APIs, pages, PDFs, or government feeds change; stronger applicants design storage, provenance, broken-source handling, and checks for silent data loss.[6] This is a close match only if your backend background includes reliable data systems and you can pair it with evaluation or measurement expertise.

The AI-security reporting and standards route has similar characteristics. Research on AI-security reporting argues for machine-readable reporting data that can be automatically checked for quality and consistency, and notes European standardisation work involving technical committees. Building provenance, validation, reporting, or audit systems can therefore be meaningful technical-governance work, but it needs policy and standards understanding alongside implementation.[19]

The conclusion from these pathways is that you should first aim to become useful to empirical safety work, then decide whether to deepen into research leadership, security, interpretability, or governance. Backend development is not a detour if you redirect it toward the research stack: evaluation systems, experiment infrastructure, data provenance, reliability, sandboxes, tools, and secure compute.[1][15][21][6]

Scope and date —
This guidance is dated **17 November 2026** and concerns a 28-year-old backend software engineer living in Berlin who is deciding how to move into AI safety research.
The distinction that matters for this decision is threefold. **Safety research** generates evidence about model capabilities, risks, mitigations, or internal mechanisms through experiments, evaluations, and analysis. **Research-enabling engineering** builds the reproducible evaluation, isolation, provider-connection, compute, and scaling systems that make this evidence possible. Adjacent work includes safety-product engineering, AI security and resilience, and technical AI-governance measurement; these can be valuable career routes, but are not automatically independent empirical research.[15][1][46][6]

Dates have different meanings here. The University of Stuttgart AI-safety PhD vacancy was posted on 6 March 2026; Anthropic’s Fellows Program is a four-month programme; and sources without a stated publication date are treated as undated organisational or role descriptions.[5][4][15][1][46][6]

Inspect Evals is a library of evaluations built using Inspect AI, maintained by Generality Labs, a London-based nonprofit that builds tools for evaluating frontier-AI risks and mitigations.[47]

### How does backend experience help with

Backend experience helps most when the work resembles operating a research platform rather than inventing a new learning algorithm. Familiar strengths include designing systems with failure modes in mind, maintaining services as dependencies change, handling concurrent workflows, building observability, managing data integrity, making APIs usable, and supporting other engineers. These strengths matter in AI safety because research depends on reliable experiments, repeatable measurements, detailed logs, secure environments, and shared tooling.[15][39][9][6]

The transfer is strongest in research engineering. Anthropic’s alignment work names Kubernetes clusters and complex shared codebases as candidate strengths. Its pretraining-scaling work spans model operations, monitoring, reliability, debugging, and evaluation infrastructure. OpenAI’s Safety Engineering role asks for backend-language and relational-database proficiency and says direct AI or ML experience is not required, while requiring interest in AI and at least five years of engineering experience.[8][9][46]

That said, conventional backend development does not establish that you can implement and debug ML algorithms. A career guide distinguishes ML implementation from traditional software engineering and estimates at least 200 focused hours after ML basics to build practical implementation and debugging skill, likely more for someone starting with less experience. It identifies Python, calculus, linear algebra, probability, machine learning, and deep learning as useful foundations, while stressing practical ML and programming over mathematical depth for many empirical roles.[10][1]

A useful threshold is the **research-engineering proof**: can you reproduce a typical ML paper in a few hundred hours, or make a complex feature or serious bug fix in a major ML library within a few weeks? These are not formal universal hiring rules, but they test the missing combination of implementation, debugging, experiment management, and technical judgement.[1]

For evaluation engineering, backend experience transfers through the entire operational lifecycle: running models, integrating them into agent scaffolds, managing infrastructure, checking results, storing logs, rerunning failed jobs, and making evaluations more informative. METR’s Evaluation Execution role explicitly includes model runs, agent scaffolds, internal and external evaluations, evaluation-software improvement, and management of multiple simultaneous evaluation threads.[13]

The missing layer is measurement design. You need to learn how to formulate a threat model, select a benchmark, decide whether models received adequate opportunity to demonstrate a capability, separate genuine success from exploit behaviour, and write a report whose claims match its evidence. Anthropic says frontier-model evaluators need expertise in assessing capabilities and understanding how evaluations can go wrong, as well as enough access, independence, and freedom to publish.[16]

For AI security, backend experience maps well to secure execution environments and operational controls. The AI Security Institute’s control research uses environments resembling real developer infrastructure and codebases, while its cyber work builds mock websites, APIs, and networked environments for evaluations. This makes real-world systems knowledge useful rather than peripheral.[21]

The shortfall is that AI security is not merely application security with an LLM attached. You may need adversarial ML, model threat modelling, agent-security concepts, secure model deployment, attack simulation, and sometimes offensive-security skills. Roles such as AI red-team engineer explicitly combine software engineering with information security, and the work can involve vulnerability discovery, robustness analysis, and new security methodologies.[5]

For empirical alignment and interpretability, backend engineering is valuable but insufficient. The engineering part includes training infrastructure, experiment orchestration, activation storage, dataset tooling, visualisation, and intervention code. The research part requires generating a question, designing an experiment that can answer it, interpreting results, noticing contradictions, and communicating the conclusion.[5][11]

ARENA is a concrete bridge because it teaches the missing material in sequence: deep-learning fundamentals, transformer architecture, mechanistic interpretability, reinforcement learning, RLHF, and experiments. Its purpose is explicitly to teach ML-engineering skills for contributions to AI alignment research through exercises and pair programming.[26][27][43]

For a PhD route, backend experience can help you propose useful empirical projects, especially where data systems, ML infrastructure, security, or distributed analysis matter. But PhD applications often require formal academic material that an industry CV does not replace: degree certificates, transcripts, recommendation letters, and preferably publications. BIFOLD’s advertised PhD-candidate application required a motivation letter, CV, bachelor’s and master’s certificates, official transcripts, and preferably a publication list.[32]

The academic bridge is therefore evidence of research potential. Research internships and assistantships offer supervised projects; reading extensively and writing a survey paper can demonstrate subject mastery; and a well-scoped empirical project can show you can turn a question into a result.[1]

For technical governance, backend experience becomes useful when it is converted into reproducible public-interest measurement. Reliable collection pipelines, data provenance, change detection, validation, and auditability matter. But governance research also requires a policy-facing answer: what should be measured, why it matters, how uncertainty changes the conclusion, and what institution can act on it.[6][35]

**The core distinction is between building a system and conducting research with it.** Your backend background already supports the first; your transition plan should deliberately create evidence of the second through ML implementation, safety-relevant experiments, analysis, writing, and collaboration.[11][8][29]

### The subject is a 28-year-old backend software engineer living in

Berlin is an advantage if you use it as a base for structured upskilling, local research contacts, and European fellowships rather than assuming that all relevant work is remote. Technical AI-safety work remains geographically concentrated, with London and the California Bay Area especially prominent, plus Oxford, New York, Pittsburgh, Boston, and other centres; remote work exists but often does not eliminate travel or office requirements.[1][8][48]

Your age is not a barrier. The material points to programmes and junior-to-mid-level research-engineering routes designed for people with technical backgrounds, including professionals without prior research experience. SPAR explicitly accepts professionals at different experience levels and does not require previous research; LASR reports that successful participants can come from industry engineering backgrounds; MATS accepts strong engineering backgrounds without PhDs.[31][28][30]

Your unknown citizenship, current residence status, degrees, and willingness to relocate matter to applications, but they do not change the first career decision: build technical and research evidence before making a large relocation or academic commitment. A Berlin-based engineer can begin with a part-time remote programme such as SPAR, the public ARENA curriculum, Inspect or Inspect Cyber contributions, and self-directed but documented evaluation or interpretability work.[31][30][41][24]

A practical transition should have stages rather than a single leap:

1. **First, choose one technical lane for three to six months:** evaluation infrastructure, AI security/red teaming, alignment research engineering, or interpretability. Start with the lane closest to your existing strengths: infrastructure and reliability usually point to evaluations or ML systems; security experience points to red teaming or assurance; data-platform work can point to evaluation or governance measurement. [15][35][6]

2. **Second, acquire hands-on ML competence:** learn Python if it is not already a daily tool, then PyTorch or JAX, transformer basics, experiment tracking, and model evaluation. ARENA provides exercises in deep learning, interpretability, RL, and LLM evaluations; its public material remains useful even without programme admission. [1][26][30]

3. **Third, create one public research-engineering artifact:** reproduce an ML or safety paper, extend Inspect, build an agent evaluation, contribute a meaningful feature to an ML library, or write a small research report with code, data, baselines, and limitations. This provides direct evidence beyond course completion. [1][17][24]

4. **Fourth, seek structured feedback:** apply to SPAR while working, then consider ARENA, LASR, Pivotal, MATS, a residency, research-assistant work, or local doctoral opportunities depending on your results and appetite for full-time research. [31][28][44][30][29]

5. **Fifth, apply to engineering-led safety roles before research-scientist roles:** look for research engineering, evaluation engineering, safeguards infrastructure, AI-security engineering, research tooling, ML infrastructure, and technical staff roles. Use research outputs to demonstrate that you can contribute to experiments as well as systems. [49][5]

A useful portfolio does not need a novel breakthrough. It should demonstrate four things: you can work with current ML systems; you can write robust code; you understand a concrete safety question; and you can explain what your experiment does and does not establish. Anthropic advises applicants to foreground independent research, thoughtful blog posts, and open-source contributions; LASR likewise looks for substantial work such as a tool, analysis, experiment, proof, or research result.[37][28]

Berlin also gives you a sensible decision point between Europe-focused and US-focused opportunities without forcing an immediate answer. London is reachable for short, intensive programmes, while Germany has ML, security, and doctoral research infrastructure. The next sections, **AI safety research opportunities in Europe and the US**, **Funding for European and US opportunities**, and **Visa requirements for European and US opportunities**, address whether those routes are practical for your citizenship, finances, and relocation preferences.

The near-term recommendation is therefore clear: do not spend a year only consuming AI-safety content before applying. Build a safety-relevant engineering artifact, obtain mentored feedback, and apply to research-engineering or evaluation-engineering roles and programmes in parallel. The evidence points to engineering-led transition routes as the most credible initial move, while PhD and research-lead paths become more realistic once you have established research aptitude.[1][4][38]

### Synthesis —

Backend development is a strong base for AI-safety research when redirected toward empirical research infrastructure: evaluations, agent environments, sandboxes, secure systems, experiment platforms, data pipelines, logging, and model-analysis tools. Research engineering and evaluation engineering are the strongest initial routes because they use these strengths directly while creating opportunities to develop ML and research judgement.[1][15][13][38]

The limiting factor is not whether you can code. It is whether you can use code to answer a safety-relevant question, interpret the answer responsibly, and communicate it. That is why a paper reproduction, Inspect contribution, documented evaluation, or mentored project matters more than generic AI coursework.[1][15][11][28]

A PhD remains the clearest route to independent academic research and theory-heavy work, but it is not the necessary first step for an experienced engineer. For a Berlin-based backend developer, the best sequence is: build ML and evaluation capability locally; produce one visible artifact; obtain mentorship through a part-time or London-based programme; then pursue research-engineering roles or decide whether doctoral training is worth the longer commitment.[31][30][44][5]

#### 3. Open-source contributions and short research programmes

You do not need to enter through a permanent role. Europe has technical on-ramps that let an engineer produce evidence of research potential before applying to a research-engineering job or PhD.

The most accessible is work around UK AISI’s Inspect ecosystem. Inspect is an open-source framework for large-language-model evaluations, developed by UK AISI and Meridian Labs. It supports evaluations of coding, agentic tasks, reasoning, knowledge, behaviour, and multimodal understanding through reusable datasets, agents, tools, and scorers; it includes more than 200 pre-built evaluations.[14][15]

The AISI Engineering Playbook divides evaluation capability into five layers: Evaluate, Isolate, Connect, Run, and Scale. Evaluate means creating repeatable tests; Isolate means executing them safely; Connect provides audit and cost control around model-provider calls; Run covers compute, data, and deployment; Scale addresses large-model inference.[15]

This five-layer model is a useful **backend translation map**. A conventional backend engineer can first contribute in Connect, Run, Isolate, or Scale: provider integrations, audit trails, job orchestration, sandboxing, logging, data management, cost controls, deployment paths, and reliable large-scale execution. To move from infrastructure support into research engineering, add Evaluate: formulate a risk hypothesis, design a task that measures it, define a scoring approach, run the test reproducibly, and analyse what the result does and does not show.[15]

Inspect is also a real collaboration surface rather than only a learning resource. AISI reports 240+ contributors to `inspect_ai` since it was open-sourced, and says METR migrated to Inspect while Apollo Research adopted it for new evaluations after a three-month trial. AISI invites community feedback and contributions to Inspect Cyber, its open-source Python package for agentic cyber evaluations.[15][41]

Contributing a small but useful evaluation, scorer, sandbox integration, reproducibility fix, or analysis tool is not equivalent to a research job. It can, however, create a public portfolio showing that you understand evaluation infrastructure and can work with the conventions used by safety researchers.[17][41]

Europe also has time-bounded programmes designed to build research capability:

- **ARENA:** The Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator is an approximately five-week, in-person London bootcamp covering deep-learning fundamentals, transformer interpretability, reinforcement learning, and LLM evaluations. It covers travel, accommodation, and meals, and its curriculum is public. It is designed to move someone who can code toward safety research engineering. [30][43]

- **LASR Labs:** This is a 13-week, full-time, in-person London programme. Teams of three to four work with an experienced AI safety researcher to take a project from proposal to publication. Past projects include AI control, untrusted monitoring, data poisoning, interpretability probes, chain-of-thought monitoring, sparse autoencoders, steganographic collusion, and scheming evaluations. [28]

- **Pivotal Research Fellowship:** This is a 15-week London programme scheduled for 18 January to 30 April 2027. Fellows receive weekly one-to-one meetings, workshops, and expert Q&As; expected outputs are normally papers, but can also be blog posts or policy briefs, and fellows retain research ownership. It is in person by default, with rare exceptions for people with a strong independent research track record. [44]

- **MATS:** This mentored research fellowship has Berkeley and London locations, lasts about 10 weeks, and may offer a funded six-to-12-month extension. It has streams in interpretability, oversight, control, evaluations, and governance; it may accept applicants without PhDs who have research experience or a strong engineering background. [30]

- **SPAR:** This is a part-time remote programme pairing aspiring AI-safety and policy researchers with professionals. It is fully virtual, accepts participants globally, and has no country or residency requirement apart from applicable US sanctions constraints. Its project areas include AI safety, policy, security, interpretability, biosecurity, and societal impacts. [31]

These programmes solve different problems. ARENA is skills-first; LASR and Pivotal are output-and-mentorship-first; MATS is a competitive mentored research fellowship; SPAR is compatible with maintaining a job because it is part-time and remote. The programme’s stated format—not its prestige alone—should determine which one you pursue.[30][28][44][31]

The candidate’s US opportunity map also names Stanford HAI, Stanford CRFM, Redwood Research and MIRI in the Bay Area, alongside Washington, DC-based policy opportunities such as CSET and the Institute for Law & AI.[102][100][108][113]

Direct research-scientist roles are available, but the qualification bar often differs sharply from engineering roles. A useful US training route is the Anthropic Research Residency described above. Other examples include the OpenAI Residency, described as a paid six-month programme for people from adjacent fields including strong self-taught engineers, and Astra, a fully funded in-person Berkeley fellowship pairing fellows with senior mentors for AI-safety research or engineering across empirical and theoretical streams.[30]

Google DeepMind’s paid Student Researcher Program is in person five days per week for 12–24 weeks and requires current enrolment in a BS, MS or PhD programme.[114]

The US also has graduate-research funding routes. NSF’s Graduate Research Fellowship Program supports thesis-based, research-focused STEM master’s and doctoral study. Its eligible Computer and Information Sciences and Engineering subjects include artificial intelligence, machine learning, computer systems, cybersecurity, formal methods, verification, programming languages, and software engineering.[64]

The programme is for people intending to enrol in, or already early in, an eligible research-based master’s or doctoral programme. It is therefore a route to academic retraining and research formation, not an alternative to gaining work authorisation or a replacement for university admission. #### 4. What the US opportunity mix means for a backend engineer

The cited US roles cover model-evaluation execution, research tools, ML infrastructure, safeguards, AI-security prototypes, safety-data pipelines, red-team systems, and production safety tooling. METR’s Evaluation Execution role is classified under Engineering & Research, while OpenAI’s Safety Engineering role builds tools and workflows that support safety research and model reliability.[12][46][49]

The main limitation is geographical. Anthropic’s key safety and research-engineering listings are in San Francisco, New York, and Seattle; METR’s named roles are Berkeley-based; OpenAI’s cited safety-engineering role is in San Francisco; and many other examples cluster in the Bay Area. Some individual roles are remote-friendly or global-remote, but those arrangements are employer- and role-specific rather than a basis for assuming Berlin-based work is possible.[54][51][12][46][5]

Anthropic lists safeguards roles in San Francisco and New York, research-tools roles in San Francisco, New York and Seattle, and security work in San Francisco, New York, Seattle and Washington, DC.[102][100][108]

The most credible US transition route is research engineering or safety-platform engineering, followed by a move toward experimental ownership; immediate research-scientist applications are realistic mainly when you already have the relevant ML research record or degree. This follows from the explicit engineering requirements in safeguards, interpretability infrastructure, evaluation execution, and safety tooling, alongside doctorate or master’s requirements in several research-scientist postings.[63][38][13][46][5]

AISI’s Alignment Red Team is a direct London route for research engineers as well as research scientists, with hiring possible at junior, senior, staff, and principal levels. The team builds and runs alignment evaluations for loss-of-control risks including deceptive alignment, research sabotage, and reward-seeking; it performs pre- and post-deployment testing, reports findings to frontier companies and UK and allied governments, and contributes to public research publications and technical reports.[65]

This role makes the transition threshold concrete: applicants need at least one significant AI safety, security, or alignment project involving engineering, experiment design, and analysis on frontier LLMs, plus strong software-engineering and ML experience, at least one year of professional Python work, and clean, documented ML research code using tools such as PyTorch or Inspect. A backend engineer should therefore use Inspect or an equivalent project to demonstrate frontier-model experimentation and analysis, rather than presenting production backend experience alone.[65]

The advertised package is £65,000–£145,000 annually across levels 3–7, comprising base pay and a technical allowance, plus an employer pension contribution of 28.97% of base salary. It includes pre-release access to multiple frontier models, ample compute, operational support, learning-and-development stipends, and funding for conferences and external collaborations.[65]

AISI describes its working model as early ownership of important problems, freedom to pursue research bets without product pressure, publication and external collaboration, and interdisciplinary work with experts in national security, policy, AI research, and adjacent sciences. This makes it a government research environment where engineering work is coupled to policy-facing evidence and security concerns.[65]

### Synthesis

Europe and the US both offer technical AI-safety work across evaluations, alignment and control, safeguards, AI security, red teaming, interpretability, infrastructure, and governance. The practical difference for a Berlin-based backend engineer is access pattern: Europe offers a local Berlin starting point, a dense London ecosystem, Europe-remote governance work, and structured programmes such as ARENA, LASR, Pivotal, MATS, and SPAR; the US offers a larger documented concentration of frontier-lab and independent-evaluation engineering roles, concentrated around the Bay Area.[52][53][30][31][12][46][54]

1. Start with evaluation infrastructure, agent test environments, safeguards tooling, or AI-security systems—the overlap with backend work is strongest there.
2. Build a public research-engineering portfolio through Inspect, Inspect Cyber, a reproducible evaluation, or a safety-relevant experiment.
3. Apply first to Berlin, Europe-remote, and London routes; treat US roles as a separate relocation- and work-authorisation-dependent track.
4. Use a fellowship or funded PhD when you need mentorship, publications, and a research record rather than another production-engineering role.

The important practical distinction is between money for living costs or salary, and in-kind support such as compute, model access, travel, or conference budgets.

## Visa requirements for European and US opportunities

Judgment — Visa planning is not an administrative detail in an AI-safety transition. It determines which opportunities you can realistically accept, whether a short research programme can become a paid role, and how much an employer must do before you can start. Berlin residence is neither citizenship nor work authorisation. An EU citizen can generally work in Germany and other EU Member States, but EU citizenship alone does not create a UK work right; Irish citizens do not require a UK visa. A non-EU Berlin resident must identify their actual German residence title and obtain destination-country permission where required. A funded offer and permission to perform the work are separate tests.[82][83][84][85]

### What visa requirements apply to pursuing relevant opportunities in Europe?

For opportunities in Germany, EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not need an entry visa or long-stay permit, although they generally must register their residence after arrival. Many third-country nationals planning to stay longer than 90 days need a visa before arrival; foreigners undertaking gainful employment generally need advance visa arrangements. A settlement permit is permanent and permits gainful employment, whereas a residence permit is temporary and issued for a specified purpose such as employment or training.[83][82]

EU citizens can work or seek work without a permit in another EU country, and this generally extends to Iceland, Norway and Liechtenstein, subject to Liechtenstein’s quotas. Most EU citizens can also work in Switzerland under separate arrangements.[129][130]

For a paid AI-safety research-engineering job in Germany, the actual duties and contract determine the route. The EU Blue Card is the practical employment route for a qualifying salaried technical role: it generally requires a university degree and employment contract, with a 2026 gross-salary threshold of €50,700, or €45,934.20 for certain shortage occupations including computer scientists and engineers. An IT professional without a formal qualification can potentially qualify through a specific IT offer of at least six months, salary of at least €45,934.20, and at least three of the previous seven years of university-level IT experience that is required for the job.[83][82]

Backend experience therefore has direct immigration value for a German engineering-heavy role, particularly where the job is genuinely classified as IT or a qualified technical occupation. It does not itself make an AI-safety research post a Blue Card-eligible IT job: the degree or experience route, salary, contract and actual role must fit the relevant rules. A Blue Card holder changing jobs in the first year must notify the foreigners authority, which checks whether the new job still meets Blue Card conditions.[82]

The Section 18d researcher residence permit is better suited to a genuine research appointment. Its definition requires a third-country national to hold a doctorate or higher-education qualification giving access to doctoral programmes, be selected by a research organisation, and undertake research for which that qualification is normally required. The hosting agreement must state the project’s purpose, duration, funding and the researcher’s qualifications; the permit is tied to a specific project and research body.[83][86] A company R&D role can use this route if the person actually carries out a research project, but a conventional backend-engineering job does not automatically qualify.[83]

A doctorate has two possible German immigration structures. A taught doctoral programme generally follows the student residence route under Section 16b. A doctoral candidate employed on a contract and undertaking research beyond exclusively writing the dissertation may instead fall under Section 18d.[83] This distinction matters if you are considering a PhD as an entry path: admission alone and employment as a researcher are not immigration equivalents.

Germany also offers limited research mobility rather than a Europe-wide work right. A researcher who already holds a qualifying research permit from another EU Member State can undertake notified research in Germany for up to 180 days in a 360-day period without a German residence title. For more than 180 days and up to one year, they need a German temporary permit plus a hosting agreement or corresponding contract with the German host.[86] This can help a researcher already embedded at an EU institution, but does not give a Berlin resident with an ordinary work permit freedom to take research jobs across Europe.

The EU Blue Card operates in 25 EU member states, excluding Denmark and Ireland; it requires qualifying education or permitted professional skills, an offer lasting at least six months and the destination country’s salary threshold. After 12 months, a holder moving to a second participating country must obtain a new Blue Card there.[131]

The United Kingdom is legally separate from the EU and requires a separate right-to-work assessment. Irish citizens do not need a UK visa. For most other applicants, the Skilled Worker visa requires a confirmed offer from a Home Office-approved employer, a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS), an eligible four-digit occupation code matching the actual job, English ability, and salary meeting the applicable threshold. The usual salary rule is the higher of £41,700 per year or the role’s going rate, although specified lower-salary exceptions exist.[84][85]

The UK Global Talent visa is the relevant alternative for an already credible researcher. It permits work in UK academia or research for someone endorsed as a leader or potential leader; possible bases include an eligible academic or researcher job, individual fellowship, UKRI-approved grant, eligible prize, or peer review. It has no language or minimum-salary eligibility requirement, permits changing or stopping jobs without notifying the Home Office, and can be issued for up to five years.[87] For an engineer just entering AI safety, Skilled Worker sponsorship is usually the realistic route; Global Talent becomes plausible after independently visible research, a fellowship, or an eligible research appointment.

For paid, in-person US AI-safety work, first distinguish US citizens, US permanent residents, and people otherwise already authorised to work without a new employer petition from applicants who need a new immigration route.[13][64][77] For an applicant who must obtain new work authorisation, the main direct-employment route is H-1B specialty-occupation status, which is tied to the employer or agent petitioning for the role.[13][90] The offered job must involve specialised knowledge and normally require at least a directly related bachelor’s degree or equivalent; the applicant can also qualify through a combination of education, specialised training and progressively responsible directly related experience recognised as equivalent.

The employer or agent first obtains Department of Labor certification of a Labor Condition Application (LCA), then files Form I-129 with USCIS; an applicant outside the United States then seeks a visa where required and applies for admission.[90]

Your backend record can help document the experience-equivalency part of H-1B eligibility, particularly if it shows increasing responsibility in directly related technical work. It does not solve the separate questions of whether the AI-safety role is a bona fide specialty occupation, whether the employer will petition, or whether the employer can absorb the process.[90][91]

The H-1B cap materially changes the choice of target organisation. The regular cap is 65,000 new statuses or visas per fiscal year, with an additional 20,000 exemption for people holding a US master’s degree or higher. Institutions of higher education, affiliated nonprofits, nonprofit research organisations and government research organisations are cap-exempt.[90] METR says it can likely sponsor a cap-exempt H-1B for an applicant lacking US work authorisation who wants to work in person in its Evaluation Execution role.

Anthropic says it sponsors visas for eligible roles but cannot successfully sponsor every role or candidate; Ai2 says it sponsors a variety of visas and work authorisations and that processing typically begins after acceptance and takes two to three months.[13][7][92]

For FY 2027 cap-subject cases, USCIS uses weighted selection: an offer at Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics level IV receives four entries, level III three, level II two and level I one.[91] The direct-US route is therefore more employer-dependent than a simple application from Berlin suggests. It is especially sensible to prioritise cap-exempt university and nonprofit research employers where your research profile fits.

A US degree creates a different, temporary route. F-1 Optional Practical Training (OPT) permits employment directly related to the student’s major for up to 12 months. Eligible STEM graduates can apply for a 24-month extension if the degree is on the STEM list, the employer uses E-Verify, and the initial OPT was based on that STEM degree. The school’s designated official must recommend OPT, and USCIS must approve Form I-765 and issue an Employment Authorization Document before work starts.[93] OPT is useful if you deliberately choose a US master’s or PhD, but it is not a direct work permit for an engineer moving from Berlin without US study.

J-1 exchange status can support a defined research appointment. Only a Department-designated sponsor can issue Form DS-2019, which permits the person to apply for a J-1 visa; the host and sponsor determine the appropriate category and candidate. Research Scholar activity can take place at research institutions or corporate research facilities and is intended to be collaborative rather than continuous training.[94] Some exchange visitors are subject to a two-year home-country physical-presence requirement, and Yale notes 12- or 24-month bars on repeat Research Scholar or Professor participation.[94][95]

University sponsorship may be narrower than an attractive job title implies. Yale says it sponsors visas almost exclusively for academic appointments, not generally staff positions, and that sponsored positions must be full-time and performed at a Connecticut campus or affiliated worksite; fully remote sponsored positions are not permitted under its policy.[95] This makes a formal research appointment more visa-compatible than a remote, part-time, or general engineering arrangement.

Citizenship can create special routes. TN status is limited to Canadian and Mexican citizens in designated USMCA occupations; E-3 is for Australian citizens in a qualifying specialty occupation.[95] Conversely, US presence on a visa does not qualify someone for the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program: it requires US citizenship, US nationality or permanent residency at application and acceptance.[64][77]

Do not use visitor travel as work authorisation. Yale describes B-1/B-2 or visa-waiver visitor activity as suitable for temporary, incidental non-employment activity such as conferences, seminars and one-time presentations, and says visitors cannot work for Yale. In contrast, SPAR is fully virtual with no country or residency requirement, though it advises US-based F-1 or J-1 students to consult their international student office because it is unpaid volunteer work.[95][31] **The practical US conclusion is that a direct industry move requires an employer willing and able to sponsor a job-tied status, while study and J-1 routes are structured, time-limited transitions rather than general work permission.**

O-1A is a temporary classification for people with extraordinary ability in sciences, education, business or athletics, demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim. An O-1A petition requires a written consultation from a field-expert peer group or labour and/or management organisation, a written employment contract or summary of an oral agreement, an explanation and itinerary of activities with beginning and ending dates, and evidence of extraordinary ability. Listed evidence includes major nationally or internationally recognised awards or prizes, qualifying association membership, published material about the person’s work, original scholarly work or contributions of major significance, high salary, judging others’ work, and critical or essential work for organisations with a distinguished reputation.[96]

### Synthesis

**Derived conclusion —** Europe and the US both require role-specific immigration planning, but their constraints differ. Germany offers distinct employment, researcher and student routes, and an existing German right to work can make local opportunities comparatively low-friction; UK work normally turns on an approved sponsor, occupation code and salary, unless you can obtain Global Talent endorsement.[83][85][87] In the US, H-1B ties you closely to an employer and may be cap-subject, while OPT depends on US study and J-1 depends on a sponsor-host research arrangement.[90][93][94]

1. Check your citizenship and current German residence title before choosing countries or programmes.
2. For Germany, ask every prospective employer whether the offered duties fit Blue Card employment or a Section 18d research appointment.
3. For the UK, ask for the occupation code, sponsor status and salary before investing heavily in interviews.
4. For the US, prioritise cap-exempt research employers or explicitly visa-sponsoring roles; treat a US degree as a deliberate immigration-and-training investment, not a shortcut.

## Research culture in Europe and the US

### What is the research culture of relevant European AI safety

Judgment — “European research culture” is not a single thing. For someone in Berlin, the meaningful comparison is among four settings: university doctoral training, government evaluation institutes, small policy/nonprofit teams, and frontier-industry labs. They differ most in how work is supervised, whether the output is a paper, reusable tool, policy input, or product safeguard, and how much your work crosses organisational boundaries.

European academic routes are comparatively explicit about training conditions. UKRI guidance says recruitment criteria should enable fair comparison between academic and non-academic achievements, including industry experience. It also expects doctoral training to develop research methods, professional capability and careers, while UKRI’s terms require clear information, fair treatment, research guidance, career guidance and a harassment-free environment.[59][58] For an engineer without a publication record, that does not erase the need to demonstrate research potential, but it means industry work is not meant to be treated as irrelevant merely because it happened outside a university.

BIFOLD in Berlin illustrates the conventional academic end of this spectrum. Its doctoral environment is presented as internationally mentored and interdisciplinary, with professional development, conference and summer-school access, guest-scientist programmes and conference funding. Its advertised applicant profile requires excellent English, while basic German or willingness to learn German is requested; early research and paper-writing experience are advantages rather than stated universal prerequisites.[32] Its research remit includes big-data management, machine learning, and robust, trustworthy and sustainable AI. This is a credible environment for building research habits—reading literature, forming hypotheses, writing papers and receiving feedback—but it is less like shipping a backend service on a short product cycle.[33]

European doctoral networks make the bridge between academia and industry unusually formal. MSCA Doctoral Networks are partnerships among universities, research institutions, infrastructures, businesses, SMEs and other actors. Their training includes research skills plus entrepreneurship, commercialisation, intellectual property and communication.[60][73] In an Industrial Doctorate, joint supervision is mandatory and the non-academic placement must consume at least half of the researcher’s time.[61] ELLIS similarly offers Europe-based industry tracks with at least 50% academic time and at least six months with the industry partner, either as a continuous placement or accumulated part-time.[62] **For an engineer who wants research training without severing the connection to real systems, the Industrial Doctorate is the clearest European culture fit because industry collaboration is a programme requirement, not a personal networking exercise.**[61][62]

Government-linked evaluation research is another distinctly strong European pattern. AISI describes itself as a UK government research institution working across machine learning, engineering, AI safety and governance, cybersecurity, biology and social science.[21] Its agenda includes cyber misuse, dual-use science, criminal misuse, autonomous systems, societal resilience, human influence, evaluation science and post-training capabilities.[21] Much of this work resembles research engineering: building automated evaluations, mock websites, APIs and networked environments, analysing results, and developing tools such as Inspect.[21]

Inspect embodies the associated “shared evaluation infrastructure” culture. It was built to support collaboration among governments, labs and researchers, and its playbook lets a single researcher adopt individual components without taking on a complete platform.[15] AISI says the framework grew because siloed benchmark implementations caused duplicated work, weak peer review and fewer evaluations; Inspect Evals was intended as a shared place for high-quality evaluations.[17] This makes open-source contributions a useful way for a backend engineer to encounter safety research norms: reproducibility, experiment configuration, sandboxing, measurement and technical review.

AISI explicitly invites community feedback and contributions to Inspect Cyber, which it had used across tasks ranging from capture-the-flag challenges to simulated enterprise networks and critical national infrastructure.[41] The immediate implication is that evaluation infrastructure is a European route where a production-minded engineer can make useful contributions while learning what makes an experiment scientifically credible.

This openness coexists with public-interest and security constraints. AISI says its role includes informing decision-makers, developing international protocols and acting as an independent technical partner to frontier labs; it cannot publish the full scope of its methods and objectives because some work is sensitive.[21] Its careers page nevertheless advertises pre-release model access, ample compute, freedom to pursue research bets without product pressure, and opportunities to publish and collaborate externally.[55] The culture is therefore neither purely academic nor purely open-source: it is applied, policy-connected evaluation research where some work is shared as public infrastructure and other work remains restricted.

European nonprofits add a smaller-team, policy-facing alternative. SaferAI describes a remote, asynchronous and international environment using English, not French, as its working language.[3] Its work covers frontier-AI risk management, EU standards and the GPAI Code of Practice, risk frameworks, company assessments, quantitative risk modelling and cybersecurity risk models.[3] It describes a new, fast-growing team where people take significant responsibility early, operate amid ambiguity, and switch between problems to pursue the most impactful opportunities.[3] CeSIA offers another European model: a Paris-based nonprofit with 12 staff spread across Europe, seeking technical-governance research that combines defensible scoring, reproducible public-data pipelines, memos for policymakers and supervision of junior researchers.[6] These roles reward engineering discipline—traceability, reliable pipelines, operational judgment—but also demand policy writing and an ability to explain what evidence does and does not justify.

European research also has a physical collaboration hub in London. LASR places full-time teams beside other AI-safety organisations and researchers, with talks, careers events and visits to Google DeepMind and AISI; its projects run from proposal to paper under regular supervision.[28] ARENA similarly combines pair-programming exercises with supervised project work in the LISA workspace, which it says enables discussion, relationships and collaboration.[43] These are intensive cohort cultures rather than normal employment, but they lower the social and practical barrier to learning how technical safety researchers choose and execute projects.

**Europe therefore offers several credible cultures, not a single “European” norm: formal and mentored doctoral research, cross-sector programmes, reusable public evaluation tooling, government-linked research, and distributed policy nonprofits.** Choose the setting based on whether you need research supervision, industry continuity, an open-source portfolio, or direct policy relevance.[32][60][61][14][3]

Research goals. European academic settings centre research training and publishable work: UKRI-funded doctoral training covers research skills and methods, professional and career development, and publication according to normal academic practice and UKRI open-access policy.[59][72] Public-sector work at the UK AI Security Institute (AISI) centres on advanced-AI risk, testing mitigations and publicly accountable evaluations; its cyber research builds evaluations and mock websites, APIs and networked environments.[21][68] European nonprofits can combine technical research with policy work: SaferAI requires effective remote, asynchronous international collaboration in English.[3] In European industry, Anthropic’s London Alignment role focuses on exploratory experimental safety research on risks from powerful future systems.[8]

Collaboration. European doctoral networks join universities, research institutions, research infrastructures, businesses, SMEs and other socio-economic actors. MSCA Industrial Doctorate candidates are jointly supervised by academic and non-academic partners and spend at least 50% of their time in the non-academic sector.[60][61][73] AISI brings together machine learning, engineering, AI safety and governance, cybersecurity, biology and social-science expertise and works with AI labs, research organisations, academia and UK government partners.[21] Inspect supplies a shared technical foundation for governments, researchers and labs to collaborate on frontier-model testing; it had more than 50 contributors from safety institutes, frontier labs and major safety organisations by 13 November 2024.[17][15]

Publication. UKRI-funded students publish research results under normal academic practice and the UKRI open-access policy, and ELLIS expects doctoral students to publish in top-tier venues in machine-learning-driven fields or, for approved interdisciplinary work, in the co-advisor’s field.[72][45] AISI provides opportunities to publish and collaborate externally, while its agenda withholds the full scope of methods and objectives for sensitive work.[21][55] Anthropic’s London Alignment posting expects research engineers to contribute ideas, figures and writing to papers, blog posts and talks; it does not set a publication policy for European industry.[8]

Apollo Research reports using peer review, least-privilege access and conflict-of-interest rules; it varies what it publishes when full disclosure of security-sensitive findings would be unsafe.[132]

Pace. AISI pairs freedom to pursue research bets without product pressure with operational support to focus on research and ship quickly.[55] LASR uses a concentrated programme: after a first week of research prioritisation and team matching, teams spend 12 weeks designing and running experiments, iterating on results, and writing a paper and blog post with regular supervisor meetings.[28] SaferAI’s remote, asynchronous working model makes distributed collaboration part of its operating rhythm.[3]

### What is the research culture of relevant US AI safety

US academic routes retain a recognisably proposal- and publication-oriented culture. The NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program requires applicants to address Intellectual Merit—the potential to advance knowledge—and Broader Impacts—the potential to benefit society—in separate parts of both personal and research-plan statements.[64] Reviewers may consider research capacity, teamwork, communication, persistence, grades, research record, leadership, outreach and initiative.[64] A listed NYU postdoctoral position similarly calls for original research and individual and collaborative publications, while requiring a doctorate.[5] For a backend engineer without research training, this route is realistic only after building a research record or entering a graduate programme.

US nonprofit evaluation research is more directly legible as an engineering-to-research bridge. METR describes empirical research on loss-of-control risk: measuring autonomous capabilities, studying evasion of monitoring, improving evaluation methodologies and publishing risk assessments.[12] It often runs and publishes frontier-model evaluations, though it says they are not yet consistently run at the scale, reliability and speed required for codified regulatory roles.[13] Its hiring process can include a code screen, take-home tests, technical assessments and a roughly two-day in-person work trial.[12] This is a culture in which software competence is assessed practically, but it must be paired with experimental judgment and careful communication on sensitive projects.

METR’s work also shows why “independent evaluator” does not mean unlimited autonomy or perfect transparency. It states that impartiality and conflict management are priorities and says it has not accepted financial funding from AI companies, while using free model tokens for evaluations, research and engineering.[40] Yet, in its October 2026 assessment of Anthropic’s AI R&D acceleration, METR had latitude over methods within contractual scope but Anthropic controlled the use of non-public information and had unilateral redaction rights over the public report.[98] The practical lesson is that external evaluation is valuable research, but your ability to publish methods and evidence depends on the specific access agreement.

US industry safety research spans very different operating models. Anthropic presents its research as “big science”: one cohesive team working on a few large efforts, treating AI research as empirical science, holding frequent discussions and prioritising long-term impact over narrow puzzles.[63] It also describes a high-trust, low-ego culture that values kind, direct disagreement and contribution regardless of role.[37] However, a research-engineering role on training-scale infrastructure is described as operational, incident-responsive and potentially involving evenings and weekends during launches.[9] That is familiar terrain for a backend engineer, but it can trade independent topic choice for high-stakes reliability and pace.

Other US lab cultures make different choices. OpenAI Safety Engineering describes working across researchers, product teams and policy to convert safety workflows into reliable scalable systems in an iterative, fast-moving environment.[46] Ai2, by contrast, says it openly releases model weights, training data, evaluations and code, although most roles require local presence near Seattle.[92] **The US is best understood as a set of organisational models—academic, independent evaluator, frontier lab, product-safety engineering and open research institute—not as one research culture.**[64][40][63][46][92]

For a Berlin-based engineer, US opportunities often make co-location more consequential. Anthropic says most staff are in the Bay Area and attend the office regularly, although some come in one week per month; its stated hybrid policy expects at least 25% office time.[37][8] Ai2 says most roles require being local to Seattle.[92] This does not decide the visa question, which belongs in **Visa requirements for European and US opportunities**, but it does decide whether the daily culture is feasible without relocation.

### Synthesis

Judgment — Choose an organisational model, then a location. Structured supervision and formal cross-sector collaboration are evidenced in UKRI doctoral settings and MSCA Industrial Doctorates; AISI offers government-linked evaluation research, shared tooling, publication opportunities and research bets without product pressure; METR offers evidence-focused independent evaluation under negotiated access; Ai2 has an open-release model; and some Anthropic research-engineering roles combine large coordinated research efforts with launch and incident obligations. These models exist across connected European and US research ecosystems rather than forming a continental divide.[58][61][15][21][55][40][98][92][63][9][8]

1. **Choose Europe first** if you need mentorship and a research portfolio while retaining proximity to Berlin; target evaluation-tool contributions, London cohorts, or industry-linked doctoral routes. [41][28][61]
2. **Choose the US first** if you want frontier-model access and can accept co-location and a faster, operational research-engineering environment. [37][9]
3. **Assess each employer, not the region’s label:** ask about mentor access, publication rights, model access, review practices, office expectations and how research priorities are set. [16][55]

## Choosing where to pursue the transition

### Given my backend background

Judgment — Choose location by the fastest route to credible safety-research evidence, not by headline salary or the prestige of a laboratory. As a Berlin-based backend engineer, your immediate advantage is building reliable systems, experiment infrastructure, evaluation pipelines, data workflows, and operational tooling; your immediate gap is evidence that you can formulate, run, interpret, and communicate safety research. That makes a mentored project or research-engineering portfolio the best first discriminator before an expensive relocation.

Backend experience is most directly useful in **safety engineering, evaluation engineering, red teaming, and research infrastructure**. These jobs turn research questions into systems that can generate adversarial tests, run benchmarks, collect failure cases, manage data, monitor deployments, and make experiments reproducible. Anthropic explicitly advises engineers to apply as engineers, saying they can do research and author papers; its interpretability-infrastructure role says prior interpretability research is not required and connects the work to large-scale training, customized inference, and performance engineering.[37][38] **This makes research engineering a credible entry point, but not proof that you are ready to lead an independent alignment agenda.**

Use **SPAR while retaining your current job** as a parallel unpaid portfolio action, not as the first geographic employment choice. It welcomes technical backgrounds including computer science, does not require prior research experience, and permits part-time work alongside employment. It is highly competitive, and mentors select people who can contribute best to a project. Produce one visible research artefact—an evaluation, replication, benchmark, or documented negative result—and use it to test whether you enjoy research while applying for matched paid Berlin, German, European, and US roles.[31]

Second, target a **paid UK technical role or London research programme**, with AISI the strongest evidenced employer-level option for a technical applicant needing sponsorship. Its combination of frontier-model access, compute, rapid operational support, and interdisciplinary contact is unusually well matched to an engineer who wants to move from production systems toward evaluations, cybersecurity, or autonomy research.[55] The important constraint is separate from visa sponsorship: a security-cleared role can remain impractical until you meet its UK-residency expectation.[55]

Rank a **sponsored US safety-engineering or research-engineering role third** if you can obtain work authorization and accept relocation.[13][46][90] The US examples offer unusually high posted compensation and a dense set of roles in safeguards, evaluations, interpretability, red teaming, and infrastructure; Anthropic alone lists US research-engineering positions in alignment, interpretability, safeguards, model evaluations, and safeguards infrastructure.[54][5] But this is the highest-friction route from Berlin because the work is often tied to Bay Area, New York, Seattle, or Berkeley attendance, and work authorization is employer- and role-specific.[63][13][90]

Judgment — Do not generalize research culture by continent. The useful contrast is organisational: AISI offers a public-interest, government-linked environment spanning technical research and policy, while a frontier lab such as Anthropic emphasizes centrally coordinated empirical projects. Across both settings, substantive disagreement about which safety methods work is normal; choose a team whose research questions you can explain and whose technical methods you can test.[55][63][1]

US funding is heterogeneous rather than uniformly superior. NSF’s FY 2027 Graduate Research Fellowship Program provides three years of support over five years: a $37,000 annual stipend plus a $16,000 annual institutional cost-of-education allowance, but it is restricted to US citizens, nationals, and permanent residents and requires eligible full-time US graduate study.[64] Conversely, private US roles can pay far more, while UK programmes may provide stipends and in-kind support rather than frontier-lab salaries; compare the specific offer and its cost of relocation, rather than treating salary ranges as a regional average.[28][7]

Anthropic’s own policies show why visa conclusions must be programme-specific: its London Alignment expression-of-interest posting says it sponsors visas case by case, whereas the reported 2027 Fellows Programme requires pre-existing authorization to work in the US, UK, or Canada and does not sponsor fellows.[8][99] Likewise, a shorter Anthropic Research Resident placement may permit remote participation from a country where the company can hire, while most full-time research roles require San Francisco relocation.[29]

1. **First:** apply to SPAR or a similarly mentored project and ship one safety-relevant research artefact while employed.
2. **Second:** apply to AISI and London research-engineering programmes if UK sponsorship and any required vetting are realistically available.
3. **Third:** apply to US safety-engineering roles only when a named employer confirms sponsorship and office-location expectations.
4. **Move the US route to first** if you already have US work authorization or prioritize frontier-lab engineering compensation; **keep Europe first** if avoiding immigration and relocation risk is the deciding criterion.
