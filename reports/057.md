# Array Technologies’ Segment Margins and Portfolio Strategy

## Executive summary

Array Legacy Operations was the consistently stronger gross-profit engine over the analysed period, while STI Operations added strategically valuable international reach and a complementary tracker architecture at a materially lower and more volatile reported gross margin.

The period-by-period calculations show that the Legacy–STI margin gap persisted from Q1 2023 through Q3 2024 and remained evident in the revenue-weighted nine-month comparison, including after using Array’s later recast Q1 2023 Legacy gross profit of $79.835 million on $305.204 million of revenue, or 26.16%.[1] The result supports prioritising Legacy’s linked-row platform and U.S. cost, pricing and domestic-manufacturing advantages as the portfolio’s near-term profit foundation.

STI remains strategically valuable for international customers, Brazilian and European manufacturing, dual-row product fit and cross-selling, but its margin record makes procurement, local sourcing, project selection, pricing discipline, manufacturing transfer and Brazil systems integration the priority. The margin differential demonstrates different reported gross-profit conversion, not by itself superior factory efficiency or proof that the STI acquisition has succeeded or failed.

This case covers business events and financial periods from Q1 2023 through September 30, 2024; dates when underlying results were published are distinguished from the periods those results describe.

## Business context and comparable margin basis

**Scope and cutoff —** This section assesses business events and financial activity through September 30, 2024.On January 11, 2022, Array acquired 100% of STI’s share capital with cash and Array common stock and began operating as Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations, its two reportable operating segments.[2] Array’s two reported operating segments are not simply two sales labels.

They represent a legacy U.S.-anchored tracker platform and an acquired international tracker business with different product designs, manufacturing footprints, customer bases and exposure to local market conditions.[2] That distinction is essential before comparing gross margins: a gross-margin gap can identify where reported gross profit is being generated, but it cannot by itself prove a difference in factory productivity, product quality or acquisition success.

### What do Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations contribute to

Array Technologies supplies utility-scale solar tracking systems: steel supports, electric motors, gearboxes and electronic controllers that move photovoltaic panels during the day to keep them oriented toward the sun. Array states that trackers increase energy production and typically reduce levelized cost of energy (LCOE), the lifetime cost per unit of electricity generated, relative to fixed-tilt systems that do not move. It sells systems, parts and services to engineering, procurement and construction firms (EPCs), utilities, large developers and independent power producers.[3][4]

**Array Legacy Operations contributes Array’s established linked-row tracker platform and its U.S. market position; STI Operations contributes a complementary dual-row platform, international manufacturing and international customer access.** The two segments therefore broaden the range of project conditions and geographies Array can serve, rather than representing interchangeable versions of the same business.[2][3]

#### Array Legacy Operations: linked-row tracker platform

Array Legacy Operations is the pre-STI legacy business. Its core offering is Array’s flagship linked-row single-axis tracker, commonly described as DuraTrack. The design uses one motor to drive multiple rows of solar panels through articulated driveline joints. Array states that the design is protected by a U.S. patent and that competitors must use designs it believes are less efficient and reliable in order to avoid infringement.[2]

Array attributes several customer and production advantages to this configuration:

- **Reliability:** Array says the linked-row design offers greater reliability than alternative designs that require more motors and moving parts. [2]
- **Installation and maintenance economics:** Array says the configuration reduces installation costs and maintenance requirements for customers. [2]
- **Manufacturing economics:** Array says the design supports competitive manufacturing costs. This is a company claim about the product’s economics rather than an independently measured cost comparison. [2]
- **Suitability for utility-scale deployment:** Array’s legacy platform forms part of a portfolio of integrated tracker systems for utility-scale solar projects, a market in which project developers weigh upfront equipment cost, installation labor, operating risk, energy yield and long-term maintenance. [3][4]

The strategic importance of the Legacy business is therefore not only its reported gross profit. It is Array’s established technical and commercial platform in the U.S. market, where the company described its tracker design as differentiated by patented linked-row mechanics and lower lifetime-cost characteristics.[3][2]

Array’s product development also broadened the platform beyond conventional sites. In September 2022, Array introduced a third tracker product designed to require significantly less grading and civil-works permitting before installation and to accommodate uneven terrain. The cited disclosures call this product an addition to Array’s portfolio, but do not assign it to either Array Legacy Operations or STI Operations.[2][5]

What follows: Array Legacy Operations should be understood as the differentiated core platform against which the acquired business is compared, not merely as the larger of two reporting segments.

#### STI Operations: acquired international platform

STI Operations is the business Array acquired from Soluciones Técnicas Integrales Norland, S.L.U. and its subsidiaries, collectively STI, on January 11, 2022. Array acquired 100% of STI’s equity interests for $410.5 million in cash and 13,894,800 Array common shares; the reported fair value of total consideration was $610.8 million. After the transaction, Array began reporting Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations as separate operating and reportable segments.[3]

STI’s principal product contribution was a dual-row tracker: one motor drives two connected rows of panels. Array describes this tracker as suited to sites with irregular or highly angled boundaries and fragmented project areas.[3][2] At announcement and closing, Array also described STI’s dual-row design as especially appropriate for markets with lower installation-labor costs and lower wind- and snow-load requirements than the U.S. market.[6][7]

STI also brought operating capabilities that extend beyond the product itself:

- **Manufacturing footprint:** STI was headquartered in Pamplona, Spain, with manufacturing facilities in Spain and Brazil. [3][8]
- **International sales and delivery:** STI designed, manufactured and sold utility-scale trackers in markets including Spain, Brazil, the United States and South Africa. [3][1]
- **Regional market access:** At the time of the acquisition announcement, Array described STI as having an established sales presence in Europe, Latin America, Australia and South Africa. [9]
- **Brazilian position and local supply chain:** Array said STI was the leading provider of solar trackers in Brazil and that its position was reinforced by local manufacturing and supply-chain presence. These were company statements in the acquisition announcement. [9]
- **International customer diversification:** Array said approximately 95% of STI revenue was generated outside the United States and that the companies’ major customers had virtually no overlap. [6][9]

Array described STI as a top-three solar-tracker provider in Spain as well as the number-one provider in Brazil.[22]

At the time of the acquisition, STI had shipped or been awarded more than 12 GW of solar trackers.[22]

As of September 30, 2021, STI had approximately $416 million of executed contracts and awarded orders.[22]

This makes STI strategically different from a simple extension of Legacy volume. STI supplied access to international markets, local operations and customers, as well as a product configuration intended for site and labor conditions that differ from those for which Array positioned its flagship linked-row platform.[6][3][9]

What follows: STI’s contribution is product-market and geographic complementarity. It should not be evaluated solely by asking whether its gross margin matches that of the legacy U.S. platform.

#### Combined portfolio: product choice by project economics

The acquisition combined tracker designs that Array presented as suited to different project conditions. Array’s linked-row platform was positioned around reliability, lower installation cost, lower maintenance and competitive manufacturing cost. STI’s dual-row system was positioned around irregular terrain, fragmented parcels, lower labor-cost markets and lower wind/snow-load conditions.[2][7]

Array’s stated portfolio objective was to give global customers a choice based on site conditions, local-content requirements, labor costs, upfront cost and total lifetime cost.[9][10] This is a practical solar-project distinction.

Utility-scale tracker procurement is not governed solely by equipment price: developers and EPCs also assess civil works, grading, labor availability, shipping, local sourcing, terrain, weather loading, construction schedule and expected operating expenditure.

Judgment — A portfolio that can offer different tracker architectures for different site conditions can improve commercial positioning even when the products have different gross-margin profiles. The strategic test is whether the business selects the right product for the project while protecting price, delivery and cost discipline; forcing every project toward the highest-margin product can weaken win rates or customer value.

Array also planned to use STI’s sales organization to offer DuraTrack to STI customers. Array characterized DuraTrack as having low lifetime cost and strong wind- and snow-load performance. That plan shows that the intended combined portfolio was two-way: STI’s product would broaden Array’s solution set, while STI’s international channel could distribute Array’s legacy product into new customer relationships.[6][7]

What follows: The strategic logic was not to eliminate product differences. It was to retain differentiated offerings, match them to project conditions and exploit cross-selling where Array’s technology offered a better fit.

Array describes the combined business as a global provider of integrated solar-tracking systems—software and hardware including steel tubing and supports, drivelines, motors, controllers, bearings and gearboxes—for utility-scale and distributed-generation solar customers. Both Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations generate revenue by designing, manufacturing and selling utility-scale tracker systems; STI sells in Spain, Brazil, the United States and South Africa.[11]

Array identifies DuraTrack HZ v3 as its flagship product. In addition to the linked-row design, Array says it has a patented single-bolt-per-module mounting system that reduces installation time, passive wind-load mitigation and a low number of motors and controls per megawatt.[12]

The Array STI H250 can use one motor to move up to 120 photovoltaic modules and is designed to deliver favorable LCOE through its robust dual-row system.[11][12] STI’s facilities added approximately 54,000 square feet in Spain and 632,000 square feet in Brazil for manufacturing and assembling components for local and international markets.[12]

Array’s tracker portfolio includes DuraTrack, the STI H250 dual-row system and OmniTrack. Array states that site characteristics determine which of its three tracker systems offers differentiated benefits, and that the suite extends its target applications for utility-scale tracker solutions.[11][12]

Array sells tracker systems, parts and services to EPC firms, solar developers, independent power producers and utilities, often under master supply agreements or multi-year procurement contracts; EPC sales represented approximately 69% of consolidated 2023 revenue.[12]

STI was founded in 1996 and is headquartered in Pamplona, Spain.[3]

Array’s January 2022 acquisition added the dual-row design later identified as the Array STI H250. Array states that one motor can move up to 120 photovoltaic modules in this system.[11]

In its November 2021 acquisition announcement, Array stated that STI was Brazil’s leading solar-tracker provider and that its local manufacturing presence and supply chain reinforced that position. Array also stated that nearly 10 GW of Brazilian utility-scale solar installations were forecast for 2022 and 2023 and that more than 90% of those projects were expected to use trackers.[9]

In 2023, 74% of Array’s revenue came from U.S. customers and 26% from customers in the rest of the world.[13]

Array Legacy Operations contributed Array-branded single-axis solar trackers, including DuraTrack and OmniTrack, to the portfolio through September 30, 2024.

### What is the basis for calculating comparable segment gross margins?

The appropriate comparison is each segment’s **GAAP gross margin**, calculated as **(segment revenue − segment cost of revenue) ÷ segment revenue**, equivalently reported segment gross profit ÷ the same segment’s revenue. Cost of revenue is Array’s reported gross-profit cost basis, including applicable product cost, amortization of developed technology and backlog, depreciation and other costs; it is not limited to direct product and delivery cost. This calculation measures the portion of each revenue dollar retained after reported cost of revenue and before selling, general and administrative expense, research and development, interest, taxes and goodwill impairment.[15][16]

Array reports segment revenue and segment gross profit for Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations on a basis consistent with internal management reporting. Array’s chief executive officer, the chief operating decision maker (CODM), uses gross profit to assess segment performance, to prepare annual budgets and forecasts, and to allocate operating and capital resources.[17][16]

**The comparable-margin rule is: segment gross margin equals reported segment gross profit divided by reported segment revenue for the same period.** This compares gross profit generated per dollar of revenue using the same disclosed numerator and denominator for each segment.[16][17]

#### Reported gross profit, not product cost alone

The calculation must use reported segment gross profit rather than treating product cost as the whole cost base. Array’s reported gross-profit measure includes multiple cost categories. In the September 2024 segment table, gross profit was calculated after deducting product cost, amortization of developed technology and backlog where applicable, depreciation and other costs.[15]

The cost base includes:

- **Product costs:** Raw materials, purchased components, manufacturing personnel, freight, tariffs, customer support and warranty costs. [1][17]
- **Developed-technology and backlog amortization:** Amortization of acquired product technology and backlog included in cost of revenue where applicable. [15][17]
- **Depreciation:** Depreciation of manufacturing and testing equipment. [17][15]
- **Other costs:** Primarily outbound freight and certain overhead costs. [17][15]
- **Incentives and rebates:** Product cost includes realized Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credit benefits, and vendor rebates reduce inventory cost before being recognized in cost of revenue when the inventory is sold. [17][16]

Section 45X is a U.S. manufacturing production tax credit for qualifying clean-energy components made domestically and sold by a manufacturer. Array reported that certain parts it manufactured qualified during the second quarter of 2024, and that it had arrangements with suppliers to share some eligible-credit benefits.[16][17]

The reported segment gross margin therefore captures more than manufacturing conversion efficiency. It also incorporates pricing, product and customer mix, geographic mix, freight, warranty experience, incentive realization, depreciation, amortization and overhead allocations.[1][17]

Judgment — Gross margin is a strong portfolio-performance measure because it tells management how much gross profit each business retains from its revenue. It is not a pure operational-efficiency measure. A segment can have a high gross margin because of superior production cost, premium pricing, favorable mix, tax credits, a one-time cost reduction or some combination of those factors.

#### Period discipline: standalone quarters versus cumulative periods

The requested analysis covers Q1 2023 through Q3 2024 and the nine months ended September 30 in both 2023 and 2024. The comparison must distinguish a three-month standalone quarter from a cumulative January-to-September result.

- **Standalone quarter:** Divide that quarter’s reported segment gross profit by that quarter’s segment revenue.
- **Nine-month period:** Divide nine-month segment gross profit by nine-month segment revenue directly. Do not treat the cumulative figure as a single quarter, and do not average the three quarterly percentage margins because quarterly revenue levels differ. [18][2]
- **Derived quarter:** If only cumulative and prior-period cumulative data are reported, derive the quarter by subtracting the earlier cumulative amount from the later cumulative amount for both revenue and gross profit. Then calculate the quarter’s margin from those derived values.

Judgment — The nine-month gross margin is a revenue-weighted outcome. A quarter with high revenue has more influence on the nine-month margin than a quarter with low revenue. Averaging quarterly margins would assign each quarter equal weight and can produce a misleading result.

Array itself cautioned that quarterly gross profit varies with average selling prices (ASPs), product cost, product mix, customer mix, geography, shipping method, warranty cost and seasonality.[3][5] For component-sale contracts, Array generally recognizes revenue when control transfers, usually on delivery to the customer’s site; integrated tracker projects are predominantly recognized over time using a cost-to-cost measure of progress. Customer-project delays and changes in contract-completion estimates can therefore move revenue and margin between quarters.[12][1][16]

What follows: The next section should show quarterly margins as discrete period outcomes and compare nine months of 2024 directly with nine months of 2023, not with an average or a single quarter.

#### GAAP gross margin rather than adjusted gross margin

The primary comparison should use GAAP segment gross profit and GAAP segment revenue. Array’s **Adjusted Gross Profit** is a non-GAAP measure that adds back developed-technology amortization and, where applicable, other costs. Array states that non-GAAP measures are supplemental and should not be considered substitutes for GAAP results.[19][20]

Using adjusted gross margin would create two analytical problems. First, the company reported adjusted gross margin at the consolidated level, not as a comparable segment measure in the cited disclosures. Second, adding back costs would move the analysis away from the reported gross-profit measure that Array’s CODM uses to evaluate the segments.[17][19]

What follows: GAAP segment gross margin provides the cleanest common basis for the requested portfolio comparison; adjusted consolidated gross margin is useful context but not a substitute.

### What in Array Technologies’ STI acquisition

#### Acquisition decision and intended portfolio role

Array completed the STI acquisition on January 11, 2022 and then began operating as two reportable segments. The acquisition was not presented merely as a domestic market-share transaction. Array’s stated rationale was to accelerate international expansion, address rising global utility-scale solar demand and strengthen relationships with international customers, particularly in developing markets in South America and Africa.[3][1][2]

At announcement, Array framed STI as a means to establish leading positions in North America, Latin America and Europe and to combine manufacturing capacity and engineering resources across three continents. These were company expectations and positioning claims at the time of the transaction.[9]

The acquisition thesis had four linked components:

- **Geographic expansion:** STI was primarily international, with Array stating that approximately 95% of STI revenue was outside the U.S. [6][9]
- **Customer diversification:** Array said the major customer bases had virtually no overlap and identified STI relationships with Acciona Energia, Iberdrola and EDP Renewables. [7][9]
- **Product complementarity:** STI added a dual-row tracker suited to irregular terrain and particular labor and weather-load conditions. [7][22]
- **Supply-chain and manufacturing diversification:** STI brought manufacturing facilities in Spain and Brazil, which Array described as complementary supply-chain capacity. [8]

Array said the acquisition was expected to be significantly accretive to margins and earnings per share in 2022 before synergies. That was a forward-looking statement made at announcement; it is not evidence that STI would maintain a margin equal to or above Legacy’s margin in every later period.[6][9]

**What follows:** The acquisition’s stated purpose was portfolio expansion—geography, customers, product range and supply-chain capability—rather than an explicit commitment that the two segments would have identical gross margins.

#### Planned integration mechanisms

Array identified specific post-closing cost-reduction and operating-integration plans. It intended to evaluate supplier discounts available from combined purchasing volumes, identify common components where either business had advantaged pricing and implement manufacturing best practices across the combined facilities.[6][9]

Array identified more than $750 million of combined purchasing as a potential source of cost reductions following the STI acquisition.[42]

Array identified more than $750 million of combined purchasing as a potential source of cost reduction.[42]

These are the key economic mechanisms by which an acquisition of two tracker manufacturers could improve combined gross margin:

1. **Procurement scale:** Greater purchasing volume can improve supplier pricing, payment terms, availability and freight planning.
2. **Common-component sourcing:** Standardizing selected components or sourcing them from the business with better pricing can reduce cost per tracker while retaining product-specific architecture.
3. **Manufacturing practice transfer:** One plant or supply chain may have better methods for fabrication, quality control, inventory management, throughput or local procurement.
4. **Cross-selling:** A broader commercial organization can match the most suitable tracker to each project and potentially sell a higher-value product into existing customer relationships.
5. **Shared operating disciplines:** Common forecasting, contract review, inventory controls and project-delivery processes can reduce avoidable expedite cost, working-capital strain and margin leakage.

The record also shows that integration had a governance and systems dimension. Array identified STI-related material weaknesses in information-technology controls and formal accounting policies, procedures and controls across substantially all STI business processes in 2023.[2] It responded by engaging external consulting support, adding accounting and IT resources and focusing remediation on STI, inventory, revenue recognition, accounts receivable and business-combination controls.[2][5]

Array’s incentive plan treated STI integration as a separate performance measure: “STI Integration Success” accounted for 10% of incentives, with 40% of that measure tied to adjusted EBITDA and synergy savings and 60% tied to go-to-market strategy, Sarbanes-Oxley readiness and a management operating system. STI was excluded from certain Array Operations EBITDA and cash-conversion measures.[43]

Array acknowledged integration challenges and warned that unsuccessful integration could prevent it from realizing anticipated benefits.[44]

In Q2 2024, Array implemented an enterprise resource planning (ERP) system for its Brazil operations. An ERP system is an integrated system for recording and managing operational and financial processes. Array said the ERP would enable more automated controls and general IT controls, reducing reliance on manual controls; it also said remediation would not be complete until controls had operated long enough and passed formal testing.[16]

What follows: Procurement and manufacturing integration can affect cost of revenue directly, while ERP, control and reporting integration improve the ability to identify, price and manage the sources of margin leakage. Both are necessary, but neither is itself proof of realized margin improvement.

#### Market conditions affecting the comparison

The 2023–September 2024 period contained external pressures that matter for interpreting segment economics. These pressures were not identical across markets, projects or supply chains.

First, input and logistics conditions were volatile. Array said the Russia-Ukraine conflict reduced the availability of some Europe-sourced materials and increased logistics costs for certain product inputs. In 2024, disruption to Red Sea shipping increased transit times and shipping costs, and Array said it increased local sourcing where feasible to meet delivery commitments.[16]

Second, customer-project timing was unstable. Array reported that rising interest rates, permitting delays, interconnection constraints, shortages of transformers and high-voltage equipment, financing conditions and tariff uncertainty could delay customer projects. Because revenue is recognized when title transfers, these delays can shift revenue and margin across reporting periods.[1][16]

Array reported that customer pushouts, project delays and Brazil-related cancellations affected its backlog and revenue expectations.[45]

Third, Brazil was a distinct 2024 issue. Array stated that a rapid devaluation of the Brazilian real and energy-price pressures made the economics of some project power-purchase agreements less attractive, leading developers to delay projects while renegotiating pricing.[23][24] Brazil was strategically important to STI, but the record does not quantify the portion of STI margin affected by these conditions.

Fourth, U.S. domestic-content and manufacturing-credit economics favored attention to U.S. supply arrangements. Array said it recognized 45X manufacturing-credit benefits associated with torque tubes and structural fasteners during 2024. It also reported that IRS domestic-content guidance in May 2024 clarified some issues while introducing others, including what qualified as a fastener, and that customer uncertainty could delay projects.[16]

Fifth, the tracker market remained price- and project-sensitive. Array said gross profit can change with volume, ASPs, product and project mix, customer and geographic mix, commodity prices, logistics rates, warranty costs and seasonality.[16] In this setting, the margin comparison captures the economic result of each segment’s commercial and operational conditions, not a single product-design effect.

What follows: The segment comparison must distinguish persistent portfolio signals from conditions that may be temporary, such as expedite freight, tax-credit treatment, project timing and regional currency or logistics disruption.

#### Segment-specific examples of why margin is not a pure efficiency measure

The contemporaneous explanations for Q1 2023 show why a margin differential cannot be treated as a direct productivity ranking. Array attributed the Legacy segment’s increase in gross profit as a percentage of revenue partly to improved pass-through pricing and a one-time benefit from lower-than-expected logistics costs.[3] Array attributed STI’s margin improvement to improved pass-through pricing and a reduced impact from lower-margin construction-related services.[3]

The same analytical caution applies in 2024. Array attributed Legacy’s Q1 2024 margin increase to structural cost enhancements, realization of 45X benefits associated with torque tubes and a one-time $4.0 million vendor settlement recorded as a reduction to cost of revenue.[1] For STI, Array attributed the Q1 2024 margin decline primarily to changes in statutory reductions from incentive tax credits in Brazil and expedite fees incurred to meet customer delivery schedules.[1]

In Q2 2024, Array said STI’s revenue decline reflected fewer megawatts shipped, a shift from larger utility-scale projects toward smaller distributed-generation projects and a smaller share of projects carrying construction services.[16] Management also said STI’s year-to-date gross margins were affected by a shift from Asian supply to more expensive local sources because of longer transit times, principally affecting Europe and Latin America, and expected that pressure to be transitory as Asian shipments normalized.[23]

Judgment — These examples establish an interpretation rule: reported gross-margin differences are economically important, but the cause should be decomposed into price, mix, freight, incentives, service content, volume absorption and manufacturing cost before calling one segment inherently more efficient.

What follows: A sustained margin gap is a management signal to investigate and act; it is not, by itself, a diagnosis.

#### Competitive positioning implied by the combined portfolio

Array’s competitive position through September 2024 rested on product breadth, project-fit capability, customer access and supply-chain reach. Its core tracker proposition was that a linked-row design could lower installation and maintenance requirements and provide reliable performance. STI added a dual-row system and international operating presence.[2][3]

Array also continued to develop the portfolio for difficult site conditions. It introduced terrain-adaptive offerings and, in 2024, discussed SkyLink as an addition to DuraTrack and OmniTrack platforms. Array said SkyLink used PV-powered wireless communication to reduce trenching and cable-management requirements and to increase layout flexibility for challenging sites.[23] The cited material does not allocate SkyLink, DuraTrack or OmniTrack revenues between the two segments.

Array’s commercial position was also affected by policy and supply-chain capability. It stated that domestic-content requirements could become a competitive disadvantage if it could not supply qualifying trackers while competitors could, and it reported plans to offer a 100% domestic Array tracker in the first half of 2025.[16][23] As of Q2 2024, Array reported more than 50 gigawatts of secured supplier capacity globally, including more than 30 gigawatts in the U.S., and stated that on-time delivery exceeded 95% in recent quarters.[23]

Judgment — Competitive positioning in utility-scale trackers is not measured by gross margin alone. A tracker supplier competes on delivered project economics: equipment cost, installation labor, terrain fit, energy yield, weather resilience, local-content qualification, supply reliability, financing context and ability to execute against construction schedules.

What follows: The combined business had broader competitive coverage than either platform alone, but the portfolio must convert that breadth into disciplined pricing, cost control and dependable delivery for it to improve total gross profitability.

Wood Mackenzie ranked Array second globally in the 2023 tracker market, behind Nextracker, for the ninth consecutive year that the two companies held the first and second positions.[25] Global tracker shipments rose 28% to 92 GWdc in 2023, and the ten largest vendors accounted for 90% of global market share.[25] Nextracker grew 20% in 2023 and GameChange grew 55%, narrowing the gap between Array and the third-ranked supplier; Nextracker, Array and GameChange together accounted for more than 50% of global tracker shipments and 90% of the U.S. market.[25]

For the nine months ended September 30, 2024, Array attributed Array Legacy Operations’ gross-margin increase to 45X benefits associated with torque tubes and structural fasteners and the $4.0 million vendor settlement, while it attributed STI Operations’ decline to lower revenue and a 19% decrease in average selling prices, partly offset by lower commodity prices.[26] For Q3 2024, Array attributed Legacy’s gross-margin increase to 45X benefits and STI’s decline to an approximately 12% decrease in average selling prices, partly offset by lower commodity prices.[26]

The September 30, 2024 impairment assessment adds a separate acquisition-value signal to the gross-margin comparison. Array recorded a $162.0 million goodwill impairment for STI Operations, following a sustained stock-price decline, a 52-week low, lower market capitalization, updated long-term projections and an assessment of execution risk. STI’s estimated fair value was $455.9 million; Array stated that the estimated fair value of Array Legacy Operations was significantly higher than its carrying balance.[26] The impairment was recorded as an operating expense below gross profit, so it does not alter either segment’s reported gross margin.[26]

### Synthesis

Array Legacy Operations supplied Array’s established patented linked-row tracker platform and core U.S. position; STI Operations supplied a dual-row tracker, manufacturing in Spain and Brazil, international sales infrastructure and customer diversification. The STI acquisition was designed to broaden Array’s product, geographic and supply-chain portfolio, with intended synergies from procurement scale, common components, manufacturing practices and cross-selling.[2][3][6]

Comparable segment gross margin should therefore be calculated as reported segment gross profit divided by reported segment revenue for the same standalone quarter or cumulative nine-month period. It is the management-relevant measure of gross profit per revenue dollar, but it includes pricing, mix, freight, tax incentives, warranty, amortization, depreciation and overhead effects.[17][15]

The central interpretive conclusion is that segment margins are a portfolio-management signal, not a stand-alone verdict on operational efficiency or acquisition success. The following analysis should quantify the gap and then assess it against the acquisition’s stated purposes: broader market access and complementary product fit, alongside realization of cost and operating synergies.

Array stated that its three tracker systems offer differentiated benefits according to project-site characteristics and that the three-product suite extends its target applications. ### Synthesis

The segment record changes the description of the period from a generic Legacy-versus-STI comparison to a dated performance shift. Early 2023 was close to parity, and STI led in Q3; derived Q4 marked the break; and every reported 2024 quarter through September retained a Legacy lead above 23 pp. The cumulative comparison confirms the same result: a 0.33-pp Legacy lead for the first nine months of 2023 became a 26.71-pp lead in the corresponding 2024 period.[27][4][17]

For the portfolio assessment that follows, the practical conclusion is that Array Legacy supplied materially more gross profit per revenue dollar in 2024, while STI’s observed gross-profit conversion was under acute pressure. That is a strong performance fact, but it should be kept distinct from claims about the underlying operational cause, overall strategic value, or acquisition success.

Array completed the STI acquisition on January 11, 2022, for $410.5 million in cash and 13,894,800 Array common shares, with purchase consideration fair-valued at $610.8 million, obtaining 100% of STI’s equity interests. Array described the integration as an opportunity to accelerate international expansion and address rising utility-scale solar demand, particularly in developing South American and African markets.[3]

STI brought a reliable dual-row tracker designed for markets with lower installation labor costs and lower wind and snow loads than the United States. Array said that this broader portfolio enabled it to match products to global customers’ site conditions, local-content requirements, labor costs, upfront-cost objectives, and total-lifetime-cost objectives; against that strategic role, STI’s nine-month 2024 gross margin was 26.71 percentage points below Array Legacy’s.[6][17]

Array’s announced integration plan called for supplier-cost reductions through combined purchasing volumes, additional supplier discounts, common components with advantaged pricing, and mutual manufacturing best practices across both companies’ facilities. These actions address the STI sourcing pressure management attributed to shifting supply from Asia to more expensive local sources amid increased transit times and Red Sea shipping challenges, while STI reported a 26.71-percentage-point nine-month gross-margin deficit to Array Legacy.[6][23][33][17]

## Strategic value, operational efficiency and competitive positioning

Gross margin measures the share of revenue left after direct product and delivery costs. It is therefore a useful indicator of **gross-profit conversion**—how much gross profit a business retains per sales dollar—but it is not a complete measure of operational efficiency, competitive strength, or acquisition success. In Array’s case, the widening gap between its two reporting segments matters because the businesses serve different but complementary parts of the global utility-scale solar-tracker market.

The relevant pattern is clear: Array Legacy Operations became the portfolio’s high-margin economic core in 2024, while STI Operations retained strategic importance as an international and site-specific product platform but produced far less gross profit per revenue dollar. The following interpretation relies on the period comparison established in Segment gross margins and the performance gap.

### What does the margin differential reveal about the strategic value

**Array Legacy Operations was Array’s principal gross-profit engine in the nine months ended September 30, 2024.** It generated $192.118 million of gross profit from $459.807 million of revenue, producing a calculated 41.78% gross margin. Thus, Legacy’s gross margin exceeded STI’s by 26.71 percentage points in the nine-month period.[17]

This was a major change from the corresponding nine months of 2023. A tracker supplier that can offer lower project installation cost, lower lifetime maintenance burden, or higher reliability may have more room to preserve price or win projects without conceding as much margin. Legacy’s 2024 results are consistent with that proposition: its quarterly gross margins remained near 41%–43% despite revenue declines caused partly by customer project delays and lower average selling prices associated with lower input costs.[1][30][17][16]

The strategic conclusion is that Array Legacy Operations was the portfolio’s high-margin economic anchor through September 2024: it combined the largest gross-profit contribution with a differentiated U.S. tracker proposition and a gross-margin advantage that persisted for three consecutive quarters.[1][30][17][3]

That conclusion should not be overstated into a claim that the product technology alone created the margin outcome. Array reported that Legacy’s 2023 improvement reflected better pass-through pricing, logistics and raw-material savings, and a higher share of higher-margin non-tracker revenue. Its 2024 margin improvement also included structural cost enhancements, realized 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credit benefits related to torque tubes and structural fasteners, and a one-time $4.0 million vendor settlement recognized as a reduction of cost of revenue.[4][1][16]

The 45X credit is a U.S. manufacturing tax credit that can reduce production costs when qualifying components are made domestically. Its presence reinforces the strategic attractiveness of Legacy’s domestic manufacturing model, but it also means reported margin is not a pure measure of product economics. Product cost in Array’s segment tables includes realized 45X benefits, while gross profit also includes the effects of freight, overhead, depreciation, and acquired-technology amortization.[17][15]

Legacy also has strategic value in a policy-sensitive U.S. market. Domestic-content rules under the Inflation Reduction Act can raise a solar project’s investment tax credit from 30% to 40% if requirements are met, subject to wage and apprenticeship conditions. Array stated that uncertainty around tracker-component definitions and qualifying costs could affect customer decisions, while it also described the ability to supply domestic tracker products as competitively important.[16]Judgment — In a market where developers value domestic content, reliable local supply, and installation economics, a high-margin domestic tracker platform gives Array both profit capacity and negotiating flexibility.

For Q1 2023, Array Legacy Operations reported $305.204 million of revenue and $83.474 million of gross profit, implying a calculated 27.35% gross margin; STI Operations reported $71.569 million of revenue and $17.705 million of gross profit, implying a calculated 24.74% gross margin. Legacy’s calculated lead was 2.61 percentage points.[3]

The implication is not that Legacy should displace STI strategically. Rather, Legacy provides the economic base from which Array can fund international reach, product development, and supply-chain resilience; STI must demonstrate that its strategic reach can be translated into more comparable unit economics.

STI Operations’ strategic value lies primarily in **market access, customer diversification, and product breadth**, rather than in superior gross profitability during 2024. Array acquired STI in January 2022 for a stated fair value of $610.8 million, obtaining 100% of its equity interests. Array said the transaction would accelerate international expansion and help address growing utility-scale solar demand, particularly in developing countries in South America and Africa.[3]

STI added a dual-row tracker design in which one motor drives two connected rows. Array says the design is suited to irregular, highly angled, or fragmented project areas. This complements Legacy’s linked-row design because tracker selection depends on terrain, wind and snow conditions, installation labor costs, local-content requirements, and customer priorities between up-front and lifetime cost.[3][5][9]

STI also brought an international operating footprint. It was founded in Pamplona, Spain, and has manufacturing facilities in Spain and Brazil. Array reported that STI designs, manufactures, and sells utility-scale trackers in Spain, Brazil, the United States, and South Africa. Before the acquisition, Array said STI generated approximately 95% of revenue outside the United States and had virtually no overlap with Array’s major customers.[3][6]

This matters competitively because it gives Array access to markets and customer relationships that Legacy alone did not provide. Array described STI as having an established sales presence in Europe, Latin America, Australia, and South Africa, including longstanding relationships with customers such as Acciona Energia, Iberdrola, and EDP Renewables. It also described STI as a leading provider in Brazil, supported by local manufacturing and supply-chain presence. These were company statements at the time of the acquisition, but they identify the strategic assets that the segment contributed to the combined portfolio.[9]

STI’s strategic role was to make Array a broader global tracker supplier; it added $27.240 million of gross profit in the nine months ended September 30, 2024, while its 15.07% margin lowered the combined, revenue-weighted segment margin relative to Legacy’s 41.78% standalone margin. This does not mean that the international platform lacked strategic value.[17][27]

The financial trade-off is visible in the first nine months of 2024. STI supplied $180.768 million of revenue, or 28.2% of combined segment revenue, but generated only $27.240 million of gross profit, or 12.4% of combined segment gross profit. That is a material commercial contribution, but a low gross-profit contribution relative to sales.[27]

STI’s position was different in the corresponding 2023 period. It generated a 26.6% calculated gross margin, effectively matching Legacy’s 26.9%. Thus, the 2024 shortfall was not a stable feature of the post-acquisition portfolio throughout the entire comparison period.[27]

The segment’s 2024 performance must also be considered alongside its role in serving markets where cost and supply conditions differ from the United States. Array stated that STI’s dual-row tracker was intended for markets with lower installation labor cost and lower wind and snow loads than the U.S. Market fit can create strategic value even where gross margins are temporarily lower, especially if the product enables Array to participate in projects that a U.S.-optimized design would not win.[6][8]

Judgment — A diversified tracker portfolio should not force every product into the same margin target. A site-specific or region-specific product can be strategically valuable if it expands addressable market, protects customer relationships, or raises total portfolio win rates. But it must at least earn a return above its fully loaded cost of capital over the cycle; strategic reach that persistently destroys gross profit or requires recurring subsidies from the domestic segment is not sustainable.

STI’s strategic value therefore depends on whether Array can convert its international network into better economics. Array’s stated integration agenda included procurement coordination, organizational alignment, cross-selling, and broadening a U.S.-centric business into a global platform. These are directly relevant to STI because each could improve purchasing leverage, reduce duplicative processes, increase factory utilization, and match the best tracker design to each customer and site.[34]

The implication is that STI should be managed as a complementary platform with explicit economic milestones. Its role is not to reproduce Legacy’s U.S. business model in every geography; its role is to turn its regional channels, local manufacturing, and specialized tracker design into acceptable gross-profit conversion.

From the first nine months of 2023 to the corresponding 2024 period, Legacy revenue declined 48.6% and gross profit declined 20.3%, whereas STI revenue declined 46.8% and gross profit declined 69.8%. Thus, Legacy retained substantially more gross profit despite a slightly steeper revenue contraction, while STI’s gross-profit contraction materially exceeded its sales decline.[27]

Array stated that Array Legacy Operations’ U.S.-based status and products manufactured in the United States reduce potential exposure to U.S. government tariffs and other U.S. government regulatory actions directed at products manufactured in foreign countries and imported into the United States.[12] In the U.S. tracker market, domestic-content compliance can determine whether customers receive an incremental tax credit, although compliance can also increase production cost.[12]

Array stated that it actively manages contract risk in multi-year fixed-price and commodity-index-linked agreements and may decline projects when it cannot mitigate risk, even when doing so sends customers or projects to competitors, to maintain a high-quality portfolio and consistent margins over time. This contracting discipline is a strategic mechanism through which the Legacy platform can protect gross-profit conversion in a steel- and component-intensive business.[12]

STI’s acquisition value also included a substantial acquired-customer and commercial asset base. The purchase-price allocation included $50.000 million of backlog with a one-year weighted-average useful life, $228.408 million of customer relationships with a 10-year life, and $26.023 million of trade name with a 20-year life, or $304.431 million in identifiable intangible assets.[11]

At December 31, 2023, $365.864 million of Array’s $435.591 million goodwill balance was allocated to STI Operations, compared with $69.727 million for Array Legacy Operations; the Legacy amount was net of $51.9 million of impairment. Array’s annual goodwill assessment found STI’s fair value exceeded carrying value at that date and recognized no impairment.[12]

At December 31, 2023, Array Legacy Operations held $1,437.6 million of assets, approximately 84% of Array’s consolidated assets, compared with STI Operations’ $269.1 million, approximately 16%. This asset distribution placed the larger operating and capital base behind the business that became the higher-margin segment in 2024.[12][31]

The STI acquisition’s September 30, 2024 valuation outcome adds a financial test to the margin evidence. Array recorded a $162.0 million goodwill impairment for STI Operations after updating long-term projections and assessing execution risk; STI’s estimated fair value was $455.9 million. By contrast, Array stated that Legacy’s estimated fair value was significantly higher than its carrying balance. The impairment was recorded below gross profit, so it does not change the segment gross-margin calculation, but it shows that STI’s expected acquisition value had been reduced while Legacy retained valuation headroom.[26]

Array also tested STI’s long-lived assets, including amortizable intangible assets, at September 30, 2024 and recorded no impairment because expected future undiscounted cash flows supported recovery of the asset groups’ carrying amounts. This leaves STI with an operating asset platform that Array must convert into stronger revenue growth and forecast EBITDA margin—the valuation assumptions identified as significant in the STI reporting-unit assessment.[26]

For the nine months ended September 2023, Array Legacy Operations reported $895.322 million of revenue and $241.019 million of gross profit, or a 26.9% margin; STI Operations reported $339.614 million of revenue and $90.303 million of gross profit, or a 26.6% margin. The 0.3-percentage-point Legacy lead in that period demonstrates the near parity from which the 2024 differential widened.[27]

For the nine months ended September 2024, Array Legacy Operations reported $459.807 million of revenue and $192.118 million of gross profit, while STI Operations reported $180.768 million of revenue and $27.240 million of gross profit. The calculated margins were 41.78% and 15.07%, respectively, producing a 26.71-percentage-point Legacy lead.[31]

When Array announced the proposed STI Norland acquisition in November 2021, it stated that STI was expected to be significantly accretive to Array’s margins and earnings per share in 2022 before synergies.[6]

For full-year 2023, Array Legacy Operations generated $1,172.827 million of revenue and $317.605 million of gross profit, a calculated 27.1% margin, versus STI Operations’ $403.724 million of revenue and $97.946 million of gross profit, a calculated 24.3% margin. Legacy’s full-year lead was 2.8 percentage points.[21]

Array stated that 45X tax credits were helping it increase domestic production and onshore critical components and jobs through the groundbreaking of its new Albuquerque manufacturing facility.[33]

Array Legacy Operations generated 2.67 times STI Operations’ gross profit in the nine months ended September 2023 ($241.019 million versus $90.303 million); by the nine months ended September 2024, that multiple had risen to 7.05 times ($192.118 million versus $27.240 million). The 4.38-times increase quantifies how sharply the portfolio’s gross-profit contribution became concentrated in Legacy.[27]

The STI acquisition’s financial success ultimately depends on more than segment margin: purchase price, integration cost, customer and employee retention, incremental revenue, cash generation, capital needs, and returns relative to Array’s cost of capital determine whether the combined portfolio created value.

### What does the margin differential reveal about the operational efficiency

Array Legacy Operations demonstrated markedly stronger **reported gross-profit conversion** in 2024. Its nine-month gross margin rose from 26.9% in 2023 to 41.8% in 2024, while STI’s fell from 26.6% to 15.1%. This changed the portfolio from near gross-margin parity between the two segments to a 26.7-percentage-point Legacy advantage.[27]

A useful way to interpret the result is through gross-profit contribution. Legacy represented 71.8% of combined segment revenue in the first nine months of 2024, but it generated 87.6% of combined segment gross profit. STI represented 28.2% of revenue but only 12.4% of gross profit. The difference means Legacy was not simply larger; it supplied a disproportionately large share of the portfolio’s gross profit.[27]

Legacy’s stronger conversion was associated with both commercial and cost factors. The company’s explanation shows why gross margin should be understood as a combined measure of commercial discipline, sourcing, manufacturing cost, product mix, and delivery economics. Pass-through pricing means contractual mechanisms allow the supplier to recover changes in commodity or other input costs from customers. That ability is strategically important in a steel- and logistics-intensive tracker business because it reduces exposure to sudden cost inflation. Array’s 2023 disclosures explicitly linked Legacy’s improvement to stronger pass-through pricing and logistics and raw-material savings.[5][4]

**Legacy was the stronger gross-profit engine in 2024, but its margin advantage should be read as superior realized revenue-to-cost conversion rather than proof that every underlying operating process was inherently more efficient.**[27][17]

The distinction matters because Array states that gross profit can vary with volume, average selling prices, product, project, customer and geographic mix, commodity prices, freight and logistics rates, warranty cost, seasonality, and tax incentives. Cost of revenue also contains manufacturing labor, purchased components, tariffs, customer support, depreciation, and amortization. Some of those items are fixed or semi-fixed for a period and do not move directly with sales volume.[17][4]

Legacy’s reported margin also bears acquired-technology and backlog amortization that STI did not bear in the Q3 2024 segment table. In the nine months ended September 2024, Legacy recorded $10.918 million of amortization and $1.480 million of depreciation within the gross-profit calculation, compared with no developed-technology or backlog amortization and $0.051 million of depreciation for STI. This means Legacy’s higher gross margin was achieved despite certain reported cost burdens, but the disclosed accounting allocations do not establish a clean manufacturing-cost comparison.[17][15]

Judgment — Operational efficiency in solar trackers should be assessed with at least three linked measures: gross margin, cost per watt, and on-time project delivery. Gross margin shows financial conversion; cost per watt separates cost performance from shipment volume; and delivery performance tests whether cost savings were achieved without compromising customer schedules. Array defines average selling price as applicable revenue divided by applicable megawatts and cost per watt as applicable cost of goods sold divided by applicable megawatts, specifically to evaluate pricing, manufacturing cost, and customer profitability.[1][4]

Legacy’s result therefore makes one decision clear: Array should protect the contracting, domestic manufacturing, sourcing, and cost-control disciplines that supported Legacy’s 2024 margin profile. It also provides the reference operating model against which the rest of the portfolio can be measured, while allowing for legitimate differences in product design, regional supply chains, and project mix.

STI Operations showed materially weaker reported gross-profit conversion in 2024. Its calculated nine-month gross margin fell from 26.6% in 2023 to 15.1% in 2024, while Legacy rose from 26.9% to 41.8%. That relative deterioration produced the 26.7-percentage-point gap at September 30, 2024.[27]

Array identified specific reasons for STI’s early-2024 weakness. The supply-chain context was real. Array reported that Red Sea disruption created port congestion, longer transit times, capacity constraints, and higher shipping costs on routes connecting Asia with the rest of the world, and it increased local sourcing where feasible to get products to project sites on time.

STI’s 2024 margin shortfall reflects weaker realized revenue-to-cost conversion, but the disclosed drivers point to a combined sourcing, logistics, incentive, delivery, and project-mix problem rather than a demonstrated intrinsic manufacturing-efficiency deficit.[1][16][17]

Project mix also changed. Array said STI’s Q2 2024 revenue decline reflected fewer megawatts shipped, a shift from larger utility-scale projects to smaller distributed-generation projects, and lower average selling prices associated with a smaller share of projects containing construction services. These changes affect reported gross margin because services, product configuration, logistics, and customer contracts can carry different cost and pricing characteristics.[16]

Operational systems were an additional integration issue. As of September 30, 2024, Array’s disclosure controls and procedures were not effective at a reasonable assurance level because STI lacked designed, implemented, and monitored general IT controls over systems supporting substantially all internal-control processes and lacked formal accounting policies, procedures, and controls across substantially all STI business processes. Array implemented an enterprise resource planning, or ERP, system for Brazil in Q2 2024 to enable automated and general IT controls and reduce reliance on manual controls; remediation required full implementation, sufficient operating time, and formal testing.[26]

Those control weaknesses do not prove that they caused STI’s margin decline. They do, however, identify a practical constraint on management: weak systems make it harder to obtain timely, consistent information on procurement cost, supplier rebates, project profitability, inventory, and delivery performance across markets.Judgment — In project-based manufacturing, reliable ERP data and control processes are essential to identify margin leakage before it becomes visible in reported gross margin.

The conclusion for STI is therefore two-sided. The unit remained commercially meaningful and strategically complementary, but its 2024 economics required active correction. Its recovery should be assessed through cost per watt, sourcing mix, freight and expedite cost, project margins, factory utilization, and on-time delivery—not by sales growth alone.

STI accounted for 56.3% of the combined segments’ $112.0 million decline in nine-month gross profit from 2023 to 2024, despite representing 28.2% of nine-month 2024 segment revenue. This identifies STI as the larger contributor to the portfolio’s gross-profit decline over the comparable period.[27]

Array’s stated manufacturing model is to outsource capital-intensive, low-value-added work where possible, minimize labor content and customer-site assembly, and minimize material movement; supplier drop-shipping is intended to improve working-capital turnover, quality and inventory management. These are portfolio operating-model priorities that can support the Legacy reference model, although the segment disclosures do not allocate their effects by segment.[12]

Cost of revenue includes raw materials, purchased components, manufacturing personnel, freight, tariffs, customer support, warranty, developed-technology amortization, and depreciation; raw-material and component costs, technological innovation, economies of scale, and production-process and automation improvements affect product cost. Revenue conversion also depends on shipment volume and average selling price, which vary with supply and demand, module and wattage mix, customer geography, competitor offerings, and government incentives.[12]

Array defines average selling price (ASP) as applicable revenue divided by applicable megawatts and cost per watt (CPW) as applicable cost of goods sold divided by applicable megawatts. Management uses these metrics to evaluate pricing, manufacturing-cost and customer-profitability trends, making them appropriate operating measures for testing whether Legacy’s margin discipline is transferable to other businesses.[12]

Array recognized $9.3 million of negotiated 45X vendor-rebate benefit as a reduction of cost of revenue in 2023 and expected to recognize the remaining $40.6 million during 2024. At December 31, 2023, it had accumulated $49.9 million of vendor rebates from current-year activity, of which $48.4 million was outstanding. Vendor rebates reduce inventory purchase price and reduce cost of revenue when the inventory is sold.[12]

At December 31, 2023, Array reported that STI had not designed, implemented and monitored general information-technology controls for systems supporting substantially all STI internal-control processes and had not designed and implemented formal accounting policies, procedures and controls across substantially all STI business processes. Array was formalizing STI accounting and business-operation policies and procedures with an outside consulting firm ahead of the planned system implementation.[12]

The operational priorities for STI are therefore standardized policies and controls, reliable information systems, supplier and logistics management, and cost-effective localized delivery. Array reported that it depends on global suppliers, faces reduced control over availability, delivery schedules and costs, and may modify production schedules and processes to mitigate disruptions and cost increases; it does not hedge raw-material price changes.[12]

Large-project delays can create unplanned downtime, higher operating costs and inefficiencies, and excess inventory. Because Array recognizes revenue when legal title to equipment transfers to customers, weather, shipping, regulatory, supply-chain, tax-incentive and interest-rate delays can shift reported results between quarters.[11][12]

Array’s 2023 full-year segment explanations show that both operations had previously improved gross-profit conversion through commercial and mix actions. It attributed STI’s improvement principally to improved commodity-volatility pass-through, raw-material savings, and a reduced impact from lower-margin construction-related services.[13]

Array’s contract discipline is part of the operating model behind sustained margins. The company actively manages fixed-price and commodity-index-linked contract risk and may decline arrangements when it cannot mitigate risk, stating that this is how it manages a high-quality portfolio and drives consistent margins over time. Product costs are affected by steel and aluminum, motors and gearboxes, technology, economies of scale, and production-process and automation improvements.[26]

Array’s announced STI integration cost-reduction plan called for evaluating supplier discounts from combined purchasing volumes, identifying common components where either business had advantaged pricing, and implementing mutual manufacturing best practices across both companies’ facilities to reduce cost.[6]

This created a 51.3-percentage-point difference in gross-profit resilience between the segments over the comparable period.[27]

Gross margin also does not measure working-capital efficiency, inventory turns, manufacturing yield, service quality, or the selling, general and administrative expense and capital required to generate segment revenue. The policy component is material to the efficiency comparison. Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credit benefits were recorded as a reduction of cost of revenue in the U.S. Legacy segment, mechanically increasing its reported gross margin; STI’s Spain and Brazil production did not receive the comparable U.S. credit. ### How do the contrasting margin trends between segments reflect Array’s

The segment contrast indicates a portfolio with two different competitive roles. Legacy represented the higher-margin U.S.-anchored tracker platform, while STI expanded Array’s ability to compete internationally and serve site conditions that favor a dual-row tracker. The 2024 margin divergence suggests that Legacy’s commercial and supply-chain model was better positioned to convert revenue into gross profit during the period, while STI’s international operating model was more exposed to regional logistics, sourcing, incentive, and project-mix pressures.[3][1][16]

Array’s Legacy product position rests on its patented linked-row architecture. Array states that one motor drives multiple rows and says competitors must use designs it believes are less efficient and reliable to avoid its patent. The company claims that this produces reliability, installation-cost, maintenance, and manufacturing-cost advantages.[5] The segment margins do not independently verify those claims, but they are consistent with a differentiated product platform capable of supporting stronger realized gross-profit conversion.

STI supports a different competitive proposition. Its dual-row configuration is suited to irregular, highly angled, and fragmented sites. Its Spanish and Brazilian manufacturing facilities and international sales coverage expand Array’s ability to compete across regions and project types rather than confining the company to the U.S. utility-scale market.[3][5]

Array’s market position is therefore not well described by a single average margin. A global tracker supplier competes on total project economics: tracker price, installation labor, terrain adaptability, local manufacturing, domestic-content eligibility, delivery reliability, wind and snow design requirements, financing conditions, and long-term operating cost. Array itself states that revenue and average selling prices depend on supply and demand, project mix, customer geography, competitors’ offerings, and government incentives; growth depends on maintaining share, expanding globally, increasing production capacity, and developing new products.[1][16]

The 2024 result nevertheless identifies the current competitive hierarchy within Array’s own portfolio. Legacy had the stronger monetization model during the period, with margins above 40% in each quarter through September. STI retained strategic market access, but its lower margins reduced the gross-profit quality of the combined sales base.[1][30][17][27]

**The competitive positioning outcome through September 2024 was a high-margin Legacy core supporting a broader but lower-margin STI international platform; the portfolio’s success depends on preserving Legacy’s differentiated economics while raising STI’s regional delivery and sourcing economics.**[3][27]

External conditions affected both businesses, though not equally in the reported results. Management attributed its revised 2024 revenue outlook to customer project-timing challenges involving interconnection and permitting, obtaining long-lead equipment, financing, labor constraints, ADCVD, domestic-content guidance, and Brazil-related dynamics. Management characterized these issues as project pushouts rather than cancellations.[23] Array also reported Brazilian real depreciation that delayed projects while developers renegotiated power-purchase agreements.[24] These conditions reinforce why the STI margin decline should not be treated as a simple measure of competitive weakness: international tracker economics can be materially affected by policy and logistics shocks that a domestic U.S. operation experiences differently.

At the same time, lower STI margins cannot be dismissed as entirely external. The strategic case for an international acquisition includes the ability to coordinate sourcing, share engineering and manufacturing practices, cross-sell differentiated products, and improve project selection. If those capabilities do not improve gross-profit conversion over time, the portfolio will remain dependent on Legacy for economic returns.

Array competes in a tracker market in which projects using trackers typically generate more energy and deliver lower levelized cost of energy (LCOE) than fixed-tilt systems. The company identified Nextracker, PV Hardware and GameChange Solar as principal tracker competitors and said competition centers on product performance and features, total cost of ownership, reliability and warranty duration, sales and distribution, and training and customer support.[12]

The portfolio is designed to address different site conditions: DuraTrack is Array’s flagship linked-row product; the Array STI H250 is a dual-row tracker for irregular boundaries, highly angled blocks and fragmented areas; and OmniTrack adds north/south terrain flexibility, minimized grading, lower civil-work requirements and accommodation of uneven terrain. Array introduced OmniTrack in September 2022 to expand its solutions for the market. Array stated that this three-product suite expands its target applications and ability to provide utility-scale tracker solutions.[12][11]

Array’s stated competitive position is nevertheless subject to a globally fragmented, intensely competitive industry in which price competition can pressure margins and competitors may have lower costs, greater resources, larger customer bases, greater market share and economies of scale. The segment-margin differential therefore identifies Legacy as the stronger internal gross-profit model through September 2024, while competitive success remains dependent on product economics, reliability, customer access and delivery capability across both segments.[12][17]

Seasonality reinforces the need to assess the quarterly separation alongside the nine-month comparison: Array generally expects higher revenue in the second and third quarters and lower revenue in the first and fourth quarters because colder conditions in North America and Europe make tracker-foundation installation more costly.[12][14]

The acquisition and integration priority is to convert STI’s international manufacturing, customer relationships and site-specific product capability into stronger gross-profit conversion while preserving Legacy’s domestic contracting and cost discipline. Array stated that its international growth strategy covers broader North America, South America, Europe, Africa and Southeast Asia, excluding China and India, and may require local raw materials, components and logistics, component re-engineering to reduce costs, region-specific training, commissioning, warranty remediation and technical services.[12][11]

Array’s competitive position combined a differentiated domestic platform with global scale. During the nine months ended September 30, 2024, 70% of revenue came from U.S. customers and 30% from customers in the rest of the world; Array had sales presence in the U.S., Spain, Brazil, South Africa, Australia, and the U.K. Management reported more than 50 gigawatts of global supplier capacity, including more than 30 gigawatts in the United States and 31 domestic factories, as well as on-time delivery above 95% in recent quarters.[26][33]

Management stated that Array’s win rate remained high through Q2 2024, was much higher than its historical market-share rate, and was winning programs at margins it viewed favorably; its pipeline was roughly three times the level at the end of Q2 2023. These company-wide indicators support the positioning of the combined portfolio, while the segment margins identify Legacy as the current source of superior gross-profit conversion within it.[33][31]

The segment ranking was not uniform in 2023: STI’s calculated Q3 2023 gross margin was about 3.8 percentage points above Legacy’s, whereas Legacy held a 0.3-percentage-point lead for the first nine months of 2023. Array also reported that, in the first nine months of 2023 versus 2022, Legacy revenue fell 10% while STI revenue increased 43%.[2]

NREL reported in its Spring 2024 Solar Industry Update that Nextracker, Array Technologies, and GameChange Solar had all significantly expanded domestic production of trackers or tracker components.[35]

Management stated that more than 50 gigawatts of global capacity and 30 gigawatts of domestic capacity enabled Array to serve regions requiring localized content. Management stated that logistics represented about 10% of the bill of materials and described local steel as the lowest-landed-cost option.[33]

The segment comparison sits within a concentrated and growing tracker market. Wood Mackenzie reported that global PV tracker shipments increased 28% to 92 GWdc in 2023 and that the top ten vendors held 90% of global market share. It ranked Array second globally behind Nextracker for the ninth consecutive year that the two companies held the first and second positions.[25]

### Synthesis — what the contrast establishes

Array Legacy Operations was the stronger gross-profit engine through September 2024. Its calculated nine-month margin rose to 41.8%, versus STI’s 15.1%, and its quarterly margin lead persisted from Q1 through Q3 2024. Legacy’s patented linked-row platform, domestic manufacturing orientation, 45X benefits, pass-through pricing, cost actions, and favorable mix together supported its role as the portfolio’s high-margin anchor.[3][1][30][17][16]

STI remained strategically valuable because it added international reach, customer diversification, Spanish and Brazilian manufacturing, and a dual-row tracker suited to specific terrain and project conditions. Its $27.240 million of positive gross profit in the first nine months of 2024 demonstrates a positive contribution, while its 15.07% margin brought the combined revenue-weighted segment margin below Legacy’s standalone 41.78% margin. The portfolio direction is therefore clear: preserve and extend Legacy’s margin discipline, while improving STI’s sourcing, project selection, systems, delivery economics, and integration benefits so that geographic breadth contributes more proportionately to gross profit.[17][27][3][16]

## Acquisition and integration implications

### What do the sustained margin differences suggest about Array’s acquisition

Judgment — An acquisition can create portfolio value in two distinct ways: by improving earnings on the acquired business and by adding market access, customers, products, or supply-chain capabilities that the acquirer did not previously possess. Array’s STI transaction needs to be assessed on both tests, because gross margin measures the profitability left after direct production and delivery costs, not the full strategic value of international reach.

Array acquired 100% of STI on January 11, 2022, for $410.5 million of cash and 13,894,800 Array shares; Array reported fair value of total consideration at $610.8 million. STI designs, manufactures and sells utility-scale solar trackers in Spain, Brazil, the United States and South Africa. Array said that integrating STI would accelerate international expansion and help it address global utility-scale solar demand, particularly in Latin America and Africa.[3][16]

The acquisition case was therefore a portfolio-expansion strategy rather than a simple revenue addition: STI’s stated role was to accelerate international expansion, address global utility-scale demand, and help Array enter markets constrained by supply-chain issues and local-content requirements.[1][36] At closing, Array described STI as providing a complementary product, a diversified supply chain, international customer relationships, and access to a lower-cost proven product; Array also said the acquisition strengthened supply-chain networks, expanded its customer base in Brazil and Europe, opened access to the Middle East and Africa, and supported development of local supply chains.[37][36] The combination was intended to create a broader global tracker offering with leading positions outside China and India.[9][37][22] STI consequently gave Array an owned international operating platform that could remain strategically valuable even if its near-term segment gross margin trailed the Legacy business.

Array also announced a financial case. In November 2021, it expected STI Norland to be significantly accretive to Array’s margins and earnings per share during 2022 before synergies, and expected scale to support supplier-volume discounts and better absorption of fixed costs. Its announced post-closing cost plans were combined purchasing, common-component sourcing where either company had better pricing, and mutual transfer of manufacturing practices across facilities.[6][9] Those plans defined an acquisition thesis based on international expansion, complementary tracker products, supply-chain diversification, supplier-volume discounts and greater fixed-cost leverage.[6][27]

The subsequent segment record is mixed. As detailed in **Segment gross margins and the performance gap**, Array Legacy Operations and STI Operations were near parity in the first nine months of 2023, at 26.9% and 26.6%, respectively. In the first nine months of 2024, however, Array Legacy reached 41.8% while STI recorded 15.1%, creating a 26.7-percentage-point Legacy advantage. **The direction changed from near-convergence in 2023 to a large and sustained Legacy margin premium in 2024, so STI’s international-access contribution had not been matched by sustained gross-margin convergence by September 30, 2024.**[27]

This does not make the acquisition strategically unsuccessful. Geographic access, customer relationships, product breadth and supply-chain diversification can improve Array’s competitive position and future option set without appearing immediately in STI’s gross margin. But it does change the investment case’s practical emphasis: STI should be managed not merely as an international sales platform, but as a margin-recovery program whose commercial and cost economics must improve without sacrificing the geographic reach that motivated the deal. Array itself continued to identify successful STI integration and realization of anticipated benefits as execution risks in 2023.[3][38][16]

The 2024 goodwill impairment reinforces that priority. Array recorded a $162 million non-cash goodwill impairment associated with the 2022 STI acquisition in Q3 2024, reducing STI Operations goodwill from $365.864 million at the beginning of 2024 to $181.146 million at September 30, 2024. Goodwill is the acquisition premium assigned to expected future benefits beyond identifiable assets; an impairment is an accounting reduction in the carrying value of that premium.[39][18] The impairment and 2024 margin gap together indicate pressure on the financial-value case, while neither identifies a single operational cause nor negates STI’s market-access rationale.

Reported gross-margin comparisons also require disciplined interpretation. Array’s Q3 2024 segment disclosures charged Array Legacy Operations $3.639 million of developed-technology and backlog amortization and $0.602 million of depreciation in cost of revenue, while STI had none of those charges; for the first nine months of 2024, Legacy bore $10.918 million of amortization and $1.480 million of depreciation, compared with STI depreciation of $0.051 million.[17] Thus, Legacy’s reported margin leadership persisted despite disclosed acquisition-related cost allocations in its cost of revenue; the figures still do not isolate the roles of product mix, pricing, commodity costs, logistics, project execution, contract terms, or accounting allocation.

The acquisition implication is clear: preserve STI as the international tracker platform that broadened Array’s addressable markets, but judge financial integration by whether the acquired operation can generate durable gross-profit improvement rather than by revenue growth or geographic expansion alone.

The purchase-price accounting confirms that the transaction’s financial thesis included combination benefits, not only standalone STI sales. Array initially allocated $343.369 million to goodwill, stating that it represented expected future economic benefits and expected synergies from the combined entities; it also recognized $304.431 million of identifiable acquired intangibles—$50.000 million of backlog, $228.408 million of customer relationships and $26.023 million of trade name.[11]

Array financed the STI acquisition primarily with Convertible Notes borrowings and Series A Shares. At December 31, 2023, it reported $238.2 million outstanding under its Senior Secured Credit Facility and $425.0 million on Convertible Notes, and stated that this indebtedness could limit financing for working capital, capital expenditures, acquisitions, debt-service requirements, stock repurchases, other purposes, and execution of its business strategy.[11][12] The portfolio implication is to prioritize demonstrable gross-profit and cash-conversion gains from STI integration before committing incremental acquisition or expansion capital.[11][12][26]

Array was not pursuing a serial-roll-up acquisition model: STI Norland was its major recent transaction, and public materials through mid-2024 characterized Array’s M&A focus as making that acquisition work operationally and financially rather than repeatedly acquiring companies.[34]

STI’s international-expansion role was a stated acquisition rationale, while STI contributed 28.2% of Array’s revenue but only 12.4% of gross profit in the first nine months of 2024.[1][27]

The acquisition’s financial-value reassessment should center on realization of expected combination benefits: Array attributed STI acquisition goodwill partly to expected synergies, and Array’s Q3 2024 impairment followed updated long-term projections and an assessment of execution risk.[12][26]

At December 31, 2023, STI’s fair value exceeded its carrying value and Array recognized no impairment; by September 30, 2024, Array had recorded an STI goodwill impairment while the nine-month gross-margin gap with Array Legacy Operations had widened to 26.7 percentage points.[12][26][27]

A complete acquisition assessment should compare the purchase consideration and integration investment with incremental after-tax cash flows and returns, including realized synergies, retained business, capital requirements and risk. Segment gross margin is an important operating input to that assessment, but it is not by itself a measure of whether the acquisition created value.

STI Operations’ strategic value included geographic diversification, reach into Europe and a number-one position in Brazil, even though its FY2024 margin was characterized as dilutive to Array’s consolidated business.[21]

Array said the increase in operating expenses included $17.4 million of additional amortization expense related to the STI acquisition.[40]

STI Norland was founded in 1996, is headquartered in Pamplona, Spain, and manufactured in Spain and Brazil.[21]

### What integration priorities could optimize Array’s overall portfolio performance?

Judgment — Integration should use Array Legacy Operations’ 41.8% nine-month 2024 gross margin as an internal benchmark, not as a mechanical target for STI. Tracker markets differ by geography, customer mix, sourcing conditions, product specification and local-content requirements; the objective is to close avoidable economic gaps while retaining STI’s distinctive international-market capabilities.

First, Array should concentrate procurement and manufacturing integration on STI’s cost of revenue. The company’s announced integration plan already identified the appropriate levers: negotiate supplier discounts using combined volumes, select common components where Array or STI has an advantaged price, and transfer manufacturing best practices between facilities.[9] Array also reported exposure to unhedged raw-material prices, supplier disruption and supply-chain interruptions, while describing selective price increases, productivity actions, supplier-base expansion and overhead containment as inflation responses.[3] These actions make STI’s input-cost, logistics and conversion-cost performance the first operational workstream, rather than treating additional lower-margin international volume as success.

Second, Array should apply common commercial discipline to both segments. Array said it actively manages multi-year fixed-price and commodity-index-linked customer contracts and may decline arrangements when risks cannot be mitigated, even if that sends projects to competitors, because it seeks a high-quality portfolio and consistent margins.[1][16] In practice, this means segment-level bid review should test price escalation, commodity pass-through, freight exposure, delivery timing, warranty risk and working-capital demands before STI accepts growth projects. Array’s operating cash generation depends on both gross-margin strength and rapid working-capital turnover.[16]

Third, Array should complete the management-information and control integration needed to identify which STI projects, countries, customers and products create or destroy margin. STI had material weaknesses in general IT controls and in formal accounting policies, procedures and controls across substantially all business processes. Array’s remediation included additional accounting and IT personnel, outside consulting support, improvements in controls over STI, inventory, revenue recognition and accounts receivable, and business-combination controls for future acquisitions.[1][2][4] During Q2 2024, Array implemented an enterprise resource planning system, or ERP, in Brazil to enable automated controls and reduce dependence on manual controls, although remediation was not yet complete because controls still required sustained operation and formal testing.[16]

Fourth, Array should make capital allocation explicitly portfolio-based. Its chief executive officer, the chief operating decision maker, assesses both operating segments using gross profit and uses annual plans and monthly segment results to allocate operating and capital resources.[17] That governance should link STI investment to measurable improvement in project gross profit, supplier economics, inventory accuracy, delivery performance and contract quality, while protecting the stronger Legacy economics from subsidizing undisciplined expansion. STI’s market presence should be retained where local supply chains, local-content requirements or customer access improve Array’s competitive position.[36]

The resulting integration priorities should be governed by four decision tests:

1. **Test delivered-cost improvement against STI’s own starting point:** Retain procurement harmonization, common-component sourcing and cross-facility manufacturing changes only where STI project gross margin improves and STI cost per watt (CPW)—applicable cost of goods sold divided by applicable megawatts—falls versus STI’s pre-change baseline. Array identifies CPW, together with average selling price, as a measure of manufacturing-cost and customer-profitability trends. [4][9]
2. **Test commercial changes on project economics and execution quality:** Retain bid, pricing and contract-risk changes only where they improve STI project margin and cost per watt while maintaining delivery and warranty quality. The test implements Array’s stated practice of selectively managing fixed-price and commodity-index-linked contract risk for consistent margins. [4][16]
3. **Test systems and control integration on decision-quality and incremental cost:** Track whether ERP, IT-control, inventory, revenue-recognition and reporting changes provide reliable project-level economics, and measure the incremental operating and integration spending required to achieve them. Array’s CEO uses gross profit, annual operating plans and monthly segment results to allocate operating and capital resources; STI’s Brazil ERP implementation was intended to enable automated controls and reduce reliance on manual controls. [17][16]
4. **Test portfolio capital allocation on cash and return:** Track STI cash generation, working-capital conversion and return on the STI investment, retaining growth or integration changes only if they improve the combined portfolio rather than merely narrow the segment margin gap. Array states that operating cash flow depends on gross margins and rapid working-capital turnover, while STI produced $27.240 million of gross profit on $180.768 million of revenue in the nine months ended September 30, 2024. [16][17]

These tests make improvement in STI’s economics and contribution to the combined portfolio, rather than margin convergence alone, the criterion for integration decisions.[4][17][16]

Fifth, Array should make localization and policy-linked manufacturing economics part of segment planning. Array’s international strategy calls for growth in broader North America, South America, Europe, Africa and Southeast Asia, excluding China and India, using local raw materials, components and logistics, select component re-engineering to reduce costs, and region-specific customer training, site commissioning, warranty remediation and technical services.[11] In the United States, domestic-content compliance can preserve customers’ access to incremental tax credits but can also raise production costs; Array negotiated with key suppliers from late 2023 through 2024 to share Section 45X credits on torque tubes and structural fasteners, and concluded in Q2 2024 that certain internally manufactured parts qualified.[11][31]

Sixth, demand, inventory and delivery coordination should accompany cost reduction. Array stated that project delays can create downtime, higher operating costs, inefficiencies and excess inventory; during 2024 it responded to Red Sea-related congestion, transit-time and shipping-cost pressures by increasing local sourcing where feasible. Management also moved order-book reviews from quarterly to monthly and linked sales and operations communications nearly weekly or every other week to limit working-capital and inventory effects from customer project deferrals.[11][26][33]

Array’s planned information-system improvements also included expanded system capabilities for greater reliance on automated controls, less reliance on manual controls, enhanced inventory accounting, processing and recording controls, and multiple levels of review and validation of inventory-in-transit information and supporting documentation.[5]

Array should maintain a common profitability dashboard by project, region, product, customer and contract vintage. It should reconcile segment revenue with material cost, freight, labor or subcontracting, warranty and other cost-of-revenue items so management can distinguish pricing, product mix, commodity and logistics exposure, project execution, and accounting timing.

### Synthesis

Array bought STI to transform a primarily U.S.-oriented tracker business into a broader global platform with complementary products, supply-chain diversification and access to international customers. Through September 30, 2024, STI continued to provide Array’s acquired international tracker platform and generated $27.240 million of gross profit in the nine months ended September 30, 2024; at the same time, STI’s 15.07% gross margin trailed Array Legacy Operations’ 41.78% by 26.71 percentage points, and Array recorded a $162 million non-cash goodwill impairment associated with the 2022 STI acquisition.[3][17][39] Yet the movement from near-equal nine-month gross margins in 2023 to a 26.7-percentage-point Legacy advantage in the first nine months of 2024, together with the $162 million STI-related goodwill impairment, makes financial integration the dominant portfolio issue.[16][37][27][39]

Judgment — Array should retain STI’s market-access role but require evidence that procurement, manufacturing, commercial discipline and information-system integration are converting that role into durable gross profit. The strongest portfolio strategy is therefore not disposal or indiscriminate expansion: it is selective international growth paired with accountable STI margin recovery and protection of Legacy’s higher-margin economics.

Array’s integration agenda also has a product and manufacturing dimension. Its R&D objectives are to enhance system performance, improve reliability, reduce product cost and simplify installation, while its development strategy seeks customer-valued features that differentiate Array from competitors. Applying these objectives across the combined product portfolio can strengthen STI’s cost position while retaining the differentiated offerings and international reach acquired with STI.[11][12]

STI’s portfolio value was not only broader geographic reach: it gave Array access to markets constrained by supply-chain and local-content barriers, while extending customer access and local supply-chain development in Brazil, Europe, the Middle East and Africa.[36]

### Open questions

The segment disclosures do not establish the separate effects of price, product mix, freight, warranty, currency, local sourcing, Section 45X credits, customer-project timing and allocated costs on either segment’s margin. Nor do they establish STI acquisition returns or realised synergies. Segment-level unit economics, project-level pricing and cost data, and measured synergy and return-on-invested-capital results would settle those questions.
