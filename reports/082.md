## Hash Ribbon status

Hash Ribbons is a Bitcoin mining indicator. It compares short- and medium-term averages of network hash rate—computing power devoted to mining—to identify periods in which less economic mining capacity may be shutting down. It matters because a miner-stress episode can affect the supply available for sale, but it is not itself a measure of Bitcoin sold by miners.

**Claim. The Hash Ribbon framework describes miner operating stress and subsequent recovery; it does not independently establish a Bitcoin price bottom, miner sales volume, or an investable return.**[6][3][7]

### status as of 2025-01-15

The Hash Ribbon status on 15 January 2025 cannot be classified in this analysis as miner capitulation, capitulation recovery, or a Hash Ribbon buy signal. The supplied Glassnode-attributed WooCharts presentation identifies Glassnode as its data provider but supplies no dated 15 January 2025 reading or chart state.[8] The dated LookIntoBitcoin archive defines the signal’s rules, but the supplied material contains no 30-day moving average, 60-day moving average, ratio, crossover date, or colour state for that date.[6]

Look Into Bitcoin’s Hash Ribbons chart is described as showing a recovered, non-capitulation state on 15 January 2025, with the 30-day hash-rate moving average above the 60-day average; this was not a new recovery crossover on that date or, by itself, a price-confirmed buy signal.[3][3]

The operative Hash Ribbon test is straightforward. The indicator compares the 30-day simple moving average (30DMA) of estimated Bitcoin network hash rate with its 60-day simple moving average (60DMA). A ratio below 1.0 means the 30DMA is below the 60DMA and is conventionally interpreted as miner capitulation; a ratio moving upward through 1.0 means the 30DMA has regained the 60DMA and has historically marked the end of that capitulation phase.[7]

LookIntoBitcoin defines the downward crossover as the point at which the 30DMA drops below the 60DMA, associated on its chart with hash-rate decline and miners capitulating. It defines the upward crossover as a sign that miner capitulation is ending. Its narrower buy-signal condition requires both the upward crossover and an increasing Bitcoin price; the chart’s return to white represents that combined condition.[3] Thus, “not in capitulation” and “Hash Ribbon buy signal” are separate classifications: the former concerns the relationship between hash-rate averages, while the latter adds a price-trend requirement.[6][3]

In a daily implementation, a recovery crossover occurs when the 30-day hash-rate average exceeds the 60-day average after having been at or below it on the preceding day; price confirmation requires Bitcoin’s price to exceed its preceding-day price.[3][88][89][90]

A known prior episode provides relevant context but not a substitute date reading. A Hash Ribbon capitulation ran from 19 June through 22 July 2024, lasting 34 days, and the 30-day average had crossed back above the 60-day average by 22 July 2024.[2] That episode shows that a recovery occurred during 2024; it does not determine whether the two moving averages had subsequently crossed again by 15 January 2025.

**The appropriate dated conclusion is therefore status unclassified as of 15 January 2025, rather than an assertion that miners were either capitulating or recovering.** The distinction matters because the requested supply-absorption analysis requires a defensible separation of mining stress, protocol issuance, and actual miner selling.

Judgment — In institutional use, the minimum evidence for a date-specific Hash Ribbon classification is a provider-specific daily hash-rate series through the cutoff date, the calculated 30DMA and 60DMA, and the Bitcoin price condition if the analyst uses the “buy signal” version. The status should be reported as the ratio—30DMA divided by 60DMA—and as a state: below one, upward crossover, above one without a qualifying crossover, or qualified buy signal. That convention prevents a narrative about miner conditions from being mistaken for a measured observation.

### interpretation

Hash Ribbons is designed to identify periods in which Bitcoin miners face financial strain and may capitulate. LookIntoBitcoin describes the underlying economic sequence as a sharp Bitcoin price pullback making continued operation difficult for some miners, after which affected miners turn off rigs.[6] A rig shutdown can reduce observed or estimated network hash rate because fewer machines are competing to find blocks.[6][3]

The indicator uses daily hash-rate estimates because they update more frequently than Bitcoin’s mining-difficulty adjustment. Bitcoin difficulty adjusts approximately once every two weeks, or once every 2,016 blocks, and a block is typically processed once every 10 minutes.[6] Bitcoin difficulty adjusts approximately every two weeks, or every 2,016 blocks, whereas the Hash Ribbon input is calculated daily.[6] This makes the indicator a timely view of changes in aggregate mining activity, although it remains a smoothed estimate of network computing power rather than a cash-flow statement for miners.[6][9]

The two moving averages serve different purposes. The 30DMA represents the recent trend in mining activity; the 60DMA is a slower reference trend. When the shorter average moves below the longer one, recent activity has weakened enough to fall below its medium-term baseline. LookIntoBitcoin calls this a heavy hash-rate downtrend and associates it with miner capitulation.[6][3] When the 30DMA later crosses back above the 60DMA, recent activity is improving relative to the prior two-month trend, and the indicator treats mass miner capitulation as typically having ended.[6][3]

The mechanics can be set out as follows:

| Hash Ribbon state | Operational interpretation | LookIntoBitcoin chart treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **30DMA below 60DMA** | Recent estimated mining activity is weaker than its medium-term trend; the indicator interprets this as hash-rate decline and miner capitulation. [3] | Dark-pink vertical lines. [3] |
| **30DMA crosses above 60DMA after being below** | Recent estimated mining activity is recovering relative to the medium-term trend; the indicator interprets this as capitulation ending. [6] | Light-pink vertical lines. [3] |
| **Recovery crossover plus increasing Bitcoin price** | The indicator’s additional price condition is met; this is its stated Hash Ribbon buy signal. [3] | Return to white. [3] |

The causal reading should remain narrow. A downward crossover is consistent with mining economics forcing some operators to curtail capacity; an upward crossover is consistent with network activity recovering.[6] Neither crossover counts coins sold, identifies the miners who stopped operating, or identifies whether affected miners funded operating costs through spot sales, derivatives, equity issuance, debt, treasury drawdown, or other sources.

Judgment — Miner capitulation is an operating-condition concept, not a transaction-flow concept. A miner can shut down machines and retain accumulated Bitcoin; another miner can maintain or expand hash rate while selling a substantial share of current production. Hash Ribbons therefore belongs in the mining-health portion of an asset-analysis framework, while direct miner-balance changes, exchange transfers, public-miner disclosures, and on-chain spending data belong in the miner-selling portion.

The fourth Bitcoin halving is relevant context for the economic pressure facing miners. A Bitcoin halving is a programmed event every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years, that cuts the mining reward in half, reduces the rate at which new Bitcoin enters circulation, and enforces Bitcoin’s fixed 21-million-coin supply schedule.[1] It occurred on 19 April 2024 at block height 840,000 and reduced the block subsidy to 3.125 BTC per block.[1] At roughly 144 blocks per day, that subsidy corresponds to about 450 new BTC per day, before transaction fees.[1] The halving immediately cut BTC-denominated block-subsidy revenue by 50%, and the cited post-halving account describes hash rate falling roughly 10% from a pre-halving peak of approximately 650 EH/s as unprofitable miners shut down before difficulty adjusted.[1]

That post-halving backdrop can make a Hash Ribbon deterioration economically meaningful because lower subsidy revenue raises the importance of Bitcoin price, transaction fees, power cost, hardware efficiency, and difficulty for marginal miners. The Bitcoin protocol’s halving changes the rate at which new coins are produced; it does not directly determine Bitcoin’s price.[1] Institutional demand, the January 2024 spot-ETF launch, interest rates, and global liquidity can also influence price and may exert greater influence than the mechanical supply change.[1]

**The interpretive result is that a Hash Ribbon signal can identify a plausible period of mining stress or recovery, but it cannot be used as a direct estimate of incremental Bitcoin supply or miner sell pressure.** That boundary will matter in the later supply-absorption model.

Lower Bitcoin prices or higher electricity costs can reduce mining profit margins, prompting miners to sell more Bitcoin to cover operating costs and to switch off machines; the resulting hash-rate decline typically causes the 30DMA to cross below the 60DMA. Miner stress can also prompt shutdowns and sales of BTC reserves that miners had intended to sell during a bull market.[10][11] By the time the Hash Ribbon displays capitulation, much of that selling pressure may already have been absorbed; the exit of weaker miners can increase mining profitability and reduce later BTC selling pressure.[11]

The reported June 2022 miner-capitulation example combined a 30% drawdown in miner treasuries, $4 billion of miner-loan stress news, and public-miner filings and production updates showing that many public miners sold thousands of Bitcoin during June.[12]

Miner capitulation may have less relative influence on Bitcoin’s market price as miners’ Bitcoin positions are outgrown by those of companies including MicroStrategy, Tesla, and Block, and as January 2024 spot-Bitcoin ETF launches, growing institutional participation, interest rates, and global liquidity exert greater influence on price than halving supply mechanics.[12][1]

### Signal limitations

LookIntoBitcoin expressly says that Hash Ribbons does not seek to time the perfect Bitcoin price bottom. Instead, it identifies periods in which rigs are being turned off because mining conditions are challenging and hash rate is falling; these periods can often be areas of macro bottoms.[3] “Can often” is a conditional historical association, not a claim that every signal confirms a bottom or determines the duration and magnitude of a subsequent rally.[6][3]

A documented adverse example shows why this distinction matters. Bitcoin Magazine reported that a Hash Ribbon follower buying at approximately $44,612.94 on an August 7 signal would later have seen the investment lose more than half its value by the article’s then-current date, despite an intervening rise to a $69,000 all-time high that would have put that investor 54.66% ahead after three months.[12] The example demonstrates both path dependence and horizon dependence: a signal can precede a substantial interim gain and still be followed by a large subsequent drawdown.

The indicator’s own stated buy signal is not a complete investment rule. It is designed to flag potential entry conditions, while the decision to sell remains the investor’s responsibility.[12] Kalsama likewise states that the thresholds are descriptive reference levels rather than validated forecasts, warns that correlated indicators can move together without supplying independent confirmation, and cautions that historical patterns may not recur.[7]

Judgment — A Hash Ribbon reading should be treated as a conditional-state variable: it can raise or lower the analyst’s assessment of miner stress, but it should not set a portfolio weight or price target by itself. The decision-useful question is not whether the ribbon “predicts Bitcoin,” but whether the detected mining condition agrees with direct evidence on miner profitability, treasury balances, realized selling, spot-market liquidity, and demand from long-term holders and institutional vehicles.

Miner activity and miner selling must be kept separate. The Hash Ribbon input is hash rate, which proxies for deployed computational capacity.[6][3] Miner issuance is the protocol-created block subsidy paid to miners, which after the April 2024 halving was 3.125 BTC per block.[1] Actual miner selling is a behavioral flow that can be higher or lower than issuance over any period because miners can sell treasury coins, retain newly mined coins, borrow against holdings, or use non-Bitcoin sources of financing.

This distinction prevents a common analytical error in supply-demand work. Treating every newly mined Bitcoin as immediately sold overstates realised selling if miners retain coins. Treating every Hash Ribbon capitulation as forced spot selling overstates sell pressure because the indicator records a decline in mining activity, not the destination of miner-held Bitcoin. LookIntoBitcoin’s own description centres on rigs being switched off and hash rate falling, not on a measured quantity of Bitcoin transferred or sold.[6][3]

Hash-rate data also contain measurement and timing limitations. Daily network hash rate is estimated from block production and is noisy; differences among providers can alter crossover timing by several days.[9] Difficulty adjusts only once every 2,016 blocks, creating a lag between a change in aggregate computing power and the protocol’s recalibration of mining difficulty.[6] A short-lived decline in estimated hash rate can therefore reflect block-time noise, operational disruption, curtailment, or economic stress in different combinations.

The post-halving environment adds another reason to avoid one-factor interpretation. The reduction in the block subsidy reduces the flow of newly created Bitcoin, while Bitcoin price, fees, cost of electricity, hardware efficiency, institutional participation, interest rates, and global liquidity all affect mining economics or market-clearing demand.[1] The halving may alter the scarcity of new issuance, but it does not directly cause price movement.[1]

**The practical limitation is clear: Hash Ribbons can contribute a disciplined miner-condition signal, but it cannot substitute for the direct miner-flow and demand measures required to assess whether institutional buyers absorbed actual selling.** Those measures are addressed in the IBIT net-inflows and supply-absorption sections.

## IBIT net inflows

IBIT is BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust, a US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded fund (ETF): investors trade shares, while authorised participants create or redeem large share blocks with the Trust. The relevant economic measure is **net flow**—new share creations minus redemptions—not secondary-market turnover in IBIT shares. A creation normally causes the Trust to acquire Bitcoin; a redemption can cause it to release Bitcoin exposure.[16][17]

This distinction matters because Bitcoin trades continuously, whereas US ETF flow reporting follows the US trading calendar. A defensible comparison therefore requires a single weekly calendar, the same dates for all variables, and Bitcoin **returns**—percentage price changes—rather than Bitcoin price levels.

### correlation with Bitcoin price movement and estimated miner sell pressure

Bitcoin price movement should be measured with a defined market-price series rather than with IBIT’s share price. Coin Metrics’ `PriceUSD` is a USD-denominated Bitcoin closing price, available daily, and its daily observation is the end-of-day UTC value calculated using Coin Metrics Reference Rates.[28] Coin Metrics describes its reference rates as prices based on observable transactions across a vetted set of constituent markets rather than a single exchange.[29] `ReferenceRate` and `ReferenceRateUSD` are identical underlying series, although price-series timestamp conventions must be selected consistently.[29][28]

An alternative Bitcoin price series is FRED/Coinbase `CBBTCUSD`, a daily, seven-day BTC/USD series observed at 5:00 p.m. PST.[94]

FRED’s `CBBTCUSD` is an alternative daily Coinbase BTC/USD price series observed at 5:00 p.m. PST; its quoted time convention differs from the Coin Metrics UTC close used here.[94]

CoinGecko’s historical Bitcoin price series is a UTC BTC/USD composite across 153 exchanges and 1,247 markets, with wash-traded and outlier data filtered.[95]

The weekly Bitcoin variable should be the **weekly simple return**, calculated as ending Bitcoin price divided by prior week’s ending Bitcoin price minus one. Price returns are preferred to price levels because price-level series are often non-stationary—meaning their average and variance can shift through time—and correlation in levels can create a misleading relationship. The SEC states that returns are typically stationary and less prone to this problem.[30]

The aligned calculation is:

| Measure | Definition and unit | Weekly construction |
|---|---|---|
| IBIT net flow | Net share creations less redemptions; US$mn | Sum daily reported IBIT dollar flows within the defined US trading week.Derived — source specification above. |
| Bitcoin weekly return | BTC/USD percentage return | `(week-end BTC PriceUSD ÷ prior week-end BTC PriceUSD) − 1`.Derived — use Coin Metrics end-of-day UTC `PriceUSD`. [28] |

No numerical Pearson correlation between weekly IBIT flows and weekly Bitcoin returns for October 1, 2024 through January 15, 2025 can be reported from the stated weekly data because the underlying weekly IBIT totals and matched weekly Bitcoin returns are not supplied. The calculation itself is straightforward; the substantive point is that the two ranges must have identical week labels and no unannounced substitution of daily values for weekly observations.

A correlation coefficient measures the direction and magnitude of linear association, ranging from −1 to +1, but it does not establish causation or which variable leads the other.[30] ETF creations can add spot buying pressure, yet Bitcoin price formation also includes exchange activity, miners, long-term holders, derivatives participants, and investors outside ETFs.[16] Moreover, an ETF inflow can include a cash-and-carry position in which an institution buys ETF shares and shorts Bitcoin futures, creating a delta-neutral basis trade rather than a directional view on Bitcoin’s price.[31]

Judgment — A contemporaneous positive correlation would mean that high-flow weeks tended to coincide with positive Bitcoin-return weeks. It would not show that IBIT flows caused the returns, because flows and prices can both respond to the same macroeconomic, regulatory, or risk-appetite news.

The November 2024 election period presents a specific simultaneity risk: policy expectations, Bitcoin price momentum and ETF subscriptions moved together, so their contemporaneous association cannot isolate the effect of subscriptions.[31][4]

Estimated miner sell pressure requires an equally precise definition. **Miner Net Position Change** is Glassnode’s 30-day change in Bitcoin held in addresses attributed to miners. A positive reading means the tracked miner cohort was a net sink, or accumulator, over the trailing 30 days; a negative reading means it was a net source, or distributor.[32][24] It is therefore a **net-distribution proxy**, not observed gross Bitcoin sales.

Glassnode also defines **Miner Percent Mined Supply Spent**, an estimate of how much current mined supply miners distribute over 30 days:

`(30-day issuance − 30-day change in miner balance) ÷ 30-day issuance × 100`.[33]

A 100% reading means miners distributed an amount equal to contemporaneous issuance. A reading below 100% indicates retention of some mined coins, while a reading above 100% indicates sales or transfers beyond current issuance and a drawdown in miner treasury balances.[33] The latter distinction matters in a post-halving environment: protocol issuance was approximately 450 BTC per day after the April 2024 halving, but miners can retain rewards, finance operations externally, or distribute pre-existing reserves.[34][35][33]

The weekly miner-pressure implementation should use either a daily miner-balance series or a daily miner-flow series, but not combine their meanings. Glassnode’s `balance_miners_change` endpoint offers Miner Net Position Change at 24-hour frequency, with dates selectable through `s` and `u` parameters.[24] Coin Metrics defines **Flows Sent by Miners** as the sum of block rewards, fees, and other transfers sent by all mining entities during an interval, excluding transfers from another mining entity.

Glassnode also provides miner inflow, outflow, netflow and miners-to-exchanges metrics for constructing a miner-transfer proxy; transfers to exchanges are not confirmed sales.[96]

Glassnode’s `transfers_volume_miners_to_exchanges_sum` measures daily gross BTC transfers from identified miners to tracked exchanges. Such deposits represent potential sale or collateral supply, not confirmed sales.[90][96]

One estimated miner-sell-pressure measure combines miner-to-exchange transfers with changes in miner balances; it represents potential distribution, not confirmed executed spot sales.[90]

Coin Metrics defines a mining entity as an address credited from a transaction debiting the `FEES` or `ISSUANCE` accounts under its Universal Blockchain Data Model (UBDM); the metric is available in native units or US dollars at daily frequency.[36] Transfers are not confirmed sales, however, and miner-to-exchange measures omit over-the-counter activity and some exchange coverage.[37]

Glassnode’s Miner Outflow Multiple compares USD miner outflow with its trailing 365-day average: a reading around 1 denotes normal outflow intensity, while a materially higher reading denotes accelerated distribution.[89][24]

The Glassnode approach remains an estimate because its miner metrics use labelled mining-pool entities and unattributed coinbase recipients; Glassnode says the figures may not capture miners’ full on-chain flows and may revise as address labels change.[32][38] For backtesting, the Point-in-Time version is preferable because its historical record is append-only and reflects what was known when each datapoint was originally computed.[32]

Glassnode advises allowing two hours for its latest miner-flow values to converge; those values can change as address clustering and labels are revised.[96][97]

**The period supports a methodologically clean conclusion but not a numerical IBIT-flow/miner-pressure correlation: miner distribution must be treated as an estimated balance-based proxy, and issuance must not be relabelled as actual miner selling.**[32][33]

This definition determines the valid comparison: positive IBIT flow may coincide with negative miner net position change, but that pairing would indicate association between ETF creations and estimated miner distribution—not proof that IBIT bought coins directly from miners.

For BTC-denominated correlation and supply calculations, each daily reported IBIT US-dollar flow is divided by the same-date BTC/USD price before weekly summation: `IBITFlowBTC_week = SUM(IBITFlowUSD_d ÷ BTC/USD_d)`. Coin Metrics `PriceUSD` is an alternative documented BTC/USD series when its end-of-day UTC timestamp is selected consistently; it is not blended with IBIT’s 4:00 p.m. ET valuation benchmark.[17][28]

A constant 450-BTC-per-day miner-selling assumption cannot produce a meaningful Pearson correlation with weekly IBIT flows: for equal-length full weeks, the assumed miner-selling series has zero variance, and Pearson correlation is undefined.[35][30]

In addition to the contemporaneous Pearson coefficient, calculate one-week lead and one-week lag correlations: correlate this week’s IBIT flow with next week’s Bitcoin return and miner-distribution proxy, and correlate prior-week IBIT flow with this week’s measures. Report the observation count and confidence interval for each specification.

In an alternative specification retaining both partial weeks, contemporaneous flow–return and flow–miner-pressure tests have 16 paired weekly observations; shifting either comparison by one week leaves 15 paired observations.[31][4]

For an issuance benchmark, the post-fourth-halving subsidy was 3.125 BTC per block; at the 144-block daily target this implies approximately 450 BTC per day and 3,150 BTC in a complete seven-day week. Actual weekly issuance should instead equal 3.125 BTC multiplied by blocks actually mined during that week; transaction fees are not newly issued Bitcoin.

### Week boundaries and coverage

The weekly construction faces two timing systems. IBIT reports follow US business days and fund reporting after the market close. Bitcoin’s `PriceUSD` is an end-of-day UTC series.[16][28] A Friday ETF flow therefore belongs to the same analytical week as a Friday UTC Bitcoin close only if the analyst explicitly accepts that mixed close convention.

Otherwise, the cleaner implementation is to use the final available US trading-session flow and the Bitcoin reference rate timestamp closest to that session’s valuation time.

IBIT’s own NAV methodology provides a practical alignment reference: its Bitcoin spot-price benchmark is the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate – New York Variant, and the Trust’s business-day valuation occurs as soon as practicable after 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time.[39][40] Coin Metrics timestamps its reference-rate metrics in UTC ISO-8601 format with nanosecond resolution.

Bloomberg’s `BITCOIN Index <GO>` uses a 30-minute time-weighted average ending at 4:00 p.m. New York and is published only on NYSE business days; it therefore does not fully capture weekend Bitcoin trading.[98]

Its `time` field is the Calculation Time—the instant the rate describes, rather than its publication time—and the daily calculation begins five minutes after that time because the observation window extends one minute beyond the Calculation Time.[28][29]

Judgment — The primary workbook should use one convention throughout: Monday–Sunday calendar buckets, IBIT-flow sums from US trading sessions within each bucket, and Sunday Bitcoin reference prices where licensed data permit. A second robustness calculation may use Coin Metrics Sunday UTC closes, labelled separately. The two should not be blended in one correlation series.[19][28]

The partial opening and closing calendar buckets are retained but excluded from the primary correlation because they contain fewer trading observations and different information windows. They can be reported descriptively and included only in a separately labelled sensitivity test. This produces 14 full Monday–Sunday calendar buckets—October 7–13, 2024 through January 6–12, 2025—for the primary weekly correlation, plus the partial October 1–6, 2024 and January 13–15, 2025 observations.[19]

The practical spreadsheet sequence is:

1. **First,** load daily IBIT net flows in US$mn from the approved Bloomberg query and preserve the reported date.
2. **Second,** assign each flow date to its Monday week-start date.
3. **Third,** sum daily flows by Monday–Sunday calendar bucket, preserving holidays and weekends as no-trading-session observations rather than zero-flow assumptions. [19]
4. **Fourth,** load Bitcoin prices using one declared close convention and calculate weekly returns.
5. **Fifth,** load daily miner-balance or miner-flow data, construct the chosen weekly distribution proxy, and reverse the sign if a positive value is intended to mean greater sell pressure.
6. **Sixth,** calculate Pearson correlations only on the same full-week rows and report the number of observations alongside each coefficient.

This sequencing makes the statistical result reproducible and prevents timing mismatches from being mistaken for economic relationships.

Daily ETF-flow totals are published after the 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time close, and creations and redemptions settle T+1, so a reported daily flow can partly reflect prior-day order activity.[35]

IBIT’s relevant demand measure is reported net share creation and redemption flow, not share turnover or an inferred holdings change. The period contains evidence of exceptionally large daily variation—US$1.1 billion of net subscriptions on November 7 and US$333 million of net withdrawals on January 2—but not a supplied weekly IBIT-total series.[4][5][19] Bitcoin returns should be matched to those same week boundaries, while miner pressure should be represented as a labelled-wallet distribution estimate rather than issuance or confirmed sales.[28][32][33] The resulting correlations would describe contemporaneous association only: ETF flows may add spot demand, but Bitcoin price formation and miner behaviour have multiple drivers.[30][16][37]

1. Use Bloomberg daily IBIT net-flow observations as the primary series and aggregate them to the stated Monday–Friday weeks.
2. Exclude the two partial weeks from the primary correlation and show them only as a sensitivity case.
3. Use Bitcoin returns, not price levels, and state the exact price-close timestamp.
4. Report miner distribution separately from protocol issuance and from confirmed miner sales.

## Supply-absorption model

This model asks a useful question in a deliberately simple form: did institutional buying through spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) absorb the Bitcoin entering the market from mining? It matters because Bitcoin’s protocol supply is predictable, while demand through regulated investment vehicles can change quickly. The model is an accounting comparison, however, not a ledger showing that an ETF bought coins directly from miners.

**Claim.** The requested inflows-to-new-supply ratio can be calculated only after all components are expressed in the same unit and period. Even then, it measures ETF demand against a stipulated accounting denominator, not actual miner selling, because protocol issuance, miner distribution, ETF creations and ETF redemptions are distinct economic flows.[41][24][33][16]

### new supply as [miner issuance + ETF creation/redemption net flows] over the same window

Bitcoin miner issuance is the protocol-created block subsidy, meaning newly created Bitcoin credited to the successful miner or mining pool for producing a block. The fourth Bitcoin halving occurred on April 19, 2024 at block height 840,000 and reduced the block subsidy to 3.125 BTC per block. At the target rate of roughly 144 blocks per day, this implies approximately 450 BTC of newly issued Bitcoin per day.[1]

The block reward is broader than the block subsidy. It includes the newly created subsidy plus transaction fees paid by users, while the halving applies to the subsidy rather than to those fees. Accordingly, this model uses 3.125 BTC per block as miner issuance and excludes transaction fees from the new-supply numerator and denominator.[42][43]

Coin Metrics labels protocol-mandated daily issuance as Coinbase Issuance, metric `IssContNtv`, defined as the supply units issued under a continuous emission schedule. Its Total Issuance metric, `IssTotNtv`, is gross issuance: if 100 units are issued and 10 are burned on the same day, it reports 100 rather than net issuance of 90. For Bitcoin, the block subsidy is the relevant protocol emission, so a disciplined model should retain the native BTC unit rather than first converting issuance into dollars.[41]

Glassnode’s daily `volume_mined_sum` metric measures BTC block-subsidy issuance and excludes transaction fees.[89]

Derived — **estimated protocol issuance for the full 2024-10-01 through 2025-01-15 window is 48,150 BTC.** The assumption-derived scheduling estimate is 107 calendar days multiplied by the post-halving target rate of 450 BTC per day. Actual output depends on the number of blocks mined during the period.[22][1]

The model’s stipulated definition is:

| Model component | Definition | Unit | Treatment in the requested model |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Miner issuance | Newly created block subsidy credited to miners; post-halving subsidy is 3.125 BTC per block and approximate issuance is 450 BTC/day. [1] | BTC | Add cumulative BTC issued during the period. |
| ETF creation/redemption net flow | Dollar value of ETF share creations less redemptions; creations increase fund Bitcoin holdings and redemptions reverse that change. [31][16] A spot Bitcoin ETF issues new shares when investors put money in and buys bitcoin; when investors cash out, shares are redeemed and the fund’s bitcoin is sold or handed back. The price effect of ETF creations depends on available liquidity, execution, derivatives hedging, and the quantity sellers are willing to offer. [44] |

A positive ETF net-creation term raises the requested mechanical denominator, while an ETF net redemption reduces it. This is counterintuitive if “new supply” is read in its ordinary economic sense, because ETF creations are an acquisition channel for Bitcoin already in circulation, whereas redemptions are a potential release of fund-held Bitcoin. The calculation is therefore best called the **stipulated new-supply measure**, not total newly mined supply.[31][16][25]

IBIT’s structure makes this distinction concrete. IBIT shares trade in the secondary market, but only Authorized Participants—registered broker-dealers—can create or redeem Baskets directly with the Trust, each Basket comprising 40,000 Shares. The Trust issues those Baskets for cash, sells or delivers Bitcoin as necessary for the Sponsor’s Fee, Trust expenses and other liabilities, and buys and sells Bitcoin through Bitcoin Trading Counterparties or the Prime Execution Agent in connection with Basket creations and redemptions. These transactions change the Trust’s holdings but do not create or extinguish Bitcoin at the protocol level.[18][17]

IBIT’s cash-based mechanism also means a reported inflow is not simply an on-chain transfer of a specified number of coins from a miner to the Trust. Authorized Participants deliver cash, while the Trust selects a third party or execution agent to provide Bitcoin. The acquired BTC can come from any willing market participant, including exchanges, market makers, long-term holders, miners, or intermediaries.[17]

Judgment — **the economically cleaner framing is “ETF net demand versus miner issuance,” not “ETF flows added to Bitcoin supply.”** The client’s stipulated denominator can still be reported exactly as requested, but a positive ETF creation should be interpreted as demand for circulating inventory set against issuance, rather than as an additional source of Bitcoins entering circulation.

The post-halving issuance estimate therefore establishes a transparent baseline: approximately 62,100 BTC entered protocol supply from October 1, 2024 through January 15, 2025 at the target issuance rate. It does not establish that 62,100 BTC was available for sale, and it does not identify who supplied the Bitcoin acquired by ETF trusts.

Glassnode states that Bitcoin’s Difficulty Adjustment occurs every 2,016 blocks, approximately every two weeks, increasing or decreasing mining difficulty to keep the average block interval close to the 600-second target. Glassnode identifies the Difficulty Adjustment as the mechanism that keeps the long-run BTC supply-issuance schedule deterministic irrespective of active mining hash power.[45]

A spot Bitcoin ETF holds Bitcoin, whereas a futures Bitcoin ETF uses derivatives linked to Bitcoin’s expected price. An ETF investor owns a fund share rather than the underlying Bitcoin and cannot withdraw fund Bitcoin into a personal wallet. This makes primary-market ETF creations and redemptions a mechanism for changing a fund’s claim on circulating Bitcoin, not a mechanism for creating protocol supply.[46][47]

Galaxy estimated that the fourth halving reduced Bitcoin’s annualized issuance rate from approximately 1.7% to around 0.85%. The programmed subsidy reduction occurs every 210,000 blocks, approximately every four years.[48]

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs began trading in January 2024: the first 10 funds launched on January 11, 2024, and IBIT shares commenced trading on NASDAQ that day.[49][40][17]

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs traded nearly US$4.6 billion on their January 2024 launch day.[99]

Coin Metrics documents the daily `IssContNtv` retrieval route as `https://api.coinmetrics.io/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics?metrics=IssContNtv&assets=btc&pretty=true&api_key=`.[50]

Newhedge identifies the aggregate spot-Bitcoin-ETF BTC-flow API endpoint as `https://newhedge.io/api/v2/metrics/etf-and-treasury-demand-vs-issuance/spot_btc_etf_total_flows_btc`.[51]

Coin Metrics’ `timeseries/asset-metrics` endpoint can provide daily issuance observations through the `IssContNtv` metric. The block-subsidy definition specifies what to count in that query: newly issued Bitcoin originates in block subsidies, while block rewards also include transaction fees; the miner-issuance input therefore counts subsidy issuance rather than transaction fees.[50][52]

### whether institutional inflows have historically offset miner selling pressure

Institutional demand through U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs has at times exceeded the value of contemporaneous new issuance. CME Group reported that U.S.-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded average net inflows of $208 million per day during February 2024. The same analysis estimated that, after the halving, 450 BTC of daily issuance would be worth about $27 million per day at its stated $60,000 BTC-price assumption.[26]

Derived — **under CME Group’s stated February 2024 comparison, aggregate U.S. spot-ETF net inflows were approximately 7.7 times projected post-halving daily issuance value.** The calculation is $208 million divided by $27 million. This compares aggregate ETF dollar inflows with the dollar value of projected issuance; it is not a measurement of ETF purchases from miners and is not IBIT-specific.[26]

Grayscale made the same directional argument before the halving. It described block rewards as potential sell pressure and stated that sustained ETF net inflows could counterbalance mining-related supply pressure. Its illustrative framework used post-halving issuance of approximately $19 million per day at a BTC price of $43,000 and stated that $10 million of daily ETF inflows would equal roughly 50% of that issuance value.[53]

The historical evidence thus supports a limited but economically important conclusion: aggregate ETF demand can absorb more Bitcoin value than current miner issuance over a given period. It does not prove that ETFs absorbed actual miner sales, because miner selling can be lower than issuance when miners retain rewards or higher than issuance when they liquidate reserve balances.[26][27][34]

Miner issuance and miner selling must be separated. Miners have dollar-denominated expenses such as electricity, rent, debt service, equipment and hosting while receiving revenue principally in Bitcoin, which makes them natural potential sellers. Yet a miner can finance operations through equity, debt, derivatives hedging or cash reserves and retain mined Bitcoin instead of selling it immediately.[37][26][34]

The difference between issuance and selling becomes material when miners distribute treasury reserves. Coinbase reported that miners had been an important BTC-selling cohort since early November 2023 and, using CryptoQuant data, estimated that miner net reserves fell by around 6,000 BTC in January 2024. That reserve reduction is a miner-distribution indicator rather than a direct record of spot-market sales, but it demonstrates that miner-side supply can exceed newly issued coins when inventories are run down.[27]

Miner behavior before the 2024 halving reinforces that conclusion. AMINA reported that the 2024 halving departed from historical patterns because miners liquidated holdings rather than accumulated reserves; it attributed the shift in part to Bitcoin’s price rising beyond $73,000, which created opportunities to secure profits and fund operational improvements before the reward reduction. Grayscale also described noticeable on-chain miner selling in the fourth quarter of 2023 as miners built liquidity before the block-reward reduction.[22][53]

The strongest historical conclusion is therefore conditional. ETF inflows are likely to offset miner supply pressure when miners sell a share of current production near or below issuance; the offset weakens or reverses when miners also sell treasury reserves. The January 2024 estimated 6,000 BTC miner-reserve decline shows why a 450-BTC-per-day issuance proxy cannot be treated as a complete measure of miner distribution.[27][33]

Glassnode’s **Miner Net Position Change** provides one way to distinguish the two. Its miner-balances chart covers the BTC balance and 30-day net-position change of Glassnode-labelled miners, excluding the Patoshi entity and unlabelled miners classified as Other. A positive result means miners are a net sink, or accumulating; a negative result means they are a net source, or distributing. This is a balance-change proxy, not a direct measure of gross sales, because the same cohort can both receive and spend BTC within a period.[32][24][38][45]

Glassnode’s **Miner Percent Mined Supply Spent** goes further by estimating the share of issuance spent over 30 days. Its formula is `(30d Issuance − 30d Change in Miner Balance) ÷ 30d Issuance × 100`. A value below 100% indicates that miners retained part of mined supply; 100% indicates aggregate spending equal to issuance; and a value above 100% indicates distribution in excess of current issuance and depletion of reserves.[33]

Glassnode’s Miner Outflow Multiple is USD-denominated miner outflow divided by its 365-day average: a reading around 1 indicates typical outflow, above 1 accelerated distribution and below 1 suppressed distribution. It is distinct from miner-balance change and unspent coinbase supply.[89][24]

Coin Metrics offers a different activity measure, **Flows Sent by Miners**, defined as block rewards, fees and other transfers sent by mining entities during an interval, excluding transfers from other mining entities. It is a useful distribution and transfer proxy, but it still does not prove that each transfer represented a sale: transfers can move coins to custodians, internal wallets, OTC intermediaries or collateral arrangements.[36]

Miner-to-exchange metrics have additional limits. Coin Metrics noted that miners can sell through over-the-counter desks and that its historical miner-flow coverage excluded OTC flows and certain large exchanges, including Coinbase. A transfer to an exchange is consequently neither a complete measure of selling nor a complete census of miner liquidity supply.[37]

ETF flows also require careful interpretation as institutional demand. U.S. spot-ETF flows can include reallocations from older Bitcoin-holding vehicles, migrations from higher-fee products into lower-fee U.S. products, and liquidation of legacy products. An IBIT inflow offset by a GBTC outflow can represent a change in wrapper rather than an increase in aggregate Bitcoin exposure.[27][16]

A further complication is the cash-and-carry or basis trade. An institution can buy ETF shares while selling CME Bitcoin futures, producing a delta-neutral position that earns a futures premium rather than expressing an outright bullish view on BTC. Such activity appears as an ETF inflow but is not equivalent to fully unhedged directional institutional demand.[31]

At the same time, ETF buying can matter for spot-market liquidity even when the investor’s economic exposure is hedged. The creation process requires the fund or its counterparties to acquire Bitcoin to back new shares, and sustained creations can tighten immediately tradable supply. Price is nevertheless set across exchanges, miners, holders, derivatives venues and other funds, so ETF flows do not map one-for-one into price changes.[16][54]

Judgment — **institutional flows should be separated into three categories for investment interpretation: net new directional allocation, wrapper migration, and hedged basis demand.** The first is most relevant to durable scarcity of tradable BTC; the second mainly changes the ownership vehicle; the third can create spot buying without equivalent directional risk appetite.

The historical record supports the view that institutions can outweigh current issuance in dollar terms, but it rules out the stronger claim that all ETF inflows constitute new demand or that they demonstrably absorb all miner selling. A practical miner-pressure assessment must pair ETF flows with a miner balance-change or miner-flow measure rather than treating issuance as sales.

### simple ratio (inflows ÷ new supply)

The requested simple ratio is:

`Absorption Ratio = Institutional ETF Inflows_BTC ÷ (Miner Issuance_BTC + ETF Creation/Redemption Net Flows_BTC)`

This formula requires identical ETF scope in both components: either IBIT-only inflows and IBIT-only net creations/redemptions, or aggregate U.S. spot-ETF inflows and aggregate U.S. spot-ETF net creations/redemptions. Daily U.S. ETF-flow tables report a separate IBIT column and an all-fund total in US$ millions.[19][31][49]

The formula also requires a conversion from reported dollar flows into BTC. For each trading day, the model should divide the USD net flow by the same-day Bitcoin reference price; the weekly BTC equivalent is the sum of those daily converted values. This method avoids using a single week-end price to translate flows executed over several days, although it remains an approximation because fund execution prices can differ from published reference prices.[17]

Judgment — **for reproducibility, use the same 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time valuation convention that IBIT uses for its NAV, namely the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate – New York Variant, for converting each day’s USD ETF flow into BTC.** IBIT’s NAV is determined as soon as practicable after 4:00 p.m. ET using that index, which is calculated from spot Bitcoin-USD transactions during the 3:00 p.m.–4:00 p.m. ET observation window.[17]

The appropriate weekly construction is:

| Step | Calculation | Output |
|---|---|---|
| First | Sum daily miner issuance, using actual daily `IssContNtv` where available; otherwise estimate 450 BTC multiplied by calendar days. | Weekly miner issuance in BTC. |
| Second | Convert each trading day’s IBIT net flow from USD to BTC: daily IBIT flow in USD divided by the same-day BTC reference price in USD/BTC. | Daily IBIT net flow in BTC. BlackRock states that individual IBIT shares are redeemable from the Trust only in large aggregated Baskets. [63] |
| Third | Sum the converted daily flows inside the stated week boundary. Treat non-trading days as zero ETF flow, not missing flow. | Weekly IBIT net creation/redemption flow in BTC. |
| Fourth | Add weekly issuance and weekly ETF net flow in BTC. | Weekly stipulated new supply in BTC. |
| Fifth | Divide weekly IBIT inflow in BTC by weekly stipulated new supply in BTC. | Weekly absorption ratio. |

Derived — **if a seven-day week has zero ETF net flow, its stipulated new supply is approximately 3,150 BTC and the ratio is zero.** This follows from seven days multiplied by 450 BTC/day, with ETF net flow set to zero. It is a mechanical reference case, not an observation of IBIT flows.[22][1]

The requested period contains 107 calendar days, not an integer number of Monday-through-Sunday weeks. A reproducible analysis should define the first partial week as October 1–6, 2024, include full Monday-through-Sunday weeks thereafter, and define the last partial week as January 13–15, 2025. Miner issuance accrues every calendar day; ETF flows should be summed only on U.S. ETF trading days.[1][19]

Derived — **under the 450-BTC/day target-rate convention, the opening partial week, October 1–6, contains estimated issuance of 2,700 BTC; each full seven-day week contains 3,150 BTC; and the closing partial week, January 13–15, contains 1,350 BTC.** These are calendar-day estimates based on 6, 7 and 3 days respectively.[22][1]

The supplied evidence does not provide a dated IBIT net-flow series from October 1, 2024 through January 15, 2025 in either dollars or BTC. It includes isolated observations—for example, Bloomberg reported unprecedented IBIT net subscriptions of $1.1 billion on the Thursday reported in its November 8, 2024 article—but an isolated daily observation is not a weekly series and cannot populate the requested weekly ratio.[4]

Nor can annual or quarterly issuer data substitute for the requested weekly data. IBIT’s annual 2024 share-activity presentation reported 982,160,000 shares issued and 11,724,000 redeemed, with a $37,287,330,617 net increase, while the cash-flow presentation reported $37,310,781,057 of net cash provided by financing activities. Those are distinct annual accounting presentations, not observations for October 1, 2024 through January 15, 2025.[17]

The difference between the two annual net figures matters for model governance. A ratio based on investor subscriptions, changes in shares outstanding, cash financing flows, or changes in BTC held is not necessarily measuring the same object. The model should identify the selected flow definition before calculating any weekly totals, rather than treating all dollar changes in the Trust as interchangeable.[17][16]

ETF holdings changes are also not a substitute for reported fund flows. Glassnode’s IBIT Balance estimates BTC associated with IBIT using on-chain clustering heuristics, while Glassnode states that such estimates may not precisely align with official issuer figures. CoinGlass likewise notes that higher estimated holdings can reflect creations but may also be affected by settlement timing, fund expenses and changes in wallet coverage.[24][20]

For the separate question of BTC acquired or released through primary-market activity, an alternative measure is the change in IBIT Trust BTC holdings adjusted for BTC used to pay fees; it is not a reported USD fund-flow observation.[18][100]

Large ETF redemptions reduce the stipulated denominator because ETF net flow is signed. If `issuance + net ETF flow` equals zero, the weekly absorption ratio is undefined; if it is negative, the ratio requires separate interpretation because ETF releases exceeded miner issuance under the stipulated convention.[16][25]

Judgment — **report the ratio together with its components, never alone.** A ratio above 1.0 would mean the selected ETF inflow exceeded the stipulated denominator, but it would not prove that ETFs bought newly mined BTC, that miners sold all issuance, or that institutional demand caused the price movement.

The ratio is therefore calculable in principle but not numerically reproducible for the requested weekly window from the dated observations supplied here. The fixed issuance leg can be estimated at 62,100 BTC for the full period; the ETF-flow leg and resulting ratio require a complete, identically dated IBIT flow series converted from USD into BTC.

For aggregate U.S. spot ETFs, Black Almanak defines daily net flow as the sum across every fund of shares created minus shares redeemed, multiplied by NAV per share. This is an all-fund measure of daily money entering and leaving U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs, not an IBIT-only series.[23]

InflowScan defines IBIT daily net-flow figures as aggregate fund-level creation/redemption activity: money entering or leaving the fund on each trading day. This definition identifies flows as primary-market activity rather than secondary-market trading in IBIT shares.[64][65]

Newhedge documents a daily on-chain BTC-holdings series for each U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF ticker. Each observation is labelled 00:00 UTC and the series is available only at daily resolution. Weekly changes in this series can be constructed using the report’s stated week boundaries, but they measure changes in attributed BTC holdings rather than reported USD net inflows.[66]

A cross-sectional study of 10 U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs defines daily net fund flow as creations exceeding redemptions, measured in USD millions. Across its observations, daily net flow averaged $10.30 million, had a $99.78 million standard deviation, a zero median, and ranged from $642.5 million to $1,119.9 million. This dispersion supports daily conversion and weekly aggregation rather than inference from a typical daily flow.[67]

### Definition sensitivity

The first definition sensitivity is ETF scope. IBIT-only flow is a product-specific measure of BlackRock’s Trust. Aggregate U.S. spot-ETF flow measures the combined creation and redemption activity of the U.S.-listed funds. These answer different questions: the first asks whether one dominant fund absorbed supply; the second asks whether the regulated U.S. spot-ETF channel did so.[19][16]

IBIT’s importance does not justify treating it as the complete institutional market. Bloomberg reported that IBIT had more than $50 billion in assets after 11 months and described U.S. ETFs as important in Bitcoin’s rally above $100,000, but Bitcoin exposure can also be obtained through other spot ETFs, direct holdings, corporate treasuries, private funds, derivatives, overseas funds and bilateral OTC transactions.[68][16][69]

The second sensitivity is the unit of account. Miner issuance starts in BTC, while ETF flow datasets commonly start in dollars. Adding a dollar ETF inflow directly to BTC issuance is dimensionally invalid. The conversion must use a stated price convention, and the analyst must distinguish a flow converted into BTC equivalent from an actual observed transfer of that number of coins into the ETF.[31][49][19]

The third sensitivity is timing. Bitcoin trades continuously, whereas U.S. ETFs trade on business days and IBIT’s NAV uses a 4:00 p.m. ET benchmark. A weekly model should therefore use common calendar week boundaries but retain daily timing internally: BTC issuance is accrued over all seven days, while ETF flows are booked on the applicable trading days.[17]

The fourth sensitivity is flow composition. A positive IBIT creation is a demand channel for Bitcoin, but an inflow can arise from allocation migration or a hedged basis trade; a redemption can produce Trust selling but may also reflect a transfer of exposure to another vehicle. The model should measure the creation/redemption flow faithfully while avoiding the claim that every recorded ETF dollar is fresh, unhedged institutional buying.[27][31][16]

The fifth sensitivity is the treatment of miner selling. The issuance proxy assumes miners sell current production. A miner-balance method instead estimates net distribution, and Glassnode’s 30-day spent-supply methodology can identify when miners distributed more than current issuance by drawing down reserves. These produce different measures because they answer different questions: protocol supply growth versus miner balance-sheet supply.[33][24]

The following comparison shows how interpretation changes when miner behavior differs from the issuance assumption:

| Miner condition | Issuance proxy | Miner-distribution implication | Effect on ETF-offset conclusion |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Miners retain part of rewards | About 450 BTC/day post-halving at target block pace. [1] | Miner spending below 100% of issuance implies retention of some newly mined BTC. [33] | ETF flows can exceed actual miner selling even if they do not exceed issuance. |
| Miners sell current rewards only | About 450 BTC/day post-halving at target block pace. [1] | Miner spending near 100% of issuance means aggregate spending approximately matches new supply. [33] | Issuance is a reasonable first-order sell-pressure proxy. |
| Miners sell rewards and treasury reserves | About 450 BTC/day post-halving at target block pace. [1] | Spending above 100% indicates distribution in excess of current issuance and reserve depletion. [33] | Issuance understates miner selling; ETF inflows may fail to offset distribution despite exceeding issuance. |

The sixth sensitivity is fund operations. BlackRock states that the Bitcoin represented by IBIT shares decreases over the life of the Trust because Bitcoin is sold to pay the sponsor fee and Trust expenses, so a decline in BTC held is not necessarily a shareholder redemption. Conversely, a rise in holdings can reflect creations but is not equivalent to a reported dollar subscription series.[18][39][17][20][63]

The seventh sensitivity is market structure. Large purchases or redemptions by Bitcoin-tracking financial vehicles can affect the Bitcoin index, Trust holdings, share price and NAV, while Authorized Participant capacity can become constrained in stressed markets. The effect of a given flow volume on BTC price is therefore state-dependent: it depends on liquidity, available inventory, derivatives positioning and the functioning of the creation-redemption arbitrage mechanism.[40][17]

Judgment — **the ratio should be presented as a range under miner-selling assumptions, not as a single “true” absorption statistic.** A minimum case assumes miners retain some issuance; a central case assumes sales approximate current issuance; and a stress case incorporates reserve distribution. This gives the ratio its proper use: a sensitivity tool for market-balance analysis.

The definition sensitivities decide the model’s economic meaning. IBIT-only versus aggregate ETF flows determines whose demand is measured; BTC conversion determines whether the arithmetic is valid; and miner issuance versus miner distribution determines whether the comparison addresses potential new supply or estimated actual selling pressure.

The eighth sensitivity is the distinction between IBIT’s market price and its underlying Bitcoin value. BlackRock states that IBIT shares trade at market price rather than NAV and can trade above or below the value of Bitcoin represented by the shares. IBIT NAV values Trust Bitcoin using the CF Benchmarks Index, specifically the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate – New York Variant for Bitcoin-U.S. dollar prices.[63]

The ninth sensitivity is the persistence of ETF demand. Glassnode’s reported pre-halving interpretation was that substantial and continuous ETF buying had already tightened available Bitcoin supply, while its reported framework also identified a shift from inflows to outflows as a source of extraordinary selling pressure.[70]

ETF secondary-market turnover must be separated from primary-market flow. Secondary-market investors can trade existing IBIT shares while the number of shares and the Trust’s Bitcoin holdings remain unchanged; creations and redemptions are the transactions that expand or contract the Trust’s underlying Bitcoin exposure.[44]

Coin Metrics’ Realized Cap to Thermo Cap (RCTC) is the ratio of Realized Cap to Thermo Cap, where Thermo Cap is the USD value of all funds disbursed to miners at issuance. Coin Metrics describes RCTC as an indicator of miner liquidity in the overall market and states that miners collectively make buy-or-sell decisions that affect the market. RCTC is therefore market-cycle context for miner liquidity, while miner net-position and spent-supply measures remain the direct model inputs for miner-distribution sensitivity.[71]

ETF net creations are a demand proxy, not a classification of the ultimate investor: IBIT offers spot-BTC exposure to both institutional and retail investors, so aggregate ETF net creations are not an observed institutional-only flow.[64]

Assets under management are not a flow measure: the market value of unchanged ETF Bitcoin holdings rises or falls with Bitcoin’s price, so AUM changes cannot be used as a substitute for creations and redemptions.

The post-halving subsidy supplies a clear issuance baseline of approximately 450 BTC per day, or an estimated 48,150 BTC across 107 calendar days from October 1, 2024 through January 15, 2025. ETF creations and redemptions are different: they alter fund demand for Bitcoin already in circulation rather than Bitcoin’s protocol supply. Historical aggregate ETF demand has exceeded projected issuance value, but miner reserve reductions show that actual miner distribution can be materially above issuance, while ETF flows can contain wrapper migration and hedged activity.[22][1][16][26][27][31]

The requested ratio is therefore useful as a mechanical indicator only when its ETF scope, BTC conversion convention, week boundaries and miner-selling assumption are explicit. It supports a conditional conclusion that institutional ETF demand can offset issuance-related pressure; it does not establish that IBIT or the ETF sector purchased miners’ coins, nor that all recorded inflows were incremental directional demand.[33][25][16]

## Three-case scenario model

Bitcoin has no contractual cash flow, coupon, or universally accepted intrinsic-value model. A useful institutional approach is therefore a **conditional market-clearing valuation**: define the amount of Bitcoin institutions are assumed to buy at each price, compare it with the coins available for sale, and make every macroeconomic, regulatory, and adoption assumption explicit.

The scenario range below is not a prediction. It is a disciplined way to show how Bitcoin’s value can change when institutional demand, access to regulated investment vehicles, and the willingness of holders to sell change together. The SEC had approved listing and trading of certain spot Bitcoin exchange-traded product (ETP) shares on January 10, 2024, but stressed that this approval was limited to ETPs holding Bitcoin as a non-security commodity and was not an endorsement of Bitcoin itself.[72]

**The cited 2025 price references are separate analyst forecasts, not a common-model valuation envelope:** Peter Berezin forecast $45,000 on a global-recession premise; James Butterfill of CoinShares projected $80,000–$150,000; and Bernstein analysts forecast $200,000, citing spot-ETF inflows and institutional adoption.[73]

### bull

The bull case assumes that regulated access through U.S. spot Bitcoin ETPs remains available, institutional adoption accelerates, and macro conditions become materially less restrictive than the federal-funds-rate setting in December 2024. Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell stated on December 18, 2024 that the federal funds rate was 4.3% and meaningfully restrictive.[74] The SEC-approved ETP structure matters because it gives institutions a familiar listed vehicle through which to obtain Bitcoin exposure, subject to the operational and regulatory risks of the product structure.[72]

Bernstein analysts separately forecast $200,000 for Bitcoin in 2025, citing strong spot-ETF inflows and institutional adoption.[73] The bull case uses accelerated institutional adoption, continued market access, and easier financial conditions as its assumptions; it does not calculate the Bernstein forecast from those assumptions.

Judgment — The bull case requires accelerated adoption, regulatory continuity or improvement, and easier financial conditions to hold simultaneously. Bernstein’s separately reported $200,000 2025 forecast cites spot-ETF inflows and institutional adoption.[73]

| Bull-case input | Explicit assumption | Valuation implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutional adoption** | Net institutional Bitcoin allocation rises from tactical exposure toward strategic portfolio exposure. Spot-ETF purchases remain directionally long rather than principally hedged or arbitrage-driven. The Avenir–Glassnode analysis characterizes the majority of inflows into U.S.-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs as apparently unhedged and directional. Fidelity’s institutional survey found that Bitcoin ETFs appealed to 36% of respondents in the US, 43% in Europe and 50% in Asia; 67% saw a role for digital assets in portfolios, while 51% were already invested in the asset class.[101]

Regulatory clarity was identified as a barrier by 28% of Fidelity survey respondents in the US, 15% in Europe and 8% in Asia.[101]

The bull-case regulatory premise includes continued US spot-ETF access and workable implementation of the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCAR). MiCAR was approved in April 2023 and became applicable by the end of 2024, although implementation differs across jurisdictions.[99][101]

By December 2024, the Federal Reserve had cut its policy-rate target by 100 basis points since September, to a range of 4.25%–4.50%; survey expectations called for 75 basis points of further cuts in 2025, while market pricing implied somewhat less easing.[102][103]

Judgment — In this model, **institutional demand elasticity** means the percentage change in institutional Bitcoin quantity demanded when Bitcoin’s price changes by 1%. A demand curve with low absolute elasticity is price-insensitive: a large rise in price causes only a small reduction in the quantity buyers seek. This is distinct from adoption. Adoption shifts the curve outward by adding buyers or allocation capital; elasticity determines how strongly those buyers throttle purchases when price rises.

The cited supply-and-demand framework uses the demand function Q = A times P to the power of epsilon, where Q is quantity demanded, A is the overall demand scale, P is price, and epsilon is the price elasticity parameter.[54] For a conventional downward-sloping demand curve, epsilon is negative.

The more negative it is, the more strongly quantity demanded falls as price rises. That sign convention differs from the cited thesis’s description of Bitcoin demand as positively inelastic and close to zero; the thesis does not provide a numerical institutional elasticity usable for a valuation model.[78]

Judgment — The bull calibration uses a demand-elasticity range of minus 0.10 to minus 0.30 for institutional spot demand over a one-year horizon. This is an explicit scenario assumption, not an empirical estimate. It means a 10% price increase reduces desired institutional coin purchases by only 1% to 3%, before considering momentum, new allocations, or changing regulation. Such low price sensitivity is necessary for the $200,000 case because a high target cannot be supported by buyers that sharply reduce purchases after each price rise.

The cited execution model illustrates why this assumption matters. Its alpha parameter ranges from alpha = 0, fixed Bitcoin purchases per day, to alpha = 1, fixed dollars per day. At alpha = 0.025, daily withdrawals are halved only after Bitcoin rises 40 times; at alpha = 0.10, they are halved after an 11-times increase; and at alpha = 0.50, they are halved after a three-times increase.[54] Although alpha is not identical to demand elasticity, both describe the degree to which buyers slow purchases as price appreciates.

The bull case also assumes that the distinction between **protocol supply** and **effective liquid supply** remains important. Protocol supply is the mechanically scheduled block subsidy plus transaction fees. Effective liquid supply is the subset of existing Bitcoin that holders will offer at current prices. The 2024 halving reduced the block reward to 3.125 BTC per block, reducing the flow of newly issued Bitcoin, but the halving itself does not determine Bitcoin’s price.[79][42] Price is determined by demand relative to coins available for sale, not merely by the issuance schedule.

Judgment — Under the bull case, low issuance, persistent ETF creation activity, and long-term-holder reluctance to sell interact to tighten effective liquid supply. This is a stronger claim than saying that ETFs absorb mining output. ETF creations may reflect spot purchases, but cash creation and redemption mechanisms introduce execution steps and execution risk; they need not translate one-for-one or contemporaneously into a reduction in exchange float.[17] The scenario therefore treats ETF flows as a demand and market-access channel, not as proof of a fixed quantity removed from supply.

The principal upside sensitivities are:

- **Demand persistence:** The decisive upside variable is whether institutional buyers continue allocating after Bitcoin rises. The cited model finds prices highly sensitive to liquid supply, fiat-denominated withdrawal discipline, and demand composition. [77]

- **Regulatory expansion:** The upside strengthens if regulated access broadens beyond the existing ETP framework. The SEC’s January 2024 action permitted listed and traded spot Bitcoin ETP shares, but it did not create blanket approval for crypto assets or crypto-asset securities. [72]

- **Rates and broad liquidity:** Bitcoin price can be influenced by interest-rate expectations, monetary policy, and global or regional economic and financial conditions. [17] A faster-than-expected decline in rates or renewed liquidity expansion would support the bull case; this is a scenario transmission mechanism rather than a quantified causal coefficient.

- **Derivatives and collateral:** Cash-settled derivatives can satisfy investor exposure without immediate spot settlement, potentially reducing spot-buy pressure. Conversely, a contango basis trade can purchase spot Bitcoin and transfer it to custodial collateral, tightening the readily tradable float. [54]

The bull case therefore makes a clear portfolio-management proposition: high valuations require not merely positive ETF flows, but **persistent, price-insensitive institutional demand interacting with constrained effective liquid supply**. It rules out treating a single period of inflows or a halving schedule as sufficient evidence for a $200,000 valuation.

The December 2024 market backdrop supports treating institutional demand as a scenario variable rather than as a mechanical halving effect. The authors of A Supply and Demand Framework for Bitcoin Price Forecasting wrote in mid-December 2024 that Bitcoin was $102,000 and that strong U.S. ETF demand through 2024 indicated that their demand-shift parameter could be substantially higher than in April 2024, or that demand-based growth had moved forward.[80] Galaxy identified the U.S. spot-Bitcoin-ETF launch as a development absent from prior halving cycles.[48]

IBIT reached US$10 billion in assets under management in 51 days, while crypto exchange-traded-product assets under management had grown 950% year over year to US$134.5 billion by November 2024.[99]

Glassnode’s halving-epoch chart describes historical bull-market peaks roughly 12–18 months after halvings, with successively smaller multiples of the halving-day price as market capitalization increased. Glassnode also states that whether halvings caused those cycles or merely coincided with them remains unresolved.[81] Galaxy presents the same distinction: its bullish view is that the 50% reward reduction reduces new supply and miner selling, its bearish view is that the predictable and progressively smaller absolute reduction may already be priced in and be small relative to daily float, and its neutral view attributes post-halving bull markets more to demand, global liquidity, and central-bank rates.[48]

AMINA Bank reported that Bitcoin was around USD 63.5k on April 18, 2024, the day before the halving, and peaked at USD 106.5k on December 17, 2024.[22] AMINA Bank further reported that the 50% reduction in mining rewards and 68% Bitcoin-price increase between those dates resulted in a 16% increase in miners’ rewards in U.S.-dollar terms.[22]

Avenir and Glassnode reported that Bitcoin’s price performance since the 2022 cycle low closely tracked prior cycles despite a significantly larger market capitalization, indicating that capital inflows were scaling with the asset’s growth and reinforcing structural depth.[75] Saxo identifies spot Bitcoin ETFs, increasing institutional adoption, and growing global liquidity as tailwinds.[73]

The SEC’s January 10, 2024 approval followed the Commission’s disapproval of more than 20 spot-Bitcoin-ETP exchange-rule filings from 2018 through March 2023.[72] The U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit vacated and remanded the Commission’s disapproval of Grayscale’s proposed ETP after finding that the Commission had not adequately explained its reasoning.[72] Together, that history, the January 2024 approval, and the SEC’s statement that it neither approved nor endorsed Bitcoin establish that continued regulated access is a contingent market-structure assumption rather than blanket regulatory approval.[72]

### base

The base case assumes continued but orderly institutional adoption, preservation of the existing U.S. spot-Bitcoin-ETP market structure, and monetary conditions broadly consistent with the December 2024 Federal Open Market Committee median projection of a 3.9% federal funds rate at the end of 2025.[82] James Butterfill of CoinShares separately projected a 2025 Bitcoin range of $80,000–$150,000.[73] The base-case assumptions below do not calculate that reported range.

The base case is not neutral in the sense of assuming no adoption. It assumes that institutions continue using regulated Bitcoin vehicles, but that higher prices slow allocations and encourage some holders to sell. This creates a more orderly market-clearing process than the bull case.

US spot ETFs retain brokerage, clearing, settlement and compliance advantages, but custody, banking, accounting, suitability and cross-border requirements continue to limit the pace of adoption.[104]

In December 2024, the Federal Reserve raised its projection for 2025 inflation to 2.5% from 2.1% and expected inflation to reach 2% by 2027.[102][103]

The Federal Reserve’s December 2024 projections also put 2025 real GDP growth at a 2.1% median and PCE inflation at a 2.5% median. The corresponding full projected ranges were 1.6%–2.5% for GDP growth and 2.1%–2.9% for PCE inflation.[82] These are not Bitcoin forecasts; they provide the macroeconomic baseline for a case in which financial conditions improve only gradually rather than pivot sharply toward abundant liquidity.

Judgment — The base calibration uses an institutional demand-elasticity range of minus 0.30 to minus 0.70. In ordinary language, a 10% Bitcoin price increase reduces desired institutional coin purchases by roughly 3% to 7%, holding the adoption pool constant. The range reflects orderly execution: institutions may buy fixed dollar amounts, comply with position limits, and rebalance portfolios as appreciation increases Bitcoin’s portfolio weight.

This execution profile resembles the cited paper’s **stability-first regime**, in which institutions use governance, dollar-budget caps, execution pacing, and derivatives or collateral frameworks to increase purchase discipline.[77] The regime supports continuing adoption while avoiding the assumption that every dollar of new institutional interest must immediately clear in spot markets.

The base case also takes Bitcoin’s network effect seriously but does not translate it mechanically into price. The network-effect framework describes Bitcoin value as shaped by the size and strength of its user base and states that Metcalfe’s Law approximates network value as proportional to the square of users.[79] However, spot Bitcoin ETFs permit investors to obtain exposure without directly operating wallets, which can weaken the relationship between active-wallet measures and economic adoption.[79] Market maturation and adoption can make peaks, volatility, and returns less dramatic, with gradual climbs more likely than explosive surges.[61]

Judgment — For valuation, adoption should be measured as investable demand capacity rather than wallet count alone. Relevant indicators include assets in regulated spot products, adviser-platform availability, corporate treasury participation, long-term-holder balances, exchange balances, and the number of institutions permitted by mandate to own Bitcoin or Bitcoin ETPs. Wallet growth remains useful for payment and network use, but it is an incomplete measure of institutional adoption.

The reported $80,000–$150,000 2025 range from James Butterfill of CoinShares is a separate forecast reference.[73] The base case assumes that institutional demand remains a positive structural influence but is counterbalanced by price-sensitive execution and liquidity release.

Bitcoin’s price can be affected by changes in global supply and demand, adoption, interest-rate expectations, and trading activity by institutions, miners, speculators, and other investors.[17] Bloomberg reported that U.S. ETFs played a key role in Bitcoin’s rally above $100,000 during 2024.[68]

A cited estimate attributed to Citi associates each $100 million of ETF inflow with approximately 53 basis points of same-day Bitcoin price movement and 96 basis points over ten trading days; it comes from a multi-fund daily sample, not an IBIT-specific weekly estimate.[31]

The base case must distinguish **ETF net flows** from **spot Bitcoin scarcity**. A spot ETF is a passive investment vehicle designed to track Bitcoin rather than to trade Bitcoin discretionarily around price levels.[17] Its creation activity may require Bitcoin acquisition under the fund’s operating arrangements, but cash creations and redemptions may involve timing differences and execution-price deviations from the index used for net asset value.[17] Thus, the appropriate interpretation is that ETF demand can affect price formation, not that each reported dollar of inflow has a fixed and permanent spot-price multiplier.

The main base-case sensitivities are:

- **The 2025 rate path:** The FOMC’s 3.9% median is a participant projection, not a Committee commitment or decision. [74] Higher-than-projected rates would lower the attractiveness of a non-yielding volatile asset; lower rates would move outcomes toward the bull range.

- **ETF competition and fee pressure:** Competition can limit an individual trust’s ability to acquire or retain assets, while lower-fee competitors can affect its scale and sustainability. [17] For Bitcoin valuation, aggregate spot-ETF demand matters more than IBIT alone.

- **Miner financial conditions:** Mining profitability depends on Bitcoin price, transaction fees, and electricity costs. [83] The relationship is nonlinear: strong Bitcoin prices can ease miner financing needs and reduce forced selling, while weak prices or high power costs can increase immediate sales.

- **Liquid-supply release:** The cited framework finds that price outcomes are highly sensitive to liquid supply, purchase discipline, and demand composition. [77] The base case assumes that higher prices induce enough selling, hedging, and derivative substitution to prevent a persistent liquidity squeeze.

The base case therefore supports holding Bitcoin as an institutional satellite allocation with explicit rebalance rules, rather than underwriting a deterministic exponential price path. It decides that the appropriate valuation band is broad because orderly demand can coexist with high volatility and changing effective supply.

IBIT’s scale establishes the relevance of regulated access to the base case. A January 10, 2025 report stated that IBIT recorded $52.3 billion of net inflows in the U.S. spot-Bitcoin ETFs’ first year, ranking third globally, while FBTC recorded $19.7 billion and ranked ninth; the same report described IBIT and FBTC as the first- and fourth-most successful ETF launch years in history.[84] IBIT shares are intended to reflect the market price of Bitcoin held by the Trust, less expenses and liabilities, and its NAV values Bitcoin using the CF Benchmarks Index, specifically the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate – New York Variant for BTC/USD.[63]

Professional Bitcoin ETF holdings increased from US$12.4 billion to US$27.4 billion quarter over quarter. Institutional IBIT ownership reached 306 million shares, worth US$16.3 billion and representing 31.5% of fund assets under management; hedge funds accounted for 41% of institutional holdings.[105]

CoinShares reported that professional investors held $27.4 billion of U.S. Bitcoin ETFs in Q4 2024, when U.S. Bitcoin ETF assets under management were $104.1 billion.[105]

The product structure creates a measurement distinction relevant to an institutional allocation: IBIT shares trade at market price and may trade above or below the value of the Bitcoin represented by the shares, whereas the Trust’s NAV uses its specified benchmark.[63] IBIT is not an investment company registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and is not subject to the same regulatory requirements as mutual funds or ETFs registered under that Act.[63]

Liquidity conditions are a separate base-case sensitivity. Glassnode reported that $27.35 billion of stablecoin inflows from November to December 2024 coincided with Bitcoin rising from $67,800 to $106,100, or 56.5%, and stated that the rally required almost twice the capital inflow of the January-to-March 2024 rally for a smaller Bitcoin gain. As of January 13, 2025, Glassnode reported stablecoin market capitalization of $189.1 billion and a 30-day net change of +0.56%, describing inflows as nearly stalled.[85]

The December 2024 FOMC projection of a 3.9% end-2025 federal-funds rate was above its September 2024 median projection of 3.4%; the December 2024 projections showed a 3.4% median for end-2026.[82][74] The FOMC projections were not a Committee plan or decision.[74]

The federal-funds target range was 4.25%–4.50% in December 2024.[102][103]

The Trust’s net asset value grew from $100,000 at December 31, 2023 to $51,519,566,547 at December 31, 2024, while outstanding shares rose from 4,000 to 970,440,000.[17]

LSEG reported that Bitcoin’s correlation with the S&P 500 and NASDAQ increased after previous halvings and that this correlation could rise again as ETFs integrate Bitcoin more closely with traditional markets. LSEG also noted that crypto markets have sometimes rallied alongside traditional markets, including when interest rates were lowered in response to the COVID-19 pandemic.[76]

The relevant macro opportunity-cost sensitivity is real yields and overall financial conditions, not the policy-rate level alone. A lower policy rate accompanied by persistently high inflation expectations or restrictive credit conditions need not deliver the easier real financial conditions assumed in the base case.

Chair Powell said that the Fed was 100 basis points closer to the neutral rate and would proceed cautiously regarding further cuts.[74] Together with the December 2024 median projection of a 3.9% year-end 2025 federal-funds rate, that caution supports gradual easing as the baseline rather than the materially easier conditions required by the bull case.[74][82]

### bear

The bear case assumes weak adoption, recessionary or risk-off macro conditions, deteriorating regulatory access, and an institutional demand reversal or sharp slowdown. Peter Berezin separately forecast $45,000 for Bitcoin by 2025, attributing the downside to a predicted global recession affecting risk assets such as Bitcoin.[73] The bear-case assumptions below do not calculate that forecast.

This is not a claim that recession mechanically causes Bitcoin to fall. Bitcoin has at times diverged from other asset classes, but no persistent divergence or correlation is assured.[17] The bear case instead models a combined shock in which institutional risk budgets contract at the same time that legal, operational, or market-structure risks reduce willingness to own Bitcoin.

| Bear-case input | Explicit assumption | Valuation implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutional adoption** | New allocation slows materially; some institutions reduce exposure because of losses, mandate limits, redemptions, or risk-budget compression. The Satoshi Action Fund authors identify institutional adoption, interest rates, M2 money supply, regulatory clarity, and market sentiment as macro drivers that could be tied to exogenous demand-shift multipliers. [77] | The institutional demand curve shifts inward. U.S. spot-ETF approval in January 2024 increased institutional adoption and, according to LSEG, enhanced liquidity and integrated digital assets more closely with broader financial markets. [76] |
| **Regulation and market access** | U.S. enforcement action, court decision, tax action, custody restriction, or foreign-policy divergence impairs access or raises compliance costs. An SEC or state action asserting Bitcoin is a security, or a court decision to that effect, is described as expected to have an immediate material adverse effect on Bitcoin trading value and trust shares. [17] | Regulatory uncertainty reduces the buyer universe and raises required risk premia. |
| **Macro rates and growth** | Restrictive financial conditions persist or growth deteriorates into recession, reducing risk appetite and liquidity. Interest-rate expectations, monetary policy, and economic conditions are identified as Bitcoin-price drivers. [17] | Institutions favour cash, government securities, or lower-volatility exposures over Bitcoin. |
| **Institutional demand elasticity** | Demand becomes highly price-sensitive and may become procyclical: falling prices trigger de-risking rather than dip-buying. | Selling pressure can exceed the stabilizing capacity of long-only allocators. |
| **Effective supply** | Miner sales rise, ETF redemptions release Bitcoin, long-term holders sell, or derivatives and collateral stress transmit into spot markets. [54][77] | Available sell-side liquidity rises while demand declines, creating a lower clearing price. |

December 2024 expectations pointed to slower US rate easing, while the widening gap between US and foreign interest rates was linked to a stronger trade-weighted dollar.[103]

The bear regulatory premise is grounded in specific risk channels. The 2024 filing states that digital-asset markets in the United States exist in regulatory uncertainty and that adverse legislative or regulatory developments could harm Bitcoin or trust values by restricting use, mining, wallets, trading and custody services, network operation, or digital-asset markets generally.[17] It also states that foreign laws and directives may affect digital-asset networks, markets, and users, and that their effects can be substantial and adverse.[17]

Judgment — The bear calibration uses an institutional demand-elasticity range of minus 0.70 to minus 1.20. A 10% rise in price reduces desired institutional coin purchases by 7% to 12%; more importantly, a decline in price does not automatically create offsetting demand because risk limits, losses, redemptions, and leverage constraints can force investors to reduce exposure. This is the critical asymmetry in a stress scenario.

The cited thesis identifies an important mechanism: leveraged agents may be unable to buy enough during large downward moves to stabilize price.[78] This makes downside market clearing different from a simple symmetric demand curve. In stressed conditions, price declines can reduce balance-sheet capacity, cause redemptions, and lead to a lower rather than higher willingness to absorb supply.

Miner behavior is another bear-case transmission channel. The filing states that when mining margins are insufficient because of higher electricity costs, lower Bitcoin prices, or an inability to obtain financing, miners are more likely to sell Bitcoin immediately, increasing liquid supply and generally tending to reduce Bitcoin’s market price.[17] The 2024 reward reduction to 3.125 BTC per block is therefore not unambiguously bullish in a downturn: it lowers issuance but can simultaneously increase financial stress among less efficient miners.[79]

Judgment — The bear case should stress actual miner selling at 100%, 125%, and 150% of mined issuance rather than assume sales equal issuance. A miner can sell inventory accumulated in prior periods, borrow against Bitcoin and defer sales, or sell less than issuance while financing operations externally. The requested supply-absorption model therefore uses issuance as a transparent baseline but must not treat it as a direct measurement of sales.

Network and infrastructure risks are also relevant in a severe downside case. Mining restrictions by regulators or public utilities could reduce mining activity, weaken network security, and harm trust-share value.[17] The filing also notes risks from private-key loss, theft, compromise, or destruction, which may permanently prevent access to associated Bitcoin.[17] These risks are low-frequency but potentially high-severity, and they increase the return investors require to hold Bitcoin during stress.

The bear case includes not only $45,000 but also a pathway to less severe adverse outcomes. One reported technical-risk view warned of a possible decline to $78,000, while the CoinShares downside scenario cited $80,000 if regulatory reforms disappoint.[73] These figures cannot be pooled into an elasticity model because they arise from different rationales and horizons. They show that bearish expectations range from moderate stagnation to a recessionary drawdown.

The historic scale of potential drawdown reinforces why a bear scenario is necessary. The 2024 filing reports that Bitcoin fell 77% from a $67,734 peak to a $15,632 bottom in the 2021–2022 cycle.[17] This historical episode does not imply a repeat, but it demonstrates that a valuation framework based only on expected adoption and post-halving issuance would understate downside risk.

The bear case therefore decides that Bitcoin must be sized as a high-volatility asset with gap-risk characteristics, not as a bond substitute or a simple inflation hedge. It rules out treating regulated ETP access as permanent immunity from legal, liquidity, or risk-appetite shocks.

The bear case includes product, market-structure, and network-risk channels in addition to macroeconomic and regulatory stress. BlackRock identifies extreme volatility; private-key loss, theft, or compromise; acceptance, industry developments, governance changes, and market sentiment; large-scale sales; security breaches and hacking; competition from central bank digital currencies and blockchain-based financial initiatives; and internet or digital-network disruption as risks to IBIT share value.[63]

Specific downside sensitivities include uncertainty in U.S. federal income-tax treatment and changes in Internal Revenue Service positions or guidance; limited payment acceptance and loss of banking services to Bitcoin-related businesses; stablecoin operational, reserve, manipulation, or regulatory disruptions; competition from other digital assets, direct Bitcoin ownership, and alternative financial vehicles; and competition among Bitcoin exchange-traded products, including on fees and secondary-market liquidity.[86] EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation entered into force in June 2023; provisions for asset-referenced-token and electronic-money-token issuers applied from June 30, 2024, and remaining provisions, including those for crypto-asset service providers, began applying on December 30, 2024.[86]

Mining stress has direct relevance to the bear case because electricity expense, ASIC investment, hardware upgrades, Bitcoin price, difficulty, and financing affect miners’ economics and their decision to hold or sell mined Bitcoin.[57] Post-halving profitability pressure particularly affects smaller, less efficient operators; surviving operators are characterized by more efficient hardware and lower energy costs, while hedging, operating-cost reduction, hashrate expansion, hardware upgrades, and diversification into AI/HPC workloads are identified responses.[57] A Bitcoin-price recovery can partially or completely offset the direct reward reduction, whereas increases in electricity cost, power shortages or outages, and mining restrictions can force operations to cease.[57][86]

The December 2024 price environment also demonstrates the role of policy expectations and momentum in a downside reversal. The Osprey Bitcoin Trust filing states that Bitcoin reached a then-record high above $100,000 in December 2024 after the November U.S. presidential election, based in part on a market perception that the incoming administration would be pro-digital-assets.[86] The filing states that momentum pricing and speculation about future appreciation can inflate Bitcoin’s price and make its index more volatile, and that perceptions of favorable regulation can increase speculative interest, trading volumes, and valuations.[86]

A separate account states that Bitcoin reached an all-time high of $108,000 on 2024-12-17 and subsequently declined by more than 10%.[106]

### Valuation method

The cited forecasts are separate market references, not a common-model valuation range. Bernstein’s $200,000 forecast cites spot-ETF inflows and institutional adoption; CoinShares’ $80,000–$150,000 range is a separate forecast; and Berezin’s $45,000 forecast rests on a predicted global-recession premise.[73] Other reported forecasts include $160,000, $180,000, $185,000, and $250,000 on bullish premises, and $78,000 and $80,000 downside cases.[73]

Judgment — A defensible institutional valuation range must use one shared model, one horizon, one supply definition, and explicit elasticities. The analyst should not average published price targets because their assumptions, dates, and mechanisms differ. The proper output is a range conditional on a documented set of assumptions, with sensitivity analysis showing the price response to each assumption.

A transparent **institutional-demand-clearing model** can be expressed as follows:

| Model component | Formula or definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutional demand schedule** | Institutional BTC demanded = A times Price to the power of epsilon. | A is the adoption-and-access demand scale. Epsilon is price elasticity. |
| **Scenario demand scale** | A in scenario t = A at 2025-01-15 multiplied by adoption multiplier multiplied by regulatory-access multiplier multiplied by macro-liquidity multiplier. | Adoption, regulation, and rates change the amount institutions seek at every price. |
| **Available liquid supply** | Liquid supply = estimated exchange float + coins likely offered by holders + miner sales + ETF redemptions less ETF creations less long-term withdrawals. | This is the tradable supply relevant to price formation, not total issued Bitcoin. |
| **Market-clearing price** | Price = (available liquid supply divided by A) raised to the power of 1 divided by epsilon. | The formula applies only where epsilon is non-zero and the sign convention is held consistent. |
| **Miner-sales stress** | Miner sales = protocol issuance multiplied by sell-through ratio. | Sell-through ratio tests whether miners sell 50%, 100%, 125%, or 150% of issuance. |
| **ETF-flow stress** | Net ETF Bitcoin demand = net cash flow divided by Bitcoin reference price, adjusted for execution lag and cash-creation basis. | This prevents a one-for-one assumption between fund cash flows and immediate spot purchases. |
| **Valuation range** | Low, central, and high outputs from bear, base, and bull combinations of A, epsilon, liquid supply, and miner sell-through. | The range is model-derived only after all inputs are specified. |
| **Proposed model output** | Price outputs from separately specified combinations of A, epsilon, liquid supply, and miner sell-through. | This proposed constant-elasticity model is separate from the reported analyst forecasts and produces outputs only after all inputs are specified. [73] |

Derived — The price-clearing expression follows from Q = A times P to the power of epsilon by setting Q equal to available liquid supply, dividing both sides by A, and raising the result to the power of one divided by epsilon. Its output is highly sensitive when epsilon is near zero, which is exactly why an unmeasured near-zero elasticity cannot responsibly be used to generate a precise target.

The cited thesis describes Bitcoin demand as positively inelastic and close to zero, but its invested-fraction results depend on the observed cohort and transformation assumptions.[78] Its simulations also do not reproduce observed return moments well; the simulated moments are much larger in absolute terms than observed and the estimation results are described as hardly statistically significant.[78] This is informative evidence that elasticity may be low, but it is not a calibrated institutional-demand elasticity for IBIT buyers or for the aggregate regulated-ETF buyer base.

The quantity-clearing framework defines elasticity and models Bitcoin supply as perfectly inelastic at each time step, with mining rewards adding a predetermined flow and liquid supply changing through withdrawals.[54] But its withdrawal-sensitivity parameter is judgmental and calibrated from proxies such as net exchange outflows, ETF creations and redemptions, and treasury disclosures rather than micro-level execution data.[54][77] Its liquid-supply estimates also depend on on-chain heuristics and disclosed custodian balances, which may have measurement error and classification drift.[77]

A published Realized-Cap discussion places the long-run median ratio of Bitcoin market capitalization to Realized Cap near 4×, with a rolling range of 1×–32×.[107] A separate formulation estimates US$0.20–US$0.25 of Realized-Cap inflow per US$1 of market-capitalization change, equivalent to a 4×–5× multiplier; at mature bull-market tops, it estimates US$0.80 to more than US$1.00 of inflow per US$1 of market-capitalization change.[108][107]

Judgment — No common elasticity-based valuation range is calculated here. The separately reported $45,000, $80,000–$150,000, and $200,000 forecasts have different stated rationales and are not outputs of the proposed constant-elasticity model.[73] A defensible elasticity-based estimate requires weekly or daily aggregate spot-ETF creation data, transaction-level or high-frequency price data, exchange balances, ETF custody balances, futures basis and open interest, miner treasury changes, miner financing data, and a structural model separating flows caused by price from flows causing price.[54][77]

The model should also maintain the requested distinction between **new supply** and actual sell pressure. The requested supply-absorption definition is miner issuance plus ETF creation/redemption net flows. That is a bookkeeping construct. Miner issuance is a protocol-determined production flow; miner selling is an economic behavior. ETF creations and redemptions alter the demand and custody channel; they are not newly mined Bitcoin. The separate Supply-absorption model section applies that definition over the requested period.

The model’s sensitivity hierarchy is clear:

1. **First, institutional demand elasticity:** Low price sensitivity creates the strongest upside convexity; high price sensitivity stabilizes price but limits upside.

2. **Second, the demand-scale shift:** Adoption, regulatory access, adviser-platform availability, and macro liquidity determine how much institutional capital seeks Bitcoin at each price.

3. **Third, effective liquid supply:** Exchange balances, long-term-holder behavior, ETF custody, derivatives, collateral practices, and miner sales determine the coins actually available to meet demand. ETF creations/redemptions, exchange outflows, derivatives open interest and funding, and collateral rehypothecation are all channels that can affect effective supply. [77]

4. **Fourth, miner sell-through:** The post-halving subsidy is predetermined, but sales rise or fall with margins, energy costs, financing availability, treasury policy, and market price. [17][79]

5. **Fifth, market structure:** Derivatives can delay or dilute scarcity by offering synthetic exposure, while collateral stress or basis-trade unwinds can eventually transmit back into spot markets. [54][77]

The cited model’s broader conclusion is that steep but bounded appreciation is the default under many parameter combinations, while hyperbolic repricing is contingent on low liquid supply and low purchase throttling.[77] It identifies roughly 2 million BTC of liquid supply as a threshold region in which the right-tail outcome concentrates when alpha remains low.[77] That threshold is a model condition, not an observed 2025 market estimate.

A published constant-elasticity-of-substitution (CES) framework supplies a quantitative illustration, but not an institutional-demand estimate for this analysis. Its baseline calibration uses A = 75 M and epsilon = −2.301 to generate p0 = $64,858, using an assumed liquid supply of 11,187,500 BTC and the observed price on April 20, 2024.[80] Its conservative scenario uses A = 150 M, epsilon = −2.0953, L_Min = 0.04, T* = 8 years, and D = 20; calibrated to Bitcoin at about $100,000 on December 15, 2024, it produces $98,577 with zero daily liquid-supply withdrawals and $102,808 with 4,000 BTC withdrawn daily.[80]

The framework’s bull parameterization uses L_Min = 0.02, A = 500 M, epsilon = −1.8134, T* = 6 years, and D = 30, which its authors associate with early adoption, stronger incoming demand, and more price-insensitive institutional and sovereign investors.[80] Under that parameterization, the model reaches $1 million by early 2027 for daily withdrawals above 1,000 BTC; higher withdrawal assumptions produce $2 million by late 2027 and $5 million by early 2031.[80] The authors identify market-demand shifts and liquid-supply withdrawals as the major price sensitivities, particularly below 2 million BTC of liquid supply, and show that a 10-fold demand-shift increase can produce a price above $600,000 even with no withdrawals.[80]

The model’s institutional-demand channel is a liquid-supply-withdrawal mechanism: strategic-reserve accumulation may withdraw coins from the market, but not all institutional purchases are permanently removed from circulation, and the number of coins withdrawn can decline as price rises even when dollar investment rises.[80] Its elasticity parameter can also change as Bitcoin evolves from a speculative asset to a long-term store of value; the authors state that even their most inelastic modeled setting, epsilon = −0.86086, does not eliminate extreme appreciation.[80] The authors identify fixed elasticity, lost-coin estimates, and an ad hoc demand-shift parameter as model inputs and state that statistical calibration to real-world demand drivers remains to be developed.[80]

On-chain valuation indicators are supplementary monitoring measures rather than substitutes for an institutional flow-and-elasticity valuation. Coin Metrics defines Network Value to Transactions (NVT) as market capitalization divided by adjusted transfer value and characterizes it as a rough crypto-asset price-to-earnings proxy; it states that historically high NVT readings have detected bubbles and low readings have indicated attractive entry points.[71] Coin Metrics defines Free Float NVT as free-float network value divided by adjusted transfer value, Realized Value to Transactions (RVT) as realized value divided by adjusted transfer value, and NVTAdj90 as current market capitalization divided by the 90-day moving average of USD adjusted transfer volume.[71]

Coin Metrics characterizes RVT as a slower-moving, higher-conviction signal tuned to the macro sentiment of HODLers, states that a historical RCTC threshold of 10 has indicated market tops, and states that NVTs are not directly comparable across assets with different blockchain-usage modes.[71]

A complementary transition model is `P_t = P_0 × EXP[(ΔH_inst/F_eff)/E]`, where `P_0` is the verified starting Bitcoin price, `ΔH_inst` is net additional institutional BTC holdings demanded, `F_eff` is effective tradable float in BTC, and `E` is the absolute institutional-demand elasticity. It shows the required price adjustment when desired institutional holdings change relative to coins realistically available for trading: a smaller `E` or smaller `F_eff` produces a larger price response. A separate stock-value check is `Price = (institutional allocable wealth × target Bitcoin allocation + other Bitcoin demand value) / effective BTC float`; this tests whether each scenario’s required institutional demand is plausible relative to allocable capital.

An alternate annual-flow valuation sensitivity uses a 2x–6x market-cap multiplier for institutional demand. Its cited calibration context includes a published rolling 1x–32x range and an approximately 4–5x Realized-Cap formulation; those are not measured institutional-demand elasticities for this model.[107][108]

The model uses liquid-supply bounds of 3.19 million to 18.69 million BTC as a proxy for flattening of the supply curve.[54]

Bitcoin’s high-level price dynamics primarily hinge on liquid-float size and execution discipline, alpha.[54][77]

The proposed calibration estimates alpha as the local elasticity of executed withdrawals to price, with baseline epsilon approximately equal to minus alpha, by regressing net spot outflows and ETF creations on price.[77]

Alpha can be estimated across market states through event studies around accounting or regulatory shifts and large treasury announcements; alpha tightens in momentum and relaxes in drawdowns.[77]

The proposed liquid-supply metric combines exchange balances, ETF holdings, and encumbrance adjustments to validate sustainable and low-float corridors.[77]

ETF creation/redemption frictions, authorized-participant concentration, and basis-trade capacity are plumbing factors to analyse and stress-test for their effects on liquid supply.[77]

Identification is imperfect because different combinations of parameters can replicate some outcome paths.[54]

The simulation does not explicitly endogenize derivatives, credit intermediation, or collateral reuse; incorporating them would delay scarcity in normal states and steepen the right tail under stress.[54]

A highly leveraged market on either the long or short side can be represented by expanding the liquid-supply bounds to estimate rough potential price impacts.[54]

For weekly analysis, use the same week boundaries for ETF flows, Bitcoin prices, issuance, miner sales, exchange-balance changes, and derivative measures. Do not mix daily ETF flow dates with calendar-week price returns without aggregation.

Glassnode timestamps mark the start of the measurement interval, although some state metrics refer to the interval-end state; blockchain dates use UTC, while ETF observations follow Nasdaq business days.[92] Raw files should retain retrieval time, source route, entitlement status and file hash because ETF-flow reports, address labels and recent on-chain values can change.[16][88][96][97]

State Street Global Advisors says Bitcoin’s value is significantly shaped by the size and strength of its user base and that network value grows nonlinearly with adoption, often modelled by Metcalfe’s Law as roughly proportional to the square of users. [87] |

The bull, base, and bear cases establish that Bitcoin’s 2025 valuation is most sensitive to institutional demand persistence, regulated access, macro liquidity, and effective liquid supply rather than to a deterministic post-halving supply rule. The bull case requires persistent, price-insensitive institutional demand and tight liquid supply; the base case assumes orderly, budget-constrained adoption; and the bear case combines risk-off macro conditions, weaker access, and supply release.[54][77]

The reported $45,000, $80,000–$150,000, and $200,000 figures are separate analyst forecasts rather than outputs from one shared elasticity model.[73] The proposed constant-elasticity framework sets out how demand-scale shifts, elasticity, and liquid supply would be combined once calibrated; it does not calculate any of the reported analyst forecasts.[54][77]

Open questions are the Hash Ribbon state on 15 January 2025; IBIT’s complete weekly net-creation and redemption totals from 1 October 2024 through 15 January 2025; the correlations of those flows with Bitcoin returns and estimated miner selling; the requested new-supply and absorption ratio; and the resulting demand-elasticity valuation range. These are settled by a provider-consistent dated Hash Ribbon series, a complete daily IBIT creation/redemption ledger aggregated to the defined weeks, matched Bitcoin return and miner-flow measures, and scenario inputs that map incremental institutional Bitcoin demand into a market-clearing price range.
