Toowoomba is the preferred relocation, provided you secure a part-time role at or above A$35 an hour before committing to the move. The semi-retirement funding issue applies in all three locations. At age 52 in November 2026, the client cannot use preserved super for ordinary living expenses before age 60. From preservation age, a transition-to-retirement income stream (TRIS) can top up part-time earnings, but while the client remains working it is a non-commutable regular income stream rather than a lump sum. Employers must continue compulsory super guarantee contributions for eligible employees on a TRIS.[121][116][122]

Retiring at 60 also creates a seven-year funding period before Age Pension age 67. ASFA says periods out of paid work or working part-time or casually can produce lower or no superannuation contributions and lower retirement balances; it says the retirement-outcome effect is typically less for people retiring as part of a couple than for single people.[97][98][99]

Services Australia, updated 20 September 2026, assesses both partners’ income from all sources for the Age Pension, including financial assets such as savings, shares and superannuation through deeming; employment income must be reported when either partner works.[126]

Under Services Australia’s standard Age Pension income test, a single person has a $226 fortnightly free area and the pension reduces by 50 cents for each dollar above it. For a couple living together, or apart due to ill health, the combined free area is $396 a fortnight and each person’s pension reduces by 25 cents for each dollar above it.[126]

ASFA estimates that a comfortable retirement at age 67 requires super of about $630,000 for a single person or $730,000 for a couple, assuming home ownership and receipt of a part Age Pension.[119]

ASFA states that older people spend a greater and increasing proportion of income on health services than younger people, and that this proportion has risen over time.[99]

ASFA identifies elevated retirement expenditure, including expensive medical procedures, as a retirement risk. ASFA also reports that hospital-service prices outpaced general inflation by a factor of three over the 20 years preceding its 2020 Retirement Income Review submission.[99]

The Actuaries Institute defines sequencing risk as the order in which investment returns occur: withdrawing during market falls can require assets to be sold at low values, and withdrawn money cannot recover when markets subsequently rise. The Institute says this is especially problematic for account-based pensions.[127] The Institute also says that returns below the projected 6%, including negative returns, or higher inflation cause a projected balance to run out sooner.[127]

The Actuaries Institute says the ASFA Retirement Standard estimates the lump-sum super balance required at age 67 for a modest or comfortable lifestyle on the assumption that all super is placed in an account-based pension, drawn down over time, and supplemented by a part Age Pension.[127] Moneysmart states ASFA’s estimated lump sums are $110,000 for a single person with a modest lifestyle and $630,000 for a single person with a comfortable lifestyle, with the comfortable estimate assuming a partial Age Pension.[118] The cited ASFA Retirement Standard is for home-owning retirees aged 65–84 and presents December-quarter 2025 figures.[128]

For a super income stream paid from an element taxed in the fund, the ATO tax table states that from age 60 it is not assessable and not exempt income.[115]

Wealthlab Financial Planners gives a general annual retirement-spending estimate of $42,000 to $48,000 for homeowners in regional Queensland, regional Victoria and coastal NSW. It says these areas have lower homeowner housing and living costs, potentially higher transport costs and reduced specialist-healthcare access, and that individual circumstances vary considerably.[129]

Wealthlab Financial Planners states that retirement spending varies by city because ongoing housing costs, rates, insurance, maintenance and proximity to services vary significantly. It identifies Melbourne and Sydney as having higher council rates and, for unit residents, body-corporate fees, with comfortable homeowner spending there trending toward $56,000 to $60,000 annually.[129]

Wealthlab Financial Planners recommends allowing for a 3% to 4% annual spending increase in retirement projections rather than assuming flat costs.[129]

At age 52, the plan is to relocate within 18 months and retire at age 60.

The verdict changes to Cairns if a qualifying role with adequate hours is secured and a suitable sub-$550,000 home has acceptable insurance and property-risk costs. It changes to Bundaberg if the same income certainty is paired with a confirmed local and escalation cardiac pathway.

The unresolved items are the annual 2020–2024 sale-price trajectory for three-bedroom homes in each location, the exact travel time from a shortlisted property to the chosen cardiologist and hospital, and the role rate and weekly hours available when you move within 18 months. Annual three-bedroom transaction medians, an address-specific travel check, and a written employment offer would settle these decisions.
