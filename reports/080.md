For a 2-year horizon, neither fund is suitable as a core investment because both are concentrated overseas equity FoFs with material drawdown, currency and theme or regional risk. If an investor must choose between them for two years, DSP Global Innovation Overseas Omni FoF is the relatively broader choice, but not a low-risk one: its three largest underlying vehicles were 81.92% of assets in March 2024 and it remained heavily exposed to technology and growth-stock cycles.[1] For a 5-year horizon, DSP is the better fit for an investor seeking a satellite allocation to global innovation, because it combines global technology, Nasdaq, China-internet and semiconductor vehicles rather than relying on one region.[1] Edelweiss Greater China Equity Offshore Fund is the more appropriate 5-year choice only for an investor deliberately seeking a high-risk Greater China allocation, since its closest published look-through split was China 56%, Taiwan 32% and Hong Kong 11%, with Information Technology at 34%.[2] Neither past portfolio positioning nor any historical return should be treated as a promise of future performance.

This December 2024 comparison covers, in order, portfolio composition and geographic/sector concentration, overlap in the funds’ top 20 holdings, and expense ratios and historical returns. The 2-year and 5-year suitability judgments are separate from the 1-year, 3-year and 5-year historical-return periods.

## Portfolio composition and geographic/sector concentration

This comparison uses 31 December 2024 as the observation date and 31 December 2024 as the publication cutoff. The schemes were described in the contemporaneous materials as Edelweiss Greater China Equity Offshore Fund and DSP Global Innovation Fund of Fund; DSP subsequently identifies the latter as DSP Global Innovation Overseas Equity Omni FoF (Ex- DSP Global Innovation Fund of Fund).[2][3][4] Direct and Regular plans are distinct plan classes: DSP’s December 2024 factsheet reports separate scheme expenses for each plan, although the weighted-average underlying-fund expense is the same.[3] A fund of funds (FoF) is a mutual fund that buys units of other funds or exchange-traded funds (ETFs), rather than directly buying a portfolio of company shares.

The Indian schemes’ own holdings therefore identify the underlying funds they own; country, sector and company exposure comes principally from those underlying portfolios. An annualised return is the compound return expressed for one year; it is used for multi-year return periods elsewhere in this comparison.[5][2][6]

The comparison therefore uses two levels of composition: first, the Indian FoF wrapper’s investments; second, the underlying funds’ look-through exposure to companies, countries and sectors. A look-through view is essential because a one-line holding such as an overseas mutual fund may conceal a concentrated country or technology allocation.

## Expense ratios and historical returns

Total expense ratio (TER) is the annual cost charged to a mutual-fund scheme, expressed as a percentage of assets. It reduces the NAV and therefore the return investors receive. Both schemes are funds of funds (FoFs): they invest in other funds, so an investor bears the Indian scheme’s recurring expenses as well as costs inside the underlying funds. The meaningful comparison is therefore plan-consistent—Direct versus Direct or Regular versus Regular—and must state whether the quoted TER incorporates underlying-fund expenses.

The December 2024 comparison cannot replace a month-end TER or trailing return with a number from another date. A calendar-year return is also not automatically the same as a trailing one-year return ending 31 December 2024.

For monthly ₹10,000 SIPs in Regular Growth, the presentation reported XIRRs—internal rates of return reflecting the timing of each cash flow—of **16.63% for one year**, **-2.26% for three years**, and **6.24% for five years** as at 31 January 2023.[7] These are not comparable with a trailing lump-sum CAGR because a SIP commits money gradually rather than all at the beginning.

### DSP Global Innovation Overseas Omni FOF

DSP’s December 2024 factsheet reports total expense ratios of **1.16% for the Direct Plan** and **2.04% for the Regular Plan**. The Direct total comprises a 0.43% scheme TER and a 0.73% weighted-average underlying-fund TER; the Regular total comprises a 1.31% scheme TER and the same 0.73% weighted-average underlying-fund TER. DSP’s April 2024 Key Information Memorandum gives historical FY2022–23 scheme-level expenses of 0.31% for Direct and 1.37% for Regular.[3][34]

DSP’s FoF structure also creates two layers of cost. Investors bear the recurring expenses of the DSP scheme as well as the expenses of the schemes in which it invests.[20] The KIM separately listed, as at **30 September 2023**, underlying-fund expense ratios of **0.35%** for iShares PHLX Semiconductor ETF, **0.33%** for iShares NASDAQ 100 UCITS ETF, **1.35%** for Bluebox Global Technology Fund, and **1.00%** for BGF Next Generation Technology Fund.[34] These are underlying-scheme rates, not the TER of DSP Global Innovation Overseas Equity Omni FOF itself.

The KIM sets out a permitted expense framework rather than an actual December 2024 charge: recurring expenses of up to **2.25% of total assets** plus an additional TER of **0.30%**. It provides that TER charged above the weighted-average TER of the underlying schemes must not exceed twice that weighted average, subject to the overall ceilings. Following SEBI’s 24 February 2023 directions and AMFI’s 2 March 2023 communication, no additional expense was chargeable on new inflows from specified cities received on or after 1 March 2023 until further SEBI guidance.[34] Expense ceilings describe what may be charged; they do not identify what was charged in a particular month.

**DSP’s actual December 2024 TER was 1.16% for Direct and 2.04% for Regular, including the stated 0.73% weighted-average underlying-fund TER in each plan.** The appropriate like-for-like comparison is Direct Edelweiss against Direct DSP, or Regular Edelweiss against Regular DSP; comparing a Direct-plan TER with a Regular-plan TER would embed distributor commission in only one side of the comparison.[3][34][16][24]

DSP’s historical-return constraint is more fundamental than Edelweiss’s. The scheme’s date of allotment was **14 February 2022**.[5][24] It therefore had existed for less than three years at 31 December 2024. A full trailing three-year return and a full trailing five-year return were unavailable at that date; they should be shown as unavailable, not as 0%.[5][16][24]

The only requested trailing period that could have existed for DSP at the cutoff was one year. DSP states that its fund-return calculations are for the Growth option, net of TER, and use the last day of the month-end preceding the advertisement date.[16] Net of TER means the reported fund return already reflects the scheme’s annual expenses. However, the supplied DSP pages do not give a one-year Growth-plan figure ending in December 2024.[16][15]

DSP’s September 2023 KIM reported compounded annualised Growth-option returns of **35.04% for Regular Growth** and **36.36% for Direct Growth** over one year, with no three- or five-year figures. It reported since-inception returns of **3.26% for Regular Growth** and **4.22% for Direct Growth**, from the 14 February 2022 allotment date.[34] These figures are relevant evidence that plan choice affected reported returns even over one year, but they end on **29 September 2023**, not December 2024.[34] The 1.32-percentage-point difference between the Direct and Regular one-year numbers is consistent with the Direct Plan’s lower expense structure, although market timing and exact NAV calculation conventions also determine realised return.

Some published comparison displays should not be used to override DSP’s inception date. One comparison page lists **22.74% for one year**, **25.39% for three years**, and **16.21% for five years** in a column labelled DSP US Specific Equity Omni FoF while linking to the Global Innovation scheme.[31] A full five-year return for a scheme allotted in February 2022 could not have existed as at December 2024.[16] The page therefore cannot provide a valid December 2024 performance series for the named DSP fund.

DSP’s web disclosures also contain contradictory plan and performance-period statements: the page says performance details are for both the Regular Plan and the Direct Plan, and gives a since-inception end date of 30 June 2021 while separately listing a 14 February 2022 allotment date.[15] The date of allotment is the relevant constraint for the requested three- and five-year history: DSP did not have those histories at the December 2024 cutoff.[5][16][24]

The return record available by December 2024 consequently permits an assessment of the fund’s strategy and one-year performance only once a dated plan-specific figure is supplied; it cannot support a five-year track-record comparison with Edelweiss.

DSP Global Innovation Fund of Fund’s Regular Plan—Growth Option returned **23.59% for one year** as at **30 August 2024**, against **25.19%** for its benchmark. Its three-year and five-year scheme returns were shown as NA, while the corresponding benchmark returns were **10.79%** and **15.75%**. Its since-inception CAGR was **13.12%** from 14 February 2022 to 30 August 2024.[21]

Derived — DSP’s December 2024 Direct Plan total expense ratio was 0.88 percentage points below the Regular Plan’s (1.16% versus 2.04%), a saving equal to 43.14% of the Regular Plan TER.[3]

The fund’s Indian-rupee NAV return reflects the performance of its overseas underlying investments, foreign-market movements, INR exchange-rate movements and FoF expenses; it is not simply the return of a global-innovation stock index.

DSP Global Innovation Overseas Omni FOF was allotted on 14 February 2022 and had existed for 2 years, 10 months and 17 days at 31 December 2024, so it had completed neither a three-year nor a five-year return period.[40]

Both funds are offshore FoFs, so both impose expenses at the Indian-scheme and underlying-fund levels. Their December 2024 TERs cannot be compared numerically from the stated figures, because Edelweiss’s dated TER reference is from January 2022 and DSP’s is for FY2022–23.[7][34][20][2] As at 31 December 2024, Edelweiss Greater China Equity Offshore Fund’s one-year return was 14.76% and DSP Global Innovation Overseas Equity Omni FoF’s one-year return was 20.80%.[10] DSP, allotted on 14 February 2022, had no complete three-year or five-year return record at that date.[5][24]

Judgment — **For a two-year horizon, DSP is the relatively more suitable choice for an investor who accepts overseas-equity risk because its December 2024 portfolio used five overseas vehicles, while Edelweiss held 98.89% in two JPM Greater China fund-unit lines.** The deciding consideration is the December 2024 holding structure, not a head-to-head identified-plan return comparison.[3][10][13][21]

Judgment — **For a five-year horizon, DSP is the more suitable choice for a growth-oriented investor seeking exposure through multiple overseas innovation vehicles, while Edelweiss is the specialised choice for a deliberate Greater China allocation.** DSP lacks a five-year scheme record because it was allotted in February 2022; the selection rests on the December 2024 holding structure rather than a proven five-year return advantage.[3][10][5][16][24]

1. **For two years:** prefer neither as a core capital-preservation investment; if required to choose, use DSP only as a small, high-risk satellite allocation.
2. **For five years:** prefer DSP for diversified global-innovation exposure; choose Edelweiss instead only when a deliberate Greater China allocation is the objective.

Derived — On the reported, plan-unidentified NAV calculations ending in December 2024, DSP’s 20.80% one-year return exceeded Edelweiss’s 14.76% by 6.04 percentage points.[10]

The open December 2024 comparison items are each scheme’s exact year-end look-through country and sector weights, complete top-20 company holdings, the shared holdings and numerical top-20 overlap, plan-specific expense ratios, and comparable 1-year, 3-year and 5-year returns. December 31, 2024 portfolio and plan-level performance disclosures, together with consistent underlying-fund look-through holdings, would settle these figures.
