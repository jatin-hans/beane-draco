# ClearPoint Neuro’s Cash Burn, Segment Growth and Strategic Transition

## Executive summary

ClearPoint Neuro was improving its capital efficiency, but it was not yet self-financing: nine-month 2024 free cash flow was negative $7.719 million and average operating cash burn was $2.569 million per quarter.[1] The March equity raise supplied $16.183 million of liquidity, while the August $10.1 million debt repayment removed the January 2025 maturity but reduced the cash buffer, leaving $21.573 million of cash and no debt at September 30.[1] The company therefore had management-asserted liquidity for at least 12 months, but likely still needed continued revenue, margin and cash-conversion improvement before it could reliably avoid further external financing.[1][2] Biologics and drug-delivery activity is the strategically important growth engine, while weaker capital-equipment performance underscores a transition from episodic system placements toward recurring, partner-led products and services.

Manufacturing transfers and higher volumes can expand gross margin and operating leverage, but those benefits remain contingent on sustained volume and successful execution rather than realized proof of durable profitability.

## Cash generation and financing

Judgment — Cash generation is the clearest test of financial sustainability during a strategic transition. Revenue growth can validate demand, but a company becomes self-funding only when cash from operations covers the cash required to run and develop the business, including necessary capital expenditure. ClearPoint Neuro was improving on that path through September 2024, but it had not reached it: its liquidity was reinforced by a March equity raise, while an August debt repayment traded part of that liquidity for a debt-free balance sheet.

### What was ClearPoint Neuro's free cash flow for the nine

Free cash flow (FCF) is commonly defined as cash flow from operating activities less capital expenditures—cash spending on long-lived physical assets such as equipment. A broader cash-flow measure instead adds operating cash flow and all investing cash flow. The latter can include capital expenditure but also licensing-right acquisitions, investment purchases, and proceeds from investment maturities. Both measures are useful, but they answer slightly different questions.

ClearPoint Neuro’s cash-flow statement reported net cash flows used in operating activities of $7.707 million and net cash flows used in investing activities of $0.012 million during the nine months ended September 30, 2024. Purchases of property and equipment were $0.012 million, while acquisition of licensing rights and proceeds from maturities of short-term investments were zero.[1][3]

The following table reconciles the requested free-cash-flow calculation. It compares the broader all-investing-cash-flow approach with the conventional capital-expenditure-only approach.

| Measure — nine months ended September 30, 2024 | Calculation ($ millions) | Result ($ millions) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Net cash flow from operating activities | ClearPoint reported cash used in operations | **(7.707)** [1] |
| Net cash flow from investing activities | ClearPoint reported cash used in investing | **(0.012)** [1] |
| Derived — **FCF using all investing cash flow** | (7.707) + (0.012) | **(7.719)** |
| Purchases of property and equipment | Reported capital expenditure | **(0.012)** [1] |
| Derived — **FCF using operating cash flow less capital expenditure** | (7.707) − 0.012 | **(7.719)** |

**ClearPoint consumed $7.719 million of cash through operations and investing during the first nine months of 2024; it did not generate free cash flow.**[4]

The two definitions produce the same result in this particular period because the only investing item was $12,000 of equipment purchases. That equality should not be treated as a permanent feature of ClearPoint’s cash flow. The company’s investing-cash-flow statement can include items beyond capital expenditure, including acquisition of licensing rights and proceeds from maturities of short-term investments.[5][3]

Operating activities accounted for approximately 99.8% of ClearPoint’s combined operating and investing cash use during the nine months ended September 30, 2024.[13]

The distinction mattered materially in the first half of 2023. For the six months ended June 30, 2023, ClearPoint used $10.755 million in operating activities but received $9.372 million from investing activities, including $10.000 million of proceeds from short-term-investment maturities; investing activities also included $0.461 million of property-and-equipment purchases and a $0.167 million acquisition of licensing rights. The broader calculation therefore gives an outflow of $1.383 million, whereas operating cash flow less $0.461 million of property-and-equipment purchases gives an outflow of $11.216 million.[6] Investment-maturity proceeds release cash that was already owned; they are not operating cash generation. Thus, the capital-expenditure-based measure is generally more useful for assessing whether the business itself is funding ongoing operations and physical investment.

The requested September 2024 calculation is unusually clean: both definitions show the same $7.719 million outflow. That makes it possible to conclude without a definitional dispute that ClearPoint still required external cash to fund its operations and modest investment spending.

### What was the quarterly cash burn rate from operating activities?

Cash burn is the rate at which a company consumes cash. Here, the relevant measure is net cash used in operating activities: cash consumed by core operations after working-capital movements, such as receivables, inventory and deferred revenue, rather than an accounting loss alone.

ClearPoint used $7.707 million in operating activities in the nine months ended September 30, 2024, compared with $12.551 million in the comparable 2023 period.[1] The requested quarterly burn rate is therefore a nine-month average:

| Operating-cash-burn measure | Calculation | Result |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nine-month operating cash use, 2024 | Reported net cash flow from operating activities | $7.707 million outflow [1] |
| Derived — **average quarterly operating cash burn** | $7.707 million ÷ 3 quarters | **$2.569 million per quarter** |
| Nine-month operating cash use, 2023 | Reported net cash flow from operating activities | $12.551 million outflow [1] |
| Derived — **year-over-year reduction in nine-month operating cash use** | ($12.551 million − $7.707 million) ÷ $12.551 million | **38.6% reduction** [1] |

ClearPoint reported Q3 2024 quarterly operational cash burn of $1.2 million, a 33% year-over-year decrease.[7] This is lower than the $2.569 million nine-month average, but it must not be treated as the actual Q3 cash-flow-statement line item.[8][2] The company did not reconcile “operational cash burn” to net cash flow from operating activities, and the two metrics have different scopes as presented.

The $2.569 million figure is an arithmetic average of reported cash flow across Q1–Q3; the $1.2 million figure is management’s reported operational-burn measure for Q3.[7][2]

A separate estimate puts free cash flow for the single quarter ended September 30, 2024, at negative $1.21 million; that estimate covers a different period from the nine-month free-cash-flow calculation.[53]

ClearPoint’s quarterly pattern nevertheless points in a favorable direction. ClearPoint reported Q1 2024 operational cash burn of $3.8 million and net cash used in operating activities of $3.840 million, compared with $5.680 million in Q1 2023; it reported Q2 operational cash burn of $2.7 million, down 47% year over year, and Q3 operational cash burn of $1.2 million.[9] The cash-flow statement confirms that the first nine months remained cash consumptive, but the declining reported operational-burn figures are consistent with lower cash use later in the period.[10][11][8]

The operating-cash-burn calculation establishes two things: first, ClearPoint’s core business was still not self-funding through September; second, its cash-consumption rate was substantially better than in the corresponding nine months of 2023 and appeared to improve further by Q3.[1][12]

### What do the cash generation pattern and operating cash burn

Capital efficiency describes how effectively a business turns invested cash into revenue growth, gross profit and, ultimately, cash generation. It is not simply low spending. A growing company may spend heavily and still be capital efficient if each dollar of spending produces durable revenue and margins. Conversely, a company that lowers cash burn only by reducing investment may weaken future growth.

ClearPoint’s nine-month pattern was favourable on the first part of that test. Revenue increased to $23.619 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2024 from $17.145 million in the comparable 2023 period, while operating cash use fell to $7.707 million from $12.551 million. Net loss narrowed to $13.528 million from $17.470 million.[1] This means that ClearPoint generated more revenue while requiring less operating cash, rather than merely shrinking costs alongside sales.

**The evidence supports improving capital efficiency, not a conclusion that ClearPoint had reached cash-flow self-sufficiency.**

ClearPoint stated that net cash used in operating activities declined from $12.551 million in the first nine months of 2023 to $7.707 million in the first nine months of 2024 because of a lower net loss and a $1.1 million net decrease in operating assets and liabilities, partly offset by a $0.2 million net decrease in non-cash items.[1] This matters because operating cash flow can improve through both underlying operating performance and timing effects in receivables, inventory, payables and customer prepayments.

In Q1 2024, ClearPoint said net cash used in operating activities fell by $1.8 million year over year, primarily because of a $1.5 million lower net loss, lower accounts receivable from increased cash collections, and lower inventory purchases after inventory stock had been increased for supply-chain disruptions; lower deferred revenue partly offset those factors.[10] The reduction in cash use is real, but the full improvement should not automatically be read as recurring operating profit.

The scale of the improvement remains meaningful. Derived operating cash use as a share of revenue was approximately 32.6% for the first nine months of 2024: $7.707 million divided by $23.619 million.[13] For the same 2023 period, operating cash use was $12.551 million on revenue of $17.145 million, or approximately 73.2%, derived from the reported inputs.[1] This sharp reduction indicates materially improved conversion of revenue into operating cash retention, although the ratio remains negative because the business still consumed cash.

ClearPoint’s Q3 results also show why the operating trajectory should be interpreted with balance. Q3 2024 revenue was $8.122 million, compared with $5.762 million in Q3 2023, a 41% year-over-year increase; gross margin improved from 57% to 60%, and ClearPoint reported quarterly operational cash burn of $1.2 million, down 33% year over year.[2] However, operating expenses increased to $10.0 million from $8.2 million, driven mainly by higher personnel and product-development costs, and the operating loss rose to $5.172 million from $4.897 million.[3][14] This is a company investing for growth while narrowing cash consumption, not one that has eliminated the need to finance development and commercialization.

The operating trend therefore makes a stronger business case for future self-funding than the prior year’s trend did, but it does not remove dependence on liquidity while revenue, service uptake and gross-margin expansion develop.

ClearPoint’s Q3 FY2024 quarterly-report summary states that research and development and sales and marketing expense increased during the nine-month period because of headcount and development costs, while general and administrative expense decreased slightly, aided by a $1.4 million reduction in bad-debt expense from recoveries.[15] The recovery in the allowance for credit losses positively affected operating cash flow and reduced bad-debt expense.[15] This mixed expense pattern means the lower operating cash use occurred while ClearPoint continued to invest in growth-related headcount and development activity.[15]

ClearPoint reported that first-half 2024 operating expenses were about 4% below the corresponding 2023 period despite 36% revenue growth; Q2 2024 operating expenses were $9.7 million, compared with $10.3 million in Q2 2023.[16]

ClearPoint reported a net loss of $4.974 million for Q3 2024 and $13.528 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2024.[1]

First-half expense performance also supported operating leverage: management reported first-half 2024 operating expenses about 4% below the corresponding 2023 period while revenue grew 36%, and Q2 operating expenses of $9.7 million versus $10.3 million in Q2 2023.[16] This performance alongside revenue growth is consistent with operating leverage, although the Q2 research-and-development reduction included $1.0 million lower product and software development costs from reprioritizing certain initiatives, partly offset by $0.3 million higher personnel costs and $0.2 million of trial costs.[16]

Management said expanded preclinical services and capabilities could enable cash breakeven without relying on commercialization of partners’ drugs. It also said ClearPoint did not bear the capital risk of funding partners’ expensive clinical trials.[17] Management described existing partners as a potential revenue opportunity of up to $10 million per partner before drug approval or commercialization, or close to $500 million over the next 10 years across the existing partner group, and said realizing that opportunity would generate positive cash flow and fund the company into commercialization.[17]

The Q3 2023 gross-margin explanation shows that a greater mix of lower-margin biologics and drug-delivery preclinical services and manufacturing-transition costs weighed on margins.[18] Management said the Carlsbad, California manufacturing transition was completed ahead of schedule, improved gross margin and eliminated redundant costs heading into 2024, while ClearPoint executed new biologics and drug-delivery agreements, expanded PRISM laser therapy to new centers, and made product FDA submissions.[19] Together, these factors indicate that subsequent margin improvement could reflect both a manufacturing cost reset and changing service mix.[18][19]

At March 31, ClearPoint had $35.353 million in cash and cash equivalents, up from $23.140 million at year-end 2023. The company attributed that increase primarily to the offering, partly offset by $3.840 million used in operating activities during Q1.[9] By June 30, cash remained $32.845 million, compared with $23.140 million at the prior year-end; ClearPoint attributed the higher first-half cash balance to the $16.2 million equity proceeds, partly offset by $6.5 million of operating cash use.[10][12]

The offering therefore materially strengthened near-term liquidity. It allowed ClearPoint to fund operations, maintain development and commercial activity, and retain flexibility around its $10 million convertible note rather than requiring a near-term operating cash inflow to do so. Management stated at March 31 that, based on then-current forecasts, cash balances were sufficient to support operations and obligations for at least 12 months.[10]

The financing also carried the normal economic cost of an equity raise: ClearPoint issued 2,653,848 new shares.[1] The cash received did not create a debt-repayment obligation, but it diluted pre-existing shareholders’ ownership. That trade-off was particularly relevant because ClearPoint had historically financed operations and internal growth principally through equity securities and notes payable.[1]

By September 30, the equity raise had been substantially absorbed by both operating cash use and debt retirement. ClearPoint ended the period with $21.573 million of cash and cash equivalents, $1.567 million below the $23.140 million balance at December 31, 2023, despite the $16.183 million offering proceeds.[2] The balance-sheet effect is therefore clear: the raise meaningfully extended liquidity and enabled a capital-structure decision, but it did not make operations cash generative.

The 2,653,848 shares comprised 2,307,694 shares sold at $6.50 per share and 346,154 additional shares sold through the underwriters’ option at the same price.[4]

The reported share count and offering price imply **derived gross proceeds of $17,250,012**: 2,653,848 shares × $6.50 per share. This differs from the $16.183 million cash-flow-statement amount because that amount is net of underwriting discounts, commissions and other offering expenses.[4][1]

### How did the August 2024 debt repayment affect ClearPoint's liquidity?

At June 30, 2024, ClearPoint had $10 million of principal outstanding on its secured convertible First Closing Note. The note arose under a 2020 Securities Purchase Agreement (SPA), under which ClearPoint issued secured convertible notes to two investors and raised $25 million of gross proceeds; $15 million had previously converted to common stock. The note was due to mature on January 29, 2025 unless converted or redeemed earlier, and carried a 7.3% interest rate at June 30, linked to three-month SOFR plus a margin.[1][21]

On August 23, 2024, ClearPoint repaid all amounts owing under its remaining First Closing Note: $10.0 million of principal and $0.1 million of accrued interest, or $10.1 million in total. The noteholder waived all prohibitions on prepayment and the collateral securing the note was released.[1] The cash-flow statement records the $10.0 million principal repayment.[1]

**The August transaction exchanged $10.1 million of cash for removal of a near-term debt maturity, interest burden and secured creditor claim.**

The immediate liquidity cost was substantial. Cash and cash equivalents declined from $32.845 million at June 30 to $21.573 million at September 30, a derived decrease of $11.272 million over Q3.[21] The repayment accounted for most of that decline, but not all of it: operations continued to consume cash, while other cash-flow items also affected the balance.[2] ClearPoint’s management described the repayment as an early repayment of principal and interest totaling $10.1 million and reported no outstanding debt at September 30.[22]

The balance-sheet benefit was equally tangible. ClearPoint repaid the remaining $10 million outstanding under the 2020 Securities Purchase Agreement, and the 2020 senior secured convertible note payable had a net carrying value of $9.949 million at December 31, 2023 and was zero at September 30, 2024.[1] The repayment eliminated the scheduled 2025 principal obligation, future variable-rate interest payments, and the collateral claim.

It also removed potential conversion dilution associated with the note: PTC Therapeutics stated that repayment ended its potential right to receive up to 1,666,667 ClearPoint shares through conversion.[23]

Management characterized debt retirement as providing “significant optionality” to service future capital needs.[22] In ordinary financial terms, that means ClearPoint entered the subsequent period without a scheduled debt maturity, interest-cost exposure or lender collateral restrictions.

The company still faced the need to fund operating losses and growth investment, but it could direct cash and any future financing toward commercial and strategic priorities rather than a January 2025 repayment.

The debt repayment was therefore strategically defensible if management’s liquidity forecasts and revenue-growth plans were achieved. It lowered financial risk and avoided another potential source of dilution, but it also reduced the cash cushion available if biologics and drug-delivery revenue, system utilization or partner activity were to underperform expectations.

At March 31, 2024, the First Closing Note carried a 7.3% interest rate because three-month SOFR exceeded its 2% floor. ClearPoint stated that a one- to two-percentage-point increase in interest rates would add $0.4 million to $0.5 million of annual interest expense above the floor.[10]

The more conservative interpretation is that ClearPoint had adequate reported near-term liquidity, but future funding needs depended on execution. Judgment — The central financing question is not whether ClearPoint had cash at September 30—it did—but whether its declining cash-burn trajectory can converge with revenue and gross-margin expansion before its cash reserve is materially depleted. The 2024 results show tangible progress toward that outcome. They do not yet show that the company can fund its biologics and drug-delivery transition indefinitely without further external capital.

The nine-month cash movement reconciles directly: operating cash use of $7.707 million, investing cash use of $0.012 million and financing cash provided of $6.152 million produced a $1.567 million decrease in cash and cash equivalents, from $23.140 million at December 31, 2023 to $21.573 million at September 30, 2024: $(7.707)m − $0.012m + $6.152m = $(1.567)m.[4]

ClearPoint stated that future revenue from its platform products and services may not be sufficient to offset continuing research-and-development and selling, general-and-administrative expenses.[25]

ClearPoint operates as one reportable segment. Its Chief Executive Officer, the chief operating decision maker, reviews disaggregated revenue by product line, while consolidated net income is the measure used to assess business performance and allocate resources.[26]

ClearPoint reaffirmed full-year 2024 revenue guidance of $30.0 million to $33.0 million and said it expected its three growth vectors to continue into 2025, complemented by additional FDA and CE Mark submissions.[7]

At June 30, 2024, ClearPoint had approximately $1.5 million of remaining performance obligations and expected to recognize 71% of that revenue during the following 12 months.[25]

### Implications for capital efficiency and financing

Taken together, the March raise and August repayment show active balance-sheet management rather than a contradiction. ClearPoint raised equity when it had an outstanding note approaching its January 2025 maturity, then used part of its stronger cash position to retire that note early. At September 30, it had $21.573 million in cash and no debt, compared with $23.140 million of cash and a $9.949 million net convertible-note liability at December 31, 2023.[2][1]

This sequence improved financial flexibility in one important respect: ClearPoint no longer had to reserve cash for a fixed debt maturity or service a variable-rate note. ClearPoint reported that cash and cash equivalents fell from $23.140 million at December 31, 2023 to $21.573 million at September 30, 2024 because the $10.0 million debt repayment and $7.7 million of operating cash use more than offset the $16.2 million net equity-offering proceeds.[8] The company’s ability to remain debt-free without raising additional capital will therefore depend on operational progress, not simply on the absence of scheduled debt.

The pattern also clarifies what the March financing did and did not accomplish. It funded liquidity, absorbed operating losses, and enabled early debt retirement. It did not constitute cash generated by ClearPoint’s core activities. ClearPoint’s operating cash flow remained negative, its calculated FCF was negative $7.719 million, and it remained a loss-making company pursuing operational cash-flow breakeven.[1]

Management stated that ClearPoint had over $21.6 million of cash and cash equivalents and no debt, and that a cash-flow-breakeven quarter by the end of 2025 was possible.[2] That target is plausible only if the trends visible through September continue: revenue must grow, gross margins must remain resilient, operating expense growth must remain controlled, and working-capital demands must not absorb the improvement.[24] It is a target rather than an achieved result.

ClearPoint’s Q3 2024 growth profile was broad but uneven: Biologics and Drug Delivery revenue increased 27% to $4.4 million from $3.5 million; Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy increased 49% to $2.9 million from $1.9 million; and Capital equipment and software increased 133% to $0.8 million from $0.4 million. Capital equipment and software therefore had the highest percentage growth rate, albeit from the smallest reported revenue base.[7]

Gross margin improved by 3 percentage points in Q3, to 60% in 2024 from 57% in 2023, as lower costs following the 2023 manufacturing-facility transition and higher volumes supported gross-margin improvement. These growth and margin gains had not yet made operations self-funding. For the nine months ended September 30, 2024, ClearPoint used $7.707 million in operating activities and $0.012 million in investing activities, producing derived free cash flow of negative $7.719 million and an average quarterly operating cash burn of $2.569 million. ## Segment growth and strategic positioning

Judgment — ClearPoint Neuro’s revenue comparison matters because it captures a business-model transition, not merely three unrelated sales lines. The company is extending an established neurosurgical-device franchise into products and services that support pharmaceutical partners developing cell, gene and other biologic therapies for direct delivery to the brain. That transition changes both the source and the timing of revenue: a hospital equipment placement can lead to recurring disposable-product sales, while a biologics partner may first buy consulting and preclinical services and later buy devices as its program advances.

### What business-segment mix and strategic transition are needed to interpret

ClearPoint describes its foundational business as medical devices for neurosurgery applications and its second business component as partnerships in biologics and drug delivery. Its established ClearPoint system combines hardware, disposable components and software for minimally invasive brain procedures, including deep-brain stimulation, biopsy, laser-catheter placement and pharmaceutical infusion. Since 2021, the company has expanded beyond the MRI suite into operating-room products and has derived a growing part of revenue from services for pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, academic institutions and contract research organizations.[1][21]

ClearPoint has described neurosurgical medical devices and biologics-and-drug-delivery partnerships as its two business components since 2020.[26]

The three labels used in ClearPoint’s Q3 2024 revenue disclosure are **Biologics and Drug Delivery**, **Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy**, and **Capital equipment and software**. ClearPoint describes revenue more broadly as neurosurgery-navigation, therapy and biologics-and-drug-delivery disposable-product sales; capital-equipment and software sales; preclinical- and clinical-trial service and case-support revenue; SmartFlow Cannula license revenue; and capital-equipment service, installation, training and shipping revenue. These are revenue service lines—or product-line views of the business—not as three separately managed accounting businesses: ClearPoint states that it operates in one industry segment.[1]

- **Biologics and Drug Delivery:** This line includes sales of disposable products and services related to customer-sponsored preclinical and clinical trials. Preclinical means research performed before testing in humans; clinical trials are studies involving people to assess therapies. ClearPoint’s offering includes protocol consultation, preclinical study design and execution, clinical-trial support, regulatory consultation and development of devices used to administer therapies into the central nervous system. ### What does the divergent performance between biologics/drug delivery and capital

The Q3 divergence is not a choice between a legacy device business and a new biologics business. It is evidence that ClearPoint is building both sides of a platform: hospital-based navigation and therapy infrastructure, and partner-facing delivery products and services for biologics development.[8][14][22]

Biologics and Drug Delivery grew 27% to $4.4 million, driven by increased disposable-product demand as multiple partners progressed through trials. At the same time, fewer preclinical studies reduced service and other revenue by $0.7 million.[7][22]

Capital equipment and software grew 133% to $0.8 million because ClearPoint placed more navigation capital equipment, software and Prism laser units. This reflects rapid installed-base expansion, but the category remained far smaller than Biologics and Drug Delivery in Q3 revenue terms.[7]

The dual pattern shows that ClearPoint’s strategic positioning is broader than a pure clinical-services model. It is using clinical equipment and software placements to broaden access to navigation and laser-therapy procedures while using biologics partnerships to place its delivery technology, cannulas, consulting and trial support into pharmaceutical-development workflows.[26][7]

The biologics offering is designed to make ClearPoint relevant before a partner’s therapy is commercial. Its services include preclinical studies, clinical-trial support, regulatory consultation, device-development work and surgical-workflow guidance. The company also generates revenue from SmartFlow cannula products, consulting, development services and partnership arrangements.[26]

This earlier-stage involvement may deepen customer relationships and create product demand as a partner advances. Management’s description of the customer lifecycle is explicit: early work tends to carry higher service content, while later-stage work tends to require more navigation and infusion products. The Q3 shift toward higher biologics product revenue and lower services revenue is consistent with some partners moving along that progression.[24][22]

The strategic attraction is that ClearPoint does not fund pharmaceutical partners’ expensive drug-development trials. Management described the company as diversified across partners and diseases while avoiding the capital burden of funding those trials itself; it also said expanded preclinical capabilities could support operational cash breakeven without requiring a partner drug to be commercialized.[37][17]

The strategic exposure is that ClearPoint’s biologics growth depends substantially on decisions and outcomes outside its direct control. Customers must retain ClearPoint, continue research and development, progress through clinical trials and obtain regulatory approvals for their therapies. A customer’s program delay, discontinuation or trial failure can delay or remove expected demand for services and delivery products.[1][26]

The Q3 biologics result therefore has two signals. First, growing disposable demand suggests active partner-program progression. Second, falling service revenue illustrates the volatility of project-based preclinical work and confirms that revenue can shift among service and product components as trial timing changes.[22][24]

Capital equipment provides a different strategic signal. More placements of navigation capital, software and Prism units broaden the company’s clinical footprint. More installed sites can support future procedure volumes, disposable use, training activity and adoption of newer operating-room and laser products.[7][9]

At the time of the Q3 2024 discussion, SmartFrame OR had been installed at roughly six or seven of ClearPoint’s approximately 90 accounts, with another approximately 10 accounts in advanced hospital value-analysis discussions.[24]

ClearPoint reported eight new hospital customer activations in Q1 2024, which brought its global-center count to more than 80 sites. Management said those centers were undergoing training and were expected to support case-volume and disposable-revenue growth beginning in Q2 through both the legacy MRI-guided system and SmartFrame OR.[9]

ClearPoint described its historical annual activation rate as six to eight customers. It reported more than 90 active global customers and more than 20 activations by its November 7, 2024 call, and expected to reach 100 activated global sites by the first half of 2025, if not sooner.[8][24]

The subsequent Q3 49% growth in Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy, attributed to new account activations, higher case counts and SmartFrame OR and Prism offerings, is consistent with that installation-and-utilization strategy beginning to translate into product consumption.[7]

Judgment — In medical technology, capital equipment is often an adoption enabler rather than the ultimate earnings engine. Hospitals need the reusable platform before they can use procedure-specific disposables; therefore, a period of rising system placements can precede a period of recurring consumable revenue. ClearPoint’s reported mix fits that commercial logic.

The divergence also matters for margin interpretation. Management said capital-equipment margins were in the 35%–45% range and that a quarter with several system sales could lower total gross margin through mix. Management’s 70%-plus margin scenario assumes $50 million to $70 million of annual disposable revenue while capital revenue remains roughly flat; it includes drug-delivery cannulas.[22]

That margin contrast does not diminish the strategic value of capital placements. Rather, it makes the sequencing clear: equipment can be lower-margin infrastructure that creates a base for higher-margin disposables, while biologics programs can create demand for trial and eventually commercial delivery products.[26][22]

ClearPoint’s own 2024 strategy combined these routes. It aimed to expand biologics and drug-delivery services and customers, add long-term pharmaceutical-partner agreements, support partners’ first-in-human studies, expand SmartFrame OR use in operating rooms, grow the Prism installed base and activate capital customers.[38]

Management also identified four growth pillars: biologics and drug delivery, neurosurgery navigation, therapy and access products, and global scale. Those pillars do not map exactly onto the three revenue categories, but they show that management views drug delivery and clinical-device adoption as complementary sources of growth.[22][34]

The divergence between Q3 biologics and capital-equipment growth is therefore strategically coherent. Capital equipment’s 133% increase reflects active placement of reusable systems from a small base; Biologics and Drug Delivery’s 27% increase reflects a larger, partner-linked line in which product demand rose as services declined.[7][22]

The decision implication is that ClearPoint should be evaluated on conversion, not on any one quarterly growth rate alone. The key conversion chain is: equipment placement to account activation; account activation to cases; cases to disposable demand; and biologics service engagement to recurring trial-product demand, commercial supply arrangements, milestones or royalties.[26][16][22]

The practical consequence is that rapid equipment growth strengthens the commercial infrastructure, while biologics growth expands ClearPoint’s role in pharmaceutical partners’ development programs. Together, trial-related disposable demand and FDA De Novo marketing authorization for the SmartFlow Neuro Cannula to deliver eladocagene exuparvovec-tneq for AADC deficiency show that ClearPoint’s biologics activity spans partner-program use and a product-specific regulatory milestone.[8][26]

Management said commercial device pricing is at a premium to non-clinical and clinical-trial device pricing.[22]

ClearPoint states that pharmaceutical and biotechnology customers rely on predictable pricing to fund research and development; if pricing flexibility is constrained, those customers may limit pipeline spending and adversely affect future Biologics and Drug Delivery revenue.[26]

Capital equipment and software revenue rose 133% year over year from $0.4 million to $0.8 million in Q3 2024, reflecting more placements from a comparatively small prior-year base.[42]

### ClearPoint Neuro's existing business mix

ClearPoint’s Q3 category growth was supported by a manufacturing transition completed before the quarter. The company moved manufacturing to a Carlsbad, California facility, reported in Q3 2023 that the facility was producing and shipping sellable products, and later reported certification of the Carlsbad development and manufacturing facility and shutdown of the Irvine manufacturing facility.[43][44]

The Carlsbad manufacturing-facility lease began in June 2023. ClearPoint recorded an approximately $2.5 million right-of-use asset and matching operating-lease liability at commencement; the later annual disclosure describes a lease term from June 1, 2023 through May 31, 2033.[1][26]

The transition carried near-term costs. ClearPoint reported a Q3 2023 gross margin of 57%, down 14 percentage points from 71% in Q3 2022. ClearPoint attributed the decline primarily to increased lower-margin biologics and drug-delivery preclinical services and to costs of the transition to the new manufacturing facility.[45]

By 2024, the company attributed gross-margin improvement to lower costs following the 2023 facility transition and to higher volumes. The detailed gross-margin comparison belongs in **Gross margins and manufacturing scalability**, but the strategic implication for this section is direct: the new manufacturing platform was intended to support a growing mix of navigation, therapy and drug-delivery disposables.[1][11]

ClearPoint’s manufacturing model combines in-house assembly with outside production of some components. The company assembles the ClearPoint system and SmartFlow cannula in Carlsbad, while using third parties for certain components to use supplier expertise, minimize ClearPoint’s capital investment and help control costs.[26]

Judgment — This is a common medtech scaling structure. Outsourcing specialized component production limits the fixed capital required for manufacturing, while retaining final assembly and quality control can protect product configuration, regulatory control and supply coordination. It can improve capital efficiency if volumes grow without requiring a proportionate expansion in owned manufacturing assets.

ClearPoint’s Q3 2024 product-volume indicators are consistent with rising use of that operating platform. Biologics product demand increased as multiple partners progressed in trials; Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy product revenue rose because of newly activated accounts, increased case counts and new products; and capital-equipment revenue rose through more placements of navigation, software and Prism systems.[1][7]

The company’s factory-throughput comments link volume to margin. Management said product sales more than doubled year over year in Q3 and that increased throughput at its factory had a positive effect on gross margin.[22]

The relationship is economically intuitive. Manufacturing facilities carry fixed costs—quality systems, supervisors, equipment depreciation, rent and process overhead—that can be spread across more units as output rises. Higher throughput can therefore improve gross margin if pricing and component costs remain stable. ClearPoint’s reported improvement was attributed to both lower transition costs and higher volumes.[1][11]

ClearPoint’s product mix is particularly relevant to this scale effect. Cost of revenue includes components and assembly for navigation products, biologics and drug-delivery products, therapy products and capital equipment, plus labor for preclinical, consulting and service revenue, manufacturing overhead, depreciation on loaned systems, and provisions for obsolete, impaired or excess inventory. The margin consequence of growth therefore depends on the mix between disposables, capital equipment and labor-intensive services.[26]

Biologics expansion can initially dilute margin when service work is labor intensive. ClearPoint stated that its Q3 2023 margin decline was primarily caused by increased biologics and drug-delivery preclinical services, which had lower margins at that time as the company launched new services and expanded its presence in that area.[43]

Later-stage biologics work may have a different mix. Q3 2024 biologics product revenue rose sharply as partners progressed through trials, while service revenue declined. If that progression produces sustained disposable-product demand, the resulting mix may be more favorable than an early-stage, service-heavy mix; this is a strategic inference from ClearPoint’s stated lifecycle and volume comments, not a reported segment-margin result.[22]

The Carlsbad facility also supports international activity. ClearPoint reported that a first product had been approved under European MDR and that products were shipped to Europe from the new Carlsbad development and manufacturing facility.[44]

The manufacturing transition eliminated a redundant site but concentrated operations. ClearPoint states that it has no redundant facilities and conducts research and development, component processing, final assembly, packaging and distribution for most products at its Southern California facility.[26]

Supplier concentration is a related scale constraint. ClearPoint buys most custom-made ClearPoint-system components from single-source suppliers, although it has identified alternatives for certain components and believes alternatives may be available for others. It generally purchases through purchase orders rather than long-term contracts.[26]

Judgment — A single-site, single-source-dependent manufacturing model can be efficient at current scale, but it makes resilience an operating requirement. As placement activity and disposable demand rise, the company must ensure that quality, inventory, supplier qualification and contingency planning scale with demand; otherwise, supply interruptions could reduce the revenue and margin benefits of higher utilization.

ClearPoint’s 2024 commitments demonstrate that scale was a management priority. It planned to expand biologics and drug-delivery services and customers, add pharmaceutical-partner agreements, support first-in-human studies, expand SmartFrame OR, grow Prism installations and activate new capital customers.[38]

ClearPoint also stated an aim to demonstrate operating leverage, meaning expenses should increase more slowly than revenue. That is the central profitability test for the transition: whether added product, service and installation revenue can be supported without proportionate increases in manufacturing, clinical-support, sales and administrative costs.[46]

The manufacturing move is therefore strategically important in two ways. First, it removed transition-related inefficiency and enabled sellable-product shipments from Carlsbad. Second, it gives ClearPoint a production base for greater disposable and equipment volume, although the financial benefit depends on maintaining throughput and supply continuity.[43][1][26]

For the first nine months of 2024, the same product-line pattern persisted: Biologics and Drug Delivery revenue increased 37% to $13.065 million, Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy increased 14% to $7.373 million, and Capital equipment and software increased 180% to $3.181 million.[4]

ClearPoint anticipated that research and development costs may increase as it develops central-nervous-system therapeutic-delivery devices and services, expands products into operating-room and therapeutics applications, and expands the application of its technological platforms internationally.[4]

ClearPoint also stated that certain manufacturing components had relatively few alternative sources of supply and that establishing additional or replacement suppliers could not be accomplished quickly. A supplier’s inability to meet ClearPoint’s requirements could negatively affect future operating results.[25]

For Q3 2024, disposable-product revenue in Neurosurgery Navigation and Therapy increased 53%, and year-to-date disposable-product revenue increased 33%; Biologics and Drug Delivery disposable-product revenue increased 353% in Q3 and 207% year to date. ClearPoint linked the neurosurgery and therapy growth to newly activated accounts, higher case counts, SmartFrame OR and Prism Laser Therapy.[47]

For the three months ended June 30, 2024, gross margin was 63%, compared with 53% for the three months ended June 30, 2023, a 10-percentage-point increase. ClearPoint attributed the increase primarily to lower costs after the 2023 transition to the new manufacturing facility and to higher 2024 volumes.[11]

In its August 7, 2024 release, ClearPoint raised its full-year 2024 revenue outlook to $30.0 million to $33.0 million. ClearPoint stated that these facilities were sufficient for current purposes and that it could obtain additional space on commercially reasonable terms as needed.[26]

For the six months ended June 30, 2024, research-and-development expense fell $0.9 million, or 13%, to $5.7 million from $6.6 million, primarily because reprioritized research-and-development initiatives reduced product and software-development costs by $1.1 million, partly offset by $0.2 million higher personnel costs. ## Gross margins and manufacturing scalability

Judgment — Gross margin is gross profit divided by revenue: it shows how much of each sales dollar remains after the direct costs of products and services, before research, selling, and corporate costs. For ClearPoint Neuro, margin is a central test of whether growth in biologics and drug delivery, disposable products, and procedure volumes is beginning to absorb manufacturing costs more efficiently during the shift from a primarily navigation-capital business toward a broader product-and-services platform.

The conclusion is that Q3 2024 demonstrated a measurable improvement in manufacturing economics—60% reported gross margin versus 57%—but the result remains a consolidated, mix-sensitive measure rather than proof that the biologics-and-drug-delivery model has reached its mature margin structure.[1][22]

Before the move to Carlsbad, final assembly of many disposable components took place in Irvine, California.[59]

Those design features matter because ClearPoint must assemble, package, and distribute regulated medical-device products while serving a growing number of hospital and biopharma customers.

**The Irvine-to-Carlsbad transition moved ClearPoint from a period of construction, qualification, equipment spending, and transition costs in 2023 to an operating, certified production-and-shipping base in 2024.**[6][43][44][1]

The near-term economics were mixed. In Q2 2023, management said transition costs contributed to the lower gross margin.[28] By Q3 2024, the company said lower costs resulting from the completed transition helped lift gross margin.[1] This sequence supports a conclusion of improving operating leverage after a one-time operational disruption: ClearPoint incurred costs to consolidate manufacturing, then began to gain from lower ongoing costs and higher utilization.

Carlsbad also gives the company a base for a broader biologics-and-drug-delivery product portfolio. Management said that if the facility reaches meaningful scale, disposable-product gross margin could reach 70%.[24] That is a management expectation conditional on scale, not a reported current margin.

The key implication is not that 70% is assured, but that the economic value of the new site depends on moving beyond small, irregular orders toward repeatable disposable-product throughput.

The facility transition makes a more scalable operating model possible by removing duplicate manufacturing arrangements and centralizing production, but it also creates a longer-term fixed operating commitment and concentrates manufacturing execution in one location.[1]

### What relevant volume increases affect operational scalability?

ClearPoint reported several Q3 2024 indicators of increased commercial activity. It activated five new global centers in the quarter, bringing the year-to-date total to 19—approximately three times its historic activation rate. The company also reported higher disposable demand as multiple biopharma partners progressed in their trials, higher case counts, and more placements of navigation capital, software, and Prism laser units.[7][8]

These sources of activity matter because ClearPoint’s commercial model has two reinforcing volume pathways. Biologics and drug delivery generates product and service revenue from customer-sponsored preclinical and clinical trials, including disposable products used in delivery procedures. Neurosurgery navigation and therapy growth comes from new hospital accounts, increasing use at existing accounts, cases performed, capital placements, and newer products such as SmartFrame OR and Prism.[7][1]

ClearPoint said the Q3 2024 increase in total revenue reflected a $1.6 million rise in product revenue, driven by disposable demand as partners advanced trials, partly offset by a $0.7 million decline in service and other revenue because fewer preclinical studies were performed.[1] This distinction is important: disposable demand increased even as the number of preclinical studies fell, illustrating that service revenue and product revenue do not necessarily move together in a given quarter.

The company identified a second volume driver in navigation and therapy. For Q3 2024, it attributed a $1.0 million increase in product revenue to newly activated accounts, increased case count, and new offerings including SmartFrame OR and Prism Laser Therapy.[1] Capital equipment and software revenue also rose 133% to $0.8 million from $0.4 million, due to increased placements of navigation capital, software, and Prism laser units.[8] Capital placements can carry lower immediate gross margins, but they can expand the hospital installed base that later consumes disposables and related services.

**The 19 new center activations, higher case counts, advancing partner trials, and stronger disposable demand show that ClearPoint was expanding both the installed base and utilization of that base during 2024.**[7][8]

Management’s factory-throughput comments link this activity to gross-margin mechanics. It said product sales more than doubled year over year in Q3 2024 and that higher factory throughput positively affected gross margin.[24] Higher throughput means a greater volume of products moves through the same production infrastructure. When additional units use existing facility, equipment, quality-control, and supervisory capacity, the manufacturing cost allocated to each unit can decline.

Management also described the prospective scale mechanism for cannulas used in drug-delivery studies. It contrasted small purchases of five to ten prototype cannulas with potential orders of roughly 100 or 200 cannulas for larger preclinical or clinical trials. Larger orders could be produced in batches rather than as many small shipments through sterilization, which management said would create margin expansion.[16] The 100–200-cannula quantities are illustrative examples, not reported Q3 production volume.

Management extended the same reasoning to Good Laboratory Practice, or GLP, studies. GLP is a quality-system framework for nonclinical safety studies that helps make work acceptable to regulators. ClearPoint described potential GLP studies involving 50 or 60 subjects over six weeks rather than one or two subjects at a time, which could permit more efficient batching of procedures and support gross-margin expansion.[16] At Q3 2024, ClearPoint said it was expanding preclinical-services capacity, targeted GLP readiness during 2024, and planned to commence its initial GLP studies in 2025.[16][24]

The volume data therefore show a credible route to improved scalability: more activated centers and cases can increase recurring procedure-related demand, while larger partner studies can change production from bespoke small-batch work toward more efficient batches. The outcome depends on actual conversion of those sites and partner programs into sustained product orders, not simply on placement or activation counts.[7][8][16][24]

The pace of account expansion accelerated throughout 2024. ClearPoint added eight customers in Q1 2024, compared with seven during all of 2023, then reported six new global-center activations in Q2, taking the year-to-date total to 14 before the Q3 total reached 19. Management said a newly placed system could ramp within about three months and that a hospital could place a stocking or par-level order three to six months after installation.[36][51][41]

Across all categories, ClearPoint’s Q2 2024 product revenue rose 112% year over year to $4.9 million.[11]

Biologics and Drug Delivery revenue rose 28% year over year to $4.3 million in Q2 2024: a $1.3 million product-revenue increase was partly offset by a $0.4 million decline in services and other revenue.[11]

ClearPoint reported more than 90 active customers, targeted double-digit annual hospital placements, was in advanced discussions with more than 150 hospitals about operating-room technology, and sought an installed base of 100 sites by 2025.[11][60][34][54]

Disposable-product growth was particularly strong in Q3 2024: biologics and drug-delivery disposable-product revenue grew 353% year over year, while neurosurgery navigation-and-therapy disposable-product revenue grew 53%. For the year to date, biologics-and-drug-delivery disposable-product revenue grew 207%, while neurosurgery-and-therapy disposable-product revenue grew 33%. These segment product-growth measures align with ClearPoint’s reported increased trial-related disposable demand, higher case counts, and higher factory throughput.[47][4][24]

This complements the later-stage batch examples discussed above and identifies how advancing partner programs can raise product and service volumes.[36]

### How do manufacturing facility transitions and volume increases affect operational

ClearPoint’s results indicate that the manufacturing transition and volume growth were beginning to work together. The company completed the Carlsbad transition in 2023, and in 2024 it attributed improved gross margin to lower post-transition costs and higher volume.[1] Q3 2024’s 60% gross margin, compared with 57% a year earlier, and management’s statement that increased factory throughput aided margin, are consistent with a manufacturing operation becoming more efficiently utilized.[1][24]

Judgment — This is the operating-leverage case. If production capacity, quality systems, and factory overhead are already in place, a larger number of similar disposable products can increase gross profit faster than the direct cost of making them. That effect is especially valuable for a medical-technology company because regulated clean-room production and quality control create fixed costs that can burden margins when output is low.

The expected profitability benefit is strongest if ClearPoint’s revenue mix moves toward recurring disposable products and services rather than episodic capital-equipment sales. Management described capital-equipment margins as approximately 35% to 45%, while stating that meaningful scale at Carlsbad could support a 70% gross margin on disposable products, including cannulas used in drug-delivery trials.[22][24] It also said that if disposable revenue ultimately reached $50 million to $70 million annually while capital revenue stayed roughly flat, total margin could move comfortably into the 70% range.[22] These are forward-looking mix scenarios, not achieved company-wide margin results.

The strategic logic is that capital placements may initially depress consolidated gross margin but establish hospital access and procedure capacity. More procedures can then generate recurring sales of disposables and related services. ClearPoint’s reported growth in center activations, cases, disposable demand, and placement activity is evidence that both the installed-base and recurring-use sides of that model were developing in Q3 2024.[7][8] Management said more than 95% of procedures occur in the operating room and identified expansion of navigation into that setting as a growth opportunity.

It also said SmartFrame OR can often work with existing hospital equipment without ClearPoint hardware or software capital components, which may broaden access to operating-room procedures and reduce the need for a capital placement in every use case.[37]

The same business-model evolution creates cost and execution demands. Cost of revenue includes direct component and assembly cost, labor for preclinical and consulting services, manufacturing overhead, depreciation of loaned systems, and provisions for obsolete or excess inventory.[26] Expanding preclinical services can therefore raise service labor and facility costs before the associated program reaches enough scale to improve economics.

This was visible in 2023, when ClearPoint said a larger contribution from biologics and drug-delivery preclinical services had a lower margin than prior-year mix.[18]

Scalability is also constrained by manufacturing concentration. ClearPoint’s final assembly of many product components occurs at Carlsbad, and it stated that it had no backup manufacturing facility. A disruption could halt assembly until the facility was restored or replicated elsewhere.[26] The company also relies on single-source suppliers for many custom components and generally does not maintain large inventories of components, component assemblies, or finished products.[26] These arrangements can preserve capital and reduce inventory carrying costs in normal operations, but they increase the risk that supply disruption limits the ability to meet rising demand.

ClearPoint does use third parties to manufacture certain components, stating that this approach employs supplier expertise, minimizes its capital investment, and helps control costs; it assembles the ClearPoint system and SmartFlow cannula in Carlsbad.[26] That hybrid model can be capital-efficient because it avoids building every production capability internally, while retaining final assembly and quality control. Its effectiveness depends on supplier reliability, regulatory compliance, and the company’s ability to add capacity without disrupting controlled-environment production.

The company also identified future growth as operationally demanding. It said that continued growth requires expanding assembly capacity and increasing production, and it could not be certain that systems, controls, infrastructure, and personnel would be adequate for future operations.[26] The implication is direct: rising product demand is favorable only if Carlsbad, suppliers, quality systems, and clinical-support resources scale at least as fast as demand.

ClearPoint warned of potential difficulties scaling production while maintaining yield and quality assurance, securing skilled labor, and avoiding products that fail specification.[59]

The appropriate interpretation is that ClearPoint had begun to demonstrate manufacturing operating leverage by Q3 2024, rather than that future profitability had been secured. Lower costs after the facility transition, higher volume, and the three-point gross-margin improvement provide the positive evidence; lower-margin early-stage services, capital-equipment mix, single-site assembly, and supplier concentration define the limits on how quickly and reliably that advantage can expand.[1][18][22][26]

ClearPoint’s cost-of-revenue definition identifies the operating items affected by this scaling: direct component and assembly costs for neurosurgery-navigation, biologics-and-drug-delivery, non-neurosurgery-therapy, and capital-equipment products; labor hours for preclinical and consulting services; allocated manufacturing overhead; depreciation of loaned systems installed under the ClearPoint placement program; and provisions for obsolete, impaired, or excess inventory. Higher throughput can improve the absorption of allocated manufacturing overhead, while expanding service work increases labor requirements.[39]

In its September 2024 quarterly report, ClearPoint stated that future product-and-service revenue may not be sufficient to offset continuing research-and-development and selling, general-and-administrative expenses. It anticipated that research-and-development costs may increase as it develops central-nervous-system therapeutic-delivery devices and services, expands products into operating-room and therapeutics applications, and expands internationally. This means that gross-margin improvement at Carlsbad must be accompanied by sufficient revenue growth to cover the wider operating-cost base.[4]

The manufacturing-scale plan also included future operator hiring and manufacturing-automation studies. In February 2025, management said that manufacturing operators would both build product and participate in automation studies as part of its path toward 70% gross margins in the years ahead. Management also expected to add preclinical-team capacity and Good Laboratory Practice capabilities in 2025 to support larger and more complex FDA-required studies, with more meaningful revenue expected in 2026.[50]

ClearPoint reported an operating loss of $14.142 million for the first nine months of 2024, compared with $17.740 million for the corresponding 2023 period.[15]

ClearPoint said its biologics-and-drug-delivery business potentially represents its largest growth opportunity, but growth depends on retaining and establishing pharmaceutical-customer relationships, customers continuing research and development, successful clinical trials, regulatory approvals, and commercial success for customers’ therapies, including reimbursement, physician adoption, and patient access.[29][21]

ClearPoint said that some manufacturing components have relatively few alternative supply sources and that additional or replacement suppliers cannot be established quickly.[21] The ClearPoint Prism Neuro Laser Therapy System is manufactured exclusively by CLS.[26]

Replacing a supplier can require validation of the new supplier’s facility and procedures, delaying ClearPoint’s ability to meet demand.[59]

ClearPoint stated that failure by it or third-party suppliers to comply with applicable manufacturing regulations, or to respond timely and adequately to adverse inspectional observations, could impair cost-effective and timely production. It also stated that failure to comply with FDA Quality Management System Regulation requirements or applicable state equivalents could require compliance costs that negatively affect future sales and profitability.[26]

ClearPoint reported full-year 2024 operating expenses of $38.9 million, up 8% from $36.1 million in 2023, mainly because personnel-related expenses, including share-based compensation, increased as it expanded research-and-development, clinical, and support headcount.[52]

Management said the timing of growth from expanded preclinical-services capacity and GLP capabilities depended on when the expanded facility became available, identifying June, September, and December as possible timing points.[50]

ClearPoint attributed its Q3 2024 gross-margin improvement to higher volumes and efficiencies from the new manufacturing facility. At the same time, full-year 2024 operating expenses increased 8% to $38.9 million from $36.1 million, and the new Carlsbad site added $0.5 million of rent and occupancy costs. Better manufacturing economics therefore did not by themselves reduce the wider cost base.[15][52][50]

In Q3 2024, revenue increased 41% year over year, gross margin improved by three percentage points to 60%, and quarterly operational cash burn declined 33% to $1.2 million. Operating expenses nevertheless increased to $10.0 million from $8.2 million, and operating loss increased to $5.172 million from $4.897 million. Better manufacturing economics had not yet translated into improved operating profitability.[2][14]

Higher volume improves profitability only when pricing, product mix, material costs, labor requirements, and production yields remain favorable enough for additional revenue to produce incremental gross profit. If volume requires substantial new capacity, materials, or labor, the expected fixed-cost absorption benefit can be reduced.

### Synthesis

ClearPoint’s margin history maps the economics of its transition. First-half gross margin fell from a derived 63.4% in 2022 to 55.6% in 2023 as lower-margin preclinical services expanded and the Carlsbad facility transition imposed costs.[28][6] By Q3 2024, the completed Irvine-to-Carlsbad move, lower manufacturing costs, higher volume, and greater factory throughput coincided with a rise in reported gross margin from 57% to 60%.[1][24]

The strongest implication is emerging, not complete, operating leverage. Rapid center activation, more cases, advancing partner trials, and higher disposable demand can increase utilization of Carlsbad and improve unit economics.[7][8] The larger margin opportunity depends on a recurring disposable mix and large study batches, while lower-margin capital sales, service labor, single-site assembly, and supplier concentration remain material constraints.[16][22][26]

ClearPoint’s investor presentation characterized its model as a razor/razorblade model, with approximately 95% of revenue from single-use disposables and services.[28]

### Open questions

The timing of sustained reported operating-cash-flow breakeven, the durability of biologics and drug-delivery demand, and the amount and timing of any future financing remain open. They will be settled by subsequent quarterly operating cash flow, segment revenue and gross-margin performance, working-capital needs, and management’s actual financing actions.
