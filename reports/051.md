Adopt a common modular cookline and supplier-service specification, but award and commission it locally rather than forcing one identical kitchen into Singapore, Bangkok, Manila and Jakarta. Singapore is the best launch market for the standard platform because it has the strongest documented integrated equipment, exhaust-maintenance and 24/7 repair routes, while Bangkok is the strongest fit for a gas-led heavy wok and grill line.

Manila is the best broad-line alternative through Fabtech, with Foodmach for Asian cooking, and Jakarta should use Rotaryana for project delivery subject to contracted response and parts terms. Keep dedicated gas wok and clay/gas tandoor capacity where flame tossing, wall-baked naan or char are menu-critical, while using combi and induction equipment for repeatable batch work and constrained sites.

Electricity economics require site-level modelling, with Singapore’s stated Q4 2026 tariff of S$0.3116/kWh making idle-load control particularly material, Bangkok’s demand charges making electric peak load material, and Jakarta and Manila requiring the applicable customer tariff and utility capacity to be confirmed.[1][2][3][4]

Ventilation is a particularly important Singapore standardization issue. Singapore’s Fire Code requires a mechanical exhaust system for the cooking area of a restaurant, hotel, coffee house or similar establishment to be independent of systems serving other parts of the building.[6] Singapore food-establishment guidance says that grease accumulation in kitchen exhaust systems and ducting is a cause of fires and that regular maintenance can prevent it.[109] The Fire Code requires the entire kitchen exhaust system, including interior and exterior ducts and hoods, to be degreased and cleaned at least once every 12 months by a specialist; the operator must retain cleaning and degreasing records.[31] Records must state the maintenance company, contact number, cleaning method and inspection or cleaning date and include dated photographic evidence.[31]

YEK describes make-up air as outdoor air brought in to replace exhausted air.[110] It states that inadequate make-up air causes negative pressure, weakens exhaust performance and makes conditions uncomfortable for staff and customers.[110] Replacement air must replace air exhausted through hoods and fans; insufficient replacement air creates excessive negative building pressure and reduces exhaust flow, hood capture and containment.[111] In Singapore’s hot, humid climate, make-up air must be tempered because untempered supply adds latent load and can cause condensation on cold surfaces.[112] A Singapore high-heat wok/grill line therefore needs a matched hood, duct, fan, fire-suppression, tempered make-up-air and commissioning package.[111][112]
